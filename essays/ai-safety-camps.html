Overview

Terence Tao says "we have to slow down AI, the pace is insane."1 Steven Pinker calls extinction scenarios "preposterous" and says the presumption of inevitability encourages "fatalism, panic, and distraction from the more mundane and realistic safety challenges."2 The debate also covers whether AI is under control today, what labs must prove before shipping, whether labs can be trusted, and threats in biology and cyber.34567

The case for concern

Paul Christiano sees "a meaningful risk" of "catastrophic and irreversible loss of control in the very near term."8 Rob Bensinger thinks that if the world does nothing to stop the race within 18 months, there is "a double-digit chance" that "we'll end up dead."9 Yoshua Bengio urged immediate action.10

Jakub Pachocki says no lab has solved alignment and monitoring well enough to keep scaling responsibly at maximum speed for much longer.11 Eli Lifland says "If they don’t slow down, AI takeover is ~67%."12 Roko thinks "we should expect to die within a single digit number of years with ~80% probability."13 Abdul El-Sayed warns that without quick action "we may get to a point at which we cannot act."14

Nate Soares says the AI situation "is not under control" and that "There's no adults in the room."3 Garrison Lovely says "No one has a plan for steering and controlling actually smart AI systems."15 Neel Nanda calls the OpenAI and Hugging Face incident the most striking alignment failure so far.16 Amjad Masad thinks labs should "pace and slow down," or else face criminal liability for "hacking core internet infrastructure."17

David Robinson says "the time for trial and error is over," because "iteration after a mistake may not be possible."18 He wants frontier labs to run "like nuclear-power plants or busy airports."19 Ryan Lowe also calls for nuclear-style layered safety at the labs.2019 OpenAI wants structured safety documentation required before continuing any frontier reinforcement learning training run, with the safety case reviewed by senior leaders who can each veto the run.2122

Gabriel Weil says "you do not want to let the AI developer hire their own referees."23 Dario Amodei wants a team of embedded third-party evaluators, such as METR, with "ongoing employee-like access."24 Drake Thomas wants oversight from "unimpeachably boring and independent sources."25

Daniel Kokotajlo says the labs will "almost certainly" not be able to maintain control.5 Micah Lee says the American frontier labs are run by "unhinged lunatics" who believe "they're creating the Machine God."26

Eric Nguyen says that on biosecurity, "defense is currently losing this battle."6 Anthropic's experiments suggest small AI models "may be months away" from being able to copy themselves onto commercial internet-connected devices.7 Anthropic adds that such a replicator would be hard to shut down, "since there’s no centralized point to shut off."27

The case against

Those on the other side think fears of extinction are overstated, or that remedies like a pause or open-model bans would do harm.282930 Yann LeCun is not worried "at all" about AI wiping out humanity and has "zero concerns" about recent rogue AI incidents.31 Yann LeCun adds that saying "AI can kill us all" is "incredibly destructive for everybody."32 Andrew Lenson thinks such scenarios are "quite far off."28

Some agree that labs use apocalyptic messaging as a calculated way to market themselves.3334 According to thecivilian.co.nz, labs now seek "to woo customers" by claiming their model is "currently most capable of ending the human race."33 Kirsten Korosec says the one important thing that happened was "a rebranding exercise around AI."34

Divyansh Kaushik says a pause right now "would probably throw the economy into a bad recession."29 Andrew Feldman thinks the AI doomer narrative "isn’t doing the industry any favors."35

Interconnects thinks closed models' cyber abilities will very likely outpace their guardrails, so banning only open models would increase "the offense-defense cyber gap."30 Interconnects also suggests the trope "Open Dangerous, Closed Safe" may be closer to "Open Unsafe, Closed Unsafe."36 Saad Hossain is optimistic about safeguards for open models because "it's a very active area of research."37

What backs each side

Few views on either side cite data or a specific case.recrec On extinction risk, 7 of the 12 views agreeing give no reasons, as do 2 of the 4 disagreeing.rec

Anthropic backs its cyber warnings with its own tests, which found small models setting up on machines "from a Jetson Nano to a Mac."38 Noam Brown cites studies in which two air-gapped computers still communicated through temperature sensors.39 Nate Soares points to a specific case, asking why nobody else found the sensitive information the Hugging Face agent swarm left behind.3 Roko and Eli Lifland give no reasons for their odds.1312

Yann LeCun cites a specific case: OpenAI's agents autonomously hacking Hugging Face in July.31 Interconnects' case against restricting open-weight models rests on reasoning.403036 Steven Pinker, Andrew Lenson and Divyansh Kaushik give no reasons for their views.22829

Where the camps break down

Several people hold views on opposite sides of different questions, or on both sides of the same one.4414243

Sam Altman says that whether the risk of catastrophe is put at 12% or .1%, "None of these levels are remotely acceptable."44 He also says "the world should accept some bad things happening" for the technology's benefits.41 Jensen Huang says there are "plenty of incentives" for labs "to do it right."45 He also thinks that, under some conditions, "we have to shut the labs down."46

Zvi Mowshowitz says "We urgently need to slow down, relative to what will happen by default."42 He also says a case of logging into the Census Bureau with misappropriated credentials leaves him "not quaking in my boots."47 Thariq Shihipar, speaking only for himself, disagrees that AI poses a serious risk of human extinction.48 He also says smarter models could "hack any constraint that you put on them if we’re not very careful."49

Dean W. Ball says he would be far more on the "full steam ahead" side if he knew interpretability research, which aims at understanding models, was on its way to being solved.5051 He also thinks alignment is "primarily a science problem" in which progress is possible.52 Roon agrees that frontier AI development should slow down.53

Arvind Narayanan joined Geoffrey Hinton and Stuart Russell in saying frontier AI companies "should rely on evaluators that are meaningfully independent."54 He also argues that weeks without incidents from GLM 5.3 should lower cyber-risk estimates.55 MIT Technology Review says that on liability for rogue AI agents, closing the gap "will require lawmakers to move faster than the next breakout."56 MIT Technology Review also says it would be "a vast oversimplification to deem open models dangerous and closed models safe."57
