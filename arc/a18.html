Claude scientific discoveries

What happened

Anthropic reported on October 16, 2026, that Claude had found the origin of the NIST ECDSA curve seeds.1 According to Anthropic, Claude recovered seeds for five NIST curves, P-192 to P-521, as lrand48 output seeded by Unix timestamps.1 The report said there was no weakness and that a backdoor was less plausible.1 Latent Space reported on October 17 that, per an explainer, Claude traced the seeds to a timestamp-seeded RNG.2

Quantum circuit claims

Anthropic also reported on October 16 that Claude had designed improved quantum circuits.3 Anthropic said Claude's circuits beat the best published designs on eight primitives.3 Those designs included new modular inversion and a faster DQI decoder, according to Anthropic.3 Latent Space reported on October 17 that Claude Mythos 5 beat the best-known circuits on 7 of 8 tasks.4 Latent Space said that result came with OpenEvolve and a verifying evaluator.4

Scorpion toxin binders

On October 20, Anthropic reported that its internal model had designed binders against scorpion toxins.5 According to Anthropic, Adaptyv Bio and Twist Bioscience tested 211 designs, and 17 met binding criteria.5 Anthropic said all data was released.5

Latent Space, on October 21, called them the Anthropic and Citrini scorpion-toxin binders.6 Latent Space reported that none of the binders bound multiple toxins or was tested for neutralization.6 For that reason, Latent Space said, the work was not an antivenom.6

Latest reported

On October 22, Zvi Mowshowitz reported that Citrini Research worked with Anthropic staff to test Claude on a medical breakthrough.7 The report said Citrini Research found promising early results but that real-world testing was still required.7
