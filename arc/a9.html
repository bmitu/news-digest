OpenAI AI-generated math papers

5 stories · Sep 27, 2026 to Oct 30, 2026
The stories of one ongoing situation, in the order they were first reported. Which stories belong together is a model's grouping; each story's sources are the reports that carried it.

What happened

Latent Space reported on September 22, 2026, that OpenAI had announced an independent advisory group of mathematicians to guide assessment of AI math advances.1 On October 6, The Verge reported that OpenAI had published solutions to open math problems from 722 manuscripts on GitHub.2 OpenAI's own report that day said the results came with Lean formalizations, reasoning summaries and compute statistics, following advice from the IAS advisory group.3 Latent Space said on October 7 that the papers came from an unreleased internal model and that the claims were unverified.4

Pushback and withdrawn papers

Latent Space reported on October 8 that the release faced credibility pushback, with three papers withdrawn, 14 revised and proofs not fully Lean-checked.5 The same report said the release's README conceded that issues may exist.5 TechCrunch reported that day that OpenAI followed some principles of AGMAI, the Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence.67 TechCrunch also said only 10 of 719 manuscripts released chain-of-thought and 42% of proofs had not undergone formalization.6

Zvi Mowshowitz reported 719 manuscripts on October 9, claimed to include 90 of the top 500 open problems, at about three compute hours each.8 The Verge reported that day that OpenAI had released nearly 400 results across more than 700 manuscripts.9 The same report said mathematicians say verification and digestion will take years.9

The Verge reported on October 9 that the three papers were removed over a sign error, which the company said invalidates an argument.10 Mowshowitz reported that day that the three papers were in the unformalized set and that Lean-checked proofs were holding up so far.11

A challenge to the Fourier transform claim

An author at argmin.net argued on October 13 that OpenAI's claimed faster Fourier transform improvement was galactic.12 The author said its hidden constants were so large that it beats Cooley-Tukey only at impossibly large n.12

New results in late October

The Verge reported on October 26 that OpenAI published claimed results in math, computer science and physics, with outside experts guiding or verifying them.13 The Verge said on October 27 that math output was published, computer science explained by an external group and physics deferred to outside experts.14 Latent Space reported on October 28 that OpenAI said its AI invented a technique resolving a 35-year-old wave-focusing problem.15 The same report said the work was not yet peer-reviewed.15

Mowshowitz wrote on October 29 that a result on local smoothing and the Kakeya conjecture was plausibly Fields Medal-worthy.16 Mowshowitz also described an existence proof for certain quantum field theories, related to a Millennium problem.17

Latest reported

The Verge reported on October 30 that Altman acknowledged researcher frustration on a podcast and defended releasing discoveries.18 Altman also said OpenAI has more undisclosed proofs and spent heavily on math problems.18 A second Verge report that day said a claimed Yang-Mills breakthrough had not been released publicly.19 That report said mathematicians and scientists criticized the decision as gatekeeping.19
