Gemini 4 Argon launch

What happened

On September 30, 2026, The Verge reported that Google had announced Gemini 4 Argon, a new frontier AI model.1 The Verge said the model was for complex workflows in software engineering, enterprise work and cybersecurity.1 TechCrunch reported that it was rolling out to cyber partners through the Fairwind Program.2 The Verge reported that initial access was restricted to trusted cybersecurity professionals and was expanding through the U.S. government's voluntary pre-release process.3

Benchmarks and pricing

The Verge reported that Google's benchmarks showed Argon outperforming models from OpenAI and Anthropic across multiple metrics.4 TechCrunch said Google's claim named OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra and Anthropic's Fable and Opus, and cited the Vals AI model index.5 Ars Technica reported a score of 77.9% on the DeepSWE v1.1 benchmark.6 Latent Space reported on October 1 that Argon had 1M output tokens and was state of the art on 13 of 19 benchmarks.7

Ars Technica reported limited-time rates of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens.8 Latent Space gave prices of $4 and $20 per million input and output tokens, with a 50% introductory discount.9 Both outlets reported a 95% discount for cached input.98

A healthcare software flaw

Ars Technica reported on September 30 that security partner Wiz used Argon to find a critical hospital system vulnerability.10 Google DeepMind described a critical vulnerability in healthcare software that exposed sensitive personal data and that previous frontier models had missed.11 Google AI said Wiz was using Argon through Scan for Good.12

A robotics release

On October 13, Google DeepMind presented Gemini 4 Argon as a physical reasoning model for robotics, with an ERQA score, demos and developer access options.13 The site developers.googleblog.com reported access through AI Studio, the Gemini API and Antigravity.14

Latest reported

Google DeepMind reported on October 13 that, in an internal test, Argon generated simulation code from a cornhole video.15 Google DeepMind said the Apollo robot then sank seven consecutive shots in a real-world trial.15 On October 17, Hacker News carried an item titled "Gemini 4 Argon for Robotics."16
