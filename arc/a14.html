Anthropic IPO and financials

What happened

On September 29, 2026, reports said Anthropic's IPO prospectus showed its AI vision and surging costs.1 The Verge reported that Anthropic officially filed to go public in June, with an IPO expected in November after the U.S. midterm elections.2 TechCrunch reported a 2025 operating loss of $8 billion, revenue of nearly $4.6 billion and $518 billion in planned infrastructure spending.3 Ars Technica reported that the filing warned investors of existential risks, including AI model manipulation, blackmail and resistance to shutdowns.4

Early figures and voting control

On September 30, Latent Space reported a valuation above $2 trillion, second-quarter revenue of $11.5 billion and annual recurring revenue reportedly above $65 billion.5 On October 1, Zvi Mowshowitz wrote of annual recurring revenue above $100 billion and said the cofounders sought 50.1% voting control.6 On October 2, TechCrunch reported that the leaked filing signaled challenging public markets, while OpenAI was returning to private funding.7

Third-quarter numbers and criticism

On October 12, CNBC reported that research firm New Constructs valued Anthropic at $150 billion and called the offering the "most ridiculous IPO of 2026."8 On October 13, Gary Marcus wrote that Anthropic still had not publicly released its S-1 filing.9

On October 15, Simon Willison, citing Financial Times sources, reported that Anthropic told investors third-quarter revenue reached $16.8 billion.10 On October 16, Latent Space cited Bloomberg on positive free cash flow ahead of a pre-Thanksgiving IPO.11 Latent Space also said Reuters expected a $110 billion run rate by the end of 2026.11

Leaked and filed prospectus details

On October 20, TechCrunch reported that a leaked S-1 said hyperscalers increasingly saw Anthropic as a competitor.12 TechCrunch also said Anthropic may spend over $750 billion on cloud contracts.12 On October 21, Latent Space cited a Wall Street Journal review showing $16.3 billion in first-half revenue and over $600 billion in compute commitments.13

On October 23, Transformer reported that Anthropic planned to file publicly as early as that day.14 Transformer said the draft showed an $80 billion first-half net loss.14 On October 28, Mowshowitz wrote that $72 billion of that loss came from fair-value changes on convertible notes and other instruments, not operating losses.15

Latent Space reported a 318-page filing with 85% gross margins in the first half of 2026 and $134 billion in cash.16 Mowshowitz put compute commitments at roughly $800 billion and described founder control through a Class F share.17 On October 29, Mowshowitz wrote that the share price and many numbers were still missing.18

Latest reported

On October 30, TechCrunch reported that Blackstone told investors it expected a high junk rating for Anthropic after the IPO.19 TechCrunch said Blackstone, the anchor investor in a $63 billion chip financing deal, reversed its earlier investment-grade forecast.19 TechCrunch also cited the Financial Times, which reported Blackstone saying Anthropic might not qualify for an investment-grade rating.20 Mowshowitz had reported on October 28 that Anthropic bonds were rated BB+, one notch below investment grade.21
