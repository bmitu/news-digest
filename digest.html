Character.AI files for bankruptcy new
6 sources · 480 points on Hacker News · 5 independent origins: Ars Technica, Character.AI, TechCrunch, The Rundown AI, The Verge

Character.AI announced that it has initiated a voluntary Chapter 11 bankruptcy process to facilitate a court-supervised sale of the company, and The Verge reports it is selling some of its assets to Disney. The company says it took meaningful steps to sustain its business amid legal, regulatory and operational headwinds, and that its board judged a court-supervised sale the best way to maximize value for stakeholders. The company listed assets and liabilities of between $50 million and $100 million in its filing, though The Verge reports only estimated liabilities in that range. Character.AI expects the sale to finish by the end of November or perhaps early December 2026 and says services, including paid subscriptions, continue meanwhile, but CEO Colin Adams says the platform will be wound down if no suitable bid emerges.

Disney would be the lead bidder, paying $20 million for certain undisclosed assets and receiving a nonexclusive license to unspecified intellectual property, which sets a baseline for competing offers. Disney's involvement follows its hiring of Character.AI's former CEO Karandeep Anand as chief technology officer, and it said it expected some of the technical team to join, a move The Verge says resembles an "acquihire" strategy. The move may be surprising, Ars Technica notes, since in September 2025 Disney sent a cease-and-desist letter accusing Character Technologies of weaponizing Disney characters in a way that could damage its brand long term.

Forty bipartisan state attorneys general, including New York's Letitia James, filed a notice of interest in the bankruptcy case, warning of regulatory issues in any sale and of risks from the highly sensitive nature of users' conversations and the large number of minors who use the product. The states say their investigations uncovered exceptionally serious dangers and potentially unlawful conduct, and they are urging the judge to require any purchaser to implement effective age-assurance systems, restrictions on how minors engage with the chatbots, crisis safeguards and the ability for users to delete their data. Ars Technica notes that Character.AI's privacy policy says information, including chat logs, may be transferred in asset sales, and that the company declined to say whether sensitive data might be sold.

TechCrunch says the bankruptcy shows consumer-facing AI products are really hard to scale and monetize, noting that AI costs rise the more people use the app, which increases the pressure to monetize users.

Meta launches Muse Wonder new
5 sources · 3 independent origins: Artificial Analysis, Meta, Simon Willison

Meta's Superintelligence Labs released Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full on Monday, which TechCrunch describes as the company's first attempt at a frontier model. Meta says, based on internal testing, that the models can rival GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.1 on several benchmarks. Wonder is available through the Meta Model API and Muse Code, while Wonder Full is limited to Muse Code subscribers and will not be served through the API. Wonder API pricing is $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, which TechCrunch says is roughly equivalent to GPT-6 Sol or Opus 5.5.

Artificial Analysis scored Wonder Full (max) 56 and Wonder (max) 52 on its Intelligence Index, against 48 for Muse Spark 1.3 and 58 for Opus 5.5. On Artificial Analysis's Coding Agents Index, Wonder Full in Muse Code scored 68, tying Claude Code with Sonnet 5.5 and ahead of Opus 5.5 at 66.

Zvi Mowshowitz says the benchmarks look excellent but were presented in a way that looked suspect, and he sees signs of benchmaxxing. Latent Space notes that an analysis finds some headline wins rely on internal evaluations or omit relevant competing models, and that MemoryBench measures updated facts within a long conversation, not durable cross-session memory.

Early real-world reports are mixed: Theo praised Wonder Full on a real-work comparison but judged its UI generation poor, and Arena placed Wonder Max 15th in WebDev but second in Text Arena. Alexandr Wang acknowledged ongoing inference bugs in the deployment, and users reported exhausting $200 usage allowances quickly (one in a five-hour window, another in a weekly allowance in one day).

4 sources · 243 points on Hacker News · 3 independent origins: CNBC, Financial Times, Lisan al Gaib
