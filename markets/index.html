Oil companies' Supreme Court climate lawsuit appeal new
2 sources · single origin (Investing.com)

The US Supreme Court opened its new nine-month term on Monday, Oct 5, by hearing a bid by ExxonMobil and Suncor Energy to thwart a climate-damages lawsuit brought by officials in Boulder, Colorado. The companies appealed after the Colorado Supreme Court let Boulder's lawsuit, which accuses them of state law violations, proceed. Boulder's city and county governments accuse the companies of helping drive climate change and misleading the public about fossil fuel risks, and aim to hold them liable for past and future costs such as infrastructure repairs, environmental damage, emergency management and public health harms.

Exxon and Suncor lawyer Kannon Shanmugam told the justices the case is an unprecedented effort to use state law to regulate global conduct, and argued that federal law governs interstate pollution claims. The companies also argue that Boulder's claims intrude on an area controlled by the federal government as well as the Clean Air Act. President Donald Trump's administration has backed the companies, arguing that the federal government's authority to regulate air pollution precludes Boulder's claims. Backers of the oil companies argue that allowing Boulder's most far-reaching claims could give states sweeping power over conduct far outside their borders.

Exxon and Suncor told the justices that nearly 60 state and local governments have brought similar suits seeking billions of dollars, and a ruling for the companies could lead to many of those cases being dismissed. Energy companies and allied states have built a largely winning record before the justices over the past two decades in climate liability and federal environmental regulation cases. The court has a 6-3 conservative majority; Justice Samuel Alito has recused himself, and he owns stock in several oil and gas companies but not Exxon or Suncor, according to his financial disclosure forms. A decision is expected by the end of June.
