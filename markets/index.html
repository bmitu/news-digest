Hot September PPI report new
6 sources · 4 independent origins: Axios, Bureau of Labor Statistics, CNBC, Employ America

The September Producer Price Index came in much hotter than expected, climbing 1.3% on the month and 6.3% on an annual basis, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics as reported by CNBC. CNBC reports these were the largest monthly and annual gains since March 2022 and December 2022, respectively, and that economists polled by Dow Jones had forecast 0.6% and 5.5%. Core PPI, which excludes food and energy, rose 0.7% on the month and 5% over 12 months, per CNBC.

CNBC says the increase was paced by a 20% rise in gasoline prices, but that prices excluding food, energy and trade services still rose 0.5%, indicating inflation was broader than energy alone. Wolf Richter reports that energy PPI rose 10.8% on the month and 35.6% on the year, and that services PPI rose 0.82% on the month; services make up 68% of final demand. The BLS said almost half of the September increase in processed goods for immediate demand was connected to higher diesel fuel prices, and CNBC ties the energy surge to the U.S. war with Iran.

Paul Krugman argues the PPI is often a leading indicator of consumer prices, so no relief is visible soon, and he blames President Donald Trump's tariffs and war. Employ America updated its nowcast for September core PCE to 3.09% year over year, saying the upside came from airfares, and it maintains its base case that the FOMC holds in October and hikes in December. CNBC says seven Wall Street estimates put September core PCE at a 0.32% gain, up from 0.25% in August.

Economists see pressure on the Fed: Nationwide's Kathy Bostjancic sees real risks of another hike in two weeks, and Mizuho's Alex Pelle says there is little reason for the Fed to hesitate. Market pricing differs by source: CNBC cites Fed futures at 29% for an October hike, while another CNBC report cites nearly 34% from CME's FedWatch, and both put about 90% on at least one hike by December.

CoreWeave bond and stock selloff new
4 sources · 3 independent origins: Axios, CNBC, Financial Times

CoreWeave's stock and bonds sold off sharply in mid-October 2026, with the yield to maturity on its 9.75% bond due 2031 reaching 15.78% on Thursday and 15.97% by Tuesday, Oct. 20, as investors grew worried about AI debt. The stock plunged about 15% on Wednesday, when shares of neocloud companies fell after CoreWeave's debt yields kept rising, and the Financial Times reported that the company is targeting insurance companies for further debt financing. CoreWeave closed 5.94% higher on Thursday, and on Tuesday it rose roughly 2% after hitting a 52-week low earlier in the session.

The company has about $51.6 billion in total debt, and the spread on its five-year credit default swaps reached above 1,140 basis points at one point on Thursday. S&P Global Market Intelligence's Gavan Nolan says spreads of 1,000 basis points or more generally signal credit distress, and that no one is questioning whether demand for CoreWeave's services is real, only whether the cash flows will ultimately arrive.

Options traders piled in, with CoreWeave options volume above seven times the 30-day average and traders buying just shy of 200,000 puts against 106,000 calls. Luke Kawa noted record five-day net purchases of CoreWeave on Robinhood after the drop.

CEO Mike Intrator dismissed the moves in a memo obtained by CNBC, saying the market sometimes acts irrationally and that the company's backlog more than supports its debt obligations. Janus Henderson's John Lloyd, who owns the 2031 bonds, calls the selloff "a little bit of overreacting" and says the bonds should recover, "just not in that straight line." CNBC notes that some CoreWeave debt remains deeply distressed with yields above 15%.

Others are more cautious. Jim Reid warns that the concern could prove a self-fulfilling prophecy that spreads to other AI-related names, and the Evercore ISI trading desk says the market is "basically saying 'no mas' to AI debt without operating cash flow support." Sell-side views are split: a CNBC Pro contributor writes that HSBC has a Sell rating with a $39 target, while BNP and Cantor Fitzgerald think the fears are overblown, and Deutsche Bank calls the dislocation a buying opportunity.
