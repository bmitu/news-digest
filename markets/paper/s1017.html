Legal fight over Kalshi's sports contracts with states

From reports by Investing.com, reuters.com · Oct 21

Kalshi co-founder and CEO Tarek Mansour told Reuters on Oct 21 that the legal clash between prediction markets and US states over who should regulate the industry could eventually end up at the Supreme Court.1 Dozens of states have taken legal action over sports-related trades on Kalshi, and they say prediction markets like Kalshi are subject to state gambling laws and gambling taxes.23 Kalshi argues that its wagers aren't gambling and should be exempt from state supervision, saying its sports contracts are a type of financial derivative subject to federal regulation under the CFTC.45

Federal appeals courts have so far split, with Kalshi losing some recent cases, but it won a big appeals court victory in New Jersey's action against it, and New Jersey has asked for Supreme Court review.67 Reuters writes that a Supreme Court win could cement Kalshi's leadership and boost its push to compete with exchanges like CME Group and Intercontinental Exchange, while a defeat would leave it vulnerable to states and could crimp sports profits.89

Kalshi's valuation is reportedly around $40 billion in a new funding round, according to people familiar with the matter, and Mansour said sports currently accounts for close to 65% of its revenue, compared to around 95% nine months ago.1011
