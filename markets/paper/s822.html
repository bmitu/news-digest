Eurozone inflation 3.8% in September

From reports by ECB, BBC Business · Oct 17 – Oct 21

Eurozone headline inflation rose to 3.8% in September, up from 3.2% in August, according to EU data cited by the BBC.1 The European Central Bank (ECB) says the rise mostly reflected a surge in energy inflation and, to a lesser extent, higher food and services inflation.2 The ECB says core inflation increased only slightly to 2.5%, that underlying measures remained broadly stable, and that wages have so far shown no material response to the energy shock.3

The ECB expects higher energy prices to keep headline inflation well above target into the first half of 2027, with a return to around target towards the end of 2027.45 The ECB argues the energy shock is too large to "look through" but that a measured response remains appropriate to keep inflation in check, noting it sees no evidence at this stage of significant pass-through of energy prices to other prices and wages beyond what is already included in its projections.67

The BBC reports the ECB has raised interest rates twice in recent months to 2.5%, while US inflation was 3.7% in September and UK inflation was 3.5%.8910
