China third-quarter growth

China Q3 GDP beats forecasts

China's National Bureau of Statistics reported on Monday, 19 October 2026, that third-quarter GDP grew 4.5% year on year, up from 4.3% in the second quarter and better than economists had expected, according to The Guardian. Bespoke reported that quarter-on-quarter growth was 1.2% against forecasts of 1.1%, and the statistics bureau's own release also gives 1.2% quarter on quarter. Growth for the first nine months was 4.6%, and CNN says the third-quarter figure sits at the lower end of Beijing's 4.5-5% annual target. The Guardian says this puts China on track to hit the target without extra stimulus, while the BBC says analysts see the position at the bottom of the range as giving policymakers room to address economic challenges.

Sources attribute the pickup mainly to exports driven by the global AI boom: CNN reports robust exports of chips and electronics components, and ABN Amro analysts say the pickup more than offset drags from the Iran conflict and weak domestic demand. China's exports rose 36.3% in September from a year earlier, according to CNN, which cites customs data released the previous week.

Domestic weakness persisted: fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% in the first nine months, while retail sales rose 1.1% in September, up from 0.4% in August. Sources differ slightly on property investment: CNN says it plunged 20.2% in the first three quarters, while the Guardian and the statistics bureau give 20.6%. Mao Shengyong, vice-minister of the National Bureau of Statistics, said the economy moved forward under pressure and developed in a newer and better direction, but that the problem of insufficient domestic demand remains relatively prominent.

Duncan Wrigley of Pantheon Macroeconomics said policymakers were likely to conclude their efforts to stabilise growth were successful, while Dave Chia of Moody's Analytics warned of China's reliance on a "single external engine" in exports.
