UK unemployment falls to 4.7% as vacancies drop

From reports by BBC Business, Bank of England · Oct 20

The UK's unemployment rate fell to 4.7% in the three months to August, the lowest since July last year, the Office for National Statistics (ONS) said.1 Youth unemployment for those aged 16 to 24 was 15.6% in June to August, down from 16.4% in March to May but above the 14.6% of a year earlier.2 Average earnings excluding bonuses rose 3.7% in the three months to August, up from 3.5% last month. Taking inflation into account, wages grew 0.8% in real terms.3

Vacancies fell by 21,000 in the three months to September to 691,000, with the ONS saying small firms were citing labour costs as a key factor in scaling back hiring.4 ONS deputy director David Freeman said the labour market remained "broadly flat", with the largest fall in vacancies since the start of the year concentrated mainly in smaller businesses.56

Work and Pensions Secretary Pat McFadden said the figures "show the resilience of our labour market", though he added that "too many young people still find themselves locked out of employment".78 Conservative shadow chancellor Andrew Griffith countered that "businesses can't afford to hire", blaming attacks on employers, taxes on jobs and red tape.9

Ashley Webb of Capital Economics called the fall in unemployment "unexpected" and said it suggests the labour market "continues to recover", but he still expects hiring to stay subdued.10 Louise Murphy of the Resolution Foundation said the labour market has performed poorly in recent years but that there may be "green shoots".11 Bank of England policymaker Megan Greene, citing the fall in unemployment from 5.2% to 4.7%, argues that labour market slack may have peaked and that she believes there is less slack than expected. inference12

The ONS's data quality has been criticised, and the ONS said response levels to the Labour Force Survey had improved but "some volatility" remained.1314
