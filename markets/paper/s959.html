September retail sales rise 0.8%

From reports by CNBC Markets, apnews.com · Oct 15

The government reported that U.S. retail sales grew 0.8% in September, above the 0.4% rate economists anticipated, according to data released Thursday in a report separate from the producer price release.1 That was a slowdown from the prior month, when retail sales rose by more than 1%.2 Excluding autos and gas stations, retail sales rose 0.25% from August to September, down from around 1.1% in the prior period.3 CNBC says this slowdown offers a sign that high fuel-related prices are causing consumers to tighten the belt elsewhere.4

The September producer price index rose 1.3% on the month and 6.3% annually, above the 0.6% and 5.5% economists polled by Dow Jones forecast, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics.56 Wednesday's consumer price index rose 0.6% on the month and 3.7% over 12 months, with core CPI up 0.3% on the month and 2.6% on the year, above the 0.2% and 2.5% economists expected.78

According to CME's FedWatch tool, traders now price a nearly 34% likelihood that the Fed raises rates on Oct. 28, up from under 18% a week earlier, and about 90% expect at least one hike by year-end.910 Chris Zaccarelli, investing chief at Northlight Asset Management, says that after two days of higher-than-expected inflation numbers, it seems the Fed will have no choice but to raise rates again this year.11
