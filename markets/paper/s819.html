French 10-year yield hits 5%

From reports by The Guardian, Investing.com, BBC Business and 1 more · Oct 21 – Oct 23

The South China Morning Post reported that yields topped 5% for a third consecutive day on Friday.1 The Guardian reported that the yield rose more than 8 basis points to 5% in morning trading before easing to 4.99%.2 On Thursday it rose a further nine basis points to 5.089% in early trading, its highest since July 2022, and moved above the US Treasury 10-year yield for the first time since 2013, according to The Guardian.34 Sources differ on how far back the high goes: the BBC's Briefing Room described a "26 year high", while Reuters and The Guardian said 24 years and the first time since 2002. inference567

Reuters said the premium over German Bunds was edging back toward early October's 15-year high of nearly 160 basis points, and The Guardian said the spread had risen to its highest level in almost 15 years.89 Deutsche Bank strategist Jim Reid said the sell-off was spreading, with the Italian spread over German bonds widening 3.8 basis points to 116 and Spain's widening 3.1 basis points to 66.1011

The Guardian says France's 2025 deficit was 5.1% of GDP and its debt 115.6% of GDP, and that gridlock in parliament has blocked President Emmanuel Macron's plan to raise the pension age from 62 to 64.121314 Matthew Ryan of Ebury said the political risk premium in France looked unlikely to fade soon and that it was unclear whether the 2027 budget would clear parliament, adding that any budget might be too full of concessions to address the deficit.1516 John Hardy of Saxo said the French debt situation "isn't tractable" and that France needs to make proper fiscal adjustments.17

Carsten Brzeski of ING said rising yields had reinforced the European Central Bank's "restrictive" stance, that the ECB is very likely to stay on hold next week and delay any next rate hike decision to December, and that without any solution to France's fiscal woes bond markets have taken over the ECB's role, making rate hikes less likely.1819 Reuters reported that Carrefour fell 13% after cutting its guidance due to the recent protests, and that the euro was weighed down by anti-government protests in France and a worsening fiscal outlook.2021
