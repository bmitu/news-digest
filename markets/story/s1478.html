Gold steep weekly loss on oil and Fed hike bets

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 03:40 UTC
2 sources · single origin (Reuters)

Gold edged up from a three-month low on Friday but was on track for its steepest weekly loss since June, as rising oil prices fueled higher-for-longer Federal Reserve rate expectations, according to the CNBC report.1 Spot gold was at $4,026.15 per ounce after earlier dropping to $3,986.50, its lowest since July 20, and has lost 3.5% this week, its second consecutive weekly loss.23 U.S. gold futures for December delivery fell 0.5% to $4,040.70 per ounce.4

Brent crude touched a more than five-month high after Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia stoked concern over further disruption to energy supplies.5 Hamad Hussain, climate and commodities economist at Capital Economics, says higher oil prices could keep real interest rates higher for longer, reducing gold's relative attractiveness.6

According to CME's FedWatch Tool, market expectations of a rate hike at the Fed's Oct 27–28 meeting are 9%, versus 81% for December.7 Investors are also awaiting US Personal Consumption Expenditures data on October 29, and the European Central Bank meets Thursday amid an Iran war keeping energy prices above $100.89

Other metals also fell: spot silver slid 1.2% to $53.8882 per ounce, palladium dropped 0.3% to $1,051.06, and platinum fell 0.8% to $1,557.03, with all three headed for weekly declines.101112
