Fed rate hike expectations shift to December

Last scan Mon Oct 5, 15:38 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 4 · 1 new article today · single origin (CNBC)

Expectations for the Fed's next interest rate hike shifted from October to December after softer-than-expected August inflation data and comments from New York Fed President John Williams, according to CNBC. inference12 Reuters reported that the Fed raised its benchmark rate by 25 basis points to 3.75%-4.00% in September, the first hike in three years, and flagged further increases ahead.3 Williams said in a speech that after the September action "there is no need for urgency, and we have time to gather more information," while adding that another hike "may be appropriate late this year."45

The Commerce Department reported that the PCE price index rose 0.3% in August for a 12-month gain of 3.4%, against Dow Jones economist forecasts of 0.3% and 3.7%.67 Core PCE rose 0.2% on the month and 3% annually, versus forecasts of 0.3% and 3.3%, though the BEA changed how it computes several components, which lowered the core July level by 0.36 percentage point.89

Commentators read the data differently: TradeStation's David Russell said it bolsters the case for not hiking in October but is old data that misses this month's diesel price surge, while Carson Group's Sonu Varghese said PCE inflation is "still running hot however you cut it."101112 Heather Long of Navy Federal Credit Union said the data show "no progress in August on inflation" and that September will inevitably be higher.13

How much did gasoline prices and energy costs contribute to the August PCE increase?

Energy costs were the main driver of August's rise in the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, though other sectors also posted gains.1 Gasoline prices jumped 4.4% and energy goods and services climbed 2.3% in August, while the overall PCE price index rose a seasonally adjusted 0.3% for the month.23 The energy PCE price index, covering energy goods and services consumers pay for directly, spiked 16.8% year-over-year in August.4 However, the reports did not say how many percentage points gasoline or energy added to the 0.3% monthly increase.

Why are diesel prices surging, and could they push inflation higher in the months ahead?

Diesel prices are at record highs, and the US-Israel war with Iran has worsened supply chain strains; the Middle East conflict has raised energy prices and caused shortages of commodities shipped through the Strait of Hormuz.123 Economists warn that diesel could start to exert pressure beyond the transportation and agricultural sectors.1 Business input costs are climbing: the survey's measure of prices paid by businesses for inputs jumped to 74.0 from 72.6 in August, mirroring a similar increase in the ISM manufacturing survey, and combined the two surveys pointed to higher inflation down the road.456 The latest official inflation data does not yet capture the diesel surge, according to TradeStation's David Russell, while Navy Federal's Heather Long says it is inevitable that September inflation will be higher.78
