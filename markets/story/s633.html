Fed bank supervision restructuring and asset thresholds

Last scan Tue Oct 6, 17:01 UTC
3 sources · single origin (Federal Reserve)

Federal Reserve Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman announced on Tuesday, October 6, that the Fed will restructure bank supervision, replacing a model in which regional bank presidents oversaw examinations with one more accountable to Washington, according to Reuters.12 Each of the five regions will have a regional leader accountable for all supervisory activity, still conducted by regional Reserve Bank staff, and Bowman says the regions will follow state lines rather than Reserve Bank district boundaries.34 Bowman says examiners will remain in existing Reserve Bank locations and keep overseeing the banks they currently supervise.5

Bowman argues the current structure "disincentivized a critical link between responsibility and accountability," citing an independent review of Silicon Valley Bank's collapse that she commissioned and that found Fed examiners were slow to act.6 She also criticized the Fed's heavy use of committees, saying they "became a source for plausible deniability and a disincentive for examiners to take prompt and decisive action," and argued their use should be streamlined.789

Bowman also said the Fed will consider later this year updating fixed-dollar asset thresholds that determine when banks face stricter rules, with a mechanism to update them every five years for inflation and economic growth.1011 Reuters reports the plan could give banks more room to grow before triggering new capital, liquidity and stress testing rules, and that banks have complained for years that fixed levels become outdated.1213 In her speech, Bowman said a community bank has been defined for 15 years as one with under $10 billion in assets, and that the Board will consider broader reforms and updates to the large bank tailoring framework later this year.1415

Reuters says Bowman has embarked on a massive overhaul of how the Fed oversees the largest banks; since taking the head regulatory role in 2025, she has replaced supervisory leaders, trimmed staff and issued new guidelines. She argues they became a source of delay and blurred accountability, and that a mismatch between authority and accountability needed a structural fix.8910 It is not reported how the staff cuts will interact with the new regional structure, such as whether positions will change.

What did the independent Silicon Valley Bank review say about the Fed's supervision structure, and who wrote it?

Reuters reports that the review, which Bowman commissioned, found Fed examiners were slow to take action.12 Fed Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman says the review highlighted a long-standing structural problem: a mismatch between authority for decisionmaking and accountability for supervisory decisions.3 Bowman says the Vice Chair for Supervision is responsible for executing Fed supervision, but the Reserve Banks conduct it.4 Per Bowman's account, the review noted that for decades this structure has disincentivized a link between responsibility and accountability, and that it was further complicated by a web of dozens of committees that caused dysfunction when critical decisions were needed.5

Why did Bowman model the five regions on the Conference of State Bank Supervisors' structure?

Fed Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman said the new five-region supervisory structure was informed by the regional structure the Conference of State Bank Supervisors implemented.1 Her stated goal is to fix a mismatch between who has decision-making authority and who is accountable, a problem she says an independent review of the Silicon Valley Bank failure highlighted.234 Bowman also says the regions will follow state lines rather than Reserve Bank district boundaries, which she argues will let the Fed coordinate more effectively and efficiently with state and federal regulators.5 That emphasis on state lines fits her point that the Fed's banks are state member banks that states supervise jointly with the Fed, which likely explains the choice of a state-supervisor model. inference67

When will the Fed Board vote on the asset threshold changes and the broader large bank tailoring updates, and what happens after that?

Fed Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman says the Board will consider updates to fixed-dollar asset thresholds, and separately broader structural reforms to bank portfolios defined by asset size and updates to the large bank tailoring framework, "later this year".123 The threshold proposal would raise the static thresholds and include a mechanism to update them every five years.45 Reuters reports the change could give banks more room to grow before triggering new rules on capital, liquidity and stress testing.67 Bowman did not say what happens after the Board's consideration, such as a comment period or final adoption, for these changes.
