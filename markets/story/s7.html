September FOMC rate hike

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 17:18 UTC
2 sources · single origin (Federal Reserve)

The Federal Open Market Committee voted at its September meeting to raise the target range for the federal funds rate by a quarter point, to 3-3/4 to 4 percent, according to Governor Jefferson's speech.1 Barr says the committee agreed unanimously, and Cook says she voted along with the rest of the FOMC for the 25 basis point increase.234 Employ America also says Waller and Williams were the key deciding votes, and that after the August inflation data a September hike was all but locked in.56 Employ America's Fedspeak monitor describes the Fedspeak after the decision as hawkish.7

The speakers report a labor market they see as stable, with the unemployment rate at 4.1 percent in August.8910 Barr says job creation has averaged around 80,000 a month this year, close to estimates of the breakeven pace.11

The officials point to several drivers of inflation: Jefferson says energy prices are the predominant factor behind the recent pickup in headline inflation, and Michael Barr and Cook also cite the AI buildout. inference121314 Jefferson says he sees no spillovers into broader inflation yet and that most longer-term inflation expectations remain stable, while he says the hike was meant to keep them anchored. inference1516

Barr says that in his base case further policy adjustments are likely to be needed, while Cook says the number and size of any future adjustments will depend on incoming inflation and labor data. inference1718 Employ America says the Fed moved from an easing bias to a hold to a hike over 2026, catching up to the data with a lag, and that it had moved its call to a September hike in late July.1920

What did the September jobs report show for payrolls, unemployment and wage growth?

The U.S. added a seasonally adjusted 29,000 nonfarm payrolls in September, far below the 84,000 economists surveyed by Dow Jones had expected.123 Revisions made the picture weaker: July was revised to a loss of 10,000 jobs and August to a gain of 133,000, for 60,000 fewer jobs in total than previously reported.456 The unemployment rate rose to 4.2% in September, with Employ America putting the exact figure at 4.18%, up 0.03 percentage point.78910 Wage growth cooled: average hourly earnings rose just 0.1% in September, putting the 12-month gain at 3%, the lowest since May 2021, against expectations of 0.3% and 3.1%.1112 Employ America called the report soft but not dire, and said the labor market is not a source of inflationary pressure.1314

How fast is headline PCE inflation running, and how far above the Fed's 2 percent target is it?

Headline PCE inflation was running at about 3.4 percent over the 12 months to August, according to Fed Governor Jefferson.1 Fed Governor Cook, by contrast, cites an estimated 3.8 percent total inflation rate for the 12 months into August, which she describes as almost double the target.2 Measured against the 2 percent target, Jefferson's 3.4 percent figure implies headline inflation is roughly 1.4 percentage points above target, and Cook's 3.8 percent implies about 1.8 points above. inference345 Jefferson says the recent pickup in headline inflation is driven predominantly by energy prices, including gasoline and diesel.6
