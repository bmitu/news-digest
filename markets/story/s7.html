30-year US mortgage rates rise above 7.3%

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 18:00 UTC
3 sources · single origin (Mortgage Bankers Association)

The average 30-year fixed mortgage rate has climbed to 7.30% after six straight weekly increases, according to the Mortgage Bankers Association, the highest level since late 2023.1 Paul Krugman writes that the rate was about 6 percent on the eve of the Iran war and was almost 7.3 percent as of last week.23 Krugman calculates that on a $500,000 mortgage the increase means an additional $431 in monthly payments and an additional $155,000 in interest over the life of the loan.4

Krugman says the rise was especially rapid in September and has been widely described in the financial media as a "bond market rout".56 Krugman says the current evidence does not support the view that rising long-term rates are driven by fears of a U.S. government default or concerns about runaway inflation, and says it is likely that the biggest source of the surge is the immense demand for funds created by the AI boom.78 He adds that the Iran war appears to have triggered a reset of market narratives about Federal Reserve policy, and that there are signs of a balance-sheet deleveraging spiral also pushing long rates up.910

CNBC's Diana Olick reports that the high rates have deterred potential homebuyers, who are also facing higher house prices than a year ago.11 The Guardian reports that in Salinas, California, borrowing costs are worsening a housing crisis, with mortgage rates above 7% described as a 20-month high and the Federal Reserve said to be poised to raise rates further.12

How high has the 30-year Treasury yield climbed, and is it at a multi-decade high?

The 30-year Treasury yield closed the week at 5.63% and briefly touched 5.69% on Thursday, according to Wolf Richter.1 Bespoke says the 30-year yield has climbed above 5.6%, its highest level in over 24 years.2 Bespoke adds that the yield was 3.3 standard deviations above its 50-day moving average, the most extended level since May.3 The 10-year yield has also risen sharply, from 0.5% to 5.28% over the six-year bond bear market, Richter says.4

How does today's 7.3% mortgage rate compare with the average mortgage rate over the past 25 years and with the 1976–2000 period?

How are UK mortgage rates and the Bank of England responding to the same Gulf energy and inflation pressures?

In the UK, the US-Israel war with Iran and the resulting disruption to Gulf oil and gas flows are pushing up energy costs and inflation fears, and that has fed through to higher mortgage rates.12 Nationwide's chief economist Robert Gardner says the Middle East conflict has raised expectations of Bank of England rate rises, which keeps upward pressure on the market rates that underpin mortgage pricing.3 Moneyfacts reported that average two-year fixed mortgage rates reached their highest level since July 2024 and five-year fixed rates their highest since October 2023, both above 5.9%.45 The Bank is trying to bring inflation, now 3.1%, back to its 2% target, but it forecast last month that inflation could pass 4% in the first quarter of 2027.6 Mortgage lenders are thus already pricing in the rate rises the Bank is expected to deliver, so the two are responding to the same energy-driven inflation pressure, with mortgages moving first. inference78
