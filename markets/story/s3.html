UK diesel prices hit £2 per litre

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 17:18 UTC
2 sources · 3 independent origins: BBC, RAC, The Guardian

The average UK diesel price rose above £2 a litre for the first time on Friday 2 October 2026, reaching 200.01p a litre according to the RAC motoring group.123 The RAC's Simon Williams called £2 a "price threshold no one wanted to cross" and said it would be very challenging for households and firms that drive a lot of miles.45 Before the war the RAC put average diesel at 142.38p a litre.67 The RAC says filling an average family car with diesel now costs £110, almost £32 more than at the start of the US-Iran war, while petrol has reached 174.71p a litre.8910

BBC reporting attributes the surge to the Iran war's disruption of oil and refined fuel supplies, Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries and Chinese restrictions on fuel exports.1112

On the same day, G7 leaders agreed to release up to 100 million barrels of oil and diesel, after Donald Trump threatened to ban US diesel exports.131415 The G7 statement describes the release as coordinated through the IEA, starting immediately over four months, with a frontloaded substantial diesel release within the first 20 days, and members will not impose export restrictions on each other.1617 The Guardian, citing reported proposals by Emmanuel Macron, says the split would be 50m barrels of diesel and 50m of crude, while the BBC says only that it will be a mix of diesel and crude. inference1819

Trump later said an export ban was "never really on the table" and called Europe's move "a great thing", though he had earlier said he backed a ban and was still considering it on Thursday.2021 Macquarie Group's Walt Chancellor argued the reserve release would not fix the underlying problem, saying it needs more oil through the Strait of Hormuz and that anything less is "just shuffling deck chairs".22

How much of the UK's diesel comes from the US, and how exposed is Britain to a US export ban?

Over half of the UK's diesel is imported, and 31% of those imports come from the US, which BBC reporting puts at around 17% of total UK supply. inference12 The UK is heavily reliant on US diesel, and many agree a US export ban would hurt countries that depend on it, with worries it could push UK prices even higher.345 Argus Media's chief economist David Fyfe said cutting off American supply would be likely to cause international prices to skyrocket.6 The exposure comes against a backdrop of record prices, with UK diesel averaging 200.01p a litre, and the UK's refineries do not make enough diesel to meet demand.789 However, the immediate threat has eased: the G7 agreed there would be no export restrictions between members, and Trump later said a diesel export ban was "never really on the table".1011 The UK government says it has a diverse and resilient supply and that there is no cause for concern about diesel shortages, though prices are expected to rise further.1213

Why is diesel so much more expensive than petrol in the UK, and why does Britain rely on imports?

That reliance has grown because refineries at Grangemouth and Immingham closed last year, and the UK now buys over half of its diesel from overseas.1 Global supply is also tight: the Iran war has disrupted oil and refined products from the Middle East, Ukrainian attacks on Russian refiners have cut supply, and China has restricted exports of refined fuels.234 The RAC's Simon Williams says that although the UK has stopped buying Russian diesel in response to the war, other countries have carried on, meaning there are "more countries competing for less diesel".56 Diesel is also harder to refine than petrol, and heavy use by haulage and agriculture makes demand very hard to cut.78 As a result the gap has widened: before the war diesel averaged 142.38p a litre against 132.83p for petrol, and it is now 200.01p against 174.71p. inference9101112
