G7 emergency oil reserve release

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 16:14 UTC
4 sources · tracked since Oct 3 · 2 independent origins: BBC, G7

The G7 announced on Friday 2 October 2026 that up to 100 million barrels of oil and diesel will be released from emergency reserves, after Donald Trump threatened to ban US diesel exports unless Europe tapped its own stocks. inference123 In a joint statement the leaders said the release would be coordinated through the IEA, begin immediately and run over four months, including a frontloaded substantial diesel release within the first 20 days by G7 members and partners.4 The Guardian reports, citing proposals reportedly drawn up by Emmanuel Macron, that the split would be 50m barrels of diesel and 50m of crude, while the BBC says only that the total is a mix of the two and that it is not yet clear which partner countries will release stocks. inference56

The G7 agreed there would be no export restrictions on energy products between members, and Macron said Trump "was very clear on this point".789 Trump wrote on social media that Europe had agreed to release a massive amount of its diesel, and later at the White House said an export ban was "never really on the table", though the BBC notes he had backed a ban the previous week and was still considering it on Thursday. inference10111213

Brent crude briefly fell below $100 a barrel after the news but rose back to around $102 by Friday evening, and Kpler's Matt Smith attributed the rebound to rumours of a Saudi offensive into Yemen. inference141516 Macquarie Group's Walt Chancellor argued that Europe relying on its own reserves would not fix the underlying problem, which he called a global energy problem requiring more oil through the strait of Hormuz, and said anything less is "just shuffling deck chairs".1718

How does this 100 million barrel release compare with the IEA's record 400 million barrel release in March and the 2022 release?

The G7's 100 million barrel release, split between 50 million barrels of diesel and 50 million of crude, is a quarter the size of the IEA's record 400 million barrel release in March. inference12 The March release was the largest release of government oil reserves in the IEA's history and represented a third of the group's total government stockpiles.34 The 2022 release, made after Russia's invasion of Ukraine, was 182 million barrels, so the new 100 million barrel release is smaller than that one too. inference567 The March release was more than double the 2022 release.87

How much of the UK's diesel supply comes from the US, and how exposed is the UK to a US export ban?

Over half of the UK's diesel is imported, and the BBC reports 31% of those imports come from the US.1 That works out to around 17% of total UK diesel supply, according to the BBC.2 The UK is described as heavily reliant on US diesel, and experts warn a US export ban would put further pressure on diesel prices in other countries unless they can access further supplies.34 The Guardian reported that the proposed ban on US exports was expected to fuel greater competition for cargoes on the global market, which would push prices in the UK and Europe well beyond current record levels; the Guardian also reported UK diesel at a record £2 a litre.567 However, the UK government says it has a "diverse and resilient supply" and that there is no cause for concern about potential diesel shortages.8910

Why is global diesel so tight, and how are Russia, China and the Middle East conflict adding to it?

According to the BBC, supplies of diesel internationally have been heavily constrained by the conflict in the Middle East, which has restricted the flow of both crude oil and refined diesel onto global markets.12 Iran's effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz was of particular concern to oil traders, and the disruption has also limited output from refineries in the region and in China, a leading buyer of Gulf crude.345 Russia is adding to the squeeze: Ukrainian attacks on its refineries pushed fuel output to 20-year lows, and Russia has imposed its own diesel export ban.678 China has also restricted its exports of refined fuels, further limiting supply on the world market.9 Diesel is harder to refine than petrol, and because haulage and agriculture rely on it, demand is very difficult to reduce.10 Macquarie's Walt Chancellor said the core issue the US faces is a global energy problem, and that the solution is more oil through the Strait of Hormuz and out of the Middle East, according to CNBC as reported by the Guardian.111213
