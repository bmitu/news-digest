Brazil presidential election first round

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 16:33 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 3 · single origin (CNBC)

Wall Street is preparing for sharply different market outcomes in the first round of Brazil's presidential election, held Sunday, in what CNBC describes as a neck-and-neck race.1 Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva, 80, a leftist, is running for a fourth term against 45-year-old right-winger Flavio Bolsonaro, son of former President Jair Bolsonaro, and a runoff would be held Oct. 25 if neither gets more than 50% of the vote.23

Kalshi markets show Bolsonaro favored 60% to 39% over Lula, though CNBC notes prediction markets are prohibited in Brazil and may not reflect local sentiment.45 Aurora Macro Strategies senior advisor Richard Lapper disagrees with the prediction markets' degree of confidence, saying the balance has shifted toward Flavio Bolsonaro over the past month but not nearly as far as those markets are pricing. inference6 Fernando Marengo of Black Toro Global Investments cautions that some of the move is already priced in in Brazil.7

Markets favor Bolsonaro because he promises more fiscal discipline, which many economists say Brazil needs, with debt-to-GDP at 81.9%; Citi's Leonardo Porto says a 3-3.5% fiscal adjustment is needed.89

JPMorgan noted that the MSCI Brazil rose by 0.25% on average each day Flavio Bolsonaro gained in the polls, and it says USD/BRL would move to 5.50 if Lula wins and 4.90 if Bolsonaro wins.1011 If Brazil enters another period of reform, JPMorgan analysts say interest rates could decline to their neutral level, and they would be thinking about MSCI Brazil upside potential between 21% and 41%.12

What would the MSCI Brazil forward P/E be if Bolsonaro wins and reforms go ahead, compared with where it is now?

JPMorgan analysts say that if Brazil enters another period of reform, the MSCI Brazil forward P/E could rise from its current 8.6 to as high as 13.3.1 That 13.3 level was last seen in 2020.2 JPMorgan ties this to a Bolsonaro win in which he manages to implement a "robust reform agenda", with the bank also seeing MSCI Brazil upside of 21% to 41%. inference34

Why will a permanent fiscal adjustment be so hard to achieve in Brazil, whoever wins?

Citi's Brazil head economist Leonardo Porto says Brazil needs a permanent fiscal adjustment of 3-3.5% of GDP to stabilize its public debt, and that one-off measures such as privatizations won't be enough.12 A permanent fix means cutting spending or raising taxes, and CNBC says either will be difficult.3 On the spending side, roughly 90% of Brazil's budget is mandatory, and some of it is required by the constitution.4 On the tax side, Brazil's tax burden is already the highest in Latin America at 32%, according to the OECD, and growth prospects are low.5 Political arithmetic adds uncertainty, because the whole lower house and a third of the Senate are also being elected, and the makeup of the legislature will be a key factor in whether reforms can pass.67 Since little of the budget is flexible, the limited room to raise taxes and the weak growth outlook would make the adjustment hard for either Lula or Bolsonaro, even though markets favor Bolsonaro for promising more fiscal discipline. inference84

How did Brazil's markets perform during Jair Bolsonaro's earlier reform period, including 2-year yields and equities?

JPMorgan says that during Jair Bolsonaro's period of reform, Brazil's 2-year yields fell almost to 4.7%.1 JPMorgan also says the equity market gained 130% over that same period.1 The report did not say what level the 2-year yields started from.

How much did stocks, bonds and currencies rally in Colombia and Peru after pro-business candidates won there?
