Aggregate Bond Index first negative 5-year returns

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 17:18 UTC
2 sources · single origin (Ben Carlson)

Ben Carlson of Wealth of Common Sense wrote that the Bloomberg Aggregate Bond Index is in a cycle that is the first time in the index's history to show negative 5-year returns, with the figures measured through the end of August.123 Barry Ritholtz's morning reads list highlighted Carlson's piece and repeated that claim.4

Carlson says 10-year returns are positive but close to a lost decade, and that after inflation the current 10-year real returns are as bad as they have ever been.56 He says the only time 5-year real returns were worse was in the early 1980s, when inflation was in double digits.7 Carlson notes the Agg fell 13% in 2022, its first double-digit down year, that 2021 and 2022 were its first back-to-back down years, and that the bear market that began in 2020 produced a nearly 20% drawdown, its worst ever.8910

He attributes the poor returns to three factors: starting yields that were too low, interest rates that rose quickly, and high inflation.11 Carlson is more optimistic going forward, saying the Agg's average yield to maturity is now 5.5%, which is good for forward returns.12 Carlson also notes inconsistent figures for the Agg's current yield: he gives 5.5% as the average yield to maturity in one place and says elsewhere that the Agg is approaching 6%. inference1314

He says no one he has encountered can predict interest rate moves, and he argues investors should weigh bond types by yield, credit quality, duration and maturity.1516

Carlson says rates could fall if inflation eases or economic growth slows, and he also lists a recession, a collapse of the AI trade, and an end to the war in Iran with falling energy prices as possible triggers for lower rates. inference12 On the other side, Carlson says rates could keep rising if economic growth stays strong or AI spending gets bigger, and a prolonged war in Iran is the other upside risk for rates he names. inference34

Why have bond returns this decade even underperformed what starting yields would have predicted?

Ben Carlson of Wealth of Common Sense says the usual link between starting yield and forward five-year returns broke down somewhat this decade, with returns even falling short of what yields implied.123 His explanation is that interest rates rose so far and so fast, and yields were so low to begin with.45 He also describes a threefold problem of low starting yields, a rapid rate surge and high inflation, which he calls a perfect storm for poor bond returns.67 The result was the Bloomberg Aggregate's worst drawdown on record, of nearly 20%, plus its first back-to-back down years in 2021 and 2022, with 2022 down 13%.8910
