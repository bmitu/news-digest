Global bond yields hit decades-high levels

Last scan Mon Oct 5, 15:38 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 4 · 1 new article today · single origin (BBC)

Government borrowing costs in countries including the UK, US, France and Japan hit their highest levels in decades, according to the BBC, putting pressure on public sector finances.1 The BBC reports that the yield on 30-year UK bonds rose above 6% on Thursday, its highest since 1998, while the US 10-year yield rose to 5.34%, its highest since 2002.23 The sources give different explanations for the sell-off: the BBC says analysts saw no particular trigger on Thursday but points to central banks raising rates to fight inflation caused by soaring energy prices, while the Guardian links the global sell-off in sovereign debt to the Iran war rattling markets. inference45

The Guardian reports that the yield on French 10-year government bonds hit its highest level since 2002 last week before dipping back on Friday.6 That pushed the gap between French and German borrowing costs to its widest since 2012, the Guardian says.7 In France, investors are focused on the fiscal position and fear that the presidential election and a hung parliament could make it harder to curb the deficit, according to the Guardian.8

The Guardian says the euro fell to a 17-month low against the dollar amid French debt fears, and analysts warned that stresses in the French bond market could spread to other euro area countries.910 UniCredit currency strategist Roberto Mialich says a retest of $1.10 for the euro is possible in the near term.11

Bank of England governor Andrew Bailey, speaking to the BBC, warned that AI could trigger financial market shocks, and when asked if an AI bubble could burst said: "You could see some correction of asset prices at some point."1213
