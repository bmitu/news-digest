Brookings paper proposes shrinking Fed balance sheet

Last scan Thu Oct 22, 01:18 UTC
3 sources · single origin (Brookings Institution)

A new Brookings Institution paper by Chris Hughes, a Nonresident Fellow at the Hutchins Center on Fiscal and Monetary Policy, says the Federal Reserve could substantially shrink its balance sheet by overhauling its rate control toolkit to provide liquidity on demand while changing rules so banks can hold less cash.123 The paper argues the timing is right because new Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh wants a smaller balance sheet and has named a task force to consider ways to achieve it.45

The proposed changes could allow the Fed to cut its holdings from the current $6.8 trillion to around $4.5 trillion, with the proposed level of reserves in the financial system falling from around $3 trillion to around $1 trillion.678 Hughes wrote that the Fed's footprint as a share of GDP would then be smaller than at any point since 2008, and interest paid to banks would fall by roughly two-thirds.910

The paper would replace the current "ample reserves" system with "Elastic Reserves," which Hughes wrote would be more responsive to market demand for cash.11 Hughes wrote that new liquidity facilities and policies to reduce reserve demand are necessary complements, and that neither effectively delivers a permanently smaller balance sheet without the other. He said the policy changes can be done in part by changing banking regulation rules and how the Treasury manages its cash account at the Fed.12

For many current Fed officials, ample reserves works in real world conditions; New York Fed leader John Williams said on Sept. 22 the system "has proven to be highly effective at delivering interest rate control and supporting the smooth functioning of core financial markets."1314 Critics of large Fed holdings, Warsh among them, believe the holdings distort financial markets, though Warsh has noted the current regime can't be changed overnight.1516

Hughes hopes the Fed task force will "deliver a concrete blueprint" rather than just a list of options, and noted Congress could also legislate the change.1718
