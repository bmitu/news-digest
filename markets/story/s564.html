ISM services PMI falls to 54.9 in September

Last scan Tue Oct 6, 17:01 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 5 · 1 new article today · single origin (Institute for Supply Management)

US services sector activity slowed in September, as the Institute for Supply Management's nonmanufacturing PMI fell to 54.9 from 55.4 in August, according to Reuters.12 The reading was below the 55.2 that economists polled by Reuters had forecast, though a reading above 50 still indicates growth. inference34

The survey's new orders measure eased to 59.8 after surging to 60.9 in August, which was the highest reading since February 2023.5 The prices paid measure jumped to 74.0 from 72.6 in August, and supplier deliveries rose to 53.2 from 51.3, a sign of slower deliveries.67 The services employment measure climbed to 50.1 from 47.8 in August.8

Reuters reports that supply chains are struggling to cope with strong demand, a situation worsened by the US-Israel war with Iran, with the Middle East conflict having raised energy prices and caused shortages of commodities shipped through the Strait of Hormuz.910 Economists warned that record-high diesel prices could start to exert pressure beyond the transportation and agricultural sectors.11

Combined with the ISM manufacturing survey, the data pointed to higher inflation ahead and supported economists' expectations that the Federal Reserve would raise interest rates in December, after a 25-basis-point hike last month to 3.75%-4.00%.1213 Bespoke noted that Treasury yields reached new highs on the day and that the S&P Global services PMI topped its highest level in over four years.1415

How does the ISM services employment reading of 50.1 square with the weak September payrolls report?

The ISM services employment index rose to 50.1 in September from 47.8 in August, moving back above the 50 line that signals growth.12 The payrolls report itself, by contrast, showed a sharp slowdown, with jobs up just 29,000 and unemployment rising to 4.2%.34 Economists took the ISM rise, along with an increase in ISM manufacturing employment reported the week before, as a sign that the labor market stayed stable despite September's underwhelming payroll gains.5 The ISM reading therefore fits with a picture of a stable but low-hiring labor market rather than contradicting the weak payrolls.
