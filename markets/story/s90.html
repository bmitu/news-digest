Nvidia trades at all-time high

Last scan Tue Oct 6, 17:01 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 4 · 2 independent origins: Bespoke Investment Group, CNBC

Nvidia traded at an all-time high on the day Bespoke wrote, and if the gains hold through the end of the day it will be the stock's first record close since May 14, according to Bespoke Investment Group.1 Bespoke says that over the period since May 14 the S&P 500 is up about 3% and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index about 10%, so Nvidia has lagged.2 Bespoke also says the 96-trading-day streak without a new high is the third-longest since ChatGPT launched, behind streaks of 120 days ending in April 2026 and 115 days ending in June 2025.34

CNBC puts Nvidia's value at just under $5.7 trillion and says option pricing implies about a 50% chance it reaches $6 trillion by the end of October, and about 13% this week.56 CNBC adds that the odds of reaching $6 trillion by Dec. 18 are about 67%, with about a 1-in-16 chance of $7 trillion by Nov. 20, and that Nvidia would need to touch $248 to cross $6 trillion.789

Bespoke notes that after the 115-day streak ended in June 2025 Nvidia rallied a further 34% over five months, whereas after the April 2026 streak ended it gained only 12% before pulling back, so it is unclear whether this is a breakout or a pause. inference1011 Bespoke suggests Nvidia's new high could be an early signal for the rest of the semiconductor group, saying that by Halloween we should know whether the move was a trick or a treat.1213

Why has Nvidia traded differently from the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index this year, and which one has tended to lead?

Nvidia has lagged the chip index since its May 14 record: the S&P 500 is up about 3% and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index about 10% over that stretch, while Nvidia went 96 trading days without a new high. inference12 Bespoke Investment Group says the two have often moved in separate directions since the summer, with one zigging while the other zagged.3 As for which tends to lead, Bespoke sees some evidence that Nvidia leads and the semiconductor index follows: Nvidia led both the spring run-up and the summer sell-off in chips.456 Bespoke therefore suggests Nvidia's new record could be an early signal for the rest of the group, if Nvidia is once again leading.7 The reasons for the divergence were not explained beyond the timing patterns.

Are traders leaning toward hedging for a rally or for a drop in Nvidia, and what does the options skew show?

Options traders appear to have a bias toward hedging for a rally in Nvidia, according to CNBC, citing Barchart data on "call skew."1 The skew shows some of Nvidia's call options trading at a higher implied volatility than the equivalent puts, which is what produces the "call skew."2 CNBC notes that deltas imply a move in the opposite direction is just as likely as a given move, and that this is largely the case for Nvidia, though some call options trade at a higher implied volatility than equivalent puts.34 Options pricing also implies about a 50% chance Nvidia reaches a $6 trillion valuation by the end of October.5
