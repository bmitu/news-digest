Manchester City Premier League case

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 03:32 UTC
2 sources · single origin (BBC)

An independent commission found Manchester City guilty of breaching the Premier League's financial regulations between the 2009-10 and 2017-18 seasons, the BBC reported, and City have signalled they will appeal.12 The BBC reported that the commission found City artificially inflated revenue through "sham" sponsorship agreements with the help of their owners. City have repeatedly denied guilt.34 Owners Abu Dhabi United Group are said to have topped up the value of commercial deals as part of a "disguised funding scheme" to the scale of £830.69m, which it was argued enabled the club to appear to comply with Premier League and Uefa financial rules.56

Sources told BBC Sport that City are expected to tell an appeal board they have evidence the money paid to state-owned sponsors came from the Abu Dhabi government, not from ADUG.7 The commission rejected City's submission as "untrue", saying it was an 'explanation' the club had concocted well after the event in an attempt to obscure and conceal the realities of the disguised funding scheme.89 City claim the decision contained "clear material errors, of law, principle and fact, and is unsafe", and chairman Khaldoon Al Mubarak referred to "irrefutable evidence" he claimed supports the club's innocence.1011

Dame Meg Hillier, chair of the Treasury Committee, said she would "welcome reassurance from HMRC that you are seized of the importance of these issues and the public interest in this case".12 A report from Tax Policy Associates claims City failed to pay as much as £12m in tax because of a "sham" contract with former manager Roberto Mancini, and that a penalty could take liabilities to £24m.1314

Etihad Airways, City's principal sponsor, says it is considering legal action against the Premier League and "categorically rejects" any suggestion it was involved in improper commercial arrangements, while the Premier League declined to comment.151617
