UK September inflation rises to 3.5%

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 03:40 UTC
4 sources · tracked since Oct 21 · 1 new article this week · single origin (Office for National Statistics)

UK annual inflation rose to 3.5% in the 12 months to September, up from 3.1% in August, driven mainly by sharply higher petrol and diesel prices, according to the Office for National Statistics.123 CNBC reports that the figure was hotter than the 3.4% economists polled by Reuters had forecast, and that it was the highest annual rate in almost a year.456

Motor fuel prices rose 29.8% over the year, and the ONS said petrol prices were the highest since August 2022.789 ONS chief economist Grant Fitzner said airfares, computer games and live events also contributed to the rise, and the BBC notes core inflation rose to 2.8% from 2.6% in August.1011

Markets are pricing in a 98% chance that the Bank of England raises rates by 25 basis points to 4% next month, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC.1213 Scott Gardner of J.P. Morgan Personal Investing says accelerating services inflation raises concern about second-round effects beyond energy and strengthens the case for a hike, and Suren Thiru of the ICAEW likewise says a November rate rise is falling into place.1415
