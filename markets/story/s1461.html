US initial jobless claims fall to 194K

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 03:40 UTC
3 sources · single origin, unclear which · Official data: Initial jobless claims (weekly), Nonfarm payrolls (monthly change), Unemployment rate

US initial jobless claims slipped to a seasonally adjusted 194,000 for the week ended Oct. 17, the Labor Department reported Thursday.1 CNBC says the level is just off the 189,000 reported for the week of July 18, which was the lowest since September 1969.2 CNBC says claims have averaged 212,000 over the past year and the four-week moving average is down to 197,750.3 Bespoke reported that continuing claims increased to 1.728 million.4

CNBC says the low claims come against an uncertain labor backdrop: payrolls rose just 29,000 in September, averaging 69,000 a month in 2026, and the unemployment rate rose to 4.2%.5 CNBC says layoffs have remained modest, with the layoff rate falling to 1% of the workforce over the prior 12 months.6 Thomas Simons, chief U.S. economist at Jefferies, said the claims data suggests labor market conditions are stable despite pessimistic consumer surveys, job-cut headlines and AI worries.7

CNBC says the Fed meets next week, and markets expect the Fed to hold off on a rate hike in October but then to institute another quarter percentage point increase in December.8
