DOJ closes criminal investigation into Fed renovation costs

Last scan Mon Oct 5, 15:38 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 4 · 1 new article today · single origin, unclear which

An internal watchdog said the Federal Reserve mismanaged costs associated with a $2.4bn renovation of its Washington DC headquarters, but noted that no criminal violations occurred, according to a report released on Wednesday.1 The report said the board "has not effectively managed" the project and "repeatedly deviated from its cost-management provisions", but it found no administrative misconduct.234 Construction costs rose from an initially budgeted $921m in February 2020 to $2.018bn by December 2024, and completion, originally due in mid-2024, is not slated until December 2027.56

Trump had said in July 2025 that the cost looked like "about $3.1bn", and Jerome Powell replied that he was not aware of that.78

The Guardian reports the Justice Department's months-long criminal investigation was dropped in April.9 Jerome Powell said in January that the investigation was a "pretext" and was about whether the Fed could set rates on evidence or under political pressure.1011 The Guardian says the investigation stalled Kevin Warsh's confirmation as Fed chair after a key Republican senator said he would hold out until it ended.12

Attorney General Todd Blanche, whom Trump asked to review the report, said the Justice Department would not reopen a criminal investigation of Powell based on the findings, but could investigate if a Fed-commissioned independent audit finds criminal misconduct.1314 Trump posted that Powell "should be forced to resign" from the board, and economic adviser Kevin Hassett said it was "time for him to move on and to respect the independence of the Fed".1516 Powell has stayed on the board since leaving the chair in May, saying threats of criminal investigations left him no option, a stance Trump and other administration members condemned.17

Did the Fed's internal watchdog say what caused the renovation cost to nearly double?

The Fed's internal watchdog attributed the cost growth to management deficiencies, according to its Sept. 30 report.1 The report said the Fed's board of governors "has not effectively managed" the construction project and "repeatedly deviated from its cost-management provisions".2 Costs rose from an initially budgeted $921m in February 2020 to $2.018bn by December 2024.3 The watchdog found no reasonable grounds to believe federal criminal law was violated and identified no administrative misconduct.456 The reports did not detail any more specific cause for the cost increase beyond these management failures.

What will the independent audit the Fed plans to commission examine, and could its findings lead to a new Justice Department investigation?

The Fed has said it will bring in an independent auditor to scrutinize the costs of its headquarters renovation.1 Attorney General Todd Blanche said the Justice Department would not reopen a criminal investigation of Powell based on the watchdog's findings.23 Blanche did say, however, that the department could investigate if the independent review turns up evidence of criminal wrongdoing, declaring, "If they learn anything that rises to any kind of criminal misconduct, you better believe we’re going to investigate that."45 The specific scope of the audit beyond the building costs was not reported.
