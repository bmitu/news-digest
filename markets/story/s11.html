Conference Board consumer confidence

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 17:18 UTC
2 sources · single origin (Conference Board)

The Conference Board reported that its Consumer Confidence Index fell to 81.9 in September, a drop of 6.7 points and well below the Dow Jones consensus forecast of 89, with CNBC reporting that consumer sentiment fell to its lowest level since 2014.12 The Present Situation Index fell 7.9 points to 109.3 and the Expectations Index slipped 5.9 points to 63.6.3 CNBC says the reading is in keeping with similar surveys, noting that the University of Michigan's survey showed sentiment fell 7% in September to its second-lowest reading on record.4

Respondents cited concerns over inflation and the jobs outlook, and for the first time in the four-year history of the survey question, more said their personal finances were bad than good.56 Dana Peterson, the Conference Board's chief economist, said consumer appraisals of current business conditions turned negative for the first time since September 2024, and that references to prices, especially oil and gas, rose to new heights, reflecting September's surge in fuel costs.78

Respondents on average expect inflation of 6.1%, up 0.3 percentage point from August, and the median expectation rose 0.3 point to 5.1%.9 CNBC links rising inflation expectations to continued uncertainty over the Iran war, which it says has been reflected in surging Treasury yields and mortgage rates.10

Bespoke Investment Group sees a stark disconnect between hard employment data and consumers' labor market views, noting that job openings continue to improve while consumer sentiment toward employment remains dour.1112 The sources differ on job openings: CNBC reports August openings edged lower to 7.08 million, below the 7.2 million consensus, while Bespoke says openings continue to improve. inference1314
