G7 agrees to release 100 million barrels of oil

Last scan Mon Oct 5, 15:38 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 4 · 1 new article today · single origin, unclear which

The G7 nations agreed on Friday to release 100 million barrels of oil to head off further price increases, according to the BBC.1 Investing.com reported that shipping data showed Middle East crude exports exceeded pre-war levels on four days in late September, despite ongoing vessel attacks in the Strait of Hormuz, and that the G7 nations also released oil stocks, which further increased supplies.23 Investing.com said most major Gulf stock markets opened higher on Monday as concerns about energy disruptions eased and expectations for an immediate Federal Reserve rate increase declined.4

Meanwhile, the RAC motoring group said the average UK diesel price had risen above £2 a litre for the first time, with petrol at 174.71p a litre on average.5 The RAC said price rises were "showing no signs of slowing, heaping more misery onto motorists."6

How is the 100 million barrels split between diesel and crude, and how quickly will the diesel be released?

Under proposals reportedly drawn up by France's Macron, the Guardian reports G7 members will release 50 million barrels of diesel and 50 million of crude oil.1 The G7's joint statement itself says only that the release will be a mix of diesel and crude, without giving the split.2 The diesel release is to be frontloaded: G7 leaders say a substantial diesel release will come within the first 20 days, by G7 members and partners.34 The overall 100 million barrels is to begin immediately and run over four months, and leaders have discussed additional diesel releases as necessary.567 It is not yet clear which partner countries will release stocks, or how quickly.8 The exact volume of diesel to be released within the first 20 days was not reported.

What did Donald Trump threaten to do that pushed the G7 into this deal, and what did members agree about export restrictions?

Donald Trump threatened to ban US diesel exports unless European countries put more of their own stocks onto the market.12 On export restrictions, the G7 agreed there will be no "export restrictions on energy and energy products" between its members.34 French President Emmanuel Macron said Trump "in particular, was very clear on this point" about avoiding export bans.56 However, Trump later said an export ban on diesel was "never really on the table".7

Do analysts think the G7 release will fix the underlying supply problem, or what do they say is needed instead?
