Hot September PPI report

Last scan Thu Oct 22, 01:18 UTC
8 sources · 4 independent origins: Axios, Bureau of Labor Statistics, CNBC, Employ America · Official data: Core PCE inflation (year over year)

September's Producer Price Index came in much hotter than economists forecast, rising 1.3% on the month and 6.3% year over year, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics as reported by CNBC.12 CNBC reports these were the largest monthly and annual gains since March 2022 and December 2022, respectively, against Dow Jones forecasts of 0.6% and 5.5%.345 Core PPI, which excludes food and energy, rose 0.7% on the month and 5% over 12 months, and CNBC says energy drove a sizable part of the increase amid the U.S. war with Iran.67 The BLS said almost half of September's rise in processed goods for immediate demand was tied to higher diesel fuel prices.8

CNBC says that excluding food, energy and trade services, prices still rose 0.5%, which it takes as a sign that inflation was broader than energy alone.9 Paul Krugman argues the PPI shows Donald Trump's tariffs and war threw away a benign inflation inheritance, that it is mostly about diesel, and that it is often a leading indicator of consumer prices, so no near-term relief is visible.101112

Employ America updated its nowcast for September core PCE to 3.09% year over year, said today's upside came from airfares, and maintains its base case that the FOMC holds in October and hikes in December.131415 CNBC's gathering of seven Wall Street estimates points to September core PCE rising 0.32%, up from 0.25% in August.16

Nationwide's Kathy Bostjancic sees real risks the Fed will deliver another rate hike in two weeks' time, and Mizuho's Alex Pelle says that on the economic data alone there is little reason for the Fed to hesitate to continue moving the policy rate higher.1718 Fed futures odds of an October hike differ by report: CNBC's Economy piece cites 29%, while the other CNBC article cites nearly 34% from CME's FedWatch, and both cite roughly 90% odds of at least one hike by year-end. inference192021
