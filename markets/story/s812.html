CoreWeave bond and stock selloff

Last scan Wed Oct 21, 19:55 UTC
4 sources · 3 independent origins: Axios, CNBC, Financial Times

CoreWeave's stock and bonds sold off sharply in mid-October 2026, with the yield to maturity on its 9.75% bond due 2031 reaching 15.78% on Thursday and 15.97% by Tuesday, Oct. 20, as investors grew worried about AI debt. inference12 The stock plunged about 15% on Wednesday, when shares of neocloud companies fell after CoreWeave's debt yields kept rising, and the Financial Times reported that the company is targeting insurance companies for further debt financing.34 CoreWeave closed 5.94% higher on Thursday, and on Tuesday it rose roughly 2% after hitting a 52-week low earlier in the session. inference56

The company has about $51.6 billion in total debt, and the spread on its five-year credit default swaps reached above 1,140 basis points at one point on Thursday. inference78 S&P Global Market Intelligence's Gavan Nolan says spreads of 1,000 basis points or more generally signal credit distress, and that no one is questioning whether demand for CoreWeave's services is real, only whether the cash flows will ultimately arrive.910

Options traders piled in, with CoreWeave options volume above seven times the 30-day average and traders buying just shy of 200,000 puts against 106,000 calls.1112 Luke Kawa noted record five-day net purchases of CoreWeave on Robinhood after the drop.13

CEO Mike Intrator dismissed the moves in a memo obtained by CNBC, saying the market sometimes acts irrationally and that the company's backlog more than supports its debt obligations.1415 Janus Henderson's John Lloyd, who owns the 2031 bonds, calls the selloff "a little bit of overreacting" and says the bonds should recover, "just not in that straight line." CNBC notes that some CoreWeave debt remains deeply distressed with yields above 15%.1617

Others are more cautious. Jim Reid warns that the concern could prove a self-fulfilling prophecy that spreads to other AI-related names, and the Evercore ISI trading desk says the market is "basically saying 'no mas' to AI debt without operating cash flow support."1819 Sell-side views are split: a CNBC Pro contributor writes that HSBC has a Sell rating with a $39 target, while BNP and Cantor Fitzgerald think the fears are overblown, and Deutsche Bank calls the dislocation a buying opportunity. inference20212223
