September 2026 US jobs report

Last scan Tue Oct 6, 17:01 UTC
8 sources · tracked since Oct 4 · 1 new article today · 3 independent origins: Bureau of Labor Statistics, CNBC, New Deal democrat

US employers added just 29,000 jobs in September 2026, well below expectations, and the unemployment rate rose to 4.2%, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Friday.123 Private employers added 46,000 jobs while governments shed 17,000, according to Wolf Richter.45 Healthcare drove most of the gains, adding 17,000 jobs per CNBC, while information lost 10,000 and financial activities 7,000.678

Revisions removed a combined 60,000 jobs from earlier months: August was revised to a gain of 133,000 and July to a loss of 10,000 (per CNBC).91011 Sources differ on the August figure and on the size of the miss: the Guardian says 162,000 jobs were added in August, while CNBC and the BBC cite a revised 133,000. The Guardian puts expectations at just under 70,000, versus 84,000 in CNBC's Dow Jones survey. inference121314

Average hourly earnings rose 0.1% in September for a 3% annual gain, which CNBC says is the lowest since May 2021.1516 The household survey was stronger than the payrolls survey: CNBC reports household employment rose 406,000 and the labor force 485,000, with participation up to 61.8%.1718

Bradley Saunders of Capital Economics called the figure "not disastrous", and George Brown of Schroders said a single soft report is unlikely to signal a lasting collapse.1920 Heather Long of Navy Federal Credit Union said wage growth is being wiped out by inflation, though she called the labor market "stable" and does not expect the Fed to be dissuaded from a December hike.2122

CNBC says traders read the weak report as further cementing the Fed staying put at its October meeting: CME FedWatch odds of a hold rose to 82.8%, and Jefferies' Thomas Simons called the number "the nail in the coffin for an October hike."232425

How did the report differ for men and women, and for Black workers compared with white workers?

Women outnumbered men in the workforce for eight straight months as of September, the longest stretch since 2010, according to Indeed.12 The number of employed men was lower by about 1.2 million in September than a year earlier, while women's employment rose by more than 650,000 over the same period.3 In September itself, men made up slightly over half of net job growth, according to a CNBC analysis, after accounting for just 2% of payroll growth a month earlier.4 For Black and white workers, unemployment among Black Americans rose a full percentage point to 7%, double the rate of white Americans, according to the Guardian's reading of the data.5
