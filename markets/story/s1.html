September 2026 US jobs report

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 18:00 UTC
7 sources · 3 independent origins: Bureau of Labor Statistics, CNBC, New Deal democrat

US employers added just 29,000 jobs in September, and the unemployment rate rose to 4.2%, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Friday, far below the 84,000 gain economists surveyed by Dow Jones had expected.123 Private-sector employers added 46,000 jobs while governments shed 17,000, according to Wolf Richter.45 The previous two months were revised down: August to a gain of 133,000 and July to a loss of 10,000, together 60,000 fewer jobs than first reported.678 Sources differ on the details: the Guardian says economists expected just under 70,000 jobs, and it puts the August gain at 162,000, while CNBC and the BBC cite 133,000 for August after revisions. inference9101112

Average hourly earnings rose 0.1% in September, putting the 12-month gain at 3%, which CNBC describes as the lowest since May 2021; Wall Street had looked for 0.3% and 3.1%.1314 The household survey was stronger than the payroll survey: CNBC reports household employment rose by 406,000 and the labor force by 485,000.1516

Employ America characterized the report as "soft, but not dire", saying the prime-age employment and unemployment rates are healthy, and said the labor market is not a source of inflationary pressure.1718 Thomas Simons of Jefferies said the number should be "the nail in the coffin for an October hike", while Heather Long of Navy Federal Credit Union called the market "stable" and does not think the Fed will be dissuaded from hiking in December.1920

Market-implied odds of the Fed holding at its Oct. 27-28 meeting jumped to 82.8%, according to CME's FedWatch tool, and Treasury yields slumped after the report.2122 Wolf Richter notes the 10-year yield dropped to 5.15% on Friday morning but re-climbed and closed the week at 5.28%, as bond investors found the jobs data "pretty decent".232425

The BBC says the report contrasts with President Donald Trump's insistence that the economy is booming, and notes an AP/NORC poll showing 26% approve of his handling of the economy.2627

Which industries added and lost jobs in September, and how much did healthcare contribute?

Three major private-sector industries lost jobs in September: financial activities (down 7,000), information (down 10,000) and professional and business services (down 9,000).123 Government also cut jobs, shedding 17,000 overall, mostly at local governments.45 Most other industries added jobs, including construction (up 11,000), leisure and hospitality (up 10,000), manufacturing (up 9,000) and transportation and warehousing (up 8,000).6 CNBC reports that healthcare accounted for more than half of overall payroll expansion in September, although the Bureau of Labor Statistics said the sector hired more slowly than its average pace over the past year.78

Why is the unemployment rate low even though job creation is so weak?

Wolf Richter argues that a shrinking labor force has changed the dynamics of the labor market, producing a low unemployment rate despite so-so job creation.1 He attributes the labor force decline of roughly 2 million people from the 2025 peak to the crackdown on illegal immigration and continued boomer retirements.2 CNBC reports that layoffs are low: weekly jobless claims are low and one indicator shows layoffs at their lowest rate in four years, painting a picture of a low-hire, low-fire economy.3 Employ America likewise calls the September report consistent with a low-hire, low-fire market, and says a low and shifting breakeven rate means 29,000 new jobs isn't terrible.45 CNBC reports that the household survey, which is used to calculate the jobless level, was considerably better than the establishment survey, which is used to derive the payrolls count. How did the prime-age labor force participation rate and the overall participation rate change in September?

The prime-age labor force participation rate rose to 83.7% in September, its highest level since May, according to Wolf Richter.12 The three-month average of the prime-age rate also rose, to 83.5%.3 The overall participation rate increased 0.2 percentage point to 61.8%, also its highest since May, CNBC reported.4 Both measures rose as the labor force grew by 485,000 people in September. inference56
