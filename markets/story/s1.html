September 2026 US jobs report

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 03:34 UTC
8 sources · 2 independent origins: Bureau of Labor Statistics, CNBC

US employers added just 29,000 jobs in September 2026 and the unemployment rate rose to 4.2%, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Friday.12 The Guardian and the BBC both describe it as the final jobs report before the midterm elections. inference34 Economists surveyed by Dow Jones had expected 84,000 jobs and a 4.1% unemployment rate.5 July was revised to a loss of 10,000 jobs and August to a gain of 133,000, which together is 60,000 fewer jobs than previously reported.678

Wolf Richter reports that private employers added 46,000 jobs while governments shed 17,000.910 Healthcare added 17,000 jobs, while information lost 10,000 and financial activities lost 7,000.111213

Average hourly earnings rose 0.1% in September, and the 12-month gain of 3% was the lowest since May 2021, according to CNBC.14 The household survey was stronger than the payrolls survey. CNBC reports household employment rose by 406,000 and participation rose to 61.8%.1516

CNBC reports that traders interpreted the soft jobs numbers as likely cementing the Fed staying put at its October meeting, and that market-implied odds of a hold at its Oct. 27-28 meeting jumped to 82.8%, according to CME FedWatch.17 Wolf Richter says the 10-year Treasury yield fell to 5.15% on Friday morning but closed the week at 5.28%.1819

Thomas Simons of Jefferies said the number "should be the nail in the coffin for an October hike." Heather Long of Navy Federal Credit Union called the labor market "stable" and said she doesn't think the Fed will be dissuaded from hiking in December.2021 Employ America characterized the report as "soft, but not dire," while the BBC reports Capital Economics' Bradley Saunders said the lower figure was "not disastrous."2223

The BBC says the report contrasts with President Donald Trump's insistence that the economy is booming, and notes an AP/NORC poll showing 26% approve of his handling of the economy.2425

Why did the unemployment rate rise even though household employment grew by 406,000?

The labor force grew even faster than household employment, so the jobless rate edged up. inference12 The labor force is made up of people who are working and people who are not working but are actively looking for work, so the extra people searching for jobs counted as unemployed. inference3 Employ America says the slight rise in the unemployment rate came from new entrants and re-entrants to the labor force, while the number of unemployed job losers fell by 45,000.4 The participation rate also rose 0.2 percentage point to 61.8%, its highest since May, and the unemployment rate rose to 4.2% from 4.1%.56
