UK national debt and September borrowing

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 02:27 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 21 · single origin (Office for National Statistics)

The UK's national debt topped £3 trillion for the first time in official figures published on Wednesday 21 October 2026, a week before Chancellor John Healey's first Budget.123 Despite the headline total, debt as a share of economic output, the measure used to judge the fiscal rules, fell.4

The Office for National Statistics said the government borrowed £24.5bn in September, £1.8bn more than a year earlier, as higher inflation helped push up debt interest costs.5 The cost of servicing the debt has reportedly risen sharply over the past year, and CNBC says the economy is struggling to grow.6 The borrowing figures arrived alongside inflation data showing UK inflation rose to 3.5% in the year to September from 3.1% in August, which the BBC and CNBC link mainly to fuel prices. inference78

Healey said he would maintain "fiscal discipline" in the Budget on 28 October while driving growth and giving families and businesses "some breathing space".910 The Conservatives' Shadow Chancellor Andrew Griffith said the figures showed Labour had "lost control of the economy" and urged Healey to tackle the deficit.1112

Ruth Gregory of Capital Economics said the borrowing figures "painted a dismal picture" and were higher than analysts and the OBR had expected, and she expects a cautious Budget that leaves the toughest fiscal choices for next year.1314 Suren Thiru of the Institute of Chartered Accountants in England and Wales said rising inflation makes the Budget "a perilous high-wire act for the Chancellor" by shrinking fiscal headroom.1516
