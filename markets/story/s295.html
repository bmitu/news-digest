UK mortgage rates hit multi-year highs

Last scan Tue Oct 6, 17:01 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 4 · 2 independent origins: BBC, Nationwide

The average interest rate on a new five-year fixed mortgage in the UK has reached 6% for the first time in three years, according to figures reported by the BBC.1 Moneyfacts said the average two-year fixed rate was 5.98%, and that the five-year rate was its highest since September 2023 while the two-year rate was its highest since December 2023.23 The Guardian ties the rises to the US-Israel war with Iran, which it says has disrupted Gulf energy flows and raised inflation fears, and it reports that Moneyfacts put both the two-year and five-year averages above 5.9%.45

According to Moneyfacts, about 1,500 mortgage deals priced below 5% have vanished since the start of September, with the number falling by 99% from 1,494 to nine.67 Moneyfacts said major lenders made repeated increases in September: Barclays raised selected fixed rates four times, and HSBC, Lloyds Bank, Nationwide, NatWest, Santander and TSB each did so three times.8

The BBC says lenders face higher costs amid international concern over prices, interest rates and government borrowing costs, and Moneyfacts' Rachel Springall said rises were "inevitable" because wholesale funding costs had climbed with rising gilt yields.910 Springall called the situation "disastrous news for borrowers" and said those hoping for stabilisation "will be disappointed".1112

Investors are pricing in a 92% chance that the Bank of England will raise rates from 3.75% to 4% on 5 November, the Guardian reports.13 The BBC also cites Bank of England forecasts that just over five million homeowners should expect higher monthly repayments by the end of 2028.14
