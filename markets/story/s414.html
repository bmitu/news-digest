Treasury Will Auto-Enroll 60 Million Children in Trump Accounts

Last scan Tue Oct 6, 17:01 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 4 · 1 new article today · single origin (BBC)

The BBC reports that US President Donald Trump launched a retirement investment scheme for children called Trump Accounts in July 2026.1 According to the BBC, families, friends and employers can contribute up to $5,000 (£3,800) per year per child.2 The BBC says children can access the funds from age 18, but withdrawals are subject to taxes and a possible 10% penalty if made before age 59 and a half.3 However, the BBC article is mainly about UK parents paying into children's pensions (Junior SIPPs), and it mentions Trump Accounts only as a comparison. inference45

Wally Luckeydoo, a personal finance teacher at Smyrna High School in Tennessee, has opened Trump Accounts for his two children, aged four and three.6 Luckeydoo says he does not see the accounts specifically as retirement saving, but as giving his children a head start and helping change his family's financial trajectory.7

How does the Trump Account's $5,000 annual contribution limit compare with the UK Junior SIPP's £2,880 limit plus £720 tax relief?

The Trump Account allows families, friends and employers to contribute up to $5,000 (about £3,800) per child each year.1 The UK Junior SIPP, by contrast, caps a person's own payment at £2,880 a year, and the government tops that up with £720 in tax relief to reach £3,600.2 On BBC's figures, the Trump Account's limit of roughly £3,800 is therefore slightly higher than the Junior SIPP's £3,600 total including tax relief, and well above the £2,880 that a UK family can pay in itself. inference34 The accounts also differ on access: Trump Account funds can be reached from age 18, though withdrawals are taxed and face a possible 10% penalty before 59½, while Junior SIPP money is locked until 57 under current UK rules. inference56

How much could a Junior SIPP grow to by retirement if a family pays in £50 a month from birth?

Fidelity pensions specialist Jemma Slingo says a family paying £50 a month from birth, including tax relief, would contribute £10,800 over 18 years.1 She says the pot could grow to around £135,000 by retirement.2 Slingo says the example shows the power of starting early, because relatively modest amounts have a very long time to compound.34 Under current UK private pension rules, the money can't be accessed until the child is 57.5 The growth assumptions behind the £135,000 figure were not reported.

How quickly has demand for children's pensions grown among UK providers such as Hargreaves Lansdown and Fidelity?

Hargreaves Lansdown says it opened two and a half times as many accounts in the 12 months to April 2026 as in the same period a year earlier.12 Fidelity says the number of accounts has more than tripled since December 2023.34 However, the two figures cover different periods and measures, with Hargreaves Lansdown's a year-on-year rise in new accounts and Fidelity's growth over a period of roughly two years, so they are not directly comparable. inference56
