Markets price Fed hold at October meeting

Last scan Thu Oct 22, 01:18 UTC
2 sources · single origin (CNBC) · Official data: CPI inflation (year over year)

Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh declined at an International Monetary Fund event in Bangkok on Friday to signal whether the Fed will raise rates again at its meeting later this month.12 Warsh said it is "a new chapter at the Fed" and that "we're not in that business anymore," acknowledging his stance may disappoint those hoping for a steer.3 Warsh also described a "synchronous tightening in monetary policy" by central banks worldwide, and S&P 500 and Nasdaq futures edged higher after his speech.45

Markets nonetheless lean strongly toward a hold: CME FedWatch showed a 72.3% chance of unchanged rates at the Oct. 27-28 meeting, while CNBC's analysis cited futures pricing a 15% probability of an October hike as of Friday morning. inference67 The Fed raised rates to a range of 3.75% to 4% last month, its first hike since 2023, and the majority of traders expect a 25 basis point hike in December.89 Goldman Sachs economists led by Jan Hatzius wrote that Warsh's comments and other FOMC remarks indicate a pause in October, and they continue to expect a second and final hike in December.1011

The analysis points to hot inflation data, with consumer prices up 3.7% and producer prices up 6.3% for the year through September, as a case Warsh could invoke for a hike on Oct. 28.1213 The analysis also notes that Vice Chairman Philip Jefferson and New York Fed President John Williams recently signaled little urgency to raise rates, but says outsiders cannot be certain they still speak for Warsh.1415
