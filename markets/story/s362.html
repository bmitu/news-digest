10-year Treasury yield hits 24-year high

Last scan Tue Oct 6, 17:01 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 4 · 1 new article today · single origin (Investing.com)

The benchmark 10-year Treasury yield hit 5.34% on Thursday, its highest level in 24 years, according to Reuters.1 Investing.com, however, describes the yield as reaching its highest level in more than two decades on Monday, though it has since retreated, which differs from Reuters' Thursday date. inference21

Reuters says yields have soared amid expectations for strong economic growth, surging energy costs driving up inflation, and more competition for capital as companies issue debt to fund AI expansions.3 The Fed's policy is another factor: it hiked rates last month for the first time in three years to cool above-target inflation, and minutes of that meeting are due Wednesday.45 After weaker-than-expected jobs data on Friday, markets kept bets that the Fed would likely hold off on a second straight hike at its meeting later in the month.6

Investing.com also reports that stocks shook off a Treasury bond rout, with the S&P 500 hitting an intraday record on Tuesday.78 Chuck Carlson of Horizon Investment Services calls the interest rate story "the biggest headwind" for stocks, saying a meaningful rally against it will be a struggle.910 Investors told Reuters that higher yields likely already impede the market's advance by competing with equities and raising borrowing costs.11
