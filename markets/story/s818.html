UK August GDP growth 0.1%

Last scan Wed Oct 21, 19:55 UTC
2 sources · single origin (Office for National Statistics)

CNBC reports that the UK economy grew by a better-than-expected 0.1% in the month to August, with production growth offsetting weak output in services and construction.12 Economists polled by Reuters had expected GDP to shrink by 0.1%, and the reading was a slowdown from growth of 0.4% month-on-month in June and July.34 Production grew by 0.8%, services showed no growth and construction fell by 0.2%.5 Annual GDP growth was 2.1% in August against expectations of 1.7%, which the ONS said was the highest year-on-year reading since August 2022.67

The pound rose 0.4% against the dollar after the release and gilt yields fell, with the 10-year yield 9 basis points lower at 5.3364%.89

Analysts said the slowdown added to pressure on Chancellor John Healey ahead of his first Budget on 28 October.10 Joe Nellis of accountancy firm MHA says the economy remains fragile and rising debt interest costs have severely constrained fiscal headroom, and he warns that tax rises or unfunded spending both carry risks.111213 Scott Gardner of J.P. Morgan Personal Investing says the outlook for the rest of the year "remains uncertain with a tricky Autumn Budget ahead."14

Sanjay Raja of Deutsche Bank was more upbeat, saying signs show the engine of the economy slowing from its first-half pace but "the U.K.'s resilience story continues."1516 Raja notes the UK remains the fastest-growing G7 economy, though he expects "some hiccups" before year end as energy prices and higher interest rates weigh.1718 The BBC reports that analysts see the economy as resilient against energy price shocks from the war with Iran, but economists expect growth to slow in coming months.1920
