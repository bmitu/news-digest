Brazil assets surge on election

Last scan Tue Oct 6, 17:01 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Oct 5 · 1 new article today · 2 independent origins: Financial Times, Investing.com

Right-wing senator Flavio Bolsonaro outperformed poll predictions in the first round of Brazil's presidential election and advanced to a runoff against leftist incumbent Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva, according to Reuters.12 Bespoke Investment Group describes the result as a surprise win and says Kalshi currently has Bolsonaro as the favorite in the run-off at 82%.34 Bespoke also notes that Flávio Bolsonaro is the son of former President Jair Bolsonaro, who it says is serving a 27-year sentence on house arrest after a conviction for plotting a coup.5

A Reuters Morning Bid note said investors were already gearing up for a strong market reaction in Brazil ahead of Monday's trading.6 Brazilian assets surged on the result: Reuters reports the iShares MSCI Brazil ETF jumped about 14% and the real surged against the dollar.7 Reuters attributes the rally to hopes that Bolsonaro's lead signals a more business-friendly policy agenda.8

Bespoke said the Brazil ETF (EWZ) was trading up more than 12% before the open and the iShares Latin America ETF (ILF) was up more than 6% pre-market.9 Bespoke says this would be only the fifth time EWZ has opened up more than 10%, and that the only earlier Brazil-specific such gap was on 8/8/2002 after an IMF rescue package announcement.1011

When is the Brazil presidential runoff between Bolsonaro and Lula being held?

The Brazil presidential runoff between Flávio Bolsonaro and President Lula is set for Oct. 25, according to Bloomberg.12 CNBC likewise reported that a runoff would take place Oct. 25 if neither candidate won more than 50% of the first-round vote.2

Why do investors favor Flavio Bolsonaro, and what policies like spending cuts or fiscal discipline are they expecting from him?

CNBC reports that Bolsonaro is the markets' favored candidate because he is promising more fiscal discipline, which many economists say Brazil badly needs.12 The Financial Times says the right-winger is seen as more likely than Lula to enact quick spending cuts.3 Citi's Brazil head economist, Leonardo Porto, says Brazil needs a 3-3.5% fiscal adjustment to stabilize public debt, and that it must be permanent rather than from one-offs like privatizations.45 Such an adjustment would mean cutting spending or raising taxes, which will be difficult because about 90% of the budget is mandatory and Brazil's tax burden is already the highest in Latin America.67 The specific spending cuts or fiscal measures Bolsonaro has proposed are not detailed.

How did Brazilian stocks and bonds perform under Jair Bolsonaro's earlier pension reform, and could a repeat be expected?

JPMorgan says that during Jair Bolsonaro's pension reform period, Brazil's 2-year yields fell almost to 4.7% and the equity market gained 130%.1 The reform imposed a minimum retirement age of 65 for men and 60 for women and saved hundreds of billions of dollars.2 A repeat is being priced in by markets: after his son Flavio Bolsonaro outperformed polls in the first round, the iShares MSCI Brazil ETF jumped about 14% and the real surged, on hopes of a more business-friendly agenda. inference34 Prediction market Kalshi has Flavio Bolsonaro as the favorite in the runoff at 82%, though an Aurora Macro Strategies adviser said earlier that prediction markets overstate his lead. inference567 Fiscal constraints could make reform hard: roughly 90% of Brazil's budget is mandatory, and the tax burden is already the highest in Latin America.89

Do analysts think prediction markets are overstating Bolsonaro's chances in the runoff?

After Bolsonaro won the first round, Bespoke reported that Kalshi had him as the favorite to win at 82%.12 At least one analyst says prediction markets are overstating his chances: Aurora Macro Strategies' Richard Lapper wrote that the race has shifted toward Flavio Bolsonaro over the past month, but not nearly as far as prediction markets are pricing.34 CNBC also noted that prediction markets are prohibited in Brazil, so they may not reflect local sentiment.5 Whether analysts think the 82% price overstates his chances after the first round was not reported.
