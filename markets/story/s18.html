Gold price drop to multi-month low

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 17:18 UTC
3 sources · 2 independent origins: Bespoke Investment Group, CNBC

Gold fell sharply on Monday, with CNBC reporting a 4% drop to its lowest since the first week of August as the 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 5.3% and the 30-year yield touched 5.4%.12 The size of the drop varies by source and time of day: Bespoke's Morning Lineup had gold down close to 3% before the open, and its later note had front-month gold down 3.5% at the time of writing. inference34

Bespoke says the decline further solidifies last week's break below support at the 50-day moving average, and that gold is now oversold for the first time since July 29th.56 Bespoke notes that a 3.5% daily drop ranks in the first percentile of all days over the past half century, yet it is the seventh such decline this year.78 According to Bespoke, that is the most such declines in a year since 2008, when there were 8, and 1982 was the last earlier year with as many.9

CNBC reports that options traders think gold could rebound, with about twice as many calls as puts traded in the SPDR Gold Shares ETF (GLD), more than 68,000 calls likely bought against under 32,000 puts, per Cboe LiveVol data.10 The largest single GLD trade was a seller of 2,000 375-strike puts expiring January 2028 worth $5.9 million, which CNBC says may have been a closing of a bearish position or a bet that gold finds a floor around $375.1112 CNBC adds that gold's 10-day correlation with the 10-year yield is negative 0.8, and that high-yield bonds, which have a five-day rout, drew more bearish options bets than gold. inference131415

How much has gold fallen in September, and how does that compare with stocks and long-term Treasuries?

Through Monday afternoon, with two days left in September, gold (via GLD) was down more than 7% for the month, according to Bespoke.1 That is a steeper drop than the average stock, which was down 4.2% as measured by the equal-weight S&P 500 ETF (RSP), and than long-term Treasuries, which were down 4.7% as measured by TLT. inference23 The selling in gold was heavy on Monday, when it dropped 4% as the 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 5.3%.4 Bespoke says that, barring a major rally in the last two trading days of September, this September will be the seventh time since 2005 that stocks, bonds and gold each fell at least 2.5% in the same month, and that from 2005 through most of 2021 it never happened once.567

Why are high-yield bonds seeing more bearish options bets than gold, and what do analysts say about default risk?

Options traders were far more bearish on high-yield bonds than on gold on Monday: puts outnumbered calls by more than 2.5 to 1 in the HYG ETF, with about 52,000 puts likely bought against just over 15,000 calls.1 Gold options leaned the other way, with about twice as many calls as puts traded in GLD, which suggests traders expect a rebound.23 Both assets were being hit by surging rates: gold fell 4% as the 10-year yield reached 5.3%, and high-yield bonds extended a five-day rout to their lowest level since April 2025.45 On default risk, Nigam Arora of The Arora Report says sentiment in high-yield bonds has been very complacent and that default risk is probably much higher than the market expects.6 Arora points to variable-rate debt and debt coming due next year, and says the spread isn't compensating investors, so he wouldn't touch the bonds.78 Wolf Richter also says junk bonds carry a substantial amount of credit risk (the risk of default, where unsecured bonds tend to get wiped out) and that he considers them in the same risk category as stocks.910

How is silver doing compared with gold, and has copper broken below its 50-day moving average?

How far has gold fallen from its peak, and what is a sensible price to buy back in?

Wolf Richter says gold has plunged by $1,200 an ounce, or 25%, over roughly the past seven months.1 Gold dropped 4% to its lowest since the first week of August, as the 10-year yield climbed to 5.3%, according to CNBC.23 Comex gold fell 0.9% to $4133.70 a troy ounce, snapping a three-session winning streak.4 Richter cautions against buying high, saying gold pays no interest or dividend and that a buyer at high prices might wait decades for it to catch up with inflation. He adds that gold makes sense if you buy low.56 One reader, Chris B., says $4,000 seems like a good re-entry point, while another commenter, SoCalBeachDude, says $20 is a much better entry point.78 No authoritative buy-back price for gold was reported.
