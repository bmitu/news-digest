August PCE inflation and Q2 GDP revisions

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 17:18 UTC
5 sources · single origin (Employ America)

The Commerce Department reported on Wednesday (September 30, 2026) that the PCE price index rose 0.3% in August for a 12-month gain of 3.4%, below the Dow Jones consensus of 3.7%, while core PCE rose 0.2% for a 3% annual rate against a forecast of 3.3%.123 The release coincided with Bureau of Economic Analysis methodology changes for legal services, software and computer accessories, and portfolio management, which CNBC says lowered the core July PCE level by 0.36 percentage point.45 Employ America put core PCE at +25bps month-over-month and 3.01% year-over-year, versus its nowcasts of 28bps and 3.14%, and said its portfolio management and software estimates were close while its legal services estimate missed sizably (+185bps realized vs +90bps nowcast).678

Separately, the final estimate showed second-quarter GDP growing at a 2.2% annualized rate, up from 1.5% previously, while Q2 headline PCE prices (5%) and core (3.3%) were each revised 0.3 percentage point lower.910 Commenters on Wolf Richter's site cited different Q2 GDP figures (8.5% and 8.4% nominal), and Bespoke said nominal output grew at the fastest pace since 2022 after the BEA's annual benchmark revisions. inference1112

Markets reacted by pricing a smaller chance of an October Fed hike; LSEG data cited by the Guardian put it near 35%, down from about 45%, while CNBC said the next expected increase moved to December.1314 Reactions differed: David Russell of TradeStation said the data bolster the case for not hiking in October, while Sonu Varghese of Carson Group said PCE inflation is still running hot even after the revisions, and Heather Long of Navy Federal Credit Union said the data show no progress on inflation.15161718

Wolf Richter said the changes brought PCE roughly in line with the CPI after it had run hotter, but a commenter noted that all three revisions lowered measured inflation and that this could erode public trust in the figures. inference192021

How much would September core PCE need to rise to move the 3-month annualized rate back toward 3%?

The measure is currently running at 2%, partly because it leans on very soft June and July readings. June drops out of the calculation with the September data.123 Employ America estimates that September core PCE would need to rise about 35 to 40 basis points for the 3-month annualized rate to get back to roughly 3%.4 A 35bp reading would put the measure at 2.96%, and a 40bp reading would put it at 3.16%. Both figures assume no revisions to July and August.546 That is well above August's core reading of 25bps, so a jump of that size would be a sharp acceleration. inference78 Employ America sees upside risk to September from airfares, food services, wireless communication services, tech equipment and other core goods categories.
