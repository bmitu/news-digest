August PCE inflation report

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 03:32 UTC
4 sources · single origin (Employ America)

Excluding food and energy, core PCE rose 0.2% on the month and 3% on the year, against forecasts of 0.3% and 3.3%, according to CNBC.1

The report came as the Bureau of Economic Analysis changed how it computes prices for legal services, software and computer accessories, and portfolio management, revisions that CNBC says lowered the core July PCE level by 0.36 percentage point.23 Employ America's nowcast had core PCE at +25bps month over month and 3.01% year over year, versus its own nowcasts of 28bps and 3.14%, and it says the large error bands came from the benchmark revisions and the three methodological changes.45

Employ America says its legal services nowcast missed sizably (+185bps realized vs +90bps nowcast month over month) while portfolio management and software were close, and that the miss was worth less than 1bp on core PCE.67 Employ America notes the 3-month annualized core PCE rate is running at 2%, but says it leans on very soft June and July readings and is a noisy gauge.89

The Guardian, citing LSEG data, says traders now see roughly a 35% chance of an October hike, down from about 45%, and Employ America says October pricing has moved to a coinflip. inference1011

Reactions were mixed: Sonu Varghese of Carson Group said PCE inflation is "still running hot however you cut it," and Heather Long of Navy Federal Credit Union said the data show "no progress in August on inflation."1213 Wolf Richter argues the methodology changes brought PCE roughly in line with CPI again, while a commenter on his site said that all three revisions lowered measured inflation.1415

How much did the headline PCE price index rise in August, and how did that compare with forecasts?

The headline PCE price index rose a seasonally adjusted 0.3% in August, putting the 12-month gain at 3.4%.1 That was softer than forecast on an annual basis: economists surveyed by Dow Jones had expected 0.3% for the month and 3.7% for the year.2 Reuters-polled economists also expected an annual rate of 3.7%, against the 3.4% reported.3 The monthly headline gain therefore matched forecasts, while the annual rate came in 0.3 percentage point below them. inference245
