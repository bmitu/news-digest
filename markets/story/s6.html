September FOMC 25bp rate hike

Last scan Mon Oct 5, 15:38 UTC

The FOMC voted to raise the target range for the federal funds rate by 1/4 percentage point to 3-3/4 to 4 percent, according to Jefferson, who described the vote as occurring in the month before his speech.12 Governor Michael Barr says the committee agreed unanimously, and Lisa Cook says she voted along with the rest of the FOMC. inference34 Employ America says Fedspeak after the decision was hawkish.5

Philip Jefferson says the 12-month PCE inflation rate was 3.4 percent in August and that inflation has exceeded the 2 percent target for more than five years.67 Lisa Cook puts total inflation at an estimated 3.8 percent and core at an estimated 3.4 percent over the 12 months to August.89 Officials point to energy prices tied to the Middle East conflict and to the AI buildout as drivers of inflation. inference101112

Officials cite an unemployment rate of 4.1 percent in August and describe the labor market as stable or roughly balanced. inference131415 Barr says further policy adjustments are likely to be needed in his base case.16 Cook says the number and magnitude of any future adjustments will be informed by observations of the economy's reaction to the Fed's policy actions thus far and the inflation and labor data over the coming months.17

Donald Trump, by contrast, told the Fed not to raise rates two days before the decision and to cut them sharply, saying "We should be at 1% or half a percent," according to the Guardian columnist Steven Greenhouse.1819 Greenhouse argues that such low rates would push prices up, and he quotes the economist Justin Wolfers calling the demand "the dumbest thing ever said by a president".2021

Did the August core PCE and July inflation readings come in softer than expected?

August core PCE came in softer than expected: it rose 0.2% on the month and 3.0% from a year earlier, against forecasts of 0.3% and 3.3%.1 Headline PCE was also lighter than expected on an annual basis, at 3.4% against a 3.7% forecast (the monthly rise was 0.3%, in line with the 0.3% forecast).23 The softer readings came as the Bureau of Economic Analysis changed how it measures legal services, software and computer accessories, and portfolio management, which lowered the core July PCE level by 0.36 percentage point.4 July's core PCE reading was cut sharply: its year-over-year increase was revised to 2.98% from 3.35%, and its monthly gain was halved to 0.12%.56 It was not reported whether July inflation, as measured by the consumer price index, was softer than expected; only the revised July PCE figures were given.

What did New York Fed President John Williams say about the need for another hike, and how did he differ from the hawkish Fedspeak after the meeting?

New York Fed President John Williams said there is "no need for urgency" in deciding whether to follow September's quarter-point hike with another increase, and that the Fed has time to gather more information.12 Williams did not rule out another hike, though: he said one "may be appropriate late this year," which led markets to price the next increase for December.3 He also said the labor market is solid and has strengthened a bit on the margin.4 Employ America says Fedspeak after the September meeting was quite hawkish, and it described Williams's speech as dovish. It said that speech and a small downside surprise in core PCE moved October pricing to a coinflip.5 The hawkish side included Governor Barr, who said that in his base case further policy adjustments are likely to be needed to bring inflation down to target in a timely fashion. Williams's message was more patient by comparison. inference67
