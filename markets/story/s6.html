US mortgage rates exceed 7%

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 17:18 UTC
4 sources · 2 independent origins: Mortgage Bankers Association, The Guardian

The Guardian reported that mortgage rates rose from 7% to 7.28% in a week, the largest weekly jump since 2022, reaching their highest level in three years.1 CNBC, citing the Mortgage Bankers Association, reports six straight weeks of increases brought the average 30-year rate to 7.30%, the highest since late 2023.23 Sources differ slightly on how the peak compares with the past: the Guardian describes it as a three-year high in one article and a 20-month high in another, while CNBC says the highest since late 2023. inference453

Paul Krugman writes that the rate was about 6 percent on the eve of the Iran war and almost 7.3 percent last week, which on a $500,000 mortgage means an extra $431 a month and about $155,000 more in interest over the loan's life.67

The Guardian says the 10-year Treasury yield, which underpins mortgages, also reached a 24-year high amid a global bond sell-off, and that the Federal Reserve raised rates last month for the first time in three years.89

Krugman argues the evidence does not support fears of a US government default or runaway inflation as the cause of rising long-term rates. He says the biggest source is likely the immense demand for funds from the AI boom, with the Iran war acting as a trigger and a deleveraging spiral also contributing.1011 Krugman says the big economic news of the past month has been a "September surprise" on interest rates, and that the overall economic environment looks really bad for Trump and his party ahead of the midterms.1213
