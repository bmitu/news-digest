September FOMC 25bp rate hike

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 19:31 UTC
2 sources · single origin (Federal Reserve)

The FOMC voted last month to raise the target range for the federal funds rate by a quarter percentage point to 3-3/4 to 4 percent, according to Jefferson.1 Governor Michael Barr says the committee agreed unanimously, and Lisa Cook says she voted along with the rest of the FOMC. inference23 Employ America says Fedspeak after the decision was hawkish.4

Cook says total inflation rose an estimated 3.8 percent in the 12 months to August and core inflation 3.4 percent, while a Fed speaker, Jefferson, puts the 12-month PCE figure at 3.4 percent for August. inference56 Officials cite an unemployment rate of 4.1 percent in August, and Barr says job creation has averaged around 80,000 a month this year.78

Jefferson says he supported the hike to keep longer-term inflation expectations anchored and to validate public confidence in the 2 percent goal.9 Barr says that in his base case further policy adjustments are likely to be needed.10 Cook says the size and number of any future adjustments will depend on how the economy reacts and on incoming inflation and labor data.11

New York Fed President John Williams said "With the policy action we took at our September meeting, there is no need for urgency, and we have time to gather more information."12 He pointed to encouraging inflation indicators, saying housing services prices have decelerated and there is no evidence the labor market is adding to inflationary pressures.34 Williams also said the pressure on goods prices from tariffs has largely abated, according to CNBC's account.5 He described the labor market as solid and even a bit stronger at the margin, which CNBC says underpins the case that the Fed can focus on inflation without hurrying another hike.67 Still, Williams did not rule out tightening: he still thinks another hike "may be appropriate late this year," and markets responded by pricing the next increase for December rather than October.89

How did the September jobs report come in, and what did it show for payrolls, unemployment and wage growth?

How are AI investment and higher energy prices from the Middle East conflict adding to inflation, and could they keep rates rising?

Fed Governor Lisa Cook says AI-driven investment is one factor keeping inflation above target, with prices for chips, computers and software surging.1 Cook warns the pressure may be spreading, since data centers use construction labor and energy that other sectors also need, and she notes electricity and water costs are each up around 5 percent over the past year.23 Fed Vice Chair Philip Jefferson says energy prices are the main driver of the recent pickup in headline inflation, while AI-related demand is raising costs of related goods and services and lifting core goods prices.45 Fed Governor Michael Barr says the Middle East conflict pushed energy prices and inflation higher, that high energy prices persist, and that the AI buildout is having a measurable effect on prices, together knocking the Fed off course toward its 2 percent goal.6789 The Fed has already raised rates, by 25 basis points in September, and Barr says further increases are likely to be needed to bring inflation down in a timely way.101112 Cook expects continued inflation pressure from the AI buildout and from higher oil pass-through in coming months, while Jefferson sees the risks to inflation as tilted to the upside. Jefferson's base case, though, is that inflation resumes falling as energy and other shocks fade.131415 Abroad, Australia's central bank raised rates to 4.6%, citing higher energy prices and AI-related demand, and said it would raise rates further if needed.161718
