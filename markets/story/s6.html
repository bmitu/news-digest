September FOMC 25bp rate hike

Last scan Tue Oct 6, 17:01 UTC
4 sources · tracked since Oct 4 · 2 new articles today · single origin (Federal Reserve)

Philip Jefferson said the FOMC voted last month to raise the target range for the federal funds rate by 1/4 percentage point to 3-3/4 to 4 percent.1 Michael Barr says the committee agreed unanimously, and Cook says she voted with the rest of the FOMC for the 25 basis point increase. inference23 CNBC reports that it was the Fed's first increase in more than three years and that the Fed signaled one more hike this year.4

Jefferson says inflation is too high: the 12-month PCE price index change was 3.4 percent in August, and inflation has exceeded the 2 percent target for more than five years.56 Fed officials point to energy prices, tariffs and AI-related demand as drivers of inflation. inference78

Jefferson says the unemployment rate ticked down to 4.1 percent in August, which he views as near maximum employment, and Barr says job creation has averaged around 80,000 a month this year.910

Jefferson says he supported the hike to keep longer-term inflation expectations anchored, while Barr says risks to inflation have increased and risks to the labor market have receded. inference1112 Barr says that in his base case further policy adjustments are likely to be needed.13

Blogger Barry Ritholtz writes that the dot plot has 16 of 19 members projecting more hikes, and that futures price 70%+ odds of another hike in October.14

Outside the Fed, Guardian columnist Steven Greenhouse criticizes President Donald Trump for urging the Fed not to raise rates but to cut them, quoting him as saying "We should be at 1% or half a percent."15

How weak was the September jobs report, and what did it show for payrolls, unemployment and wage growth?

September's jobs report was weak: nonfarm payrolls rose by only 29,000, far below the 84,000 economists surveyed by Dow Jones had expected.123 Revisions made it worse, with July now showing a loss of 10,000 jobs and August cut to 133,000, for 60,000 fewer jobs in total than previously reported.456 The unemployment rate rose to 4.2%, against a forecast of 4.1%.78 Employ America says the rise was small, 0.03 percentage point to 4.18%, and came from new entrants and re-entrants to the labor force.91011 Wage growth cooled: average hourly earnings rose just 0.13% on the month and 3.0% from a year earlier, down from around 4% at the start of the year. inference1213 Employ America calls the report "soft, but not dire" and says the labor market is not a source of inflationary pressure.1415

Did the August core PCE and July inflation readings come in softer than expected?

August core PCE came in softer than expected: it rose 0.2% on the month and 3.0% from a year earlier, against forecasts of 0.3% and 3.3%.1 Headline PCE was also lighter than expected on an annual basis, at 3.4% against a 3.7% forecast (the monthly rise was 0.3%, in line with the 0.3% forecast).23 The softer readings came as the Bureau of Economic Analysis changed how it measures legal services, software and computer accessories, and portfolio management, which lowered the core July PCE level by 0.36 percentage point.4 July's core PCE reading was cut sharply: its year-over-year increase was revised to 2.98% from 3.35%, and its monthly gain was halved to 0.12%.56 It was not reported whether July inflation, as measured by the consumer price index, was softer than expected; only the revised July PCE figures were given.
