G7 100 million barrel oil reserve release

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 17:18 UTC
5 sources · 2 independent origins: BBC, The Guardian

The G7 announced on Friday 2 October 2026 that up to 100 million barrels of oil and diesel will be released from emergency reserves, after Donald Trump threatened to ban US diesel exports unless Europe tapped its own stocks. inference123 In a joint statement, G7 leaders said the release would be co-ordinated through the IEA, begin immediately over four months, and include a frontloaded substantial diesel release within the first 20 days by G7 members and partners.4 The Guardian reports that proposals reportedly drawn up by French President Emmanuel Macron, the current G7 chair, split the release into 50m barrels of diesel and 50m of crude, while the BBC says only that it will be a mix of the two. inference56 The BBC notes it is not yet clear which partner countries will release stocks, nor how quickly.7

G7 members also agreed not to impose export restrictions on energy products on each other, and Macron said Trump "was very clear on this point".8910 Trump wrote on social media that Europe had agreed to release a massive amount of diesel, and later said at the White House that an export ban was "never really on the table".1112 That account sits awkwardly with BBC's report that Trump had first said he backed a ban early the previous week and was still considering it on Thursday. inference1312

Brent crude briefly fell to about $98 or below $100 after the news but was back around $102 by Friday evening.1415 Meanwhile the RAC said UK average diesel prices passed £2 a litre for the first time, at 200.01p, with RAC policy head Simon Williams calling it a "price threshold no one wanted to cross".1617

Why did oil prices climb back above $102 after dipping below $100 on the G7 news?

Brent crude fell to about $98 a barrel on news of the emergency stockpile release, then returned to more than $102 by the end of Friday.1 Kpler's Matt Smith attributes the rebound to renewed strikes between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis in Yemen.2 Smith says oil prices reversed course on rumours of Saudi Arabia planning an offensive into Yemen as it looks to re-establish a safe path via Bab-Al Mandeb.32 Macquarie's Walt Chancellor said Europe's decision to rely more on its own emergency reserves than US imports would not address the underlying problem in the US or the global market, which he called a global energy problem; he said the solution is more oil through the strait of Hormuz and out of the Middle East.456

How much of the UK's diesel supply comes from the US, and how exposed was Britain to a US export ban?

Over half of the UK's diesel is imported, and the BBC reports that 31% of those imports come from the US.1 The BBC describes the UK as heavily reliant on US diesel imports, and the US is a vital global supplier, exporting between 1.2 and 1.5 million barrels per day.234 The exposure was real but limited by the UK's mix of sources: about a sixth of supply was at risk directly, though a ban would also have raised prices on the wider market. The government said it has a diverse and resilient supply and no cause for concern about shortages. inference5678 The proposed US export ban was expected, according to the Guardian, to fuel greater competition for cargoes on the global market and push prices in the UK and Europe well beyond current record levels, and experts warned, per the BBC, that such a ban would put further pressure on diesel prices in other countries unless they can access further supplies.910 The threat has since eased: the G7 agreed there would be no export restrictions among members, and Trump said an export ban was "never really on the table".111213

How does this 100 million barrel release compare with the IEA's 400 million barrel release in March and the 2022 release?

The G7's new release of up to 100 million barrels, split between diesel and crude oil, is a quarter the size of the IEA's 400 million barrel release in March. inference12 The March release was the largest in the IEA's history, and the Guardian reports it represented a third of the group's total government stockpiles.345 The 2022 release after Russia's invasion of Ukraine was 182 million barrels, so the new 100 million barrel release is also well short of that, at roughly half. inference678 The new release is also structured differently: it is coordinated through the IEA over four months, with a substantial diesel release front-loaded in the first 20 days.910 Bloomberg's Anthony Di Paola says the release is already pushing fuel prices lower but will likely offer only temporary relief.11

What else did the G7 agree to do about diesel supply, such as refinery maintenance schedules and refining capacity?

G7 leaders said they will coordinate refinery maintenance schedules so that multiple refineries are not shut down at the same time.1 They also said they would encourage countries that have the capacity to ramp up refining, with a particular focus on diesel.2 Members agreed there would be no export restrictions on energy and energy products among G7 members, and the leaders said they will maintain sanctions against Russia.34 However, Bloomberg's Anthony Di Paola says the release is likely to bring only temporary relief, because refinery constraints and Middle East disruptions are keeping diesel supplies tight.5
