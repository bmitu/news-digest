30-year Treasury yield hits highest since 2002

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 19:31 UTC
4 sources · 4 independent origins: Barron's, Bespoke Investment Group, Financial Times, Wolf Street

The 30-year Treasury yield briefly hit 5.69% on Thursday, which Wolf Richter says was the highest since 2002, before closing the week at 5.63%.1 Bespoke reported the 30-year yield climbing above 5.6%, its highest level in over 24 years, and Barron's, as linked by Tadas Viskanta, said it was back to 2002 levels. inference23 The Financial Times, as cited by Barry Ritholtz, put the level as the highest since 2004 and said the rout deepened on an oil-price jump, rising inflation expectations and another weak buyback operation.45

Bespoke says the 30-year yield was 3.3 standard deviations above its 50-day moving average, the most extended since May, and its 14-day RSI reached 75, the highest since January 2025.67

Richter says the 10-year yield closed the week at 5.28%, up 11 basis points, after hitting 5.36% intraday on Thursday and dropping to 5.15% on Friday's jobs data before rebounding.8910

Richter argues that yields of about 5.6% on the 30-year and 5.3% on the 10-year are not at blood-in-the-street levels but are just sort of normal after 14 years of interest rate repression.1112 Richter says he is sniffing around maturities in the 3-7-year range, and that if he nibbles it will also be too early because he expects yields in that range to rise further.1314
