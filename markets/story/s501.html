Yemen/Houthi Bab el-Mandeb fighting

Last scan Tue Oct 6, 17:01 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 5 · single origin, unclear which

Yemen's Saudi-backed government said on Sunday it was launching a major military campaign to recapture all territory held by the Iran-backed Houthis.1 Rashad al-Alimi, head of Yemen's presidential council, said in a televised address that the offensive would continue "until the country is liberated from the grip of the terrorist militia."2 Reuters reported on Monday, citing government and military sources, that government forces took control of the Dhubab district overlooking the Bab el-Mandeb with air support from a Saudi-led coalition.3 However, Houthi-aligned media sources denied that government forces had made advances.4

Reuters reported that the Houthis had launched a surprise offensive last month that captured the Bab el-Mandeb strait area and about 150 km (90 miles) of Red Sea coast, giving them new leverage over a key shipping route.5 Investing.com's report says worries have abounded that the Houthi presence over the Bab el-Mandeb will further dent Middle East oil flows, already crimped by Iran's effective shuttering of the Strait of Hormuz.6

Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree said the group fired ballistic missiles and drones at Saudi Aramco sites in Riyadh and Khurais in retaliation for about 60 Saudi air strikes in 24 hours, a claim the Saudi-led coalition called "misleading."78 Saudi authorities and Aramco had not confirmed the cause of a fire at an Aramco refinery on Saturday.9

The Saudi Press Agency said Riyadh was allocating 224 million riyals ($59.8 million) to the Yemeni government, and Prime Minister Shaya al-Zindani urged the UN to step up its humanitarian response as displacement grows.1011

What has Iran said it needs before reopening Hormuz, and how has the US responded to its seven-day plan?

Iran says the Strait of Hormuz will stay closed until Washington meets seven conditions based on the Islamabad Memorandum, according to Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf.1 Tehran reportedly offered, at the United Nations General Assembly in September, to reopen Hormuz and restore normal maritime passage within seven days in exchange for meeting its conditions.2 Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi says the US replied to the seven-day plan through intermediaries and that the reply is under internal review.3 Reuters reports the response went through Qatari mediators last week, but its substance remains uncertain.4
