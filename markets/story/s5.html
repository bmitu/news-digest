August PCE inflation release and revisions

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 19:31 UTC
4 sources · 2 independent origins: Employ America, Wolf Street

The Commerce Department's Bureau of Economic Analysis reported that the PCE price index rose 0.3% in August for a 12-month gain of 3.4%, and that core PCE (excluding food and energy) rose 0.2% for an annual rate of 3%, both below what economists surveyed by Dow Jones expected. inference12

The release coincided with annual revisions in which the BEA changed how it computes prices for legal services, software and computer accessories, and portfolio management.34 CNBC reports the revisions lowered the core July PCE level by 0.36 percentage point.5 Wolf Richter calculates that July's year-over-year core PCE was cut by 37 basis points to 2.98% from the originally reported 3.35%, and that the revisions cut July's all-items PCE increase by 34 basis points from the originally reported 3.70%.67

Richter says the revised core PCE of 3.01% and all-items PCE of 3.4% remain substantially above the Fed's 2% target, and he points to a 12.3% revised July rate for computer software and accessories versus 21.2% under the old method. inference89 Employ America also notes the 3-month annualized core rate is about 2% but leans on soft June and July readings, and calls it a noisy gauge.1011

Markets reacted by pricing less chance of an October Fed hike; CNBC says traders pushed the next expected increase to December, while the Guardian, citing LSEG, says the October odds fell to about 35% from about 45%. inference1213

Commentators stressed that inflation is still high: Sonu Varghese of Carson Group said PCE inflation "is still running hot however you cut it," and Heather Long of Navy Federal Credit Union said the data "show no progress in August on inflation."1415 David Russell of TradeStation said the report bolsters the case for not hiking in October but noted it is relatively old data that doesn't reflect this month's surge in diesel prices.1617

How much did energy and gasoline prices rise in August, and how much did they add to the monthly increase?

CNBC reports that energy costs were the primary culprit for the August rise in prices, though other sectors also showed gains.1 Energy goods and services prices climbed 2.3% in August, and gasoline jumped 4.4%.2 The overall PCE price index rose 0.31% in August from July.3 Over the past year, energy prices were up 16.8% and gasoline 27.8% in August, according to Wolf Richter.456 The exact amount that energy and gasoline added to that monthly increase was not reported.

How much could the 3-month annualized core rate change if September's reading comes in hot, and what risks do analysts see in September data?

Employ America calculates that, assuming no revisions to July and August, a 25 basis point September reading would put the 3-month annualized rate at 2.55% and a 5 basis point reading would drop it to 1.73%. It separately describes the measure as a noisy gauge of underlying inflation, since a single month can swing it in either direction.123 On the hot side, Employ America calculates that a September core PCE reading of 35 basis points would lift the 3-month annualized rate to 2.96% and 40 basis points would lift it to 3.16%, up from about 2% now. inference456 The measure is sensitive in part because June drops out of the window with the September data, and Employ America says Governor Waller and others heavily weigh this gauge in their policy thinking.78 Employ America sees upside risk to September data from airfares, food services, wireless communication services, tech equipment and other core goods categories.910 Navy Federal Credit Union's Heather Long says it is inevitable that September will be higher, and Goldman Sachs expects the next couple of months of inflation data to be "somewhat less favorable before a more benign trend reasserts itself."111213
