Trump threatens US diesel export ban

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 17:18 UTC
3 sources · 2 independent origins: BBC, G7

Trump said on social media that Europe had agreed to release a massive amount of diesel, and later at the White House said an export ban was "never really on the table".123 The BBC notes Trump had said early last week he backed a ban and was still considering it on Thursday, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent had argued US farmers, truckers and businesses "should not be left carrying the burden".456

A joint statement says the release will be coordinated through the IEA, begin immediately and run over four months, with a frontloaded substantial diesel release within the first 20 days.7 The BBC says the statement rules out "export restrictions on energy and energy products" between G7 members.8 The Guardian reports, citing proposals reportedly drawn up by Emmanuel Macron, that the split would be 50 million barrels of diesel and 50 million of crude, while the BBC says it is not yet clear which partner countries will release stocks or how quickly. inference910

UK diesel passed £2 a litre on Friday, with the RAC putting the average at 200.01p, a record, against 142.38p before the war.111213 Brent crude briefly fell below $100 on the announcement but rose back to about $102 by Friday evening, against roughly $73 before the Iran war.1415

The Guardian reports the US exported a record 1.9 million barrels a week in early August, which pushed US distillate stocks to their lowest seasonal levels since 1996.1617 Analysts and sources differ on the underlying fix: Macquarie Group's Walt Chancellor said the issue is a global energy problem and that the solution is more oil through the Strait of Hormuz, calling anything less "shuffling deck chairs".1819 Kpler's Matt Smith said oil rose again because of rumours of a Saudi offensive into Yemen, while Argus Media's David Fyfe said cutting off American supply would likely cause international prices to skyrocket. inference2021

Why is diesel in such short supply worldwide, beyond the Iran war and Strait of Hormuz disruption?

Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries have cut Russia's fuel output to 20-year lows, and Russia has imposed its own diesel export ban.12 Chinese refiners have reportedly halted October exports of some fuel products as Beijing prioritises domestic supplies, potentially adding to the pressure on the global diesel market.34 Russia's invasion of Ukraine also led to sanctions that diverted Russian diesel away from Europe, leaving European buyers more reliant on US and other sources.56 Diesel is also harder to refine than petrol, and demand is hard to cut because haulage and agriculture rely on it.7 Some countries are structurally short of diesel. Europe makes only about 70% of what it consumes, and the UK's closure of the Grangemouth and Immingham refineries last year raised its reliance on imports to over half.89 With the UK no longer buying Russian diesel while other countries carry on, the RAC's Simon Williams says there are "more countries competing for less diesel".10 Record US exports caused US distillate stockpiles to fall to their lowest seasonal levels since 1996, and Trump said he was considering a ban on diesel exports after the surge in sales to overseas buyers pushed US diesel prices to record highs.1112
