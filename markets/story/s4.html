UK diesel hits £2 a litre; G7 100 million barrel reserve release; Trump diesel export ban threat

Last scan Tue Oct 6, 17:01 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Oct 4 · 2 independent origins: BBC, The Guardian

The G7 announced on Friday 2 October 2026 a release of 100 million barrels of oil and diesel, a move that came after Donald Trump had threatened to ban diesel exports.1234 The G7's joint statement describes a release co-ordinated through the IEA, beginning immediately over four months, with a frontloaded substantial diesel release within the first 20 days.5 The G7 also agreed there would be no export restrictions on energy products between members, and Macron said Trump "was very clear on this point".678 Trump later said at the White House that an export ban was "never really on the table", although the BBC notes he had first said he backed a ban early the previous week and was still considering it on Thursday.910

On the same day, the RAC said the average UK diesel price passed £2 a litre for the first time, reaching 200.01p, with petrol at 174.71p.1112 The RAC says filling an average diesel family car now costs £110, almost £32 more than at the start of the US-Iran war.1314 RAC policy head Simon Williams called £2 a "price threshold no one wanted to cross", and UK farmer Mark Means said he was "frightened to death" by rising diesel costs.1516

Macquarie Group's Walt Chancellor argued that relying on European reserves does not tackle the underlying problem, saying more oil must come through the Strait of Hormuz and that anything less is "just shuffling deck chairs".1718 Doomberg, in a commentary, argued that a powerful set of lobbyists and allies has worked to frame Trump's choice as binary, either "ban" or "as you were", and that Trump has many cards to play to get his way ahead of the elections.1920

How much of the UK's diesel supply comes from the US, and how exposed is Britain to a US export ban?

Over half of the UK's diesel is imported, and less than a third of those imports come from the US, which the BBC puts at around 17% of total UK diesel supply.1 A separate BBC figure, however, puts the US share of UK diesel imports at 31%.23 The BBC reports that many worry a US ban could push UK diesel prices up even further. It also says many agree a ban would hurt countries that depend heavily on US diesel, with the UK named as one of them.45 Argus Media's David Fyfe said cutting off American supply would be likely to make international prices skyrocket.67 However, the G7, which includes the UK, agreed there would be no export restrictions on energy and energy products between members, and Trump said an export ban on diesel was "never really on the table". The UK government has stressed there is no cause for concern about potential diesel shortages.891011

Why is diesel so much scarcer and dearer than petrol, and why is it hard to cut demand?

Diesel supplies internationally have been heavily constrained by the conflict in the Middle East, which has restricted the flow of both crude oil and refined diesel onto global markets, and Russia, also a major producer, has implemented its own diesel export ban following Ukrainian attacks on its refineries, further limiting supply.12 China has also restricted exports of refined fuels, and transport of refined oil products from the Middle East remains down because of damage to refineries in the region and because refined products are more flammable, making them more dangerous to move during a conflict.34 Diesel is also harder to refine than petrol, and countries such as the UK do not make enough of it: UK refineries produce more than enough petrol but not enough diesel, so over half of the diesel it uses is imported.56 Competition for scarce cargoes adds to the price: the RAC's Simon Williams says there are "more countries competing for less diesel", and UK diesel has reached a record 200.01p a litre, up from 142.38p before the war, while petrol averages 174.71p.78910 Demand is hard to cut, according to the BBC, because diesel is used heavily by the haulage industry and agriculture, so higher prices feed through into essentials such as food.111213

When will lower oil prices show up at UK pumps, and could the government cut fuel duty or VAT to ease the burden?

The RAC's Simon Williams says pump prices will not fall until there is a "sustained lower oil price - over several weeks, not days".1 Once wholesale prices do fall, the BBC reports that the change takes about a fortnight to show at the pump.23 Analysts estimate that every $10 per barrel change in oil prices moves pump prices by roughly 7p a litre.4 The RAC's Simon Williams says that lowering fuel duty further or reducing VAT could "ease the burden" at home.56 The government has already postponed a planned 5p fuel duty rise from September to the end of December, a move announced in May by the then Prime Minister Sir Keir Starmer.78 It was not reported when UK pump prices will actually fall.

How does the G7's 100 million barrel release compare with the IEA's record 400 million barrel release in March?

The G7's planned release of up to 100 million barrels is a quarter the size of the IEA's 400 million barrel release in March. inference123 The IEA's March release was the largest release of government oil reserves in its history, and represented a third of the group's total government stockpiles.45 The March IEA release was also more than double the 182 million barrels released in 2022 after Russia's invasion of Ukraine, while the G7 figure is well below that earlier release. inference678 The G7 release is split evenly, with 50 million barrels of diesel and 50 million of crude oil, and is focused on diesel, whereas the IEA's March release was emergency crude. inference91011 The G7 release will be coordinated through the IEA and run over four months, with a substantial diesel release front-loaded within the first 20 days.1213
