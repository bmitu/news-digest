Trump threat of US diesel export ban

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 16:14 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 3 · 2 independent origins: BBC, The Guardian

Trump posted that Europe had agreed to release a massive amount of its diesel, and later said at the White House that a ban was "never really on the table", which sits uneasily with the BBC's report that he had said he backed a ban and was still considering it on Thursday. inference12 Before the deal, Argus Media's David Fyfe said cutting off American supply would likely cause international prices to skyrocket.3

The BBC reports that the release is to be coordinated through the International Energy Agency, begin immediately and run over four months, with a frontloaded substantial diesel release within the first 20 days.45 The Guardian reports, citing proposals reportedly drawn up by French President Emmanuel Macron, that the split would be 50 million barrels of diesel and 50 million of crude oil.6 However, the BBC says it is not yet clear which partner countries will release stocks, or how quickly.7

Macron says all members committed to no export bans and that Trump was very clear on this point, and the G7 statement rules out export restrictions on energy products between members.89

Macquarie Group's Walt Chancellor argued that relying on European reserves would not fix the underlying problem, saying the solution is "more oil through the strait of Hormuz and out of the Middle East".10 Brent crude briefly fell below $100 a barrel on the news but rose back to around $102 by Friday evening, and Kpler's Matt Smith said it reversed course on rumours of Saudi Arabia planning an offensive into Yemen.111213

How much of the UK's diesel comes from the US, and how exposed is Britain to a ban compared with other European countries?

Over half of the UK's diesel is imported, and 31% of those imports come from the US.12 BBC reporting, however, says the UK depends on the US for around 17% of its diesel supplies.34 The UK's own refineries do not make enough diesel for its needs, which adds to its dependence on imports.56 The UK is seen as one of the countries most exposed to a US ban. Many agree a ban would hurt countries that depend heavily on US diesel, and the UK is named as one of them.78 Other European countries are also exposed. France and the Netherlands import a significant amount of diesel from the US, as the UK does, and Europe as a whole makes only about 70% of the diesel it uses.910 The threat has eased for now. Trump said an export ban was "never really on the table", and the G7 agreed there would be no export restrictions between its members.111213 The reports give no country-by-country figures for how much US diesel France, the Netherlands, Germany or other European states import, so the UK's exposure cannot be ranked precisely against theirs.

How does this 100 million barrel release compare with the IEA's emergency stock release in March and the one in 2022?

The G7's new release of up to 100 million barrels is about a quarter the size of the IEA's record March release of 400 million barrels. inference12 It is also smaller than the 182 million barrels released in 2022 after Russia's invasion of Ukraine, which the Guardian says the March release more than doubled. inference32 The March release was described as a third of the IEA group's total government stockpiles, and it was meant to calm the oil price shock from the US-Israeli attacks on Iran.45 The new release is also mixed, with the Guardian reporting 50 million barrels of diesel and 50 million of crude. It is focused on diesel, with a substantial diesel release front-loaded in the first 20 days, whereas the March release was described as emergency crude. inference678
