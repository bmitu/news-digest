September US housing starts decline

Last scan Wed Oct 21, 19:55 UTC
2 sources · single origin (CNBC)

US housing starts for September came in at 1.221 million annualized, below the forecast of 1.305 million, according to Bespoke's Morning Lineup.1 CNBC, citing Commerce Department data, reported that privately owned housing starts fell 5.2% on the month, against expectations for a decline of just 0.4%.2 Building permits were off 0.2% against a Dow Jones consensus for a 2% gain, per CNBC, while Bespoke said permits were right in line with expectations.34 Single-family permits totaled just 845,000, down 4% from August and the lowest number since 2023, CNBC reported.5

Pending home sales also fell, with the National Association of Realtors reporting a 7.3% monthly slide to an all-time low in records back to 2001, versus a Wall Street estimate of a 0.4% decline.6 Lawrence Yun, the NAR's chief economist, said market conditions weakened in September due to fast-rising mortgage rates.7 Yun also said mortgage rates could be topping out, and that buyers and sellers may simply be holding back until rates move favorably.8

Chris Rupkey of Fwdbonds wrote that "It is not winter yet but housing is going into a deep freeze," while Goldman Sachs economist Ronnie Walker wrote that in 2027 he expects the headwind from affordability to increasingly dominate, weighing further on activity.910 CNBC says the weak data complicates the Fed's position, since markets see a strong chance of rate hikes in December and March, and Bespoke says high interest rates continue to weigh on the housing industry and homebuilder stocks. inference1112
