US bans Canadian imports, Canada retaliates

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 03:34 UTC
2 sources · 2 independent origins: CNBC, The Guardian

The White House banned imports of some Canadian vehicles, dairy and alcohol products on Tuesday, as officials sent mixed signals on the prospect of a trade deal.1 The Guardian says the ban took effect at 12.01 am Eastern time Tuesday, and that Trump decided on it to punish Canada for retaliating against his tariffs.2 The ban covers motorcycles and mopeds with petrol engines larger than 800cc, whey products and molasses, and many alcoholic beverages from beer and cider to wine, whiskey and vodka.3 Bombardier Recreational Products confirmed its Can-Am Spyder and Canyon motorcycles will be excluded from the US, though it said the impact would probably not come until next year.4

Estimates of the banned trade's value differ widely: CNBC cites the American Action Forum at around $19.9 billion, while The Guardian cites the same think tank's Jacob Jensen at $967 million based on 2025 numbers. inference56 Jensen says 87% of the banned value is alcoholic beverages, which the US targeted because some Canadian provinces pulled US alcohol from store shelves.7 The Guardian describes the ban as barely a ripple in $880bn of annual two-way trade, and trade attorney Patrick Childress says the 50% tariffs were already acting as a de facto ban on many of these goods.89

Canada has imposed tariffs of 15% to 50% on 27.6 billion Canadian dollars of US goods, which it calls a dollar-for-dollar response to Washington's 50% tariffs imposed in August.1011 Ottawa has not announced fresh retaliation since its counter-tariffs took effect Sept. 8, though Jensen says the ban may result in further Canadian retaliation. inference1213

Donald Trump said he expected a "fair deal" with Canada within weeks, but U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said there was "no urgency on our side" to strike an agreement.1415 The Guardian reports the impasse imperils efforts to renew the US-Mexico-Canada Agreement.16

Why do the American Action Forum's estimates of the banned trade differ so much, roughly $19.9 billion versus $967 million?

The two figures come from different counts of what the ban covers, and the reports don't explain the gap directly. CNBC reports that the American Action Forum estimated the banned products at around $19.9 billion of Canadian imports.1 The Guardian reports that Jacob Jensen, the think tank's trade policy director, calculated that the ban covers $967 million of Canadian imports, based on 2025 numbers.2 The $19.9 billion figure is close to the roughly $20 billion of Canadian imports that Trump hit with 50% tariffs this summer, so it may reflect that wider set of goods rather than the banned list.
