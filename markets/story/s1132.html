Warsh declines to signal rate move

Last scan Thu Oct 22, 01:18 UTC
3 sources · single origin (CNBC)

Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh declined to signal whether the Fed will raise interest rates again this month, saying he does not prejudge meetings.12 He spoke at a fireside chat with the International Monetary Fund's managing director, Kristalina Georgieva, at the IMF-World Bank Annual Meetings in Bangkok on Friday.3 Warsh reiterated his opposition to forward guidance for markets, saying "It's a new chapter at the Fed [and] we're not in that business anymore," and acknowledged this may disappoint anyone hoping for a steer.45

Warsh said central banks made their own choices under their own mandates, but the result was "a synchronous tightening in monetary policy in most places around the world," which means "there's no free riders."6 CNBC reports that surging energy costs tied to the Iran war and the AI investment boom are stoking inflation and pushing up borrowing costs worldwide.7 Warsh also voiced optimism that AI will make economies that use it well more productive, while warning the path may be "very bumpy" and its near-term employment effect is uncertain.8

The Fed raised rates to a range of 3.75% to 4% last month, its first hike since 2023.9 According to CME FedWatch, traders see a 72.3% chance of no change at the Oct. 27-28 meeting, while the majority expect a 25 basis point hike in December.1011 Goldman Sachs economists led by Jan Hatzius wrote that Warsh's comments, alongside other FOMC remarks, indicate the Fed is set to pause in October, and they still expect a second and final hike in December.1213

S&P 500 and Nasdaq futures edged higher after the speech, while Asian stock indexes and Treasury yields were largely stable.14
