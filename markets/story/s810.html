Hot September CPI report

Last scan Wed Oct 21, 19:55 UTC
3 sources · 2 independent origins: Bureau of Labor Statistics, The Guardian

September's consumer price index, released on Wednesday, rose 0.6% from August and 3.7% over 12 months, according to CNBC.1 CNBC reports that economists polled by Dow Jones had forecast 0.6% and 3.6%, so the annual figure was slightly hotter than expected.2 Core CPI, excluding food and energy, added 0.3% on the month and 2.6% year over year, versus expectations of 0.2% and 2.5%.3

Bespoke Investment Group said CPI came in hotter than expected for both headline and core measures, with goods CPI a major factor, and that semiconductors, software and the Nasdaq 100 fell over 2% and silver fell over 6% that day.45 Bespoke also said the Fed's Beige Book showed climbing optimism and a historic surge in inflation concerns, and CNBC cites a Bespoke analysis finding the Beige Book had the most inflation-related mentions on record.67

The next day, producer prices rose 1.3% in September and 6.3% annually, the largest monthly and annual gains since March 2022 and December 2022, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics.89 Economists polled by Dow Jones had forecast 0.6% and 5.5%, and CNBC attributes much of the rise to energy prices amid the U.S. war with Iran.1011

Retail sales grew 0.8% in September versus 0.4% expected, but excluding autos and gas stations they rose about 0.25%, down from around 1.1%, which CNBC reads as a sign that fuel prices are making consumers tighten their belts elsewhere.1213

Fed funds futures traders now price a nearly 34% chance of a rate hike on Oct. 28, up from under 18% a week earlier, according to CME's FedWatch tool.14 Chris Zaccarelli of Northlight Asset Management said the Fed "will have no choice but to raise rates again this year."15
