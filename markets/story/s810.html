Hot September CPI report

Last scan Thu Oct 22, 01:18 UTC
2 sources · 2 independent origins: Bureau of Labor Statistics, The Guardian · Official data: CPI inflation (year over year), Retail sales (year over year)

September's producer price index came in much hotter than economists forecast, with energy pressure from the U.S. war with Iran cited as a driver, CNBC reports.1 The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported the PPI rose 1.3% in September and 6.3% annually, the largest monthly and annual gains since March 2022 and December 2022, respectively.23 Economists polled by Dow Jones had forecast 0.6% on the month and 5.5% annually, while core PPI, which excludes food and energy, rose 0.7% on the month and 5% over 12 months.45 The BLS said almost half of September's increase in processed goods for immediate demand was connected to higher diesel fuel prices.6

A day earlier, the consumer price index rose 0.6% on the month and 3.7% annually, against forecasts of 0.6% and 3.6%, while core CPI rose 0.3% and 2.6% against expected 0.2% and 2.5%.789 Retail sales rose 0.8% in September, above the 0.4% expected, but excluding autos and gas stations they rose about 0.25%, down from around 1.1% previously, which CNBC says suggests consumers are tightening their belts elsewhere. inference101112

According to CME's FedWatch tool, traders now price a nearly 34% chance of a Fed rate hike on Oct. 28, up from under 18% a week earlier.13 Chris Zaccarelli of Northlight Asset Management says that after two days of higher-than-expected inflation numbers, the Fed will seemingly have no choice but to raise rates again this year.14

The Guardian's opinion piece notes that the CPI spike to 3.7% came after Harris polling, which found 38% of Republicans believe the economy is worsening, up from 29% a year earlier, and argues this bodes badly for Trump before the midterms. inference1516
