Federal Reserve raises rates in September

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 03:40 UTC
4 sources · tracked since Oct 21 · 5 new articles this week · single origin (Federal Reserve) · Official data: CPI inflation (year over year)

The Federal Reserve raised interest rates by a quarter point to a range of 3.75%-4.00% on Sept. 16, its first hike in about three years, according to the sources. inference123 The Motley Fool's account says Fed Chair Kevin Warsh and 11 other FOMC members voted unanimously (12-0) for the increase.45 The BBC reports the Fed cited "too high" inflation, and that the move came despite fierce opposition from President Donald Trump, who had called for rates to be cut.67

Sue Roesler of Ag Update writes that the board wanted to get ahead of "stuck" inflation above 3 percent, against a 2 percent target.8 US inflation was 3.7% in September, up from 3.4% in August, according to the BBC, with fuel costs a factor.910 Motley Fool writer Sean Williams says September producer prices rose 6.3% (headline) and 5% (core), and that Trump's tariffs and the ongoing Iran war are directly lifting consumer prices, while AI hardware companies' pricing power has increased prices for chips and memory used in tech devices.1112

A Motley Fool commentator argues more hikes are coming: he reads Warsh's repeated phrase "dose of accommodation" as suggesting the rate-hiking cycle is likely to ramp up in December and throughout 2027.1314 Motley Fool articles say futures pricing implies three more quarter-point increases by mid-2027, and that CME's FedWatch tool shows a 72% probability of rates rising by at least 75 basis points by Sept. 15, 2027.1516

The Motley Fool writers differ on the market impact: one calls a rapid hiking cycle "terrible news" for the AI-driven bull market, while another says history shows no single market reaction to hikes and recommends buy-and-hold. inference171819
