RBA raises cash rate to 4.6%

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 16:14 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 3 · single origin (CNBC)

The Reserve Bank of Australia raised its cash rate by 25 basis points to 4.6%, the highest level in 15 years, on Tuesday, as CNBC reports the country looks to contain inflation stoked by the Middle East conflict.12 CNBC reports the RBA has now raised rates four times this year, by a total of 100 basis points, as inflation remains sticky.3 CNBC reports Australia's inflation has run above its 2%-3% target band for 2026, hitting a high of 4.6% in March, and last came in at 3.5% in July, while the economy grew 2.1% in the second quarter, down from 2.5% in the first.45

In its statement, the RBA said some upside risks it flagged in August were now materializing, saying the Middle East conflict had broadened and global energy prices were much higher than assumed.67 The RBA also said it would "continue to do what it considers necessary" to contain inflation, including increasing the policy rate further.89 Bank of America said inflation was accelerating rather than converging back to target, and that there was evidence of second-round effects from energy costs that risk entrenching inflation.1011

The Guardian reports that analysts tip a further hike, the fifth this year, on Melbourne Cup day, and that markets price a 60% chance of a sixth increase by mid-2027.1213 Tom Devitt, senior economist at Housing Industry Australia, said that with the anticipated hike and the prospect of more to come, "in this cycle we now don't see any improvement in affordability at all," and Taylor Nugent, a senior economist at NAB, said the prospect of more hikes suggested property values would fall further and for longer than anticipated.1415
