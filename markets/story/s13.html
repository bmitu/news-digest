Conference Board consumer confidence and JOLTS job openings

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 18:00 UTC
2 sources · single origin (Conference Board)

The Conference Board reported on Tuesday that its Consumer Confidence Index fell 6.7 points to 81.9 in September, its lowest level since 2014 and well below the Dow Jones consensus forecast of 89.12 Respondents cited concerns over inflation and the jobs outlook, and for the first time in the four-year history of the survey question, more said their personal finances were bad than good.34 The Present Situation Index fell 7.9 points to 109.3 and the Expectations Index slipped 5.9 points to 63.6, while the gap between those saying jobs are "plentiful" and "hard to get" narrowed by 2.5 percentage points to 1.7%.56

Average expected inflation rose 0.3 percentage point to 6.1%, and the median rose 0.3 point to 5.1%, with CNBC linking this to uncertainty over the Iran war and surging Treasury yields and mortgage rates.78 Conference Board chief economist Dana Peterson said consumer appraisals of current business conditions turned negative for the first time since September 2024, and that references to prices, and oil and gas prices in particular, rose to new heights, reflecting September's surge in fuel costs.910 CNBC noted the Conference Board reading is in keeping with similar surveys, as the University of Michigan survey showed sentiment fell 7% in September to its second-lowest reading on record.1112

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that August job openings edged lower by 256,000 to 7.08 million, below the Wall Street consensus of 7.2 million, with sharp declines in professional and business services and healthcare-related jobs.1314 Bespoke Investment Group, by contrast, said job openings "continue to improve" and described a stark disconnect between hard employment data and consumers' evaluations of the labor market, a framing that differs from CNBC's report of a decline in openings. inference151617

What did the rest of the August JOLTS report show for hires, quits and layoffs, and how low did layoffs fall?

CNBC summed up the report as showing hires edging higher, quits little changed and layoffs falling slightly, alongside job openings dipping to 7.08 million.12 Hires edged higher in August, rising by 46,000 from the upwardly revised July figure to 5.19 million.3 Voluntary quits were little changed, slipping by 23,000 to 3.07 million.4 Layoffs and discharges fell to 1.64 million in August, the lowest level since March 2025.5 The three-month average for layoffs declined to 1.71 million, which Wolf Richter says is at the very low end of the range of the prepandemic years.6

The report came in weaker than expected: the Bureau of Labor Statistics said payrolls grew by only 29,000 jobs in September.12 The report was expected to confirm labor market strength, which Fed officials are looking for as they shift their attention to inflation.3 It followed a surprisingly strong August gain of 162,000 jobs.4 Economists' consensus forecast was for payrolls to rise by 84,000 in September, and average hourly earnings were expected to be up 3.1% from a year earlier.56 The 84,000 forecast was close to the 2026 pace, with payroll growth averaging 80,000 a month but erratic. inference75 The consensus forecast for the September unemployment rate was not reported.
