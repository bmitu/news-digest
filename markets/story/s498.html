Fed rate hike odds fall after weak jobs data

Last scan Tue Oct 6, 17:01 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 5 · 1 new article today · single origin (Investing.com)

The probability of a Federal Reserve rate hike this month fell sharply after weak US jobs data, with CME FedWatch showing it dropping to under 20% from 64%.1 Sources gave slightly different figures: one report put the chance at 18%, while others said traders saw an 80% chance of the Fed holding rates steady in October, up from 36% a week earlier according to one of them. inference234 A rate increase in December is still expected, and one report adds that traders expect another two hikes in the first half of 2027.567

US stocks were mixed in early trading as the easing hike bets helped cushion sentiment, though Treasury yields and oil prices stayed elevated.8 Lisa Shalett of Morgan Stanley Wealth Management says equity market calm amid the bond market's "perfect storm" is understandable given accelerating growth and the AI boom's rate insensitivity.9

The dollar rose, drawing support from still-lofty Treasury yields even after a weaker-than-expected jobs report dampened expectations of a Fed rate hike this month, while the euro fell to a 17-month low on fiscal concerns in France.1011 Elias Haddad of BBH says that although a Fed pause could weigh on the dollar, US growth outperformance and strong foreign appetite for US securities keep dollar risks skewed to the upside.12

Why did the Fed turn less eager to hike in October, and what did New York Fed's John Williams say about urgency?

Fed commentary shifted market expectations for an end-of-October hike after New York Fed President John Williams said "there is no need for urgency" when weighing whether to follow up on September's quarter-point hike, and markets subsequently sharply reduced the odds for a hike at the Oct. 27-28 meeting.123 Williams said in a speech that, given the September action, there is no need for urgency and the Fed has time to gather more information. He added that he still thinks another hike "may be appropriate late this year," which led markets to price the next increase for December.45 Williams also said the labor market is solid and has even strengthened a bit on the margin.6 Weak data then pushed hike expectations down further: September payrolls rose by just 29,000 and earlier months were revised lower, and the chance of an October hike fell to under 20% from 64% in a week, according to CME FedWatch.78910 Jefferies chief U.S. economist Thomas Simons said the jobs number should be "the nail in the coffin for an October hike," though traders still expect a December hike.111213
