Judge orders Meta to pay $25bn over Facebook data misrepresentations

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 03:40 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Oct 21 · 1 new article this week · 2 independent origins: BBC, The Guardian

A New Mexico judge ordered Meta to pay $25bn on Wednesday after a trial found the company had spent years lying to the public about how it handled Facebook user data.12 The BBC reports this is the largest fine ever against Meta, and the case stemmed from the Cambridge Analytica scandal.34

A jury found Meta violated state laws more than 40 million times, and Judge Francis Mathew said the violations were "willful" and spanned more than 11 years, from 2010 to 2021.56 Mathew wrote that communications between senior executives, including Mark Zuckerberg, were "designed to mislead the public."7

The full penalty, based on thousands of violations, was calculated at $219bn, but Mathew reduced it to $25bn.8 New Mexico's lawyers sought up to $40bn, while Meta argued anything beyond $3.4bn would be unconstitutional.910

New Mexico Attorney General Raúl Torrez said the result shows that "no corporation is too powerful to be held accountable."11 A Meta spokesperson did not immediately respond to the BBC's request for comment.12

Reuters, via Investing.com, described the penalty as one of several mounting legal challenges, and noted that Meta reported better-than-expected quarterly revenue of $64.60 billion the same day. inference131415
