Hot September PPI report new
8 sources · 4 independent origins: Axios, Bureau of Labor Statistics, CNBC, Employ America · Official data: Core PCE inflation (year over year)

September's Producer Price Index came in much hotter than economists forecast, rising 1.3% on the month and 6.3% year over year, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics as reported by CNBC. CNBC reports these were the largest monthly and annual gains since March 2022 and December 2022, respectively, against Dow Jones forecasts of 0.6% and 5.5%. Core PPI, which excludes food and energy, rose 0.7% on the month and 5% over 12 months, and CNBC says energy drove a sizable part of the increase amid the U.S. war with Iran. The BLS said almost half of September's rise in processed goods for immediate demand was tied to higher diesel fuel prices.

CNBC says that excluding food, energy and trade services, prices still rose 0.5%, which it takes as a sign that inflation was broader than energy alone. Paul Krugman argues the PPI shows Donald Trump's tariffs and war threw away a benign inflation inheritance, that it is mostly about diesel, and that it is often a leading indicator of consumer prices, so no near-term relief is visible.

Employ America updated its nowcast for September core PCE to 3.09% year over year, said today's upside came from airfares, and maintains its base case that the FOMC holds in October and hikes in December. CNBC's gathering of seven Wall Street estimates points to September core PCE rising 0.32%, up from 0.25% in August.

Nationwide's Kathy Bostjancic sees real risks the Fed will deliver another rate hike in two weeks' time, and Mizuho's Alex Pelle says that on the economic data alone there is little reason for the Fed to hesitate to continue moving the policy rate higher. Fed futures odds of an October hike differ by report: CNBC's Economy piece cites 29%, while the other CNBC article cites nearly 34% from CME's FedWatch, and both cite roughly 90% odds of at least one hike by year-end.

China Q3 GDP beats forecasts new
8 sources · 4 independent origins: BBC, CNN, National Bureau of Statistics, The Guardian

China's National Bureau of Statistics reported on Monday, 19 October 2026, that third-quarter GDP grew 4.5% year on year, up from 4.3% in the second quarter and better than economists had expected, according to The Guardian. Bespoke reported that quarter-on-quarter growth was 1.2% against forecasts of 1.1%, and the statistics bureau's own release also gives 1.2% quarter on quarter. Growth for the first nine months was 4.6%, and CNN says the third-quarter figure sits at the lower end of Beijing's 4.5-5% annual target. The Guardian says this puts China on track to hit the target without extra stimulus, while the BBC says analysts see the position at the bottom of the range as giving policymakers room to address economic challenges.

Sources attribute the pickup mainly to exports driven by the global AI boom: CNN reports robust exports of chips and electronics components, and ABN Amro analysts say the pickup more than offset drags from the Iran conflict and weak domestic demand. China's exports rose 36.3% in September from a year earlier, according to CNN, which cites customs data released the previous week.

Domestic weakness persisted: fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% in the first nine months, while retail sales rose 1.1% in September, up from 0.4% in August. Sources differ slightly on property investment: CNN says it plunged 20.2% in the first three quarters, while the Guardian and the statistics bureau give 20.6%. Mao Shengyong, vice-minister of the National Bureau of Statistics, said the economy moved forward under pressure and developed in a newer and better direction, but that the problem of insufficient domestic demand remains relatively prominent.

Duncan Wrigley of Pantheon Macroeconomics said policymakers were likely to conclude their efforts to stabilise growth were successful, while Dave Chia of Moody's Analytics warned of China's reliance on a "single external engine" in exports.

      Key points
      • The current Ample Reserves system pays banks more on reserves than they pay depositors, a subsidy to bank shareholders, and keeps Treasury and mortgage-backed prices higher than private investors would.
      • Elastic Reserves lets reserves expand when banks borrow and contract when they repay, with the Fed publishing lending terms and requiring advance collateral.

      3M stock rose 6% on Q3 adjusted EPS of $2.50 versus $2.39 consensus and an expanded Amazon data center deal tied to a warrant.
      Key points
      • Q3 adjusted EPS was $2.50 versus the $2.39 consensus; revenue of approximately $6.6 billion was broadly in line.
      • Amazon's subsidiary got a seven-year warrant for up to approximately 3.16 million 3M shares at $164.89 each.
      • About 85% of warrant shares vest on Amazon's data center purchases, fully at $6 billion in cumulative payments; the other 15% depends on separate milestones.
      • Adjusted organic sales grew 3.3% while GAAP sales rose 1% year over year, on different reporting bases.
      • Full-year adjusted EPS guidance was raised to $9.10 to $9.15, incorporating a new reporting basis.
