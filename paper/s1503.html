Anthropic proposed federal AI safety bill

From reports by Zvi Mowshowitz, tomshardware.com, Transformer and 2 more · Oct 17 – Oct 20

Politico reported that Anthropic has been shopping around a new draft AI safety bill that it hopes Congress might consider adopting, and an Anthropic spokesperson said it represents the proposals in the company's Advanced AI Framework, which the company has been advocating for with federal and state lawmakers since June.123 Independent evaluators registered with the Commerce Department would assess models and could be paid by labs if payment is not contingent on results; they would have 72 hours to report an imminent risk to Commerce, which would then have to ask a Washington, D.C., judge to halt the release.456

Transformer reports the bill is much weaker than the Obernolte/Trahan FRONTIER Act: evaluators can self-certify, and the court must decide without deference to Commerce and weigh harm to the developer, users and the public from a blocking order.789 Its preemption clause would displace state frontier-AI laws such as California's SB 53, New York's RAISE and Illinois' SB 315 while carving out child safety and other laws, and Transformer says it also stops states or courts from suspending a model on catastrophic-risk grounds.1011

Charlie Bullock gave Anthropic some credit for a "pretty reasonable AI safety bill" while saying it is weaker than FRONTIER on rulemaking and emergency shutdown authority, though better on auditor employment and access to information.1213

Anthony Cimino, Anthropic's federal affairs lead, said the draft was shared with a small number of congressional offices, does not include everything Anthropic wants, and that the company plans to update its preemption language to make clearer that states keep a role.1415 Nathan Calvin said Cimino's response does not engage with the heart of his concern, and Mowshowitz argued Anthropic still does not understand what was centrally wrong with the draft.1617
