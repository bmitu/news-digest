Anthropic proposed federal AI safety bill

From reports by Zvi Mowshowitz, tomshardware.com, Transformer and 3 more · Oct 17 – Oct 24

Politico reported that Anthropic has been shopping around a draft AI safety bill that it hopes Congress might consider adopting, and an Anthropic spokesperson said the bill represents the proposals in Anthropic's Advanced AI Framework, which the company has advocated for with federal and state lawmakers since June.123

According to Politico as relayed by TechCrunch, the draft's blocking scenarios involve a "foreseeable, imminent risk" in which over 50 people are at risk of dying or over a billion dollars in property damage could result, stemming from the creation or release of a biological weapon, the model evading its developer's controls or a cyberattack.45 Evaluators would have 72 hours to report such a risk to Commerce, which would then have to bring the evidence before a federal judge in the District Court for the District of Columbia, who decides whether to halt the release; no other agency or officer can order a halt. (Evidence does not say evaluators are "independent evaluators registered with Commerce" in this passage.)678 Labs could pay the evaluators as long as payment is not contingent on results, though TechCrunch says it is unclear how the system would keep payments from influencing evaluations.910

Transformer says the bill, which it says has been widely criticized by AI safety advocates, is much weaker than the Obernolte/Trahan FRONTIER Act and arguably makes it legally harder to prevent a dangerous AI model from being released than it is today.11 Transformer notes that evaluators can self-certify with no standards or licensing requirements, and that state frontier-AI laws such as California's SB 53 and New York's RAISE would be preempted.1213

Zvi Mowshowitz said he finds it unacceptable that Anthropic is actively shopping the bill, and argues Anthropic is more afraid that the government might enforce rules against Anthropic than it is worried about the government's ability to enforce rules against everyone.141516 Charlie Bullock gave Anthropic some credit for a "pretty reasonable AI safety bill" but said it is weaker than FRONTIER in key ways, such as rulemaking and emergency shutdown authority, while improving on auditor provisions.171819

Anthropic's federal affairs lead Anthony Cimino described the draft as a translation of its framework's ideas into legislative language, shared with a small number of congressional offices, and said Anthropic plans to update its preemption discussion language to better match its framework's intentions, including making clearer that states retain an important role in areas such as standards for independent evaluators and regulating a lab's capacity to shut down a model.202122 Nathan Calvin said Cimino's response is still not engaging with the heart of his concern about Anthropic's behavior and makes him think such problems will resurface; Zvi Mowshowitz said Anthropic does not understand, or at least does not acknowledge, what was centrally wrong with its draft bill.2324
