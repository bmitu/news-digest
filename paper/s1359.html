Disney named lead bidder for Character.AI assets

From reports by TechCrunch, wired.com, Ars Technica and 1 more · Oct 18 – Oct 19

Disney has been named the lead (stalking-horse) bidder for Character.AI's assets after the chatbot startup filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on October 12, so another company could still come forward with a higher offer. inference123 Through the bankruptcy, court filings show Disney hopes to secure a perpetual, worldwide, nonexclusive license to a wide range of Character.AI's code, tools and other artifacts, including its trust and safety moderation stack and age-prediction technology.45 If Disney wins the auction and courts approve, it has agreed to pay an additional $12.5 million at closing; if it is not selected, it gets its $2 million back and a breakup fee.678

TechCrunch, citing a filing spotted by Bloomberg, reports Disney agreed to make offers to at least 60 employees at an estimated $60 million in annual compensation, and that the "vast majority" accepted; it also says the startup previously put Disney's asset purchase at $20 million.910

Ars Technica (with Wired) reports that the Disney deal seems to be structured so that whatever is left of Character.AI retains responsibility for its legal battles, and that a draft "363 Free and Clear Order" would clarify the process should not be viewed as a merger of the two companies.11121314 Character.AI's counsel says Disney required the deal to be "fully consummated" through bankruptcy because of the startup's diminishing cash and litigation and regulatory risks, while Disney declined to comment.151617 The states that filed a notice of interest have reserved their rights to challenge any bankruptcy plan, potentially setting up a fight over the outcome.1819
