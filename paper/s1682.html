Co-Existence book launches

From reports by Ethan Mollick, Zvi Mowshowitz · Oct 20 – Oct 22

Ethan Mollick announced that his second book on AI, Co-Existence, was out that day, 30 months after the early-2024 release of Co-Intelligence.1 Zvi Mowshowitz's newsletter AI #191 noted that Ethan Mollick wrote a book, Co-Existence, about how to live and work in a world where AI is sometimes, but not always, better than you.2

Mollick says the book does not try to keep up with news or model releases but focuses on trends: AI improving exponentially but unevenly, and a shift from chatbots to agents that do real work.3 He says the book addresses what this means for humans who must co-exist with these systems, arguing that humans' advantage comes down to deep knowledge, wide knowledge, taste, and agency.4

In a launch post, Mollick says 180 AI models are now better than GPT-4 was at its release and nineteen of them can run on a modern smartphone.56 Citing the METR benchmark, Mollick says the work AI could do doubled eight times from GPT-4's release, from a four minute task in 2024 to 17 hours of human work when he submitted his draft in April 2026, after which METR said it could no longer measure accurately.789 He also says a correct answer on the Google Proof Question and Answer Exam once cost 30 cents and now costs four hundredths of a cent.10

Mollick says he made the post's charts by delegating to Opus 5.5 in Claude Projects, which caught that he had gotten the math wrong in his planned title of "28 months".1112 He adds that AI remains jagged, still a bad long-form writer but superhuman at math, and he describes a third way of working with AI, the "conjurer", which he calls the most powerful but one that carries new risks as people lose control over what the AI is doing.1314
