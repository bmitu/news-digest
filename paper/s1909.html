Recursive Flint coding model

From reports by r/LocalLLaMA, TechCrunch, Latent Space · Oct 21 – Oct 22

Recursive, an AI startup trying to build self-improving AI, released its first model, Flint, a free coding model that can run on a laptop and that the company says was built largely with AI help.12 Recursive says its automated research system, the Eureka Machine, helped with nearly every step, letting a small team build the model from scratch in under four months.34 Flint is an Apache-2.0 mixture-of-experts model with roughly 40B total parameters, 2.5B active per token and a 262K-token context window.56

Recursive reports 54.9% on Terminal-Bench 2.1, 89.7% on SWE-bench Pro v2 and 74.2% on SWE-bench Verified, which Latent Space describes as release claims rather than independent replication.789 TechCrunch reports that on Recursive's own average across six tests, only Alibaba's Qwen3.8 27B scored higher than Flint.10

In Recursive's tests Flint generated about 131 tokens per second on a MacBook Pro with an M4 Pro chip, roughly twice the 58 to 69 tokens per second of similar models, and topped 150 tokens per second on Nvidia's RTX Spark.1112 NVIDIA's announcement, as relayed in a Reddit thread, claims over 220 tokens/s on a 64GB RTX Spark, a figure that differs from the 150 tokens/s cited elsewhere. inference1312 A separate user measured 114–132 output tokens/s on an M5 Max, which Latent Space calls useful corroboration of speed but not a controlled capability evaluation.14

The company acknowledged that Flint needs more guidance than big paid coding assistants, but Wired's Will Knight reported that it handled his coding requests every time on a 48GB Mac.1516 Commenters on Reddit saw it as the "35B-A3B" class model people wanted, but said the existence of Qwen3.8-27B makes the release feel less surprising.1718
