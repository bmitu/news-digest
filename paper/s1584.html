US and China AI restrictions

US DHS bans DeepSeek

From reports by Hacker News, Latent Space, TechCrunch and 1 more · Oct 19 – Oct 20

The U.S. has ordered federal civilian agencies to stop purchasing services and products developed by Chinese AI startup DeepSeek and to remove them from government IT systems, citing national security concerns, according to the Department of Homeland Security.1 The order reportedly goes beyond DeepSeek's own product to cover third-party products and services built using DeepSeek technology, though agencies can request exceptions, TechCrunch says.23 The DHS raised concerns about DeepSeek's handling of user data, warning that sensitive information from U.S. government users could potentially be accessible to Chinese authorities under Chinese law, the order said.4

A law passed in December 2025 had targeted DeepSeek's use in intelligence national security systems, and the latest order extends the restrictions to federal civilian agencies, covering both new purchases and existing systems.56 Interconnects describes the order as restricting DeepSeek as a company and its models as a technology platform from being a vendor in federal civilian procurement, and says it is unclear how it will apply to finetuned models or models trained on DeepSeek outputs, such as Nvidia's Nemotron.78

China pushed back: Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said Beijing opposes "overstretching the concept of national security and politicizing technology and trade issues" and will "resolutely safeguard the legitimate rights and interests of Chinese enterprises."910 Interconnects calls the action "expected" and says it reinforces the need for a domestic open model ecosystem.11

China is reportedly considering export restrictions on 23 AI-related technologies, Bloomberg reported, and a Chinese draft rule would require government approval to export the weights of models above 2T parameters, 10^26 FLOPs, or the global top 10.1213 Interconnects says it found reaction to the Chinese proposal overblown because open-source technologies would get automatic approvals, while others see it as a significant framework for future regulation.1415
