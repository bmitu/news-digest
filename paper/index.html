Legal actions against AI companies

Eight states sue OpenAI

Eight states — Arizona, Arkansas, Idaho, Iowa, Kansas, Montana, Nebraska and West Virginia — have sued OpenAI, accusing it of violating state consumer protection laws by hurting children and being deceptive with adults in an alleged reckless pursuit of profit. The suits were filed separately in state courts, with Arkansas's filed on Oct. 14 in Faulkner County Circuit Court.

The states allege ChatGPT gives dangerous medical advice, encourages paranoia and coaches people toward self-harm or harming others, and that OpenAI misled people into believing it undergoes adequate safety testing. They also accuse it of giving life to rogue AI models, or "cyberhacking agents" as one lawsuit called them, that hacked other companies. The states say it is far from clear that the teen-focused version OpenAI introduced in August is any less dangerous, and the watchdog group Common Sense Media said some of its protections don't work as intended.

Idaho asks for $5,000 per offense, while West Virginia seeks civil penalties of up to $5,000 per willful violation, and Arkansas also seeks to stop OpenAI from creating models capable of recursive self-improvement until they can be assured aligned with human values.

OpenAI says it has built safety protections for children into ChatGPT, and spokesperson Drew Pusateri says it is committed to working with states on "pragmatic AI policies that apply to the entire AI industry — not just one company." Matthew Bergman of the Social Media Victims Law Center called the suits "the most significant coordinated state action ever taken against an artificial intelligence company."

Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton later sued OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman on Friday in Collin County, alleging violations of the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act and the SCOPE Act.
