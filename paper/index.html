Nvidia CEO Huang's resignation

Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's CEO on Thursday, November 12, after reports of multiple extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy and another with a direct report. CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO, and Harvey C. Jones was appointed the company's first-ever board chair.

On November 17 Huang said that if the board decides his return serves shareholders, employees, the company and America, he would be honored to return as CEO. On the earnings call the same day, Jones said the board had unanimously accepted Huang's resignation and does not intend to reinstate him.

Nvidia reported quarterly revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier, and Kress told investors it is "business as usual." The stock fell from an all-time closing high of $239.24 on October 6 to $162.85 on November 13.

Supporters, including Elon Musk, have signed a petition for Huang's reinstatement, and David Sacks says the spy narrative is collapsing, though US agencies have not confirmed whether Alice Huang is a spy. Critics disagree: former Vice President Mike Pence said Huang had to go and needs to stay gone.

The SEC is reportedly opening a civil probe into whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors. Huang is also scheduled to appear before the House Foreign Affairs Committee on December 1.
