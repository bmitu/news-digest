Nvidia CEO Huang resigns

Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO

Nvidia announced on Thursday, November 12, 2026 that Jensen Huang had resigned as CEO, president and board member, effective immediately, after 33 years leading the company he co-founded. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress is interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent successor, considering internal and external candidates, and Harvey Jones becomes the company's first-ever board chair. President Donald Trump suggested former Apple CEO Tim Cook as Huang's replacement.

In a note sent to Nvidia staff, Huang acknowledged being unfaithful to his wife. A subsequent report claimed he was also involved in an affair with Kimberly Powell, the company's healthcare vice president, who answers directly to him. U.S. officials reportedly believe the first affair partner, Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, is a Chinese spy, and China denies this. Reuters reported afterward that Huang had a third affair with another Nvidia employee, which Nvidia and Huang have not confirmed.

Sources differ on why Huang left: Zvi Mowshowitz says the board was preparing to oust him, while Elizabeth Lopatto of The Verge guesses the second affair was a pretext and the real cause lies in the Megaspeed chip-smuggling concerns. Huang agreed to testify before Congress before Thanksgiving, and Democrats opened a separate inquiry into how the Trump administration handled Nvidia's China chip sales.

Nvidia shares fell sharply on the news, closing more than 4% down on Thursday and shedding more than half a trillion dollars in market value, according to the BBC. Analyst Dan Ives lowered his Nvidia valuation from $300 to $225 per share without Huang as CEO, and said Nvidia is roughly 2-3 years ahead of its nearest competitor. One investor told TechCrunch that Kress is "much more conservative" and is unlikely to commit as much money to the early-stage ecosystem, and Raymond James analyst Ed Mills said the scandal "guarantees new export controls."
