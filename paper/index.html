Nvidia leadership and chip controls

Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO

Nvidia's board said on Monday that it had unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO and president, less than two weeks after he resigned. According to Fortune, Huang resigned on Nov. 12 following reports of extramarital affairs, among them an alleged relationship with a woman whom American intelligence officials suspected of being a spy for China. The board said it had received input from senior levels of the U.S. government and was confident his return poses no national security risk. Semafor reported, and multiple sources confirmed, that President Donald Trump dialed into the Sunday board meeting to argue for Huang's return, and Trump told CNBC, "I spoke to them, let me put it that way. And I convinced them." A Fortune commentary said Trump, who had pressed for the reinstatement, acknowledged that the stock sell-off had probably swayed him. The reversal contradicted the board's recent stance: chair Harvey Jones had said during the earnings call that the company did not intend to reinstate Huang, and Nvidia had retained a search firm for a replacement.

Nvidia's stock jumped more than 30% on Monday, its biggest rise in two decades, after it had fallen more than 62% from its yearly high, according to Semafor. CNBC reported that the stock closed up 31%, while The Atlantic noted the S&P 500 rose 4.7 percent and the Nasdaq 6.4 percent that day; reports differ on how far the stock had earlier fallen. Shares of CoreWeave and Nebius, two cloud providers in which Nvidia has invested, also climbed more than 20% when Huang's return was announced. Semafor later argued the rally faded quickly, with Nvidia shares closing at their July 2025 levels by Tuesday.

Trump said the attorney general had confirmed the government has no evidence of unlawful conduct by Huang, but TechCrunch noted that Attorney General Todd Blanche had not made a formal statement, and The Atlantic argued the phrasing "means exactly nothing." Some Republicans also voiced doubts; Senator John Cornyn asked, "No national security risk? Based on what?" The Wall Street Journal reported on Wednesday that U.S. officials, briefing congressional staff in a classified session, said they had found nothing showing China had orchestrated the affair. Huang is scheduled to testify before three House committees on December 1 and the Senate Banking Committee on December 3.

Governance experts split on the board's decision: Umesh Ramakrishnan called it "incompetent," while Harvard's Jesse Fried said the board was true to its fiduciary duty given the market's positive reaction. Tech leaders such as Elon Musk, who wrote "The mistake has been corrected," celebrated, while critics in The Atlantic and The Verge described the outcome as impunity for powerful tech figures.
