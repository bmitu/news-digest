DeepSeek releases DeepInfer

From reports by github.com, r/LocalLLaMA, Interconnects and 1 more · Oct 20 – Oct 22

DeepSeek released DeepInfer, the inference engine behind V4.1 Flash, as an open-source framework that it says powers its large-scale production services.12 The release supports NVIDIA SM100 GPUs and Huawei Ascend 950 NPUs and is accompanied by deepseek-recipe, a Rust library for protocol conversion and model-output parsing across API formats.34 DeepInfer is licensed under Apache 2.0, and DeepSeek says it was originally built primarily on vLLM, with inspiration from SGLang and TensorRT-LLM.56

DeepSeek's engineering report reportedly claims 155 trillion tokens per day from one cluster, a 96% input-cache hit rate, 400 ms average time to first token, and $38 revenue per accelerator-hour at off-peak prices.78910 Latent Space cautions that these are first-party production claims rather than independently audited measurements, and that the 155T figure includes cache hits, so it should not be read as newly generated tokens.1112 Latent Space also notes that $38 per accelerator-hour is revenue, not profit, while a widely shared estimate put inference margins at 87.76–96.94%, and Interconnects cites estimates of up to 90% gross margin.13141516

Although described as fully open source, the release is offline inference only and leaves out the KV cache system, prefill/decode disaggregation, and load balancing across data-parallel replicas that a complete service needs.1718 The vLLM team reportedly measured decode throughput within 6% of DeepInfer's published results on GB200 under a matched configuration, which Latent Space says qualifies claims that DeepSeek's efficiency is inaccessible to existing open-source engines.1920 SemiAnalysis says DeepInfer is absent from InferenceX because essential serving components are missing, and its later profitability comparison was criticized for mixing hardware generations, peak and off-peak prices, and revenue with profit.212223

Interconnects calls the release one of the bigger open-source AI artifacts in recent months and expects inference companies to adopt the tools, which it says will push prices for DeepSeek's models down or margins up.2425 Tim Dettmers reportedly judges DeepSeek's inference stack comparable to OpenAI's, while Su Jin Yan finds the engineering article more valuable than the repository for an experienced inference developer, partly because important components are absent.2627 Reddit commenters were surprised that DeepSeek open-sourced a production stack, but some questioned whether it is general-purpose or mainly tuned to DeepSeek's own model architecture.282930
