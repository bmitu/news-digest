Meta commercial terms allow crawling

From reports by The Verge, Ars Technica, TechCrunch · Oct 16 – Oct 20

Meta's new commercial terms, due to take effect October 30, appeared to authorize Meta to crawl websites associated with business accounts and to use the content to "develop, improve, research" and "train" its products, according to a Nieman Lab report.12 The revised terms allow Meta to "periodically pull updates" from business users' websites to "provide, develop, improve, research, and maintain advertising and business products," and a Meta spokesperson confirmed to Ars that Meta did not alert users to the changed language.34

Legal experts told Nieman Lab the terms could let Meta "argue its commercial terms supersede other blocking instructions" such as robots.txt, and Cornell professor James Grimmelmann said they represent permission for Meta to train AI on any content from news publishers' sites and possibly to deploy some of it, even in competition with the publishers.567 Attorney Matt Topic called it "a remarkable provision to bury in [the] terms of service," saying it would give Meta access to material that would likely cost many millions of dollars in a licensing market.89

Meta spokesperson Daniel Roberts said the company was only "consolidating" its terms and that "There is no change to how Meta uses web crawlers; any suggestion to the contrary is false."1011 Andy Stone, Meta's vice president of communications, said the change was meant to pull accurate product information from third-party sites for ad and content recommendations, not to scrape news sites.12 Meta said the crawling terms it consolidated "only applied to our ads and business products" and that the updates "don’t give us new rights that supersede those preferences" of content owners signaling preferences via robots.txt.131415

The experts were not satisfied: Topic said "publishers should remain concerned" because "develop" is likely broad enough to include "train," and Grimmelmann said the language is "still broad enough to drive a truck through."161718
