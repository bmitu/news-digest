Anthropic publishes workforce composition report

From reports by TechCrunch, Zvi Mowshowitz · Oct 17 – Oct 22

Anthropic published a report on Friday saying its workforce composition and the type of work being done have changed significantly over the past year, and it argues its own trends may indicate how AI will shift work organization, hiring and labor demand across the economy.12 The report argues AI adoption has made the company more productive, citing more code per person, more sales closed per rep and more contracts processed.3 The company acknowledges that "more code does not necessarily mean better code or business impact," but TechCrunch reports it also hasn't found any indications that code quality is declining.4

Anthropic is making fewer junior hires: the proportion of junior-level employees in technical departments fell from 21% to 14% since early 2025, and the proportion of junior technical hires fell from 27% to 9%.5 Technical departments' share of the overall workforce fell from 61% to 56%, which Anthropic attributes to business functions expanding as the company scales and to coding tools letting non-technical employees build software themselves.678 Zvi Mowshowitz's summary adds that technical hiring kept growing in absolute terms but was outpaced by intensive hiring in sales, finance and recruiting.9

In an April 2026 survey, 27% of employees said Claude had made them more productive and 20% said their job had shifted from doing work to managing Claudes.10 The report suggests human attention and judgement are becoming a bottleneck as Claude produces a deluge of text, and Mowshowitz reports that AI agent hours outnumber employee hours six to one.1112 Based on calendar data, the report says meeting time per employee is up 20% from a year ago and up 16% for technical contributors, which Anthropic attributes to the importance of human trust and accountability.1314

TechCrunch notes that even if other organizations never embrace AI as thoroughly or in the exact same way as Anthropic, the report offers an intriguing glimpse at how the company currently operates.15
