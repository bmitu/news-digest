Google DeepMind staff resign over military contracts

From reports by bbc.com, Zvi Mowshowitz · Oct 21 – Oct 22

Staff at Google's AI company, Google DeepMind, have resigned or are leaving over the company's military AI contracts, the BBC reports after speaking to several tech workers.1 London-based research engineer Sofia Liguori resigned this week, saying the push for military AI makes her feel "ashamed" to work at Google, and she cited increasing work with the US and Israeli militaries.23 AI researcher Andreas Kirsch also resigned earlier this month, saying he believes Google's governance and oversight are not sufficient to deploy AI safely in military and classified settings.45

Google's Pentagon contract, signed earlier this year, allows its AI to be used for classified military work and "any lawful government purpose", while saying it should not be used for domestic mass surveillance or autonomous weapons without appropriate human oversight.67 Google is one of several firms, including OpenAI, Amazon, Microsoft, SpaceX, Oracle, Nvidia and Reflection, that signed similar contracts, and the workers say the deals greatly widen what the Pentagon can use the technology for.89

A Google spokesperson told the BBC that the company follows a consistent process for reviewing these contracts, including compliance with its acceptable use policies and AI principles, and has channels for raising concerns and strict policies against retaliation.10 However, employees told the BBC that staff are scared to speak out because their contracts prohibit publicly criticising the company, and Sofia Liguori said people fear retaliation.11121314

The Pentagon, for its part, maintains that adopting AI is essential for winning wars, with Under Secretary Emil Michael saying "Speed defines victory in the AI era".15 Critics question the speed: retired Lieutenant General Jack Shanahan, former director of Project Maven, described the apparent philosophy as deploying AI as widely as possible, potentially at the expense of test and evaluation rigour.16
