Data center ballot backlash

2026 midterms and data center ballot measures

From reports by The Verge policy, 404 Media, Transformer and 11 more · Nov 4 – Nov 6

Voters in the November 2026 midterms overwhelmingly backed local measures restricting data centers, though outlets give different tallies: The Verge says 43 of 44 local moratoria passed, 404 Media says voters chose to restrict 39 of 40 measures, and Ars Technica, citing Redbud Consulting, reports 38 of 44 passed so far. inference123 The measures included bans and caps on size, such as Wells, Maine's 180-day moratorium on large data centers and El Dorado, Kansas's 10-year ban on those with peak load over 10MW.45

Democrats also took control of the House and the Senate, and Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa has said she plans to "issue a moratorium — a ban — on all data center approvals until laws can be passed by the legislature to protect Texans."678

NBC News polling found 76 percent of voters opposed data centers in their communities, according to The Verge.910 Industry and investors expect delays: Goldman Sachs warned of short-term moratoria potentially affecting about 45% of planned US data center capacity expansion, and Meta's Dina Powell McCormick said the wins will make building data centers harder.1112

Sources dispute how much data centers drove the results: Chamber of Progress CEO Adam Kovacevich says the results show little voter mandate against AI and urges people not to "confuse an anti-Trump wave with an anti-tech mandate."131415 JD Vance reportedly said the war, cost of living and gas prices did more than data centers, and The Verge notes it is hard to draw a direct line from the backlash to any candidate's election.1617 Others read it as a clear rebuke: Wes McEnany of the Future of Life Institute called the results a "total repudiation" of the AI field.18

Democrats in Congress are promising scrutiny, with Rep. Greg Casar saying AI company CEOs should be called to testify under oath.1920 MIT Technology Review cautions that following through may be complicated in Texas, since several data-center-heavy counties went red and construction unions backed some Democrats.2122
