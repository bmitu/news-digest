2026 midterms and data center ballot measures

From reports by TechCrunch, Nate B Jones, The Hill technology and 10 more · Nov 7 – Nov 13

In the November 3 midterms, voters in ten states sided against data center development in 40 of 41 local ballot measures, according to several sources.123 However, the Center for AI Safety's count differs slightly, putting the figure at 44 of 45 cases where counts were complete.4

Other candidates who ran against data centers prevailed too. Among them were Gina Hinojosa, the incoming governor of Texas, and Annie Andrews, who won a Senate seat in South Carolina; each had pledged to pause data center construction.5 Advocates told The Hill that several newly elected candidates heading to Washington offer a "reset" on AI policy.6

Sources disagree about how much AI drove the results overall: one poll cited by the Center for AI Safety found 56% of voters called AI and data centers a crucial or major factor, though more voters cited other issues such as corruption, cost of living, the Iran war and immigration enforcement.78 TechCrunch's Kirsten Korosec said data centers probably did not bring voters to the polls but have become a grassroots issue, in part because of developers' secretive approval practices.910 Wiener says the data center revolt is tied more to local concerns such as electric bills, water use and impacts near homes than to broader questions of AI risk.11

According to the Center for AI Safety, halting data center projects in certain states won't end AI development, because the builders will just move those projects elsewhere. The group says avoiding an AI catastrophe could therefore call for rules aimed at how AI is developed.1213 Jack Clark's newsletter expects the results to slow, not stop, the data center build-out, and Semafor reported that Beijing-based commentators see a possible opening for China. inference1415 A CSET analysis warns about this very issue: absent proof, influence operations shouldn't be cited as what drove voters' decisions. It notes that a result which might favor China doesn't show that China brought it about.1617

Markets and industry reacted: The Atlantic reported that tech stocks fell sharply and that Democrats face a tension between regulating AI and protecting an economy heavily reliant on it, while OpenAI released a compute campus policy blueprint that TechCrunch's hosts said ends NDAs. inference181920
