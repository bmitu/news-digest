Nvidia CEO Huang resigns

Huang admits affair

From reports by Semafor, nvidianews.nvidia.com, Gary Marcus and 7 more · Nov 8 – Nov 13

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang admitted in a letter to employees on Sunday, November 8, 2026, that he had an affair, two days after the Financial Times reported on an alleged relationship with a woman US intelligence believes spied for China.123 The woman, Alice Huang, is an executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, a customer of the chipmaker, according to people familiar with a US government memo; the US and Singapore had both investigated Megaspeed over suspicions it was illegally sharing chips with China, and China's foreign ministry called the spy allegation "unsubstantiated."456

In the letter he apologized to his wife and children, and the board opened an independent investigation, which Huang said he would cooperate with; he did not name Alice Huang in the message.789 Nvidia had earlier dismissed the relationship as "not unusual or unexpected", which critics such as Transformer say was false; the board now says it has no reason to believe national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised. inference101112

President Donald Trump, asked whether he regretted not acting on the intelligence, said he did not think so and called Huang an honorable guy; the New York Times reportedly said he was briefed on the matter in late 2025.131415

Reports of business ties also emerged: The Information reported that a company founded by Alice Huang and her family facilitated $7.5 billion in Nvidia chip purchases, and Bloomberg reported Nvidia's general counsel intervened in the Megaspeed probe.1617 The affair then widened: the New York Times reported a second relationship with Nvidia vice president Kimberly Powell, which Nvidia, Huang and Powell have not confirmed, and Reuters reported a third with another employee.181920

Commentators disagree about the stakes: Transformer argued Huang should resign over conflict of interest and misleading statements, while Semafor's analysis said the scandal has likely doomed his case against chip-sale curbs, and Ben Thompson countered that selling to China is defensible. inference212223 Experts quoted by The Verge warn of risks to the US AI industry, with Emily Kilcrease of CNAS saying chip-control legislation odds "just went through the roof", though the piece notes there is no public evidence Huang himself steered restricted chips to China.2425
