Jacob Coxon leaves Anthropic

From reports by Zvi Mowshowitz, spectrum.ieee.org, The Atlantic · Nov 19

Jacob Coxon, a researcher at Anthropic, said he was quitting the company, cautioning that it and OpenAI are both rushing to build superintelligence able to improve itself, and that doing so puts human lives at risk.1 The Atlantic says he quit in September in order to argue that AI companies might kill us all.2 Anthropic's head of alignment science, Evan Hubinger, replied that Coxon was correct that Anthropic earnestly believes AI could kill all humans, and CEO Dario Amodei later told CNN he agrees with Coxon much more than he disagrees.34

Coxon later said a slide from the Anthropic IPO roadshow was a big factor in his decision to leave, saying it shows continued superexponential algorithmic progress in pretraining compute efficiency.56 According to Zvi Mowshowitz, Coxon is honoring his NDA and has declined to say more about the chart.78

Coxon says he committed to donating all his OpenAI equity to AI safety causes, both because donations are impactful and to back his message that the race to superintelligence should be paused or drastically slowed.910 The Atlantic says he had earlier seemed to laugh off such a donation in an interview, saying "I like the money, bro," and called this naked hypocrisy before noting he has since agreed to donate.1112 Mowshowitz says he would ideally prefer Coxon keep his stock, but that given how people see it, a pledge makes sense in Coxon's case.1314

Mowshowitz also reports that a protest outside Anthropic's San Francisco offices tied to its IPO, at which Coxon was to speak, was planned for the IPO day, when the final price was set at $83 per share, about a $1.5 trillion valuation. inference1516
