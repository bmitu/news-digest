Claude designs quantum circuits

From reports by Anthropic, Latent Space · Oct 16 – Oct 17

Anthropic says it gave Claude plain-language specifications for eight quantum circuits, along with an automated evaluator that checks and scores each design, and that Claude found designs improving on the best published circuits, in some cases by an order of magnitude or more.12 Anthropic says Claude Mythos 5 worked in a closed loop, proposing a design, receiving a score from the evaluator and improving its next attempt with no human input between rounds.3 The work was led by Andi Gu in Anthropic's STEM Fellows Program, mentored by Alek Kemeny, in collaboration with Susanne Yelin and Mikhail Lukin of Harvard.4

A Latent Space newsletter summarizing the announcement says Claude beat the best-known circuits on 7 of 8 tasks, whereas Anthropic's own text says its designs used fewer qubits and fewer gates than the best previously published algorithms across eight primitives. inference56 Anthropic says one new elliptic-curve design uses 12% more qubits but 17x fewer gates than the smallest previous design, and that another discovery makes the costliest step of a leading quantum optimization algorithm about 12x cheaper.78 Anthropic says the gains came from new structural and mathematical insights, such as reusing results made possible by an iron-sulfide molecule's symmetries, rather than from fine-tuning existing designs.910

Anthropic also reports limitations: early on Claude was conservative, repeatedly double-checking results and preferring well-known approaches, which Anthropic countered by requiring several different approaches in parallel and occasionally prompting broader searches.1112 Anthropic argues the reductions could mean practical quantum computers arrive sooner than previously anticipated, though it notes circuit design is only one part of building a useful quantum computer. inference1314
