Google Suncatcher satellite

From reports by The Verge, TechCrunch · Oct 30

Google says its Project Suncatcher prototype satellite has generated its first response from space, after the company booted up the satellite's Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) and the on-board Gemma 4 model answered a prompt to write a haiku about being in space.123 Google launched the satellite earlier this month, and TechCrunch reports it went up alongside more than 100 other spacecraft on a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket.45 TechCrunch says a roughly three-week commissioning process tested the satellite's subsystems before the first prompt was sent, and a Google spokesperson said the satellite was operating nominally.67

According to TechCrunch, the satellite carries four TPUs and generates up to 1 kW of electricity, but its power demands mean only one TPU can run at a time, for about 20 minutes.89 Google says it will next test how the TPUs perform under thermal stress and radiation exposure in orbit, and that verifying they can run workloads reliably in space paves the way for twin interconnected satellites in 2027.1011 TechCrunch notes that radiation can cause bit flips that corrupt data without triggering typical monitoring tools, and that ground testing suggested the impact would be minimal for inference workloads and more of a problem for training.1213

TechCrunch describes Suncatcher as largely alone among space data center plans in intending high-speed links between satellites for coherent distributed workloads, while SpaceX's data centers are expected to orbit too far apart to share processing effectively and Starcloud and Cowboy Space expect inference on individual satellites.1415 TechCrunch adds that both Google and SpaceX would need Starship to be fully and rapidly reusable, and that Suncatcher researchers estimated in a peer-reviewed analysis that SpaceX would need to fly Starship 1,800 times to achieve the same learning-driven cost reductions seen with earlier vehicles.1617

TechCrunch frames Suncatcher as a classic Google moonshot aimed at economical infrastructure in the 2030s, but says getting there may depend on how the rest of the company responds to the pressures and opportunities of AI in the near term, noting Alphabet's stock plunged after it reported that efforts to insert AI into its search businesses are hurting ad revenue growth.1819
