Viral Claude TV hack

From reports by TechCrunch, 404 Media · Oct 19 – Oct 20

TechCrunch reports that developers and vibe coders have recently been sharing AI-driven solutions for "vibe-coding" smart TVs to remove sponsored ads, speed up the app launcher, redesign the home screen and disable tracking features.1 The story went viral after Gathin T., creator of "cleanyourtv", posted on X on October 17, 2026 that he had Claude Code find his parents' TV on wifi, remove 25 Xiaomi packages and write a new launcher.234 404 Media says that post has allegedly been viewed more than 6 million times, and Gathin T. says a few thousand people copied the approach on their own TVs within two days.56 Gathin T. says his solution does not require root access and does not prevent a return to factory default state.7

A second project, "dumbtv", by Muzzammil Yasin Sabuwala, has been tested end-to-end on a Fire TV Edition set and on Android/Google TV platforms on TVs from Sony, TCL, Philips, Hisense, Xiaomi and Shield, according to TechCrunch, and Sabuwala told TechCrunch it was coded with Deepseek V4.1 Flash, Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Sonnet 5.5.89 Ryan Doyle claims in a viral X post that he asked Claude Code "Can you hack this?" and was talked through removing ads and bloatware, speeding up the interface and customizing his screensaver.1011

However, TechCrunch says results may be mixed: some users found Claude would not respond to simple "hack my TV" requests, others warned agents could disable important packages, and one person said they bricked their Android TV.121314 TechCrunch also says that if such hacks become popular, they have the potential to disrupt the smart TV ad industry, which profits from viewing data and personalized ads.15 Mike Nolan, who studies the politics of open source software, told 404 Media that recreating functionality is getting easier, but that maintaining software is hard and he doesn't know how many companies will adopt such clones.1617
