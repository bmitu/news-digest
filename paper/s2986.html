Publishers sue Brave

From reports by OECD AI Incidents Monitor, The Verge, TechCrunch and 2 more · Nov 2 – Nov 5

The New York Times sued Brave on Monday in federal court in Manhattan, alleging it scraped the outlet's copyrighted content.12 Separately, the New York Daily News, the Chicago Tribune and the Orlando Sentinel filed a similar lawsuit against Brave the same day.34

The Times's suit names Anthropic, Perplexity, Mistral, xAI and Cohere as recipients of the content, and calls Brave a "supplier, dealer, and distributor of stolen copyrighted content."56 The Times says Brave built an index of more than 30 billion web pages with an unidentified crawler that leaves publishers unable to block it, and, citing third-party revenue estimates, claims Brave made more than $100 million from reselling publishers' work.78 The suit also targets a Brave browser feature that bypasses paywalls and the company's practice of blocking publishers' ads while showing its own.910

Ars Technica reported that in its own test on a Mac, the optional paywall blocker, enabled by default, made it possible to read a paywalled New York Times article.11

The Times alleges direct, contributory and vicarious copyright infringement, as well as a Digital Millennium Copyright Act violation for circumventing technological measures, and asks for a permanent injunction.121314

Jesse Dwyer, Perplexity's chief communications officer, gave a statement on the suit, and Ars Technica noted that the statement did not identify any specific inaccuracies in the lawsuit against Brave.1516 Brave did not immediately respond to requests for comment in the coverage provided.1718
