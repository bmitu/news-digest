Anthropic's stock market listing

Anthropic IPO Nasdaq debut

From reports by The Verge, TechCrunch, The Atlantic and 2 more · Nov 21 – Nov 26

Anthropic began trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker "ANT" on November 19, 2026, in what sources describe as the largest IPO in history, raising $81.3 billion at $83 per share.1234 According to TechCrunch, the listing made Anthropic the first of the major AI labs to become a public company.5 The $83 price was above the $70–$78 range the company had projected, and Bloomberg reported orders for about six times the shares on offer.67 Shares opened at $111.50, about 33% above the IPO price, but closed at $97.17, up roughly 17%.8910 Anthropic's closing announcement says the offering, including the full underwriters' option, brought in about $93.5 billion gross, and a concurrent private placement of Class N shares to firms including Nvidia, Google and Microsoft brought in about $26.0 billion.1112

Valuation figures differ by source: TechCrunch said the opening price implied a market cap above $2 trillion, while The Verge and others cited about $1.5 trillion at the IPO price and Platformer cited $1.76 trillion at the close. inference131415

Before the opening bell, CEO Dario Amodei vowed that Anthropic would not bend to either business or political pressure.1617 Critics doubt this. The Verge reported a former employee saying the Long-Term Benefit Trust has no meaningful power, and Platformer noted that Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy is not binding and that more than 100 protesters gathered outside its San Francisco headquarters.181920 The Center for AI Safety newsletter reported that a public statement signed by more than 1,000 people accused Anthropic of watering down its safety commitments while racing toward superintelligence.2122

Gains faded as the broader market fell: Fortune reported the Nasdaq down 2.25% at the close, and Gary Marcus called the debut on pace to be one of the most embarrassing IPOs in history, with premarket trading near $88.232425 The Atlantic said the dominant investor mood is deep anxiety despite the record raise, and suggested the AI boom may be deflating gradually rather than bursting.2627

According to Fortune's calculations, roughly $20 billion and change went unclaimed by the company, and Jay Ritter noted that when everything is tallied, no IPO in history has ever had a bigger figure of this kind.2829 Analyst Gil Luria told TechCrunch that Anthropic will have more cash on hand than any company ever, while commitments to compute and infrastructure remain enormous, with about $451.6 billion due before the end of 2030.3031
