Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO

From reports by The Verge, The Atlantic, cnbc.com and 14 more · Nov 23 – Nov 26

Nvidia's board said on Monday that it had unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO and president, less than two weeks after he resigned.123 The board said it had received input from senior levels of the U.S. government and was confident his return poses no national security risk.45 Semafor reported, and multiple sources confirmed, that President Donald Trump dialed into the Sunday board meeting to argue for Huang's return, and Trump told CNBC, "I spoke to them, let me put it that way. And I convinced them."67 The reversal contradicted the board's recent stance: chair Harvey Jones had said during the earnings call that the company did not intend to reinstate Huang, and Nvidia had retained a search firm for a replacement.8

Nvidia's stock jumped more than 30% on Monday, its biggest rise in two decades, after it had fallen more than 62% from its yearly high, according to Semafor.910 CNBC reported that the stock closed up 31%, while The Atlantic noted the S&P 500 rose 4.7 percent and the Nasdaq 6.4 percent that day; reports differ on how far the stock had earlier fallen. inference111213 Semafor later argued the rally faded quickly, with Nvidia shares closing at their July 2025 levels by Tuesday.14

Trump said the attorney general had confirmed the government has no evidence of unlawful conduct by Huang, but TechCrunch noted that Attorney General Todd Blanche had not made a formal statement, and The Atlantic argued the phrasing "means exactly nothing." inference151617 Some Republicans also voiced doubts; Senator John Cornyn asked, "No national security risk? Based on what?"18 Huang is scheduled to testify before three House committees on December 1 and the Senate Banking Committee on December 3.1920

Governance experts split on the board's decision: Umesh Ramakrishnan called it "incompetent," while Harvard's Jesse Fried said the board was true to its fiduciary duty given the market's positive reaction.2122 Tech leaders such as Elon Musk, who wrote "The mistake has been corrected," celebrated, while critics in The Atlantic and The Verge described the outcome as impunity for powerful tech figures. inference232425
