Websites blocking AI agents

From reports by The Verge, Hacker News · Oct 17 – Oct 18

Users of several AI agents, including Meta's Muse, xAI's Grok, OpenAI's Dots and Instinct, have reported in recent weeks that blocks are stopping the agents from performing tasks on top websites.1 Yelp told TechCrunch it only allows AI agents that pay for its data licensing program, Delta Air Lines is preventing them from booking tickets, and JPMorgan Chase and Charles Schwab confirmed they block agents from accessing accounts over security concerns.23 Some users say sites such as Cars.com and Amtrak that previously worked with their AI agent are no longer accessible, which The Verge says suggests other companies could be quietly blocking agents.4

Amazon was already fighting off AI agents last year when it banned Perplexity's agentic shopping feature and sued the startup; Amazon claimed it "repeatedly requested" that Perplexity stop, adding that third-party companies purchasing on behalf of customers "should operate openly and respect service provider decisions whether or not to participate."56 Perplexity responded that Amazon wants to protect its ad revenue from human eyeballs and argued Amazon "does not believe in your right to hire labor, to have an assistant or an employee acting on your behalf."78 A judge later ordered Perplexity to stop its agents from shopping on Amazon, but an appeals court overturned that ruling in August, and Amazon has since filed an amended complaint.9

Amazon recently said Meta's Muse's "continued access" violates its site policies, and there are reports that Grok, Instinct's agent and OpenAI's Dots also cannot use Amazon.1011 Meta says "Turning away a personal agent means turning away the customer behind it," and it has partnered with retailers including Walmart, Gap, Sephora and Best Buy, while Shopify has opened its storefronts to agents.121314

Chris Canal, CEO of EquiStamp, said the blocks affect the perceived value of companies like Anthropic and OpenAI ("I would say absolutely"), said anti-bot safeguards set up to block scams or hacking can be triggered by AI agents too, and says such blocks will ultimately make the web more "annoying" to use as more sites roll out interfaces to confirm you're not a robot.151617
