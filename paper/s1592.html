Court orders Meta to name executives

From reports by OECD AI Incidents Monitor, Ars Technica · Oct 20

Magistrate Judge Thomas Hixon has largely agreed that Meta must clearly identify key decision-makers, and Meta has been ordered to name executives who authorized decisions to pirate books from shadow libraries like Anna's Archive and LibGen, TorrentFreak reported.123 The discovery order affects a class action by book authors including Richard Kadrey and Sarah Silverman, and a lawsuit from publisher Entrepreneur Media.4 Ars Technica says the order could expose a wider range of supervisors, managers and executives whom staffers said greenlit the piracy, and that it also compels Meta to produce command history files for every server it used to torrent.56

Meta argued that its early AI dataset efforts used an "informal approach" in which nobody with authority made decisions, and that a decision was made only in the sense that some staffers technically downloaded torrents.78 Hixon rejected these arguments, saying the authors' request was not limited to formal decisions and did not presume a single decision maker, since it asks about all the decision makers.910

Authors and publishers point to staff testimony and messages as evidence that approval was needed, including one staffer who said downloads required director or VP level approval, and an unsealed message allegedly showing a prior escalation to "MZ" (Mark Zuckerberg).1112

OECD.AI's automated monitor labelled the order an AI incident, noting that the court ruled training on the books was fair use but that the torrenting and seeding of pirated content remains legally challenged.1314
