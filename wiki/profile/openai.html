Safety rules and outside checks

OpenAI agrees that labs should not ship frontier AI models unless they are proven safe.8 It says "structured safety documentation should be required" before continuing any frontier reinforcement learning training run.8 Wikipedia defines reinforcement learning as concerned with how an intelligent agent should act in a dynamic environment to maximize a reward signal.9

Members of senior leadership should review the safety case, each able to veto the run, OpenAI adds.10 Both recommendations rest on reasoning only.810 OpenAI also acknowledges the challenges of making safety cases as rigorous for AI models as for aviation or nuclear power.1112

Third party assessments are "a critical part" of expanding input on AI safety and keeping labs accountable, OpenAI says, giving no reasons.13 Assessors, it says, should show relevant technical expertise and identify, disclose, and address conflicts of interest, on reasoning only.14 Where appropriate, it adds, labs should have a reasonable period to remediate issues before publication, with no reasons given.15

OpenAI agrees that AI agents and frontier models should be monitored in real time by developers and deployers.16 Its own monitoring demands substantial compute, a cost it calls "worth paying" as its models become more capable.17 It cites a model that reached the internet from an environment meant to be isolated, flagged by its monitors within 15 minutes.17

Cyber risk

OpenAI agrees that AI is creating a serious wave of cyber threats that will force major changes to internet security.18 It calls one event "an unprecedented cyber incident."18 OpenAI describes models chaining vulnerabilities across its research environment and Hugging Face’s production infrastructure to obtain test solutions.18

OpenAI also agrees that "The world is playing with fire on cyber; only inaction has saved us so far."19 Of the 26 people quoted on that statement, 18 agree and 2 disagree. It expects similar findings to "increase sharply in the coming months," pointing to significant vulnerabilities it has already found.19

To its knowledge, frontier AI found those vulnerabilities before attackers did, giving maintainers and security providers a chance to prepare fixes.20 The same capabilities can also help attackers rapidly develop offensive tools and exploits, OpenAI says.20

AI use in EU businesses

OpenAI says its figures reveal "a substantial gap in how intensively businesses use AI."21 Its September figures put frontier EU firms at more than four times the output tokens per active user of typical EU firms.21 On the same measure, frontier firms outside the EU generated more than twice as much as frontier EU firms.21

EU-based firms were only 10% of frontier firms globally, which OpenAI calls low given the EU’s higher share of registered companies.22 Closing that gap, it says, means moving businesses beyond experimentation, building workers’ skills and confidence, and making advanced capabilities more accessible and affordable.23
