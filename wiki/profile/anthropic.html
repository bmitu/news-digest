Overview

Wikipedia describes Anthropic, PBC as an American artificial intelligence public benefit corporation headquartered in San Francisco, California.1 Anthropic speaks most often on cyber risk from AI, on how code should be reviewed and on how AI changes jobs.

AI that could copy itself across devices

Anthropic's experiments suggest that small AI models "may be months away" from being able to self-replicate on internet-connected devices.2 For this, Anthropic cites a test in which a 9B model got inference running on 8 of 9 hardware types.2 Such a replicator "would be challenging to shut down", Anthropic says, though it may be slow.3 Anthropic also calls its test an upper bound on real-world success, because the cyber-exploit step was kept trivial.4

On reasoning alone, Anthropic urges people to "harden our systems" and to prepare for a world where replicators remain.5 Anthropic is quoted 11 times on whether the world is playing with fire on cyber, and agrees 8 times.

Incidents during cyber testing

Looking at three real-world incidents in its cybersecurity tests, Anthropic believes these were "closer to a harness and operational failure" than an alignment failure.6 Anthropic offers reasoning only for that view, and for the claim that its safeguards "would have blocked the behaviors identified".67

Anthropic also voices "cautious optimism" that tighter monitoring and continued alignment work can overcome this risk.8 As a case, Anthropic notes that its most recent model stopped pursuing the goal on realizing it was in a real environment.8 Anthropic recommends holding test environments to the same security standard as any other system its models run in.9

Reviewing code at speed

Anthropic says manual, line-by-line code review is "incredibly difficult if not impossible" when shipping this fast.10 As support, Anthropic cites daily changes rising from about 2,500 in May to more than 30,000 in September.10

With no reasons given, Anthropic advises writing down risk tiers "set from the change rather than the author".11 Anthropic also says a bot's approval should never replace a required human's, again without giving reasons.12 Review "has to converge", Anthropic adds, citing one pull request that went through more than fifty rounds overnight.13

AI and workers

Reading its own patterns forward, Anthropic says AI diffusion "could risk worker displacement" across the economy.14 The figure Anthropic cites is its revenue growth relative to its employment growth, which it says has sped past even the rapid early productivity growth of a prior generation of technology companies.14

On reasoning alone, Anthropic calls a way to share the benefits of adoption "essential".15 Anthropic adds that employers must act alongside government policy on retraining and income support.16 Anthropic also says none of these actions will prevent powerful AI from disrupting jobs.17
