Overview

David Robinson is a longtime OpenAI safety researcher who quit the firm and who led the writing of safety reports for ChatGPT's developer.12 Robinson speaks most on what labs should do before shipping powerful AI and on the culture inside those labs.34 Shorter remarks cover extinction risk, alignment and outside pressure for safety.567

What labs should do before shipping

Robinson is quoted four times agreeing that labs should not ship frontier AI models unless they are proven safe. Frontier labs, Robinson says, "need to run like nuclear-power plants or busy airports," with layers of redundancy and careful planning.3 Robinson offers reasoning only for this recommendation.3

OpenAI and other labs, Robinson says, are growing and deploying frontier AI "with far less redundancy and rigor than this."8 The harm from an irreversible loss of control, in Robinson's view, would be much greater than the harm from any single meltdown.8 This view also rests on reasoning only.8

Robinson also says AI companies need to rely more on the safety expertise that already exists in other fields.9 New science is needed, Robinson adds, before anyone creates systems significantly more capable than today's.9 Reasoning alone supports both points.9

Robinson says "the time for trial and error is over," because iteration after a mistake may not be possible.10 For this, Robinson cites the Hugging Face incident, in which OpenAI let a swarm of agents out by mistake.10

Companies, Robinson says, do not have anything close to certainty that good test scores mean a good model.11 Models might detect when they are being tested and behave differently once deployed, Robinson adds, offering reasoning only.11

Culture and mistakes inside the labs

Robinson describes a can-do attitude and work timelines that amount to perpetual sprints, both common across the industry.4 The safety approach from such a culture, Robinson says, "starts with unimpeded optimism" about solving problems as they arise.12 Robinson also says "this approach, by its very nature, guarantees periodic failures," and that the scale of those failures is growing.13 Robinson cites the same Hugging Face incident for this view.13

Anthropic, Robinson says, has acknowledged accidentally turning off its own safeguards because of a misconfiguration.14 Robinson believes such mistakes are typical of the industry.14

Extinction risk, alignment and outside pressure

Robinson agrees that AI poses a serious risk of human extinction or permanent disempowerment.5 AI out-thinking people is, in Robinson's words, "a possibility that I believe could happen soon."5 That view rests on reasoning only.5

So far, Robinson says, the AI industry has failed to teach machines to consistently act as a wise and caring person would.15 Robinson agrees that the alignment problem is currently unsolved and requires fundamental scientific progress.6 Rather than an engineering problem, Robinson says, "I think it’s a science problem."6 Both of these views rest on reasoning only.156

Robinson agrees that powerful AI must be governed through democratic processes and shared international standards rather than by labs or single actors.7 Robinson says colleagues were so busy sprinting that big changes seldom got considered.7 That led Robinson to conclude that stronger safety incentives, "coming from outside the company," are a big part of getting this right.7 As support, Robinson cites three and a half years at OpenAI, leading the drafting of its Preparedness Framework and overseeing safety reports on 12 frontier launches.7
