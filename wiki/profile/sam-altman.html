Overview

Sam Altman is the chief executive of OpenAI.12 The subjects Altman speaks on most are democratic governance of AI, human capability and creativity, and whether RAM belongs in data centers. Safety standards and cyber threats come next.

Who governs AI and what it is for

Altman agrees that powerful AI must be governed through democratic processes and shared international standards rather than by labs or single actors.3 Of the 11 people quoted on this, 4 agree. The most important decisions "cannot be made by labs in San Francisco alone," Altman says.4 Altman also calls for a mechanism for complementary national and international frontier AI standards.5 Those standards should not lock in incumbents or favor one business model, Altman adds, without giving reasons.6 Three of the governance views (the ones on democratic processes, decisions not made by labs alone, and a mechanism for national and international standards) rest on reasoning only.345

AI can be more like a new Renaissance or more like a new Industrial Revolution of upheaval, Altman says.7 The right tools make people more capable, in Altman's words, and Altman believes this "is going to go much further than we believe possible."8 Both of these views rest on reasoning only.78 Altman also says the best version of AI is about "giving people more agency," and offers no reasons for this.9

Risk, safety and pace

Altman agrees that AI poses a serious risk of human extinction or permanent disempowerment.10 Of the 15 people quoted on this, 11 agree and 3 disagree. Whether people put the risk of catastrophe at 10% or .1%, Altman says, "None of these levels are remotely acceptable."10

As building AI becomes more automated, the pace of progress could accelerate rapidly, Altman says, and this moment "calls for extreme care."11 Altman also says the industry must not accept too much technological risk just because the benefits feel too important to slow down.12 Beating companies on pace "is not a reason to make rash decisions," in Altman's view.13 All of these views rest on reasoning only.10111213

Cyber threats

Altman agrees that AI is creating a serious wave of cyber threats that will force major changes to internet security.14 Of the 12 people quoted on this, 11 agree. "I no longer think we’re going to be able to just patch all the bugs," Altman says.14 Altman expects a need "to completely switch the defense model," a view resting on reasoning only.14 AI models might be able to find bugs beyond what people think a hacker can do, Altman also says, without giving reasons.15

RAM in data centers and personal computers

From a global allocation perspective, Altman calls it "quite terrible" for the limited supply to sit in personal computers used five percent of the time.16 With supply this constrained, Altman says, "You want maximum utilization in the cloud."17 Both views rest on reasoning only.1617 Altman also describes an assumption that "the problem is solved now" because open-source models will run on personal computers, and gives no reasons here.18

Said differently

Altman is quoted on both sides of the statement that labs should not ship frontier AI models unless they are proven safe. The agreeing remark came at the United Nations Security Council on September 23, 2026.19 There, Altman said "we should not train models" without an extremely strong case for keeping them under human control.19 For that view, Altman cites a case: "We have unilaterally slowed down in the past."19 On October 6, 2026, Altman is reported as saying "the world should accept some bad things happening" for the technology's benefits.20 Altman offers no reasons for that remark.20
