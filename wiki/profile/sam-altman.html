Overview

Sam Altman is the CEO of OpenAI.1 Altman speaks most often on how powerful AI should be governed, how AI affects human capability, and whether RAM belongs in data centers.

Who should govern powerful AI

Altman agrees that powerful AI must be governed through democratic processes and shared international standards, not by labs or single actors.2 At the United Nations Security Council, Altman said the most important decisions "cannot be made by labs in San Francisco alone."2 No one person, company or country should be able to use the most powerful models to impose a worldview on everyone else, Altman says.3 Altman also calls for "a mechanism for complementary national and international frontier AI standards."4

These recommendations rest on reasoning only.324 Those standards "should not lock in incumbents or favor one business model over another," Altman adds, with no reasons given.5 Altman also sees a crossroads between concentrated power with opaque decisions and a world where AI works for people, offering no reasons.6 Of the 15 people quoted on this statement, 8 agree.

Risk, pace and cyber threats

Altman agrees that AI poses a serious risk of human extinction or permanent disempowerment.7 Whether people put the risk of catastrophe at 10% or .1%, Altman says, "None of these levels are remotely acceptable."7 That view rests on reasoning only.7 Of the 22 people quoted on this statement, 14 agree and 6 disagree.

Altman agrees that frontier AI development should slow down relative to its current default trajectory.8 Backing Dario Amodei, Altman says "we need to pace the frontier," and gives no reasons.8 Altman calls committing to independent evaluators with employee-like access a great idea and pledges to do the same, with no reasons given.9 As building AI becomes more automated, Altman says, AI progress could accelerate rapidly, a point resting on reasoning only.10

Altman agrees that AI is creating a serious wave of cyber threats that will force major changes to internet security.11 Altman no longer thinks all bugs can be patched and says "we’re going to have to completely switch the defense model," on reasoning only.11 AI models might also find bugs beyond what people normally think a hacker can do, Altman says, with no reasons given.12

What AI could do for people

Altman says AI can be more like "a new Renaissance of creativity and discovery" or "a new Industrial Revolution of upheaval and disarray."13 That view rests on reasoning only.13 The right tools make people more capable, Altman says, and this is going to go much further than believed possible, again on reasoning only.14 Altman says the best version of AI is about giving people "more agency," with no reasons given.15

RAM in data centers

It is "quite terrible," Altman says, for everybody to have a share of the world's limited supply sitting in a personal computer, used five percent of the time.16 Altman also says "You want maximum utilization in the cloud" when supply is very constrained.17 Both points rest on reasoning only.1617

Altman's remark about an assumption that open-source models run on personal computers will remove the need for data centers comes with no reasons given.18

Said differently

On whether labs should not ship frontier AI models unless they are proven safe, Altman is quoted both agreeing and disagreeing. At the United Nations Security Council on September 23, 2026, Altman addressed which models should be trained.19 Altman said not to "train models that we cannot make an extremely strong case that we will be able to keep under human control."19 As support, Altman cited a case: "We have unilaterally slowed down in the past."19

An AI news item dated October 6, 2026 quotes Altman saying "the world should accept some bad things happening" for the technology’s benefits.20 No reasons are given for that remark.20
