Overview

Zvi Mowshowitz describes Sam Altman as the CEO of OpenAI.1 Wikipedia describes Altman as "an American entrepreneur and investor" who has led OpenAI since 2019.2

Who should govern powerful AI

In remarks at the United Nations Security Council on September 23, 2026, Altman said key decisions "cannot be made by labs in San Francisco alone."3 Altman says no person, company or country should use the most powerful models "to impose their worldview on everyone else."4

Of the 15 people quoted on whether powerful AI must be governed this way, 8 agree.

AI risk, safety and pace

Altman agrees that AI poses a serious risk of human extinction or permanent disempowerment.5 Altman said it doesn't matter whether people put the risk of catastrophe at 10%, or 1%, or 12%, or .1%, and that none of these levels are "remotely acceptable," offering reasoning only.5 As building AI becomes more automated, Altman says, the pace of progress "could accelerate rapidly."6

On September 14, 2026, Altman agreed with Dario Amodei on the need "to pace the frontier," giving no reasons.7 Altman also backs independent evaluators with employee-like access and pledges to do the same, again with no reasons given.8 Of the 22 people quoted on extinction risk, 14 agree and 6 disagree.

Cyber threats and security

Altman agrees that AI is creating a serious wave of cyber threats that will force major changes to internet security.9 On October 27, 2026, Altman said, "I no longer think we’re going to be able to just patch all the bugs."9

Altman also says AI models "might be able to find" bugs beyond what a hacker is thought able to do, giving no reasons.10 Of the 15 people quoted on this, 14 agree.

Computer memory and the cloud

On October 30, 2026, Altman called memory in personal computers used five percent of the time "quite terrible" from a global view.11 In a supply constrained environment, Altman says, "you want maximum utilization in the cloud."12

Altman explains that memory makers hold back on expanding because they fear "the bubble might burst."13 These memory views rest on reasoning only.111213

Said differently

Altman is quoted agreeing on September 23, 2026, and disagreeing on October 6, 2026, that labs should not ship frontier AI models unless proven safe. At the Security Council, Altman said "we should not train models" without an extremely strong case for keeping them under human control.14 For this, Altman cited a specific case: "We have unilaterally slowed down in the past."14

On October 6, 2026, Altman also said "the world should accept some bad things happening" for the technology's benefits, giving no reasons.15
