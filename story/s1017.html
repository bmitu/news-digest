OpenAI former employee Apple files case

Last scan Sat Oct 17, 12:46 UTC
3 sources · 3 independent origins: Reuters, TechCrunch, Zack Korman

OpenAI claims in a filing made on Wednesday that a former employee took more than 100 files related to its product plans before joining Apple.12 OpenAI alleges the employee sent the information to a personal account and then presented it to Apple leadership, including the vice president of the company's operations in Asia.3

OpenAI alleges the files contain images of at least one unreleased device, along with supplier information, engineering data, project files, standards for cosmetic finishes, and reports about the "coating and sealing of hardware components."4 OpenAI links Apple's new HomeView smart display and other devices revealed this week to the presentation, saying it appears to have happened months ago.56

The Verge notes that Apple's product plans typically run on a very long timeline and that its smart display has been rumored for years, and Apple did not immediately respond to its request for comment.78 The filing comes in a legal battle over trade secrets in which Apple sued OpenAI in July, accusing it of a pattern of theft that includes complaints about former employees using Apple laptops to download confidential material; this time OpenAI is doing the accusing.910

The judge also approved depositions of Liu, Peng and OpenAI's chief hardware officer Tang Yew Tan, but denied expedited access to OpenAI's research in areas like metal finishes and power management, leaving the door open for Apple to narrow its requests and try again.111213

Commenting on the disclosure, Zack Korman argues it is a bad look for OpenAI's insider threat detection, saying the files were taken months ago and only found while looking for evidence in the Apple lawsuit.1415
