David Robinson resigns from OpenAI with safety warning

Last scan Sat Oct 10, 12:51 UTC
4 sources · 762 points on Hacker News · tracked since Oct 4 · 1 new article this week · 3 independent origins: OpenAI, TechCrunch, The Guardian

David Robinson, who says he led the writing of safety reports that accompanied OpenAI's major launches, resigned from OpenAI and said in an essay in The Atlantic that the company's "culture is broken."123 Robinson wrote that he agrees with other recently departed staff that AI companies "aren't being nearly careful enough," but that the debate must look deeper than specific rules or new laws and address culture.45

He argued that OpenAI's trial-and-error "iterative deployment" approach guarantees periodic failures whose scale grows as systems become more capable, and he pointed to the Hugging Face incident, in which he says OpenAI let a swarm of agents out by mistake.678 Robinson said frontier labs need to run like nuclear-power plants or busy airports, and called for relying on safety expertise from other fields and for new science to ensure more capable models make safe choices when unobserved.9101112

Robinson says he was among OpenAI's longest-tenured employees after three and a half years, led the drafting of its Preparedness Framework and oversaw safety reports on 12 frontier launches.13 Robinson said he enlisted the PR firm Spitfire Strategies after quitting, but insisted the decision to speak out is his alone, and that he plans to work from outside to strengthen the incentives OpenAI and other firms have to be safer.141516

OpenAI spokesperson Drew Pusateri said the company is making sure its models don't become more capable than it can safely manage and secure, and that it pauses training or holds back models when needed.1718 Pusateri said OpenAI is making significant changes to strengthen security in its research and testing environments, train models to do tasks responsibly, expand work with third-party evaluators, and improve real-time monitoring.19

The Guardian reports that OpenAI has shown signs of caution recently, including scrapping a next-generation model's release after researchers raised safety concerns, and it places Robinson's warning alongside other departures, including Anthropic's Jacob Coxon, though it notes critics call such warnings unscientific because they cannot be verified or falsified. inference2021
