Anthropic S-1 IPO filing

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
7 sources · 996 points on Hacker News · tracked since Oct 30 · 8 new articles this week · single origin (Anthropic)

Anthropic says it expects the IPO price to be between $70.00 and $78.00 per share.12 Anthropic's own FAQ says the roadshow began November 10, with the final price expected November 18 and trading expected to start November 19, subject to regulatory clearance.3

The offering is for 825 million shares, according to The Hill, and at the top of the range it would value Anthropic at about $1.4 trillion on a fully diluted basis.45 Zvi Mowshowitz says about 800 million of the shares come directly from Anthropic, with options on 124 million more, and puts the valuation range at $1.27 trillion to $1.41 trillion.67 Artificial Lawyer's Claude instance computes that this is roughly $56-62 billion of new money, and describes the deal as a capital raise rather than an insider exit.89

Anthropic reports Q3 2026 revenue of $17.0 billion, more than 12 times the prior year, and a net loss of $23.3 billion that includes a $21.9 billion non-cash charge tied to its higher valuation.1011 According to The Hill, the company lost a net $80.7 billion over the first six months of 2026. A blogger, pointing to the S-1 filing, says revenue for that period reached $16.3 billion, compared with $4.585 billion in 2025.1213

The valuation falls short of earlier hopes: Reuters, via The Verge, says the IPO was previously expected to be valued at up to $2 trillion, and Mowshowitz calls the range disappointing against expectations of $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion.1415 The Verge ties the lower expectations to midterm election results showing souring public opinion toward AI companies, while The Hill reports a drastic sell-off last Thursday.1617

Commentators draw different conclusions: the blogger at matteoraso.github.io argues the S-1 shows no AI bubble because Anthropic earns $2.08 per $1 of compute, while Artificial Lawyer's Claude warns that investor pressure may push Anthropic harder into legal products and strain its public-benefit commitments. inference1819
