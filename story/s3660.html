OpenAI disbands robotics team

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
2 sources · 172 points on Hacker News · 2 independent origins: Fortune, deredleritt3r

Fortune reports that OpenAI has disbanded its stand-alone robotics team and moved the researchers onto teams working on more general AI models.12 An OpenAI spokesperson says the company assumed early this year that building on models like Sora was the best route in robotics, but that its general-purpose frontier models, including GPT-6 Astra, have since shown they can be applied directly to robots.34 The spokesperson also says OpenAI remains committed to bringing general-purpose robots into the world, and OpenAI has said it is developing its own humanoid robot, with Sam Altman targeting a first public demonstration in 2027.56 In an Nvidia study, an Astra-powered agent reached a 74.8% success rate on a simulated manipulation benchmark, versus 51.0% reported by the best specialized robotic models on that leaderboard.78

Fortune also links the move to cost pressure: OpenAI has reportedly lost $10.9 billion in the most recent quarter, is seeking new funding and is preparing for a possible IPO in early 2027.910

In comments to Fortune, Guanya Shi of Carnegie Mellon said he reads the move as a sign that OpenAI now treats robotics as "no longer a special problem."11 Shi still sees a need for smaller specialized models that run on a robot's onboard computer without a constant network connection.12

Russ Tedrake of MIT and Walden Robotics argues that robotics companies keep an advantage because their robots gain real-world deployment experience and data, which becomes more valuable as foundation models improve.1314 Pete Florence of Generalist, meanwhile, said it was unclear that any existing model would dominate robotics in the long run.15
