OpenAI engineer arrested over recording device

Last scan Tue Oct 20, 18:59 UTC
5 sources · 656 points on Hacker News · tracked since Oct 17 · 1 new article this week · 2 independent origins: The San Francisco Standard, U.S. Department of Justice

OpenAI engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan, a 35-year-old Saudi national, was arrested on a charge of using or endeavoring to use a device to intercept oral communications, after OpenAI said it found a "sophisticated recording device" hidden in one of its offices.123 OpenAI says it used its AI models to understand the device and trace it to a specific employee, referred the matter to the FBI, and fired the employee.4567

According to the Department of Justice, the device was plugged into a TV's USB port in an executive conference room, wrapped in toilet paper, installed around September 28, and its contents were encrypted.8910 Prosecutors say Alfozan was not authorized to enter the room, but surveillance footage shows him near it on multiple dates before the discovery.11

The complaint alleges that a chatbot account tied to his spouse submitted prompts about building, installing and remotely accessing a recording device, including how to "encrypt the SD card", and later asked about the specific device found.1213 Prosecutors allege that on October 5 he appeared to look behind the television and left a bag unattended in another room, where security found his wallet, ID and a second recording device.1415

If convicted, Alfozan faces up to five years in prison and a $250,000 fine, and a detention hearing is set for October 19.161718 OpenAI says it has no indication that he improperly accessed or exfiltrated model weights, source code or customer data, and Ars Technica reports it is unclear who the recordings were intended for.192021

FBI Director Kash Patel said protecting US AI innovation is among the bureau's highest counterintelligence priorities, and US Attorney Craig Missakian called the case "a matter of national security."2223

Transformer argues the apparent ineptitude is not reassuring, since this is only the case known about, and says AI companies' startup culture is inadequate for their security needs.2425 Commentator Zack Korman, in a separate criticism, said OpenAI's insider threat detection looked poor because it had only just noticed a different employee's theft of files.2627
