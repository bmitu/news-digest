OpenAI engineer arrested over recording device

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 14:03 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 17 · 1 new article this week · single origin (Ars Technica)

On Monday, a court granted the US government's request to deny release of Abdulrahman Alfozan, a former OpenAI engineer accused of bugging a top-secret conference room to steal the frontier lab's secrets.1 Magistrate Judge Alex G. Tse agreed at a hearing that Alfozan should remain detained while mounting his defense, according to Business Insider as cited by Ars Technica.2 The filing says OpenAI hired him around July 2025 at a $325,000 base salary with a $2,200,000 equity grant in profit participation units.34

US attorney Craig Missakian argued that Alfozan spied either for personal business gain or to benefit Saudi Arabia, and said the government's evidence included WhatsApp chats with a member of the Saudi royal family.56 The government alleges the FBI found that on October 4 he accessed a folder called "Saudi Gov Docs" on his iPhone, and that the folder also held photos of a second bug he allegedly planted; the contents of the folder were not made public.789

Missakian called Alfozan a flight risk, saying he had a one-way ticket to Riyadh allegedly paid for by the Saudi government, that after his arrest his wife and son left the US on a one-way business-class flight to Doha booked and paid for by a member of the Saudi military, and that he has at least two Saudi bank accounts.101112 The US acknowledged it is possible Alfozan acted alone, but argued his conduct is criminal either way and endangers Silicon Valley innovation and US national security.1314
