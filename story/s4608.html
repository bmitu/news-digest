Nvidia board rules out reinstating Huang

Last scan Sat Nov 21, 06:54 UTC
3 sources · 3 independent origins: CNBC, TechCrunch, The Verge

Nvidia's board does not intend to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO, board chair Harvey Jones said at the start of the company's earnings call on Tuesday.12 According to Jones, Huang stepped down of his own accord on November 12, once it came to light that his private conduct conflicted with the company's standards of behavior, and every board member agreed to accept the departure.3 The statement came after Huang posted on X that he would be honored to return as CEO if the board decided it was in the best interest of shareholders, employees, the company and America.4

Before the call, CNBC reported, citing a source, that the board was focused on an investigation into Huang and was not in discussions to bring him back.5 An online petition for his return had gathered more than 180,000 signatures, including from Tim Sweeney, Elon Musk and Amjad Masad, and President Donald Trump said it was not a bad idea for him to return.67

Huang resigned following revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman accused of being a Chinese intelligence agent, and the board has opened an independent investigation alongside an SEC probe.89 Sources differ on the spy allegation: commentator Zvi Mowshowitz says ODNI and CIA sources have pushed back on the idea that Alice Huang was formally a Chinese spy, though he still puts the probability above 50%.1011

Meanwhile, Colette Kress, the chief financial officer, is interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent replacement, and Jones said both internal and external candidates are being considered.1213

Mowshowitz argues the board is right to refuse and that Washington would not allow Huang to run Nvidia, whereas OpenAI's Sam Altman declined to say whether Huang could return, saying it is hard to imagine the AI industry without him. inference141516
