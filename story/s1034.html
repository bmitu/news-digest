Eight states sue OpenAI

Last scan Mon Oct 19, 22:55 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 17 · 2 independent origins: Axios, Reuters

Eight US states — Iowa, Kansas, Nebraska, West Virginia, Arkansas, Arizona, Idaho and Montana — have separately sued OpenAI, accusing it of harming children and misleading customers about the safety of ChatGPT.123 Ars says the states argued ChatGPT harms children and "reinforces delusions, encourages dangerous behavior, and assists users in planning violence like mass shootings."4

The states allege OpenAI unlawfully put profits over safety when developing ChatGPT and violated local consumer protection laws by failing to warn customers about its dangers, citing several lawsuits filed by family members of teens and adults who allege the chatbot played a role in their loved ones' deaths.56 They claim OpenAI presented ChatGPT as a "safe, youth-appropriate tool that puts safety first" when it was allegedly "dangerous to vulnerable users," and they also accuse it of collecting data from minors and not doing enough to keep underage users off the chatbot.78

The states ask the courts to impose civil penalties and make OpenAI stop misleading the public about ChatGPT's safety, and some also seek deletion of unlawfully collected personal data.910 OpenAI spokesperson Drew Pusateri says the company has "built industry-leading age-specific protections" into ChatGPT, including parental controls and break reminders, and that it wants to work with states on AI policies that apply to the whole industry.111213

Ars Technica reports the suits came the same week the FTC reportedly sent civil investigative demands to OpenAI and Anthropic over consumer harms, according to an Axios source. inference1415
