Storm-0940 ARTEX ConnectWise breach

Last scan Sun Nov 22, 14:05 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Oct 24 · single origin (TechCrunch)

The FBI issued a flash advisory warning that hackers are using autonomous, AI-driven open source tools such as Artex to attack organizations in the U.S. and worldwide, including a widespread hack of ConnectWise customers and attacks on South Korea's financial industry.12 The FBI says Artex-linked attacks can in some cases go from targeting a system to active compromise in under three hours, and that its intrusions can be noisy but still outpace human defenders.34 Barracuda did not say how many customers it notified, and the FBI says the earlier open source version of Artex remains widely accessible even after its developer made the software closed-source.56

Earlier findings from Microsoft were backed up by the FBI, which verified that attackers exploited Artex to get into ConnectWise, and from there gained access to the Microsoft 365 cloud environments belonging to no fewer than 2,250 ConnectWise customers.7 Microsoft says the hackers are likely Chinese and stole data from affected customers, while a ConnectWise spokesperson did not respond to TechCrunch's request for comment on the FBI's claim.89

The South Korean attacks allowed theft of personal data of at least 68,000 people, which the report blames largely on weak defenses such as missing multi-factor authentication.1011

Barracuda disclosed it was also breached in an Artex-linked attack by financially motivated criminals, and because its logs were not detailed enough to rule out access, it assumed the worst and changed all customer credentials.1213

Cybersecurity writer Nicole Perlroth argues that Artex makes autonomous attacks as easy as pushing a button, extending capabilities once limited to advanced nation states or criminal groups to a much broader pool of people.1415 Perlroth also calls Artex proof of what Anthropic was trying to warn about with Mythos, and says it was frustrating to see the reaction from the administration, and from some people in the cybersecurity industry who said the warning was all marketing.1617

The Atlantic reports that none of the affected companies has said it thinks its systems went down because of an attack, and that Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton insisted the outages were coincidental and associated with maintenance and technical changes.1819
