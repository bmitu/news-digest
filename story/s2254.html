Meta AI training business terms

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
3 sources · 3 independent origins: 404 Media, Meta, Nieman Lab

Meta says it may push the implementation of its new business terms past November 20th as publishers push back over concerns about AI training.1 The terms were supposed to take effect on October 30th and initially appeared to let Meta train its AI models on news sites that use Facebook and Instagram.2

404 Media reports that the updated terms let Meta use content from domains, web pages or URLs to "provide, develop, improve, research, train, and maintain the integrity of" its products, and that Nieman Lab first reported the change.34 Meta later removed the word "train" and limited the uses to "advertising and business products," but legal experts told Nieman Lab the update still does not rule out AI training.56 404 Media similarly says the revised terms still give Meta plenty of leeway to scrape data to develop its products.7

Meta communications head Andy Stone said in a post on X that the company does "not plan to make policy changes without a full consultation with industry groups and leaders."8 Stone said that if the consultation extends beyond November 20, Meta would delay implementation, and that the existing terms remain in place until then.9

Beyond the News/Media Alliance, lawmakers from both parties have criticized the terms; Sen. Marsha Blackburn said they show Meta is "attempting to exploit American creators and businesses," while Sen. Adam Schiff urged Meta to reverse the change.1011 404 Media's Samantha Cole reports that many artists and small business owners are livid, and that users in the UK and EU can currently object to Meta's use of their data, but these objection steps aren't available to people outside those countries.1213
