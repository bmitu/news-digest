Coalition challenges Anthropic safety at IPO

Last scan Sat Nov 21, 06:54 UTC
7 sources · 288 points on Hacker News · 2 independent origins: MisAnthropic Statement coalition, The Verge

Nobel prize winners, researchers and technology executives have joined forces to openly criticize how Anthropic handles safety as it prepares to go public in the US, alleging that the company has diluted its safety pledges in a rush to develop AI that can improve itself.1 Their "MisAnthropic Statement" says Anthropic warns that recursive self-improvement could escape human control yet races to build it first, and that it uses safety branding to court investors while quietly weakening its pledges.234

Future of Life Institute chair Max Tegmark argues that Anthropic is the most guilty of all AI companies on recursive self-improvement, warning publicly while privately telling investors it is a "competitive advantage".56 The institute also points to the IPO filing's disclosure that Claude writes over 90% of its own code, and to a METR report it says found AI development sped up by nearly 2x.78 Platformer notes that Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy is not binding, and that after endorsing California's SB 53 it filed a weaker framework recasting ambitious commitments as voluntary.910

The Verge describes the IPO as the most valuable of all time, having raised $81.3 billion.11 The Verge expects that as a public company Anthropic will face more pressure to speed up releases and commercialization, which will likely make costly safety initiatives harder to justify.12

In his Nasdaq speech, Dario Amodei promised to pace training and deployment as long as needed for safety and said Anthropic would not bend to business or political pressure.1314 Early investor Dustin Moskovitz told the New York Times he invested to promote safety, not racing, and that Anthropic and other frontier labs should slow down.1516 More than 100 protesters gathered outside Anthropic's San Francisco headquarters, and four people were arrested for allegedly blocking the entrance.1718
