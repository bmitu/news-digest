Coalition challenges Anthropic safety at IPO

Last scan Thu Nov 26, 05:11 UTC
4 sources · tracked since Nov 20 · 1 new article today · single origin (The Verge)

According to the Center for AI Safety's newsletter, Anthropic carried out an IPO that broke records, bringing in over $81 billion and valuing the company at $1.5 trillion when it first listed.12 One day ahead of the IPO, the Center for AI Safety headed up a document titled the MisAnthropic Statement, which declared that Anthropic does not qualify as a safe AI firm. According to the newsletter, over 1,000 people signed it, among them Nobel winners, Turing Award holders and Fields Medal recipients.345 The statement accuses Anthropic of cautioning about AI that improves itself recursively while competing to be the first to build it, of leaning on a safety image to attract investors, and of quietly softening its safety commitments while pushing for regulation that serves its own interests.678

Dario Amodei, Anthropic's CEO, said in a Nasdaq speech that the company would pace AI training and deployment as long as necessary and would not bend to business or political pressure.910 Platformer notes, however, that Anthropic endorsed California's SB 53 but then submitted a weaker framework under it, recasting more ambitious commitments as voluntary, and that its Responsible Scaling Policy is not binding.1112 Platformer also counters that Anthropic faces business pressure to keep building the most capable systems, and cites the Financial Times' Robert Armstrong estimating it would need over $600 billion in annual revenue to return 20 percent on invested capital.1314

The Verge reports that Anthropic's Long-Term Benefit Trust looks weaker than billed: it picks four of seven board seats, but one needs founder approval, and a former employee said he doubts anyone thinks the trust has meaningful power.1516 Law professor Ann Lipton argues the real constraint on the founders and trust is that Anthropic needs money, and The Verge says public-company pressure will likely make costly safety initiatives harder to justify.1718

Early investor Dustin Moskovitz told the New York Times that he and Cari Tuna invested to promote safety, not racing, and that Anthropic and other frontier labs should slow down.1920 More than 100 protesters gathered outside Anthropic's San Francisco headquarters, and four people were arrested for allegedly blocking the entrance.2122
