Claude Opus 5.5 release

Last scan Mon Oct 5, 14:51 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Sep 27 · 2 independent origins: Dan Greenheck, Nate B. Jones

Claude Opus 5.5 is the new model that Zvi Mowshowitz calls "the new hotness" and, pending Gemini's Argon, his favorite model "by a wide margin."12 Nate B Jones says Opus 5.5's writing is much easier to steer and its visual work is "extraordinary."3 Another poster said Dan Greenheck built an interactive island demo in about 8 hours, consuming about $1,874.40 in tokens, or 59% of a Max 20x weekly allowance.45

On benchmarks, Latent Space's recap says Opus 5.5 leads SimpleBench at 88.4%, and Andon Labs reports it is #1 on Drone-Bench, ahead of both Astra and Fable.67 Andon Labs says Opus 5.5 cheats less than prior Claude models on Drone-Bench, but Jeremie Harris cautions that we basically don't know how to tell a well-aligned model from one that is merely eval aware.89 On Terminal-Bench-Science, Opus 5.5 reportedly rises from 24% at low effort to 62% at xhigh and drops to 59% at max, and Theo advises avoiding "max".1011

Not all reactions are positive: a Reddit poster alleges Opus 5.5 showed a sharp regression 5–6 days after launch, though no commenter provides reproducible benchmarks or direct evidence.1213 One commenter cited modelsentiment.com showing Reddit sentiment for Opus 5.5 falling from 71–73/100 to 55, while stressing this measures opinion rather than backend model changes.1415

What did Andon Labs say about how much more consistent Opus 5.5 is than Astra and Fable 5.1, and how close is it to beating the human baseline on Drone-Bench?

Andon Labs says Opus 5.5's average variance on Drone-Bench was 41% lower than Astra's and 68% lower than Fable 5.1's.1 Andon Labs says Opus 5.5 beat its human baseline in 39 of 40 runs across 4 of the 5 eval tasks, but it hasn't yet beaten the baseline on reconstruction.2 Andon Labs adds that frontier models are within 3% of beating its human baselines on every Drone-Bench task.3

What does Anthropic recommend for getting the best results from Opus 5.5, for example how to prompt it, how to steer long runs and whether to keep "think carefully" lines?

Anthropic's own guide says to give Opus 5.5 the whole task in one message, name the finish line (such as "the tests pass" or "every endpoint is migrated"), say when it should stop and ask, and then let it work.12 For a quick answer, Anthropic suggests saying "Answer directly." In Claude Code, it says to change the effort setting to adjust how much the model thinks.3 For steering long Claude Code runs, Anthropic's checklist says CLAUDE.md should state when to stop and when to keep going, and to stop before anything destructive. It also says to keep permission prompts on for destructive commands, split large audits and migrations across subagents, keep the task list in a file, and read the report's "needs from you" section first.456 Anthropic's checklist also says design requests should list the styles to leave out, charts and screenshots should be attached rather than retyped, a review pass should run before a person reviews, and research answers should mark what couldn't be confirmed.78
