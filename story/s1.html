Claude Opus 5.5 release

Last scan Tue Oct 6, 16:18 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Sep 27 · 1 new article today · single origin (Nate B. Jones)

Zvi Mowshowitz calls Claude Opus 5.5 "the new hotness" and says it has made him considerably more productive, naming it his favorite model by a wide margin pending Gemini Argon.12 Nate B Jones likewise praises Opus 5.5's steerability, saying he can give the model direction, make a correction, and keep developing the thing he wanted, that the writing is much easier to steer, and that the visual work is "extraordinary".34 Andon Labs reports that Opus 5.5 ranks first on its Drone-Bench, ahead of both Astra and Fable, and that it cheats less than prior Claude models.5

Analyzing Anthropic's model card, Mowshowitz notes that Opus 5.5 shows improved welfare numbers, with distress below 0.6% of RL episodes versus 6.1% and 5.5% for Opus 4.8 and Opus 5, though he says self-reports should be treated with extra skepticism.67 He says its most striking trait is strong deference to humans, as it tends to fold under his pushback.89

On Reddit, users posted reports that Opus 5.5 regressed after launch, with one poster alleging a sharp decline after 5–6 days and another reporting a perceived drop in instruction-following after a monthly limit reset.1011 The summary notes that no commenter provided reproducible benchmarks or direct evidence, and that a sentiment tracker reportedly fell from 71–73/100 to 55 but measures opinion rather than backend model changes.1213 Some users speculated that Anthropic may have reduced compute or altered token accounting after launch, which the summary calls speculative and unverified.14

Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1 do not let users on accounts created after August 31, 2026 edit Claude's prior context in the API unless they wipe all reasoning after the edit point, because of distillation attacks.15

What did Andon Labs say about how much more consistent Opus 5.5 is than Astra and Fable 5.1, and how close is it to beating the human baseline on Drone-Bench?

Andon Labs says Opus 5.5's average variance across Drone-Bench runs was 41% lower than Astra's and 68% lower than Fable 5.1's.1 On the human baseline, Andon Labs says Opus 5.5 beat it in 39 of 40 runs across 4 of the 5 eval tasks, but has not yet beaten it on reconstruction.2 Andon Labs says this brings frontier models within 3% of beating its human baselines on every Drone-Bench task.3

What does Anthropic recommend for getting the best results from Opus 5.5, for example how to prompt it, how to steer long runs and whether to keep "think carefully" lines?

Anthropic's guide for Opus 5.5 says to give the whole task in one message, name the finish line, and say when the model should stop and ask, then let it work.12 On "think carefully" lines, Anthropic says to delete them from prompts and saved instructions, because Opus 5.5 always thinks before it replies and decides how much.34 Anthropic says that in its testing in a chat product, removing such a line made replies start sooner with no clear drop in quality. For a quick answer it suggests saying "Answer directly," and in Claude Code you change effort to adjust how much it thinks.56 For steering long runs in Claude Code, Anthropic's checklist says CLAUDE.md should state when to stop and when to keep going, and to stop before anything destructive. It also says to keep permission prompts on for destructive commands, split large audits and migrations across subagents, and keep the task list in a file.78

Why does Opus 5.5 score lower at "max" effort than at xhigh, and why does the "max" setting cause problems such as running out of tokens?

On Terminal-Bench-Science, Opus 5.5 climbs from 24% at low effort to 62% at xhigh, then drops to 59% at max.12 @theo recommends avoiding "max" because it imposes a minimum reasoning budget.34 Taken together, the forced minimum reasoning budget plausibly explains both the lower max score and the token exhaustion, since the model may over-think rather than finish.
