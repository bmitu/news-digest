Claude Opus 5.5 release

3 sources · tracked since Sep 27 · 1 new article today · 2 independent origins: Dan Greenheck, Nate B. Jones

Anthropic introduced Claude Opus 5.5, the first model in its new Claude 5.5 family, which it says performs at the level of Claude Fable 5.1 on most work and costs 40% less to run than Opus 5.1 Zvi Mowshowitz says the pitch is conservative, that feedback is almost universally positive, and that the model should raise users' ambitions.234

On Terminal-Bench-Science, Artificial Analysis reports GPT-6 Astra at 63% and Opus 5.5 on xhigh at 62%, with no other model above 50%.56 Latent Space's recap says Opus 5.5 rises from 24% at low effort to 62% at xhigh and drops to 59% at max, and that it leads SimpleBench at 88.4%.78 Zvi Mowshowitz reports Opus 5.5 jumping to 18.5% on ProgramBench versus 5.5% for Astra and 7% for Fable, and winning Omniscience 46 to 43 because it guesses less.910 Andon Labs says Opus 5.5 ranks first on Drone-Bench and cheats less than prior Claude models, though Jeremie Harris cautions that it is hard to tell good alignment from evaluation awareness.1112

Some users report Opus 5.5 struggles with complex situations and non-obvious intent, and Jef Allbright says it processed a task less deeply than Fable 5.1 did.1314 Zvi Mowshowitz says it seems unprincipled that Opus 5.5 is available with zero data retention given it is at least as capable as Fable 5.1 and clearly more capable on cyber tasks, and that he does not buy Anthropic's technical explanation: either Fable 5.1 can have ZDR, or Opus 5.5 can't.1516

Users praise its ease of use and speed, and Nate B Jones says its writing is easier to steer and its visual work is extraordinary.1718

What does Opus 5.5 cost per million input and output tokens, and how much does fast mode add?

Anthropic priced Claude Opus 5.5 at $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, which Zvi Mowshowitz describes as 20% lower than Opus 5.1 Fast mode is offered at $8 input and $40 output per million tokens, with up to 2.5x the speed.2 That means fast mode adds $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, doubling the standard rate. inference34

What effort level should I start with on Opus 5.5, and how does it differ from Opus 5's default?

Anthropic recommends starting Opus 5.5 at medium effort, which is its default, and setting it explicitly rather than relying on the default.12 That differs from Opus 5, which defaults to high effort.3 Anthropic says its testing found Opus 5.5 at medium matches or exceeds Opus 5 at high on coding and knowledge-work evaluations, and that low comes close on several coding evaluations at much lower cost.45 Anthropic also warns that carrying over an Opus 5 effort setting means longer turns and more output tokens, because Opus 5.5 tends to think more per turn at a given level, especially at xhigh and max.67 On Terminal-Bench-Science, Opus 5.5 scored 62% at xhigh and 59% at max, and commentator Theo recommends avoiding max because it imposes a minimum reasoning budget.8910

How fast is Opus 5.5 compared with Opus 5, and does it use fewer tokens for the same task?

Anthropic says Claude Opus 5.5 generates output tokens more than 30 percent faster than Opus 5 and tends to finish the same task with fewer tokens.1 Box CEO Aaron Levie reported, from Box's testing of Opus 5.5 on enterprise knowledge work tasks, 63% fewer tokens used, 42% less verbosity and 30% faster versus Opus 5.2 Users describe it as quick too: one reviewer called it "wicked fast", and another said it is a much faster, better writer than Opus 5.34 Anthropic also says Opus 5.5 defaults to medium effort, where it matches or exceeds Opus 5 at high on coding and knowledge-work evaluations.5 However, Anthropic cautions that at the same effort level Opus 5.5 tends to think more per turn than Opus 5, especially at xhigh and max, so keeping an old effort setting means longer turns and more output tokens.678

What can Opus 5.5 do on its own in Claude Code, such as making explainer videos, and how much did those runs cost?

In one test, a user reported a true one-shot autonomous Claude Code generation using Opus 5.5 of a 30s–60s pure-JavaScript whimsical hand-drawn collage animation on "what is the purpose of life?", including script, assets, animation, concept and TTS.1 That run took about 1h20m and cost roughly $20 of Opus usage, or about 10% of a Max 5-hour quota, plus $3.21 in OpenRouter API fees.2 A second user reproduced the approach for Friendr.nl, reportedly getting a script, collage-style assets, voice-over, music and sound effects, and an MP4 animation synced to the narration in about 1.5–2 hours for about $4, with another model used for self-review.3 Another post claims Opus 5.5 made an SNES-style combat video entirely from code, including character assets, animation, fight sequencing and music, with no user-provided assets.45 A browser-based interactive island demo, built by Dan Greenheck in roughly 8 hours with Opus 5.5, reportedly consumed about $1,874.40 in tokens, or 59% of a Max 20x weekly allowance.6 Zvi Mowshowitz says the Opus 5.5-generated videos are for the first time on the border of being actually worth watching.7
