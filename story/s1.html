Claude Opus 5.5 release

4 sources · tracked since Sep 27 · 1 new article today · 3 independent origins: Benjamin Breen, Dan Greenheck, Nate B. Jones

Anthropic introduced Claude Opus 5.5, the first model in its new Claude 5.5 family, which it says performs at the level of Claude Fable 5.1 on most work and costs 40% less to run than Opus 5.1 Zvi Mowshowitz reports that Opus 5.5 was released on Tuesday and that, by all reports, it is an excellent model.23

Artificial Analysis's Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 had GPT-6 Astra at 63% and Opus 5.5 on xhigh at 62%, with no other model breaking 50%, according to Mowshowitz.45 Latent Space's recap says Opus 5.5 leads SimpleBench at 88.4%, and that its Terminal-Bench-Science score rises from 24% at low effort to 62% at xhigh before dropping to 59% at max.678 Mowshowitz says ProgramBench was a particularly impressive jump, with Astra at 5.5%, Fable at 7% and Opus 5.5 at 18.5%.9

Benchmarks are not uniformly favorable: on WeirdML v3 Astra leads at 42.2% against 31.2% for Opus 5.5, and Fable 5.1 takes all seven Vals professionals rows.1011 Mowshowitz also criticizes Anthropic's decision to offer zero data retention for Opus 5.5, saying that since it is at least as capable as Fable 5.1 and clearly more capable on cyber tasks, the decision seems unprincipled, and that Anthropic has a technical explanation but he does not buy it.1213

Some users report weaknesses: Jef Allbright found it processed a nested-meaning writing task significantly less deeply than Fable 5.1, which completed it immediately.1415 Latent Space's recap says many people claim the $200 Claude Code plan now beats Codex, while Astra remains the preferred review and audit model.1617

Which effort level should I use with Opus 5.5, and how does medium compare to Opus 5 at high?

Anthropic advises starting Opus 5.5 at medium effort, which is its default, setting it explicitly and testing several levels against your own evals.1 Anthropic says its testing shows Opus 5.5 at medium matches or exceeds Opus 5 at high on coding and knowledge-work evaluations.2 Zvi Mowshowitz, citing Artificial Analysis, says medium, high and xhigh all sit on the intelligence-cost frontier, and that you can run the model cheaper and often should.345 Higher settings cost more: Anthropic says Opus 5.5 tends to think more per turn than Opus 5, especially at xhigh and max, so carrying over an old effort setting means longer turns and more output tokens.6 On Terminal-Bench-Science, Opus 5.5 scored 62% at xhigh but 59% at max, and Theo recommends avoiding max because it imposes a minimum reasoning budget.789

How does Opus 5.5's vision ability compare with Fable 5 and GPT-6 Astra?

Opus 5.5 is Anthropic's best vision model to date, according to @skalskip92's vision evals, which put it ahead of Fable 5 and GPT-6 Sol but behind GPT-6 Astra.12 On vision-related benchmarks, Opus 5.5 is roughly level with Astra on BenchCAD Vision2Code with tools (96.2% vs. 95.9%) and slightly ahead on Furniture Assembly (83 vs. 80).34 It also scores well ahead of Fable on Chartography, 64.4 to 44.8.5 The sources disagree on Opus 5.5 versus Astra: the vision ranking puts Astra ahead, while the benchmark scores shown are near parity or slightly favor Opus 5.5. inference64

Why does Opus 5.5 win on the Omniscience benchmark even though it gives slightly fewer right answers?

Zvi Mowshowitz, reporting on Artificial Analysis, says Opus 5.5 wins on Omniscience with 46 versus 43 for both Fable 5.1 and Astra.12 Zvi Mowshowitz explains that it wins despite slightly fewer right answers because it guesses, or hallucinates, less.32 He adds that it is also more willing to admit when it doesn't know, which suggests the benchmark rewards declining to answer over giving a wrong answer. inference45
