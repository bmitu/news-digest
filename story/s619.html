Aleph Alpha Kolibri LLM release

Last scan Tue Oct 6, 16:18 UTC
2 sources · 420 points on Hacker News · tracked since Oct 4 · 1 new article today · 2 independent origins: Jenia Jitsev, Tejas

Aleph Alpha released Kolibri, an open-weight German and English large language model, on 3 October 2026 under the Apache 2.0 license, according to a tej.as write-up.12 It is a mixture-of-experts model with 78 billion parameters that uses about 3.5 billion per token, and it was trained from scratch on infrastructure in Germany and Finland.34 Latent Space lists it as 78B total and 3.46B active parameters.5

Aleph Alpha calls Kolibri sovereign, saying it was built and trained in Germany and Finland under European and German law with no foreign control, and that customers get full freedom of deployment and intellectual-property safety.67 The tej.as author notes the model card says some non-European models were used in data preparation: Gemma 4 and Mistral-NeMo rephrased text, and Qwen3-32B labeled data for quality filters.8

In Aleph Alpha's own evaluation, Kolibri scores above every compared model of its size in both languages, and its report says its tokenizer needs 11.2% fewer tokens for German text than GPT-5's tokenizer.910 On Artificial Analysis's Omniscience test, when Kolibri didn't know an answer, it said so (or gave a partial answer) 44% of the time, versus 11.1% for Qwen3.5 35B-A3B and 23.7% for GPT-OSS 120B; of the mixture-of-experts models Aleph Alpha compared, only Qwen3.6 35B-A3B did better, at 56.7%.111213 Latent Space reports self-reported scores of 96.9% on AIME 2025, 84.3% on GPQA Diamond and 66.4% on SWE-Bench Verified.14

Latent Space notes that the dataset is unreleased and that, per Jitsev, agentic evaluations sit well below Qwen.15 The tej.as author likewise says Kolibri is the wrong pick for a coding agent, where Qwen3.6 35B-A3B leads, for answering from memory, for languages besides German and English, and for anyone who cannot spare 78 GB of GPU memory.16

How much context can Kolibri handle, and how long a document has Aleph Alpha tested it on?

Kolibri handles 262,144 tokens of context natively, and Aleph Alpha has tested it up to 1,048,576 tokens, about one million.1 However, it was not reported what kind of document was used in the million-token tests, or how many pages that length equals.

What does the Merlin-Arthur training method do to make Kolibri say "I don't know"?

Arthur gets a question with parts of the supporting document hidden. When Merlin hides parts so the correct answer is easier to find, Arthur is trained to answer.1 When Morgana hides the evidence the answer depends on, Arthur is trained to say he doesn't know.2 On Artificial Analysis's Omniscience test, Kolibri said it didn't know (or gave a partial answer) 44% of the time, versus 11.1% for Qwen3.5 35B-A3B and 23.7% for GPT-OSS 120B.3

How much does Kolibri's UniBPE tokenizer actually save on German text, and how does it differ from standard byte-pair encoding?

Aleph Alpha's report says Kolibri's tokenizer needs 11.2% fewer tokens for German text than GPT-5's tokenizer, the best result among the 9 others it measured.1 The same test showed Qwen3.5 35B-A3B, Mistral Small 4 and Gemma 4 needing about 22% to 25% more German tokens than Kolibri. inference2 As an example, GPT-5's tokenizer splits "Bundesverfassungsgericht" into 6 pieces, while Kolibri's uses 2.3 UniBPE differs from standard byte-pair encoding in how it chooses merges. It keeps BPE's bottom-up merging but scores each merge with the Unigram objective, which Aleph Alpha says respects how German builds words. The tokenizer has 128,000 tokens.456

Can users control how long Kolibri thinks before it answers?

Yes. Aleph Alpha says every Kolibri request can set reasoning_effort to none, low, medium or high.1 That means a quick lookup gets an immediate answer, while a hard question gets a long think, and both run on the same model and the same server.23
