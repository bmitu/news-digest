DeepMind formally verifies Signal

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
5 sources · 2 independent origins: Google DeepMind, Wired

DeepMind describes it as the first time AI has formally verified a widely deployed cryptographic protocol at this depth, and the first time such a protocol's implementation has been formally verified by any method.12

According to DeepMind, agents powered by Gemini Carbon generated over six million lines of Lean proofs, and only about one percent of the codebase was flagged for human review.3456 The verification took four weeks, which DeepMind contrasts with tasks that historically take expert teams years; one summary says a five-person team did it, versus twenty person-years for the seL4 microkernel.78

To test the system, researchers reintroduced two previously fixed real bugs, and in both cases it produced a machine-checked proof that the buggy version was insecure.910 However, the analysis found no new vulnerabilities in the portions of the protocol it examined, and it did not cover the entire Signal mobile app.1112

Wired, via Ars Technica, says it is unclear how practical AI-aided verification will be at scale; Google did the work free as research, declined to share its cost, and declined to say whether it plans a commercial product.1314 DeepMind researcher Swarat Chaudhuri cautions that there is a long way to go before scaling this to all software, and Wired notes the team got a head start from earlier Signal Foundation verification work.1516

Ehren Kret, Signal's chief technology officer, says the work expands on prior verification of smaller code portions and gives Signal greater confidence in its privacy guarantees.17181920 Pushmeet Kohli, a Google executive, suggests banks and other companies could one day use such verification to prove properties of their software, and DeepMind says it is open-sourcing the verification artifacts.212223
