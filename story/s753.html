Goodfire agent monitors

Last scan Sat Oct 10, 12:51 UTC
2 sources · 2 independent origins: Goodfire, TechCrunch

Goodfire, an interpretability startup, launched monitors on Thursday that watch what happens inside an AI model as it works, rather than only reading its output, as a cheaper alternative to having a second AI review an agent, TechCrunch reports.1 The monitors are available to customers of Baseten, and Baseten customers choose which risks to monitor and whether the response is logging, human review or refusal.23 Small probes read the model's internal signals at every step, and only when one flags something does a separate AI model take a closer look.45 Goodfire CEO Eric Ho says the monitors are cheap because they reuse computations the model has already made in its forward pass.6

In Goodfire's tests on Kimi K3, monitoring about 1 million exchanges would cost roughly $185, against $5420 for a cheaper AI model checking every step and about $200,000 for a top-tier one.7 The probes reportedly caught 93% of malicious hacking sessions and sent 5.5% of harmless ones for a second look, and Goodfire says running four probes at once added less than 2% to the time before the model starts responding.89 A separate newsletter summary says Goodfire released probe-based cyber monitors for Kimi K3 and GLM 5.3, claims they are 50x faster and cheaper than an LLM judge, and says FAR.AI red-teaming found they greatly reduce universal jailbreaks.10

Goodfire CTO Dan Balsam says the pitch targets open models, which can have safeguards stripped and lack the monitoring closed labs run, and that guardrails will be needed at inference time.1112 TechCrunch notes Goodfire is not the first to try this approach, since Google DeepMind said in January that its research informed misuse-detection probes in Gemini.13
