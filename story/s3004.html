OpenAI Grid Protectors Program

Last scan Sat Nov 7, 03:59 UTC
2 sources · single origin (OpenAI)

OpenAI announced a "Grid Protectors Program," which it describes as a national effort in which it will work with utilities, regulators and policymakers to develop arrangements for its future data center campuses to reduce electricity demand when the grid is under strain.12 OpenAI says it will pilot the program at its planned Effingham County, Georgia campus, where its agreement with Georgia Power includes up to 1,000 megawatts of flexible demand response.345 OpenAI says that 1,000 megawatts equals the average demand of roughly 700,000 households, while The Verge describes it as enough to power air conditioners in around 700,000 homes.67

OpenAI says it has committed to pay the full infrastructure and electric-service costs for the Effingham campus, and that Georgia Power says the flexibility can reduce the new generation it needs to build.89 Georgia Power projects about $950 million in annual customer savings beginning in 2029 from its large-load agreements and expected growth, including Project Camellia, and estimates a benefit of at least $15 a month for a typical residential customer.1011 OpenAI argues that its year-round demand can spread the grid's fixed costs across more electricity sales, and it acknowledges that flexibility cannot replace investment in new energy supply and transmission.121314

The Verge frames the announcement as coming amid spreading US opposition to data centers, citing Utah residents' protests over a Box Elder County project and state laws meant to stop AI companies from passing energy costs to residents.1516 The Verge also notes that OpenAI has switched to calling its data centers "compute campuses" in an effort to separate from their poor public reputation, and it cites a study by the Houston Advanced Research Center saying that powering proposed Texas projects could undo decades of air quality gains.1718 Separately, OpenAI announced commitments to communities hosting its data center projects, including not using non-disclosure agreements with public officials, after midterms in which The Verge says voters voiced opposition to data center expansion.1920
