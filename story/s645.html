OpenAI ChatGPT visual ads

Last scan Mon Oct 5, 14:51 UTC
2 sources · single origin (OpenAI)

OpenAI announced on Monday a new visual ad format for ChatGPT, which it will begin testing in the US later this month with an initial group of advertisers.12 The ads will initially appear during image generation, and OpenAI says they will be clearly labeled, stay separate from the generated image and not influence ChatGPT's answers.34 The Verge reports that ChatGPT's ads, first introduced in February, until now showed only a company's name, logo and a link in a sponsored section beneath the chat, and that the ads won't appear for Plus, Pro or Enterprise subscribers.567

OpenAI says ChatGPT reaches 1.2 billion people each week and that advertising lets it serve a much larger market than subscriptions alone.89

OpenAI also expanded its measurement partnerships, adding integrations with Hightouch, Tealium and LiveRamp and attribution partners such as AppsFlyer and Adjust, while saying its incrementality work is still in early stages.1011 OpenAI cites early partner findings as showing strong performance: according to DV Rockerbox, WeightWatchers' cost per acquisition was 15.3% lower than its paid-search benchmark, and according to Triple Whale, 93% of Portland Leather's ChatGPT Ads visitors were new.1213

OpenAI says its placement guardrails assess whether a conversation is appropriate for ads, aiming to keep them out of emotionally vulnerable or sensitive contexts, and it is developing brand suitability pilots with DoubleVerify and Integral Ad Science.1415 Integral Ad Science CEO Lidiane Jones says the independent evaluation will assess how OpenAI's safeguards perform, while DoubleVerify CEO Mark Zagorski calls ChatGPT a fundamentally different advertising environment.1617

How will DoubleVerify and Integral Ad Science check OpenAI's brand safety without seeing users' private conversations?

OpenAI says it is developing brand suitability evaluation pilots with DoubleVerify and Integral Ad Science, in which the independent partners assess how its brand safety standards are applied in a controlled testing environment rather than in real user chats.12 OpenAI says the aim is to give advertisers more transparency into how its safeguards work while protecting conversation privacy.3 Integral Ad Science CEO Lidiane Jones says its independent evaluation will assess how OpenAI's safeguards perform across a broad and evolving range of scenarios, while preserving the privacy of real user conversations.4 DoubleVerify CEO Mark Zagorski says the goal is to bring independent measurement to this environment while respecting user privacy.5 Neither company's exact testing method, such as what test scenarios or data they will use, was detailed.

How is the new image ad different from the earlier ChatGPT ads that showed only a company name, logo and link below the chat?

The earlier ChatGPT ads, which debuted in February, showed only a company's name, logo and a link to its product in a "sponsored" section beneath the chat.1 The new format instead puts images of sponsored products and services on screen, and OpenAI says it will initially test it during image generation in ChatGPT.23 OpenAI says the images are meant to show product inspiration, product usage or the experiences a product makes possible, giving advertisers a way to tell their story through images.4 Some things stay the same: the ads will be clearly labeled, kept separate from the image being created, and OpenAI says they do not influence ChatGPT's answers.5 Testing begins later this month in the US with an initial group of advertisers.6

What are Negative Phrases, and who can use them to keep ads away from certain conversations?

Negative Phrases are a tool OpenAI describes for "narrow placement requirements specific to a brand's own policies," meaning advertisers can use them to keep their ads away from particular conversations. inference1 They are available to qualifying advertisers, so not every advertiser can use them.2 The announcement does not explain how Negative Phrases work in detail, such as how phrases are entered or matched, or what the qualifying criteria are.
