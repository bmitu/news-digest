OpenAI ChatGPT visual ads

Last scan Sat Oct 10, 12:51 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 5 · 1 new article this week · single origin (OpenAI)

OpenAI announced a new visual ad format in ChatGPT, along with expanded measurement tools and partnerships and new work on brand suitability.1 OpenAI says it will first test the format during image generation in ChatGPT, starting later this month in the US with an initial group of advertisers.234 OpenAI says the ads will be clearly labeled, will remain separate from the image being created, and will not influence ChatGPT's answers.567 OpenAI says ChatGPT reaches 1.2 billion people each week, and TechCrunch reports the ads could eventually reach those weekly users once they roll out globally.89

OpenAI says it now supports attribution partners including AppsFlyer, Triple Whale, Adjust and Kochava (among others), and that integrations with Hightouch, Tealium and LiveRamp make it easier for advertisers to send conversion data to ChatGPT Ads.101112 OpenAI cites early partner findings: DV Rockerbox said WeightWatchers' attributed cost per acquisition was 15.3% lower than its blended paid-search benchmark, WorkMagic reported that 67% of incremental purchases for Dose came from net-new customers, and Triple Whale said 93% of Portland Leather's visitors from the ads were new.131415 OpenAI describes its incrementality work as still in its early stages, with Haus, Measured and WorkMagic exploring geo-based experiments.1617

OpenAI says it is developing brand suitability evaluation pilots with DoubleVerify and Integral Ad Science, in which the partners assess its brand safety standards in a controlled environment without accessing private user conversations.1819 Integral Ad Science CEO Lidiane Jones says the independent evaluation will assess how OpenAI's safeguards perform. DoubleVerify CEO Mark Zagorski calls ChatGPT a "fundamentally different advertising environment" and describes it as an emerging and promising channel.202122

TechCrunch notes the ads could disrupt the user experience, but suggests that may be partly the point, since they could push casual users to pay for a subscription.2324
