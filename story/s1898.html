1Password for Agents

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 14:03 UTC
2 sources · 2 independent origins: 1Password, Graham Neubig

Password manager 1Password launched 1Password for Agents on Thursday, a tool that lets users allow AI agents to log into their personal accounts on their behalf without the agent having access to the user's credentials, according to TechCrunch.1 TechCrunch describes the process: the agent sends a unique digital key to 1Password to confirm its identity, the user gets an alert and can confirm the request, and 1Password then fills in the credentials to sign the agent in.234 Users can choose among agents including Anthropic's Claude Duet, OpenAI's Dots or ChatGPT Work, Grok Bot, Meta's Muse, and agents from the startup Town, and the company says it is working with other companies to expand the service.56

1Password argues in a blog post that handing over a password to an agent is a common shortcut that can leave sensitive information in places people can no longer control, and says its product lets people delegate tasks while keeping control of their credentials.78 A Latent Space newsletter roundup also listed the product among agent permissioning products, saying it autofills credentials without exposing them to the agent.910

TechCrunch notes the launch came the same day Axios reported that U.S. banks have begun to detect agentic activity inside user accounts.11 According to TechCrunch's account of the Axios report, the big issue is that most banks cannot tell whether the activity is tied to user requests, bankers called the situation "horrifying," and a Chase spokesperson said customers "should not give their usernames and passwords to anyone."1213
