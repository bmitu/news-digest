China doubles DRAM production

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 14:03 UTC
2 sources · single origin, unclear which

A Reddit post summarized in Latent Space's AINews asks when consumers will see price effects from China reportedly doubling RAM/DRAM production, likely via domestic suppliers such as CXMT, in response to rising memory demand.12 The summary frames this against a wider memory crunch, in which Samsung has allegedly pre-sold about 70% of next year's general-purpose DRAM output and 100% of its HBM capacity.3 That crunch is expected to push HBM prices up 150–200% and DRAM prices up by double digits in Q1 2027.4

A commenter cited CXMT's target of 600,000 wafers per month by 2028 as the relevant timescale for the expansion.5 The same commenter noted parallel expansions elsewhere, including U.S. fabs, Samsung adding 4th and 5th facilities, and SK Hynix investing roughly 35 trillion won in its Y2 plant plus 19 trillion won in its F17 fab.67

Top technical commenters argue the effect will be gradual, since new DRAM capacity ramps over quarters or years and may be absorbed by latent demand from edge compute and datacenters.8 They suggest the main near-term effect may be slower price increases rather than a visible consumer price drop.9 Some commenters think Chinese output will mostly serve domestic demand, reducing China's imports rather than flooding export markets.10

Another commenter argues manufacturers will not abandon high-margin enterprise and datacenter sales for low-margin consumer DRAM, so gadget prices may stay elevated.11 Commenters also described memory manufacturing as cyclical and capital-intensive, where overbuilding can turn record profits into bankruptcy.12
