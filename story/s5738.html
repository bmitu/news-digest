Tidal bans AI-generated music

Last scan Thu Nov 26, 05:11 UTC
5 sources · 336 points on Hacker News · 2 independent origins: The Rundown AI, Tidal

Tidal announced that it will ban fully AI-generated music from its platform starting December 9.1234 Tidal music lead Robert Andersen says the company will also reject roughly 80,000 to 85,000 tracks per day, and distributors will be barred from delivering 100% AI-generated tracks.567 Songs made with the help of AI tools remain allowed as long as AI is not the sole output.89 Variety describes Tidal as the first streaming platform to ban AI music entirely, while TechCrunch says it is one of the first major platforms after Bandcamp to do so. inference1011

Tidal says 43 percent of tracks delivered to it over the past 90 days were detected as AI-generated, and that 96 percent of AI-generated songs get zero plays.12131415 Tidal argues that fully AI music can mislead listeners and divert streams and royalties from artists, and Ars Technica reports it has already removed about 66,000 tracks for impersonating artists.1617

The ban follows a July policy, per Ars Technica, of labeling and demonetizing fully AI tracks, which Tidal says was not enough; Variety says the earlier policy came nearly five months ago while TechCrunch says demonetizing began in June. inference181920 Sources differ on how many existing tracks will be deleted: The Verge, TechCrunch and Variety cite about 29 million, while Ars Technica and TechCrunch's later passage cite 27 million, or 6.3 percent of the catalog. inference212223

Ars Technica doubts detection will be airtight, noting Tidal hasn't said how it detects AI music, while The Verge reports it will use the Ircam Amplify detection tool and metadata checks. inference2425 Andersen says he expects the move to resonate with artists and listeners and urges peers to follow, whereas rivals such as Spotify, YouTube and Apple Music have so far opted for labels. inference26272829
