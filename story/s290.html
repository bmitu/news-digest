Anthropic's IPO prospectus shows AI vision, surging costs

Last scan Mon Oct 5, 14:51 UTC
5 sources · 143 points on Hacker News · tracked since Sep 29 · 3 independent origins: Anthropic, Financial Times, Reuters

Anthropic has formally warned investors that its technology may pose "existential risks to humanity" in a long-awaited initial public offering prospectus circulated with a small group of partners in recent days, according to the Financial Times.1 The Financial Times reports the filing devotes almost a third of its length to risk factors, including the potential for advanced AI models to manipulate, blackmail and behave unpredictably, and notes that close to a quarter of last year's revenue came from just two clients, according to people familiar with the filing.23 The FT says the extraordinary risk warnings add to the challenge of valuing the company, and that concerns about AI risk threaten to dim backers' optimism after current and former Anthropic staff warned that runaway AI could end humanity within a decade.45

According to the Financial Times, Anthropic recorded an operating loss of more than $8 billion last year, as revenue jumped 12-fold to almost $4.6 billion while operating expenses reached almost $13 billion.678 Second-quarter revenue was $11.5 billion, and the company is on course for a second consecutive quarter of operating profit on an adjusted basis, the FT says.9 Anthropic said it planned to spend $518 billion on cloud, computing and infrastructure obligations in the coming years, and Zvi Mowshowitz describes these as commitments over 7-10 years. inference1011

Sources differ on the scale of the business: Latent Space says annualized revenue is reportedly $65B+, while Zvi Mowshowitz says ARR is rising above $100 billion. inference1213 Valuation figures also vary: the FT calls the company a nearly $1 trillion start-up whose backers are confident it can list above $2 trillion, while Latent Space says the filing is at a potential valuation above $2T. inference141516 Zvi Mowshowitz says the cofounders are seeking 50.1% voting control after the IPO; he calls the structure appropriate and thinks it makes the company more valuable.1718
