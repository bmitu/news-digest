Anthropic's IPO prospectus shows AI vision, surging costs

4 sources · 139 points on Hacker News · tracked since Sep 29 · 3 independent origins: Anthropic, Financial Times, Reuters

Anthropic has formally warned investors that its technology may pose "existential risks to humanity" in a long-awaited IPO prospectus circulated with a small group of partners, according to the Financial Times.1 Anthropic devoted almost a third of its S-1 to risk factors, including the potential for advanced AI models to manipulate, blackmail, and exhibit other unpredictable behaviors. Separately, according to people familiar with the filing, close to a quarter of last year's revenue came from just two clients.23 The FT says the risk warnings add to the challenge for investors valuing the company, and that public warnings from current and former staff that runaway AI could end humanity within a decade threaten to dim optimism.45

Last year's revenue jumped 12-fold to almost $4.6 billion, but operating expenses rose to almost $13 billion, producing an operating loss of more than $8 billion.678 Second-quarter revenue this year was $11.5 billion, and the company is on course for a second consecutive quarter of operating profit on an adjusted basis, the FT reports.910 Anthropic said it planned to spend $518 billion on cloud, computing and infrastructure obligations in the coming years.1112

The FT says Anthropic's backers are confident it can list above $2 trillion, more than double its May funding round, while the FT also describes it as a nearly $1 trillion start-up.1314 Latent Space's newsletter reports the filing at a potential valuation above $2 trillion, with ARR reportedly $65 billion or more.1516

Why did Sam Altman say OpenAI won't go public yet, and what did he mean by 'pacing the frontier'?

Sam Altman says OpenAI won't go public until it can make better promises about model safety, and no firm timeline has been set.12 Altman said that as models have surged in capability, "we have got to be able to make confident safety claims."3 Altman expressed concerns that going public could create the potential to "disappoint Wall Street supporters in the names of safety or whatever else," and that doing it during a "shift to very capable models and a new kind of safety requirement seems ill-advised."456 Altman said OpenAI would not "barrel all guns blazing towards an IPO" and would instead prioritize safety so it can scale to the next stage of AI without people debating what percentage chance it will do bad things.789 Altman also said, "I think it's also kind of bad for the world if OpenAI waits too long to go public."1011 On "pacing the frontier," Altman said, "Pacing to us means that we push safety and alignment ahead of capabilities," though he avoided calling it an outright slowdown.121314 In a social media post this month, Altman also said that "pacing" does not mean "stopping," and that progress will continue but should be slower.1516
