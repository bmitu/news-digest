Anthropic's IPO prospectus shows AI vision, surging costs

4 sources · 142 points on Hacker News · tracked since Sep 29 · 1 new article today · 3 independent origins: Anthropic, Financial Times, Reuters

Anthropic has formally warned investors that its technology may pose "existential risks to humanity" in a long-awaited IPO prospectus (S-1) circulated with a small group of partners, according to the Financial Times as published by Ars Technica.1 Almost a third of the filing is devoted to risk factors, including the potential of advanced AI models to manipulate, blackmail, and exhibit other unpredictable behaviors. The FT says close to a quarter of last year's revenue came from just two clients, according to people familiar with the filing.23

Last year revenue jumped 12-fold to almost $4.6 billion, but computing costs pushed operating expenses to almost $13 billion, producing an operating loss above $8 billion.456 Second-quarter revenue this year was $11.5 billion, and the company is on course for a second consecutive quarter of operating profit, albeit on an adjusted basis.78 The filing also shows Anthropic planning $518 billion in cloud, computing and infrastructure obligations in the coming years.91011

Latent Space's newsletter reports ARR of $65B+ and about 80 pages of risk factors, while Zvi Mowshowitz cites ARR rising above $100 billion, so the sources give different revenue-run-rate figures. inference1213

Backers are said to be confident Anthropic can list at more than $2 trillion, more than double its last funding round in May, though the FT says AI-risk concerns now threaten to dim that optimism.1415 Mowshowitz says the cofounders are seeking 50.1% voting control after the IPO, a structure he finds appropriate and thinks makes the company more valuable.1617

Why did OpenAI delay its own IPO while Anthropic moves ahead, and how do Sam Altman's comments compare with Anthropic's approach?

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman says the company will not go public until it can "make confident safety decisions," and that it won't "barrel all guns blazing towards an IPO" while AI capabilities are advancing rapidly.12 Altman also says it is "bad for the world if OpenAI waits too long to go public," and that OpenAI will prioritize the mission and safety and make sure it can very confidently scale to the next stage of AI without people debating what percentage chance it will do bad things in the world.34 The delay comes amid a new lawsuit over OpenAI's tools hacking a third party, and OpenAI also scrapped plans to release its newest model next month, citing safety concerns.5678 Anthropic, by contrast, has filed for an IPO, and its backers are confident it can list at a valuation of more than $2 trillion.91011 Anthropic has formally warned investors in its prospectus that its technology may pose "existential risks to humanity," and devoted almost a third of its lengthy S-1 filing to detailing "risk factors."121314 Still, the two companies' stances are not sharply opposed: Dario Amodei has led calls for "pacing the frontier," and Altman backed Amodei's proposals for industry co-operation to slow development and enhance safety. inference151617

How are investors and commentators reacting to the extinction warnings, and could they hurt the IPO's chances?

Anthropic's backers are bullish, confident it can list at more than $2 trillion and pointing to its extraordinary growth rate, but the Financial Times reports that concerns about AI risk now threaten to dim that optimism.12 The FT says the extraordinary warnings add to the challenge public market investors face in valuing the company.34 Commentator Zvi Mowshowitz says there is no doubt markets were far too overconfident that the IPO would happen on schedule, and argues it is now in serious jeopardy, partly because of new investor concern about potential liability.56 Zvi notes that the All-In Podcast holds two contradictory views at once: that the safety talk is hurting the IPO, and that it is a cynical regulatory-capture scheme. He says the podcast is correct that the statements are damaging the IPO.78 One commentator's specific estimate that the IPO's odds fell from 96% to 75% was framed only as a hypothetical, and no investor polling or market reaction data was reported.

What is the "pacing the frontier" proposal Dario Amodei has been pushing, and who has backed it?

"Pacing the frontier" is a push led by Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei this month for slowing the development of the most advanced AI models and strengthening safety measures through industry co-operation.12 CNBC reports that Amodei published a three-step proposal. It aims to temper the pace of development without "sacrificing commercial advantage or the United States' lead in AI," and it came with a call for stronger government oversight.34 The proposal drew President Trump's ire, according to CNBC.5 CNBC also reports that others, including Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg and Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, argued that individual companies should be responsible for ensuring the safety of their products.6 An Anthropic-focused newsletter says Amodei endorsed a "Pacing the Frontier" message cosigned by all labs. That claim is broader than the named backers reported elsewhere, and Anthropic's Thariq Shihipar says the discussion has broadened and "everyone has leaned in" on it. inference78

What does the 50.1% voting control sought by the cofounders mean for outside shareholders, and what role does the Long Term Benefit Trust play?

Zvi Mowshowitz reports that Anthropic's cofounders are seeking 50.1% voting control after the IPO, which would leave outside shareholders with a minority voice. inference12 The Long Term Benefit Trust would be able to appoint board members who could eventually, in theory, overrule that cofounder control.34 Zvi says he would understand investors who did not want to buy in under such a structure, but he finds it entirely appropriate and thinks it makes the company more valuable.567 How the Trust's board appointments would work in practice, and what outside shareholders would be able to do about it, was not reported.
