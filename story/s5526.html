US-China joint AI guardrails

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 17:01 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Nov 22 · single origin, unclear which

President Donald Trump said on a Friday that the US would work with China to develop "joint guardrails" on AI, according to Semafor.1 Latent Space's newsletter similarly reports that Trump said he and Xi Jinping agreed to joint guardrails, though it notes that no details were given.2 The Center for AI Safety's newsletter says Trump made the guardrails announcement after his meeting with Xi in Shenzhen.3 Writer Zvi Mowshowitz cautions against over-excitement, judging from what is known that the guardrails are, for now, probably akin to earlier "human in the military loop" limits, which he considers welcome but of little help in the bigger picture.4 Mowshowitz also objects to people calling it "a shift for Trump" every time he agrees to guardrails, arguing that this is not the first or second time Trump has done so.5 Trump also said he would impose "tremendous penalties" on companies that build data centers outside the US, according to the Center for AI Safety.6 Mowshowitz does see the data center threat as a major departure from earlier deals with countries including Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates.7 Semafor ties the remarks to an anti-tech backlash that helped drive a historic rebuke of Republicans in midterm elections.8 Semafor says the comments reflect accommodations even bullish AI proponents are making, noting that Trump had earlier warned regulation could "stifle" innovation and that protests against data center construction are spreading to Europe, Japan and India.910

The cooperative tone sits alongside tightening on technology: Semafor says harsher US export controls are expected to slow China's AI development and that OpenAI tightened Chinese access to its models.11 Latent Space also points to new US rules that, according to Kyle Chan, may block TSMC and Samsung from manufacturing Chinese AI chips.12 According to the Center for AI Safety, Washington has unveiled a regulation designed to block firms from China from using remote connections to reach chips subject to export controls that are housed in other countries.13

The head of Semafor's San Francisco office wrote that evidence is mounting that AI's tangible effects on the real world are arriving more slowly than people first anticipated.14
