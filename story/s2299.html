Amicus briefs in AI copyright cases

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
3 sources · 2 independent origins: The Verge, Wikimedia Foundation

The Wikimedia Foundation, the nonprofit behind Wikipedia, filed an amicus brief on Friday in the lawsuits by publishers including The New York Times against OpenAI and Microsoft, taking neither side.1 The Times alleges OpenAI infringed its copyright by scraping its website to build training datasets, while OpenAI and Microsoft contend that using LLMs is fair use; OpenAI has said regurgitation "is a rare bug."23

Wikimedia urges courts to protect training of large language models on copyrighted work but not chatbots regurgitating that work to users, and says it is concerned that both parties push for overbroad and simplified applications of fair use.45 Wikimedia argues that a publisher win could let copyright holders "gatekeep" innovative uses, while an AI company win could be "a green light to snub licensing terms and fragment the flow of knowledge between human authors and audiences."67 Wikimedia general counsel Stephen LaPorte warns that a ruling that does not apply fair use carefully could threaten the sustainability of free knowledge, and CEO Bernadette Meehan says the group is defending the free and open human knowledge Wikipedia depends on.89

OpenAI's own page lists many amicus briefs supporting fair use, including from library and disability groups, Engine and Developers Alliance, Public Knowledge, and the United States Government, which says the Times seeks to narrow fair use in a way that would hamper progress.1011 The Copia Institute told the Southern District of New York that copyright law is the wrong tool for fixing AI's problems, arguing copyright controls copying and distributing but not reading or learning from a work.1213
