OpenAI text watermarking for ChatGPT and Codex

Last scan Tue Oct 6, 16:18 UTC
3 sources · single origin (OpenAI)

OpenAI said in a Monday blog post that it will start adding an invisible watermark to text generated by ChatGPT and Codex in the European Union, to comply with the EU AI Act.1 It will reach eligible users on all plans over the coming weeks, but only in the EU, and OpenAI says it is not making watermarking a global default at launch.234 API customers worldwide can opt in to watermarked outputs for select models starting that day, and the feature is off by default.56

OpenAI says its textGrain method "matched or exceeded" other approaches such as Google DeepMind's SynthID, and TechCrunch reports OpenAI saw no meaningful change in model performance with it on.78 However, OpenAI's tests suggest editing can defeat it: replacing 10% of words with synonyms dropped detection from about 92% to 66%, and short passages, math answers and translated text are harder to detect.910 OpenAI itself says textGrain does not guarantee reliable detection, and a missing watermark does not prove human authorship.1112 Because of these limits and the risk of missed watermarks and false positives, OpenAI is giving detector access only to approved researchers and expert organizations, case by case, rather than the public.131415

The move follows Anthropic's watermarking of Claude text, which TechCrunch says is applied worldwide and drew backlash from some Claude users who argued Claude was just "the tool."16 The Wall Street Journal reported in 2024 that OpenAI had previously built a text watermark but held off releasing it, partly over concerns users would switch to rivals that did not watermark.17

Related questions

How does textGrain actually embed a watermark in the text, and can it survive copy and paste?

OpenAI says textGrain works by adding an invisible statistical signal to the model's word choices, which its detector then looks for to judge whether a passage carries an OpenAI watermark.1 TechCrunch reports the watermark is not a visible symbol but subtly shapes word choices. Its technical report, co-written with University of Pennsylvania and Yale researchers, walks through using a secret key to sort next-word predictions, and hundreds of these nudges together let the detector spot AI text using only the text and the key.23 On copy and paste, TechCrunch reports the watermark travels with the text because it lives in the words themselves.4 Editing is a different matter. TechCrunch reports that OpenAI's tests suggest the watermark can be removed by editing: in one test, replacing 10% of words with synonyms dropped detection from about 92% to 66%, and the company also said short passages, math answers and translated text are harder to detect.567 OpenAI itself cautions that strong performance under ideal conditions does not guarantee reliable detection in everyday use.8

Why is OpenAI rolling this out only in the EU instead of worldwide like Anthropic did with Claude?

OpenAI says the EU-only rollout is a regional approach that gives it room to learn from real-world use and feedback, and that it is not making watermarking a global default at launch.12 Anthropic, by contrast, applies its Claude watermarking worldwide, which drew backlash from some Claude users.3 OpenAI's more cautious approach may also reflect an earlier worry: the Wall Street Journal reported in 2024 that OpenAI held back a previously built text watermark partly over concerns users would switch to rivals that did not watermark. OpenAI has not said this is why it is limiting the rollout. inference45

Will OpenAI's image and audio verification tools stay public even though the text detector is restricted?

Yes, according to OpenAI: its verification tools for audio and images will stay publicly accessible, and the restriction applies only to text.1 Those tools are meant for organizations trying to learn whether an image or audio file was generated by one of OpenAI's systems.2

  1. The above only applies to text provenance—our verification tools for audio and images, including our openai.com/verify⁠ web tool and our Content Provenance API⁠(opens in a new window), will continue to be publicly accessible — OpenAI, Our approach to EU text provenance rules
  2. publicly accessible to organizations looking to understand whether an image or audio file was generated by one of our systems — OpenAI, Our approach to EU text provenance rules
When will watermarked OpenAI outputs be available through cloud partners, and will the detector ever be opened to the public?

OpenAI says it is working with cloud partners to make watermarking available for OpenAI model outputs accessed through their services "in the coming weeks" after its Monday, October 5, 2026 announcement, with no firmer date given.1 The detector is not public at launch: OpenAI says it is giving access case by case to approved researchers and expert organizations, citing the risk of missed watermarks and false positives.234 Separately, OpenAI's verification tools for audio and images remain publicly accessible, so the restriction applies only to text.5 OpenAI says it expects to revisit each part of this approach as the technology, standards and evidence evolve.6 Whether the text detector will ever be opened to the public was not stated.
