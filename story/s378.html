Gemini 4 Argon pricing

Last scan Mon Oct 5, 14:51 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 1 · 2 independent origins: Google DeepMind, Latent Space

Google DeepMind introduced Gemini 4 Argon for coding, enterprise knowledge work and cyber defense; access currently starts with government users and trusted cyber defenders in the Fairwind Program.123 Google says it will refine guardrails before opening access to developers, enterprises and consumers, though Ars Technica reports it is making no specific timeline promises. inference45

Standard pricing is $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, with a 50% introductory discount bringing it to $2/$10 and no end date announced, according to the Latent Space summary of posts.6 Ars Technica, however, describes the $2/$10 rates as available for a limited time.7 Cached input gets a 95% discount.89

Vals reports an average cost of $15.68 per task on its index, where Argon ranks first at 68.9%.10 The sources give conflicting per-task cost figures: one listed figure is $0.72 per task at max effort, versus $3.26 for Astra, while another gives $1.99 at discounted pricing. inference1112

How many output tokens per task does Argon use compared with Astra, and why does that matter for cost?

Artificial Analysis reports Argon averages 62K output tokens per task, against 27K for Astra, which is more than double. inference1 That matters because Argon's lower per-task cost comes from price, not efficiency: at discounted pricing it costs $1.99 per task versus $3.26 for Astra, and standard pricing would raise that to $3.98.23 Because output tokens are billed at $10 per million at the introductory rate (twice that at the standard $20), heavier token use eats into the discount, and at standard pricing Argon would cost more per task than Astra's $3.26. inference45 Ars Technica describes the $2/$10 rates as available only for a limited time, so the cost advantage could shrink. inference6

What has Google's own team done with Argon internally, such as the memory savings and the C/C++ to Rust migrations?

Google says a team of Argon agents analyzed fleet-wide profiling telemetry to find and apply memory optimizations across its data centers, freeing over 300 TiB of memory once rolled out, with an estimated 500 TiB to 1 PiB in total savings.12 Argon agents are also migrating C/C++ codebases to Rust across Google, from tens of thousands of lines in core libraries like re2 and libgav1 up to more than 800,000 lines for the Fuchsia Zircon kernel.34 For libgav1, Google's open-source video decoder, agents replaced 32K lines of SIMD code with safe Rust, producing a memory-safe decoder that Google says runs 2.7x faster than the existing Rust port with identical video output.567 Google says these rewrites are undergoing rigorous automated and manual auditing, emulation testing and review before reaching production.89
