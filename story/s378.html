Gemini 4 Argon pricing

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 03:34 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 1 · 2 independent origins: Google DeepMind, Latent Space

Google DeepMind introduced Gemini 4 Argon for coding, enterprise knowledge work and cyber defense; access currently starts with government users and trusted cyber defenders in the Fairwind Program.123 Google says it will refine guardrails before opening access to developers, enterprises and consumers, though Ars Technica reports it is making no specific timeline promises. inference45

Standard pricing is $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, with a 50% introductory discount bringing it to $2/$10 and no end date announced, according to the Latent Space summary of posts.6 Ars Technica, however, describes the $2/$10 rates as available for a limited time.7 Cached input gets a 95% discount.89

Vals reports an average cost of $15.68 per task on its index, where Argon ranks first at 68.9%.10 The sources give conflicting per-task cost figures: one listed figure is $0.72 per task at max effort, versus $3.26 for Astra, while another gives $1.99 at discounted pricing. inference1112

What has Google actually done with Argon internally, such as memory savings and the C/C++ to Rust migrations?

Google says a team of Argon agents analyzed fleet-wide profiling data to find and apply memory optimizations across its data centers, freeing over 300 TiB of memory once rolled out.12 Google estimates total savings from this work at 500 TiB to 1 PiB.3 Argon agents are also migrating C/C++ codebases to Rust across Google, with the work scaling from tens of thousands of lines in core libraries like re2 and libgav1 up to more than 800,000 lines for the Fuchsia Zircon kernel.4 Google says these large rewrites are undergoing automated and manual auditing, emulation testing and review before they roll out to production.5 In libgav1, Google's open-source video decoder, Argon agents replaced 32K lines of SIMD code in an existing Rust port with safe Rust. Google says the result is a memory-safe decoder that runs 2.7x faster than the Rust port, with identical video output.678

What safeguards is Google adding before broad release, and who gets access first once the cyber testing ends?

Google says it is strengthening critical frontier safeguards across four main areas before rolling out Gemini 4 Argon broadly, including defending against misuse, prompt injection attacks and monitoring for misalignment.12 On misuse, Google says the model is designed to refuse harmful requests, including cyber and chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear attacks, while still allowing legitimate dual-use scientific research.3 Google also says it is hardening its sandboxed testing environments by isolating and sealing them before high-risk training or evaluations begin.4 Ars Technica reports that Google built Argon with systems that monitor its chain-of-thought and can stop the model if it steps out of bounds.5 Once the cyber testing ends, general availability will start with paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers, though Google has given no specific timeline.678
