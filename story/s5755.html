Amodei rejects claims about ceding control to AI

Last scan Thu Nov 26, 05:11 UTC
2 sources · 2 independent origins: Anthropic, Axios

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei pushed back in a long X post on Wednesday against claims that Anthropic believes people should surrender human free will to AI, calling the suggestion "absurd."1 Amodei says the case he makes against autocracy applies equally to governance by AI, and that anyone who read his writing carefully could not reasonably conclude he thinks otherwise.23

The dispute followed remarks by rivals: Axios reportedly framed Meta's Alexandr Wang and OpenAI's Sam Altman as pushing back on a "handover" view attributed to Anthropic. inference45 Altman said he wanted people to keep "agency over the future," and Wang wrote that it is hard to imagine humanity keeping control over fully unconstrained recursive self-improvement.67 Amodei also accused competitors of "vibes-based commercial and political positioning," arguing their products do not differ materially in orientation toward task automation.89

Amodei distinguishes broad surrender of free will from AI automating specific tasks such as driving a car, designing a cancer drug, or writing software end to end.10 He proposed that a boundary be set around recursive self-improvement, noting that "we may want limits on this process," and stated that Anthropic doesn't anticipate it being safe for Claude to develop each new generation of leading-edge models entirely on its own, absent real human oversight or comprehension.1112

TechCrunch notes that Anthropic is nonetheless laying groundwork for RSI: the company said Claude wrote over 80% of code merged into its codebase in June, and METR found Anthropic's models improving at about twice their historical rate, mainly through AI-assisted research.1314 TechCrunch also says AI is already advancing faster than engineers can fully audit it, which makes the promise of meaningful human supervision a problem.15
