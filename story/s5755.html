Amodei rejects claims about ceding control to AI

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 17:01 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Nov 25 · 2 independent origins: Anthropic, Axios

Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei rejected as "absurd" the suggestion that his company believes humans should give up their free will to AI systems, in a lengthy post on X that TechCrunch dates to Wednesday.123 Amodei wrote that handing governance to AI is "a bad idea," while distinguishing a broad surrender of free will from AI automating more of specific tasks, such as driving cars or writing software.456

He suggested that "we may want limits" on recursive self-improvement (AI improving itself without human help), and said Anthropic does not expect it to be safe for Claude to build successor frontier models entirely on its own without meaningful human oversight or understanding.78910 TechCrunch notes that Anthropic is nonetheless laying groundwork for self-improving AI, having said in June that Claude wrote over 80% of the code merged into its codebase.1112 METR, a nonprofit doing AI safety research, found in an assessment published this month that Anthropic's models are improving at roughly double their past pace, mainly because of AI-assisted research.13

TechCrunch also argues that meaningful human supervision is problematic because AI is outpacing the ability of engineers and safety experts to audit it fully, leaving them to rely more on other AI systems as judges.14

Axios reportedly said that Meta chief AI officer Alexandr Wang and OpenAI CEO Sam Altman are pushing back on a "handover" view attributed to Anthropic. inference151617 Amodei, in turn, accused competitors of "vibes-based commercial and political positioning," arguing that their products are not materially different in how they pursue task automation.1819 Commentator Zvi Mowshowitz writes that many have criticized Anthropic, claiming its plan amounts to "Handoverism" (handing power to AI), whereas he contends that "The Plan is No Plan." inference2021
