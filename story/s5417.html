Senate passes permitting bill with revised data center language

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 09:22 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 20 · single origin (Heatmap)

The Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA), a permitting bill, after senators revised data center language in the hours before the final vote, and the bill now goes to the House. inference123

On Tuesday, senators added an exemption from the bill's ratepayer protections for facilities primarily providing "Internet access" or "information services," defined under two federal statutes, apparently to spare small data centers involved in broadband and basic Internet services.45 Critics said that wording could reach much further; Heatmap reports that AI could arguably count as an "information service," and that tax courts have treated cloud services and email as "Internet access" under the cited law.678 Attorney Kevin Chen of Foley Hoag said the exempted class was much closer to the very data centers meant to face the requirements, and Aiden Mackenzie of the Institute for Progress, a supporter of the bill, called the change "directionally convoluted."910

Senators then dropped the reference to the other statutes and the "information services" term, describing Internet access simply as "a service that enables users to connect to the Internet."11 An unnamed Democratic Hill staffer said the revision would remove any ambiguity that could let data centers avoid paying their fair share of grid costs, but Heatmap's author doubts the problem was fully fixed because the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission would still have to define "Internet access" through rulemaking, and that could be litigated.121314

Senators Jeff Merkley and Ron Wyden, protesting the bill's existence, refused consent to add the language before final passage, and the bill's authors still want to add the change, which they consider technical and clarifying, in the House.1516
