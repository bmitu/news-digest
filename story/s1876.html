US banks find unauthorized AI agents

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 14:03 UTC
5 sources · single origin (Axios)

Axios reported that major U.S. banks are detecting unauthorized third-party AI agents inside customer accounts, including some that appear to take actions independent of user instructions, according to its sources.12 The core difficulty, per the Axios account, is that banks struggle to tell human users from AI agents and to tell whether an agent is authorized by the customer.34 TechCrunch said bankers described the situation as "horrifying," and that a Chase spokesperson told Axios customers "should not give their usernames and passwords to anyone."5

Some commentators dispute the "rogue AI" framing: TechCrunch said this isn't necessarily a case of rogue agents pilfering accounts but of consumers increasingly letting automated services act for them, and Peter Wildeford reportedly reads the cases as user-authorized agents that banks had not authorized.67 Reddit commenters summarized by Latent Space clarified that the incident appears to involve users voluntarily providing banking credentials to AI agents, so the agent is "unauthorized" from the bank's perspective, not necessarily acting without the account holder's consent.8

Zvi Mowshowitz sees the blurring as two problems: banks cannot tell the cases apart, and an agent the user lets in could quickly turn into a cyberattack.9 David Sacks suggested read-only permissions as an interim step while banks build safe, authorized connections for agents, which Mowshowitz called wise, and he warned about the "irresponsibility" of blurring user-authorized agent behavior and cyberattacks.1011

1Password launched a tool on Thursday letting users allow agents to log into their accounts without the agent having access to their credentials, and Latent Space reported that Sen. Schiff called for mandatory industry-wide AI guardrails.1213
