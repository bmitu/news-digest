EU institutions go offline after AI-fueled cyberattack

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 17:01 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 20 · single origin (Politico)

POLITICO reported on a Thursday that internal services at EU institutions had gone offline following a major cyberattack fueled by AI.1 The Center for AI Safety's newsletter likewise says the EU revealed it had been targeted in an AI-assisted attack that took several digital systems offline.2 Zvi Mowshowitz writes that the European Commission was hacked and personal data stolen, which so far appears to be the full extent of the harm.3 Politico describes the incident as one in a series of disruptions, among them problems at American national security agencies and the Federal Aviation Administration's equivalent, worldwide Microsoft failures, and hacks aimed at refrigeration systems in European grocery stores.4

According to The Hormuz Letter, which cites Axios, America's National Cyber Director is leaving the post at a time of major cyberattacks and outages.5 Mowshowitz also reports that breaches at third-party suppliers of JPMorgan Chase exposed roughly 16,000 email addresses belonging to customers.6

Authorities blame the instability on new AI tools that can find vulnerabilities in existing code, which bad actors can then exploit.7 Helmut Reisinger, European chief executive of Palo Alto Networks, says the company sees "more or less" ten times as many incidents as about four months ago, and he predicts this will become the "new norm."89 In a separate report, Google's Threat Intelligence Group said that for the first time it had spotted malicious actors relying on artificial intelligence to carry out cyberattacks entirely automatically.10

Robin Bender Ginn, executive director of the OpenJS Foundation, said "We're running on fumes."11 A warning about AI-identified flaws in PHP did not lead to catastrophe, partly because organizations took systems offline or patched them and the flaws were harder to exploit than expected.12 Juhan Lepassaar of the EU cyber agency ENISA said it has seen a similar pattern of false positives in AI-found flaw reports, and that ENISA is listing the most critical open-source components to secure.1314
