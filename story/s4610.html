Nvidia halts H200 sales to China

Last scan Sun Nov 22, 14:05 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 20 · 1 new article today · 2 independent origins: Nvidia, TechCrunch

Nvidia has stopped shipping its H200 AI chips to China, interim CEO Colette Kress announced on an earnings call on Tuesday, even though the White House had approved such sales in December.12 Kress gave no reason for ending the shipments and did not say whether Nvidia would do other business with China later.3 The announcement comes days after Jensen Huang stepped down as Nvidia CEO, following reports of extramarital affairs.4

TechCrunch reports that the H200 deal was brokered by Huang and President Donald Trump, with chips routed through the U.S. so the government could collect a 25% tariff.56 According to reports, shipments of semiconductors have been held up in Hong Kong because Beijing has not given the go-ahead for bulk imports.7 Georgetown researcher Jacob Feldgoise says China would rather build demand for its domestic chip industry, even though major AI users still want the superior Nvidia hardware.8

Under regulations announced Monday, foreign data centers can no longer use top-tier American chips to serve customers in China remotely, and Jefferies projects the restrictions might reduce Nvidia's data center revenue by between $44 billion and $62 billion next year.910 TechCrunch's Anthony Ha said critics of Nvidia's China stance estimate that about 20% of its business comes from China, because chips sold to firms in Malaysia and elsewhere in Asia are likely to end up used by Chinese companies. inference1112 However, Kress takes a different view from Jefferies, saying demand for chips like Vera Rubin is so backed up that the rules would not affect the company's top-line revenue. inference1310

Anduril CEO Palmer Luckey said at the DealBook Summit that he wants Huang reinstated because China is "thrilled" he is gone.1415 More than 200,000 people, including Elon Musk, have signed a petition for Huang's return, but Nvidia executives said on the call that they have no plans to reappoint him.1617 TechCrunch's Kirsten Korosec said Huang himself is saying he is prepared to come back.18 TechCrunch suggests Wall Street is not reassured by Nvidia's earnings, pointing to the company's falling stock price.19
