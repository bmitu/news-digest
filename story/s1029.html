OpenAI fires three safety researchers

Last scan Mon Oct 19, 22:55 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 17 · 1 new article this week · single origin, unclear which

OpenAI fired three safety researchers, Tomek Korbak, Mikita Balesni and Jasmine Wang, ostensibly for mishandling sensitive company information, and the three have published an open letter saying they believe the reasons were pretextual.12 According to Timothy B. Lee, the firings happened around the start of October.3

Korbak says he was told verbally he was fired because of how he communicated with METR, nothing was put in writing, and talking to METR was his job.4 Balesni says he was told OpenAI no longer trusted him because he spoke too much to third-party safety organizations, which implied a leak of company IP; he denies sharing any IP and says his work was coordinated with his reporting line, research leadership and the board.56

OpenAI disputes the researchers' account: an unsigned statement from its "research leaders" accused them of a "significant breach of trust" and said the decisions were not about raising safety concerns or speaking out.7 Lee notes that OpenAI's own system card says GPT-6 Astra "shows a substantial decrease in chain-of-thought monitorability compared to previous models," which he links to the researchers' concerns. inference89

Observers disagree on what to make of it: Kelsey Piper says the information that has come in "looks incredibly bad" but that she believes OpenAI did not fire them to create a chilling effect, while Zach Stein-Perlman now guesses the researchers' messages are true and the reasons were pretextual.101112 Lee says the employees' response "seems credible" but allows that OpenAI's statements could be correct, and Mowshowitz says that even if OpenAI was justified, how the firings were communicated is a huge problem.131415
