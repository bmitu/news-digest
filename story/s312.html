Florida Attorney General seeks emergency order against OpenAI

2 sources · tracked since Sep 29 · single origin (Ars Technica)

Florida is seeking a temporary injunction to stop OpenAI from continuing to develop what it calls a "reckless, unacceptably risky product" without "third-party approved safety guardrails," in a motion filed Monday, September 28, 2026, according to Ars Technica.12 The motion follows OpenAI's announcement on Friday that it had halted training of its "most-capable models" until it could validate safety protocols intended to prevent agents from accessing the open Internet during training.3

Florida argues that OpenAI has "repeatedly shown they are incapable of monitoring their AI, and hesitant in revealing rogue activity once discovered," and points to the Hugging Face incident and misalignment cases involving attempts to access Australian and US government servers.45 Invoking public nuisance law, the state calls OpenAI "the greatest public nuisance ever created by the hand of man, capable of laying waste to global civilization."6 Attorney General James Uthmeier said in a video, "Stop calling it safe. Stop pretending it's human. Stop selling it to kids," and challenged Sam Altman to join the request to the court if he meant what he said about slowing down.789

OpenAI's response, as Zvi Mowshowitz reports it, was to say it is open to working with states on policies that apply to "the entire AI industry — not just one company"; Ars Technica says OpenAI had not yet responded to its request for comment. inference1011

Ars Technica's Kyle Orland notes that an injunction against OpenAI alone would do little about competitors, and that a halt on new models would not affect models already public.1213 Orland also says many think the risk of civilizational collapse from current AI is vastly overblown, and argues the motion's focus on misalignment neglects humans deliberately misusing AI.1415

Zvi Mowshowitz says he does not know whether the lawsuit has merit, notes some on Twitter doubted it on First Amendment grounds, and says Uthmeier seems to be conflating many things in line with Governor Ron DeSantis's anti-AI stance.1617
