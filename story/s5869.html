S&P considers downgrading Nvidia's credit rating

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 17:01 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Nov 25 · single origin (S&P Global Ratings)

S&P Global Ratings is weighing a downgrade of Nvidia's credit rating because of worries about how the company is managed and governed, TechCrunch reported on November 25, 2026.12 In a statement, S&P said the removal and return of CEO Jensen Huang had raised concerns over "weak executive oversight and lack of succession planning" as well as "key man risk", meaning the company leans heavily on Huang for future success.3 The agency expects to complete its review within 90 days and could lower the rating one step, shown as AA to AA-, which would be the fourth-highest level on its scale.4

According to TechCrunch, Nvidia is sure to remain investment grade, but a cut could make borrowing more expensive for it, and could also raise debt costs or collateral demands for neoclouds and other infrastructure firms whose debt it backstops.56 Semafor argues that an official downgrade, which has not yet happened, would be a dramatic blow to Nvidia and also to the large customers, startups and construction projects that depend on its financial support across the AI industry.7

In August, Nvidia announced a plan to line up $500 billion in AI infrastructure financing with six institutions, among them Goldman Sachs, Apollo and Blackstone, with original terms under which it would guarantee 25% of the loans.89 Bloomberg reported that lenders were seeking a bigger guarantee even before Huang was ousted and reinstated, and TechCrunch expects a downgrade would likely lead them to demand more still.1011

Semafor also reports that Moody's has moved its outlook on Nvidia from positive to stable, and that the company's shares are tumbling as the glow from Huang's comeback fades.1213

Zvi Mowshowitz, who writes that S&P is meeting with Nvidia's board, is skeptical of the agency's move, likening it to the "nonsense" behind rating agencies' downgrade of the United States and arguing that Nvidia faces no serious default risk and, if anything, now has government backing.1415
