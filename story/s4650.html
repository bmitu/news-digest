Baker McKenzie drops default billable hour

Last scan Sat Nov 21, 06:54 UTC
2 sources · single origin (The Wall Street Journal)

Baker McKenzie is moving away from the billable hour as its default way of charging clients, according to a Wall Street Journal report relayed by several outlets.12 Global chair Sunny Mann says the firm has begun charging on a value-based model, because charging by the hour does not always make sense when work is aided by technology and AI.34 According to Above the Law, the firm is urging its attorneys to adopt value-based billing as the standard approach, while still using hourly billing in situations that call for it. Mann is said to consider the hourly rate the wrong measure and to anticipate that the wider industry will move the same way.56 Artificial Lawyer likewise notes the change may not be a complete move off the hour.7

Mann sees AI efficiencies as a chance to win more work, suggesting that cheaper litigation could lead clients to litigate more even if the volume of work per matter falls.8 Mann additionally told the Journal that at the firm, skill with AI is becoming part of both job interviews and employee performance evaluations.9

Artificial Lawyer called the move massive and very welcome for everyone who cares about legal AI, arguing that time-based value is the antithesis of legal AI.1011 Above the Law is more cautious: it says the billable hour's obituary can wait, that the open question is how much of the AI savings clients will actually see, and that clients will presumably want more than a new name for the same invoice.121314
