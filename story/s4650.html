Baker McKenzie drops default billable hour

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 17:01 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 20 · single origin (Lawyers Weekly)

Baker McKenzie is moving away from the billable hour as its default way of charging clients, according to a Wall Street Journal report relayed by several outlets.12 Global chair Sunny Mann says the firm has begun charging on a value-based model, because charging by the hour does not always make sense when work is aided by technology and AI.34 According to Above the Law, the firm is urging its attorneys to adopt value-based billing as the standard approach, while still using hourly billing in situations that call for it. Mann is said to consider the hourly rate the wrong measure and to anticipate that the wider industry will move the same way.56 Speaking to Lawyers Weekly, Anne-Marie Allgrove, who is national managing partner for Baker McKenzie in Australia, said the firm anticipates relying more on non-hourly billing models, among them fixed pricing and pricing based on value.7 Allgrove said hourly billing will stay an option where it is the most appropriate model, but it will no longer be the default across all mandates.8 Artificial Lawyer likewise notes the change may not be a complete move off the hour.9 According to Artificial Lawyer's understanding, Baker McKenzie declared that it wants to move away from making the hour its standard way of charging clients, and the spread of New Model law firms across the globe is the direct cause.10

Mann sees AI efficiencies as a chance to win more work, suggesting that cheaper litigation could lead clients to litigate more even if the volume of work per matter falls.11 Mann additionally told the Journal that at the firm, skill with AI is becoming part of both job interviews and employee performance evaluations.12

Artificial Lawyer called the move massive and very welcome for everyone who cares about legal AI, arguing that time-based value is the antithesis of legal AI.1314 Artificial Lawyer argues that, unlike the AI spinouts and special units at other big firms, Baker McKenzie's step is a strategy for the whole firm rather than a separation strategy, implicitly meaning the firm will adapt to the New Model.1516 Above the Law is more cautious: it says the billable hour's obituary can wait, that the open question is how much of the AI savings clients will actually see, and that clients will presumably want more than a new name for the same invoice.171819
