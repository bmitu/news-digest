Alpöge Riemann Hypothesis proof claim

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 17:01 UTC
12 sources · 565 points on Hacker News · tracked since Nov 20 · 1 new article today · 3 independent origins: Lean Prover Community, Levent Alpöge, ladyxtel

On November 20, 2026, Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge announced that, working with the company's Claude model, he had proved the Riemann Hypothesis, a problem about the distribution of prime numbers first posed in 1859. inference1234 He released the proof as code in Lean, a language that allows computer verification, rather than as a traditional English paper, and says his method extends to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. inference5678

After several days of checking the formalization, the Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative jointly stated they had "full confidence" the proof is correct, though Platformer noted that no human yet understands all of it. inference910111213

On the proof's size, Timothy B. Lee reported that the original ran to 17 million lines of code; Zvi Mowshowitz wrote that Anthropic has since used Claude to cut it from 17.4 million lines to 76,000. inference14151617 The shorter version's repository, which covers both the original and generalized hypotheses, says it has passed only machine checks, with no paper and no complete read-through by a mathematician.1819 As for a widely quoted figure of more than 250,000 pages, it was, according to Alpöge, a joke; he also called the Lean code "full of garbage obviously, but compiles," while Mowshowitz put best estimates of the proof's "real" length at 1,000 to 2,000 pages before simplification. inference20212223

How much was Claude's doing is a matter of emphasis: Alpöge wrote that Claude "far outpaced me near the finish line," whereas Gary Marcus argues AI did not solve the problem alone and that months of extensive guidance from Alpöge were critical. inference2425262728

Reacting to the proof, Andrew Sutherland, an MIT mathematics professor, called it "an historic moment," while Fields Medalist Chau Ngo argued that AI-generated proofs do not by themselves bring new understanding or insight without hard study. inference293031

On the wider implications for AI, Marcus also contends the approach may not generalize, since it leaned on Lean and the math-specific library mathlib, while Eliezer Yudkowsky argues the methods should extend to anything testable, including improving AI itself. inference323334 Meta chief AI officer Alexandr Wang argued the proof signals a phase change in AI capabilities that makes safety work urgent.3536
