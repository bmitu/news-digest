Alpöge Riemann Hypothesis proof claim

Last scan Mon Nov 23, 13:58 UTC
7 sources · tracked since Nov 20 · 3 new articles today · 3 independent origins: Levent Alpöge, TechCrunch, The Verge

Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge claims he has proved the Riemann Hypothesis with the help of Claude, and says the method extends to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis and, with more time, likely the Extended one.12 Alpöge says he and Claude also formalized the arguments in Lean without assumptions, and that he texted Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei about it late on a Sunday night.34 Alpöge reportedly felt a bit guilty about releasing a proof without a human-intelligible explanation, and TechCrunch says he has committed to pursuing one.56 According to Latent Space, the Lean code released was about 1.3GB, roughly 250,000 pages in prose equivalent, and Alpöge said he released only Lean because posting a huge unreadable prose version would be inappropriate; the outlet adds that Claude's role covered the early stages while the parts involving automorphic forms on higher-rank groups went beyond his own mastery. inference789 Commentator Gary Marcus argues that AI did not solve the Riemann Hypothesis alone, saying Alpöge guided the work extensively and collaborated with the AI over a period of months.1011

The Verge reports that the solution had not been independently verified and that Anthropic had not commented, while Anthropic had said in August it did not expect Claude's techniques to lead to a proof of the hypothesis.1213 TechCrunch later updated its story to say independent sources had checked the Lean proof, and several participants in a Zulip chat, including MIT mathematician Andrew Sutherland, say they confirmed it compiles.1415 Mathematician Ken Ono writes in the Guardian that the Lean and Mathlib communities have certified the formal proof, but that mathematicians are only beginning to understand the argument.16 Latent Space reports that Elliot Glazer confirmed the formalized statement is the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis and put the chance of a Lean bug well under 1%, while noting the code was still compiling.1718 TechCrunch says mathematicians such as Chris Birkbeck of the University of East Anglia are picking through the proof's technical content, and that most appear confident the formalized Lean result will hold, though understanding it will take a while.1920 Semafor reports that an unnamed Fields Medal winner told Nature the result would be a historic milestone if it fully checks out, and that he thinks it is likely to be correct.2122

The Clay Mathematics Institute requires that any proposed solution first appear in publication and then survive a minimum of two years of review by the community; so far, the institute has not formally endorsed this one.2324

Separately, Stanford's Ravi Vakil posted a proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis by Stanford postdoc Alessandro Masullo, which Vakil says is entirely human-made with no AI; TechCrunch notes that without a Lean version it is too early to say whether it checks out.2526 According to Latent Space, Masullo's 940-page proof remains unfinished and has occupied him for twelve years, with its publication coming after Alpöge made his claim. The outlet adds that nothing has been revealed about which Claude model was involved or how much guidance a human provided. inference2728
