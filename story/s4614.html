Alpöge Riemann Hypothesis proof claim

Last scan Sat Nov 21, 06:54 UTC
4 sources · 3 independent origins: Levent Alpöge, TechCrunch, The Verge

Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge claims he has proved the Riemann Hypothesis with the help of Claude, and says the method extends to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis and, with more time, likely the Extended one.12 Alpöge says he and Claude also formalized the arguments in Lean without assumptions, and that he texted Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei about it late on a Sunday night.34 Alpöge reportedly felt a bit guilty about releasing a proof without a human-intelligible explanation, and TechCrunch says he has committed to pursuing one.56

The Verge reports that the solution had not been independently verified and that Anthropic had not commented, while Anthropic had said in August it did not expect Claude's techniques to lead to a proof of the hypothesis.78 TechCrunch later updated its story to say independent sources had checked the Lean proof, and several participants in a Zulip chat, including MIT mathematician Andrew Sutherland, say they confirmed it compiles.910 TechCrunch says mathematicians such as Chris Birkbeck of the University of East Anglia are picking through the proof's technical content, and that most appear confident the formalized Lean result will hold, though understanding it will take a while.1112

The Clay Mathematics Institute requires that any proposed solution first appear in publication and then survive a minimum of two years of review by the community; so far, the institute has not formally endorsed this one.1314

Separately, Stanford's Ravi Vakil posted a proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis by Stanford postdoc Alessandro Masullo, which Vakil says is entirely human-made with no AI; TechCrunch notes that without a Lean version it is too early to say whether it checks out.1516
