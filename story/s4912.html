Microsoft Outlook outage blamed on latent bug

Last scan Mon Nov 23, 13:58 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 20 · 1 new article today · single origin (TechCrunch)

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella said at The New York Times' DealBook Summit that the recent global Outlook outage resulted from a "latent bug" that created a "perfect storm."1 Nadella said Microsoft had put out an incident response and would take lessons, both architectural and operational, to fix the problem.2

The Verge described the Outlook problem as a multi-day outage that brought entire companies to a standstill, and it came alongside a nationwide FAA ground stop and a JPMorgan Chase banking outage.3 TechCrunch reports that concerns swirled that the outages stemmed from cyberattacks, but officials denied those claims.4 The Verge likewise says the outages were not believed to be caused by cyberattacks, a line it corrected after a typo had originally said the opposite.56

Nadella argued that much critical infrastructure is "unpatchable" and that AI-found vulnerabilities force patches into production instantly, which he said will create instability.78

Microsoft President Brad Smith warned on Face the Nation that people should be prepared for more outages in the coming months, saying the world's information infrastructure is being upgraded.910 Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told CNN that Americans should expect to invest in upgrading systems and cybersecurity and should expect "bumps in the road."1112
