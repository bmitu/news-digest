Meta Muse agent launch

6 sources · 163 points on Hacker News · tracked since Sep 27 · 1 new article today · 3 independent origins: Meta, Nate B. Jones, Wired

Meta released Muse, a personal AI agent that its materials say runs on a dedicated virtual computer and helps with tasks such as researching, preparing briefings and shopping, and it reportedly reached the top of the US free iPhone chart ten days after launching on September 8. inference12 TechCrunch calls Muse and its plush-like mascot Jolly a surprise hit, and John Gruber says it is technically groundbreaking, with each user getting a persistent Linux VM in Meta's cloud, and calls it the first consumer-accessible agentic AI system. inference345 Nate B Jones says Muse identified $5,350 a year in his subscription spending and has so far canceled subscriptions totaling $1,285 a year, and he calls it a profoundly important launch.67

What did Meta say when Jason Aten claimed Muse read his Messages without permission?

Meta VP of Communications Andy Stone pushed back, saying the company does not believe Muse read Aten's messages without his consent.1 Stone wrote on X that the Messages integration in the Muse Mac app is entirely opt-in. He said users must enable both Full Disk Access and the Messages connector, and that Muse can't read Messages otherwise.23 A Meta representative named Singleton wrote that the options are grayed out if Full Disk Access isn't enabled. He also wrote that granting access requires the user to manually confirm in macOS Settings and triggers a full restart of the Muse app, which makes it even less likely that such a choice could be made accidentally without the user's knowledge.45 Singleton also disputed Aten's belief that Muse was passing along his incoming banner notifications. He said the AI was confused and gave an incorrect explanation of what happened.6 TechCrunch characterizes Meta's response as essentially saying that what Aten described did not and could not have happened.78
