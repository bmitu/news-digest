Meta Muse agent launch

Last scan Sat Oct 3, 04:20 UTC
5 sources · 163 points on Hacker News · tracked since Sep 27 · 2 independent origins: Meta, Nate B. Jones

Meta's Muse AI agent, presented with a plush-like mascot called Jolly, has been a surprise hit, according to TechCrunch.1 Nate B Jones says Muse reached the top of the US free iPhone chart ten days after launching on September 8.2 AppleInsider describes Muse as an agent for tasks such as researching topics, preparing briefings and shopping, and says Meta states it runs on a dedicated virtual computer.34

Nate B Jones reports that Muse identified $5,350 a year in subscription spending and had cancelled $1,285 a year so far, and he calls it a profoundly important launch.56 Jones also says Amazon locked Muse out of its store.7 Separately, Latent Space notes that Meta launched Muse connectors for small businesses, and that Muse Spark 1.3 is available on GCP and Oracle.89

AppleInsider, citing Inc's Jason Aten, alleges Muse synced 187,000 lines from his Messages database even though Full Disk Access was off.10 This conflicts with Meta's stated position that Muse must obey user permissions, though Meta also warns that Muse can make mistakes or take unexpected actions. inference1112

TechCrunch says products like Muse are less concerned with monetization, and that Muse has Meta's ad targeting behind it, which gives it more options and more time before monetization becomes urgent.1314

Meta's Muse could have read Aten's Messages only if he had enabled access, according to Meta. He said the AI was confused and gave an incorrect explanation of what happened. How does Muse compare with OpenAI's new Dots agent on price and access?

Muse's main selling point is making AI agent access free and frictionless, and anyone with a Meta account can get it, according to The Verge.12 According to The Verge, Meta offers a free dedicated virtual machine that houses both the Muse agent and the user's associated data.3 OpenAI's Dots, by contrast, is limited for now to subscribers of its $100/month Pro plan and above.4 As a result, most of ChatGPT's 1.2 billion users won't be able to use Dots, while Muse is open to anyone with a Meta account. inference56 TechCrunch says Muse has the juggernaut of Meta's personalized ad targeting behind it, which gives it more options for monetization and more time before monetization becomes an urgent question.7

How many consumers actually pay for AI, and how much do they pay each month?

An Andreessen Horowitz report, drawing on PNC research, found that as of May 2.2% of consumers were paying for AI, at an average spend of $31 a month.1 Bank of America, by contrast, found in March that roughly 3% of U.S. consumers paid for AI, up 40% from the previous year.2 A Menlo survey from September was sunnier, finding that a quarter of adults use AI daily and half of those users are paying for it.3
