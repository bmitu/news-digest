Blackstone warns of Anthropic junk rating

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
3 sources · 293 points on Hacker News · single origin (Financial Times)

The Financial Times reports that Blackstone, anchor investor in a $63 billion deal to finance Anthropic's chips, has told investors it expects Anthropic to receive a "high" junk rating from credit agencies after its IPO.1 According to the reporting, Blackstone now expects a double B range rating, the top tier of speculative "junk" grade.2 The FT notes this reverses Blackstone's earlier position, when it said Anthropic would receive an investment-grade rating before 2027.3

TechCrunch says Anthropic and Blackstone both declined to comment, and it describes Blackstone's forecast as just a prediction.45 TechCrunch argues a high junk rating would make it harder and costlier for Anthropic to raise money to fund its heavy spending, which it says has worried investors.67

TechCrunch's separate piece says the warning comes amid investor anxiety over AI infrastructure spending, with Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Microsoft outlining more than $740 billion in capital expenditures this year.89 The piece also says these muddied waters could complicate Anthropic's IPO, and that a lack of investment-grade status might leave Anthropic more reliant on hyperscalers and third parties to fund its infrastructure.1011

Zvi Mowshowitz writes that Anthropic's bonds are currently rated BB+, one notch below investment grade, and that its junior debt seems to trade around 8.5%.1213 Mowshowitz says Anthropic plans to spend $111 billion on Google cloud contracts, $110 billion with Amazon Web Services and $31 billion on Microsoft "regardless of actual usage," plus up to $86 billion renting Nvidia chips from Nscale and Lambda.1415
