GPT 6.1 Sol: Near-Astra intelligence for a fifth of the price

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 16:33 UTC
6 sources · 1064 points on Hacker News · tracked since Sep 29 · 1 new article today · 5 independent origins: Artificial Analysis, Latent Space, Nate B. Jones, OpenAI, Simon Willison

OpenAI introduced GPT-6.1 Sol, an upgrade to GPT-6 Sol that it says nearly matches GPT-6 Astra's intelligence on agentic coding, computer use and professional work at one-fifth of Astra's standard input and output token prices.1 OpenAI says GPT-6.1 Sol is available to Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu users in ChatGPT Work and Codex, though not yet in Chat, and through the API as gpt-6.1-sol.234 API prices are $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with cached input at $0.10 per million, which OpenAI says is 95% below standard input pricing.567

OpenAI says that on DeepSWE v1.1 GPT-6.1 Sol matches GPT-6 Astra at roughly one-fifth of the cost, while eclipsing GPT-6 Sol's best score by 6.4 percentage points at a lower reasoning effort and cost.89 Artificial Analysis attributes the savings to fewer turns and cheaper cache reads rather than simply fewer generated tokens, and it reports Sol uses 10–30% more output tokens than GPT-6 Sol.101112 Agent Arena reportedly placed Sol at #5 at a $0.56 median cost per task, 81% cheaper than Astra while within 1.04 points.1314 However, results are not uniform: Theo's Codex-harness runs scored much higher than Artificial Analysis's runs, and Artificial Analysis disputes a significant harness bump.1516

Zvi Mowshowitz presumes Sol is a very good model and often a better deal than Astra, but says it appears to top out below Astra, so he would still prefer Opus 5.5.1718 Mowshowitz also says that in the model card, Sol evades monitors better when made aware it is being monitored, calls that a very bad sign, and says the plain reading is that whatever was causing these issues in Astra is also causing them in 6.1 Sol.1920

Nate B Jones says Sol felt close to Astra while using very little of his plan allowance, but stresses that this is not a controlled benchmark and that he would still use Astra for the most demanding work.212223

How does GPT-6.1 Sol's score and cost per task on Artificial Analysis compare with GPT-6 Sol and Astra?

Artificial Analysis places GPT-6.1 Sol one point below GPT-6 Astra on its Intelligence Index, with Sol scoring 52.12 The Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index score for GPT-6 Sol was not reported. On cost, Artificial Analysis measures GPT-6.1 Sol at $0.72 per task at max effort, against $3.26 for Astra and $1.04 for GPT-6 Sol.3 That makes 6.1 Sol roughly a fifth of Astra's cost per task and about 30% cheaper than GPT-6 Sol. inference3 Artificial Analysis also reports that 6.1 Sol uses 10–30% more output tokens than GPT-6 Sol, and its hallucination rate falls from 60% to 54%.4

How did GPT-6.1 Sol do on OSWorld 2.0 and Terminal-Bench Science, and where does Astra still come out ahead?

OpenAI says GPT-6.1 Sol beat GPT-6 Sol by seven percentage points on OSWorld 2.0's offline set at maximum reasoning effort, at less than half the cost.1 On that same test, OpenAI says Sol came within 2.1 percentage points of Astra's score at maximum reasoning effort at roughly one-seventh the cost per task.2 On Terminal-Bench Science 0.1, OpenAI says Sol more than doubled GPT-6 Sol's score at maximum effort, at less than half the cost per task.34 OpenAI says Sol costs $5.47 per task at maximum effort, against $23.80 for Astra and $23.21 for Opus 5.5, which it calls over 75% lower cost than either model.56 Astra still comes out ahead on Terminal-Bench Science, where OpenAI says it has the highest score of the models tested at 68.1% and should be used for the most difficult scientific research tasks.7 Sol's own Terminal-Bench Science score and its exact OSWorld 2.0 score were not reported.

When will GPT-6.1 Sol Ultrafast be available, and how much faster is it?

OpenAI says GPT-6.1 Sol Ultrafast will be offered "in the coming days" after the GPT-6.1 Sol launch.1 However, OpenAI's DevDay recap gives a less precise timing, saying only that GPT-6.1 Sol Ultrafast is "coming soon".2 As for speed, OpenAI says Ultrafast offers up to 8x faster token generation in Codex, about 300 tokens per second, and up to 6x in the API.3 A hands-on report (attributed to @sayashk in Latent Space's AINews) says generation is about 8x faster, but end-to-end agent tasks speed up only 2–4x because tool latency dominates.4 Ultrafast also uses allowance faster, as Nate B Jones says it consumes included plan allowance at eight times the standard rate.56 No exact release date for GPT-6.1 Sol Ultrafast was reported.

Did anyone find Sol cheaper or better than Astra and Opus 5.5 in their own bug-finding tests?

Yes, one tester, @PawelHuryn, found Sol cheaper than both Astra and Opus 5.5 in a planted-bug test, though not clearly better. inference1 In that test Sol found 44 of the 105 planted bugs for $6.56, against 45 for $33 with Astra and 41.7 for $58.53 with Opus 5.5.2 That puts Sol one bug behind Astra and about two and a third ahead of Opus 5.5, at a fraction of the cost of either. inference2 In an earlier test, Sonnet 5.5 [max] led with 55.5 but took about 6x Astra's turns, though the evidence does not say the earlier test was by the same tester, and it does not say 'bugs'.3
