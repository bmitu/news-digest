Virginia and Oregon sue SpaceX over Grok sexualized images

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 09:22 UTC
10 sources · tracked since Nov 24 · 4 new articles today · 3 independent origins: Reuters, The Verge, Virginia and Oregon attorneys general

Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones and Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield announced separate but similar lawsuits against SpaceX on Monday, alleging that its Grok chatbot generated and spread sexualized deepfakes and child sexual abuse material.12 Ars Technica reports they are the first states to sue SpaceX over Grok, and the suits also name X and SpaceX Super Intelligence, formerly xAI.34

The Virginia complaint alleges Musk's companies stripped out industry-standard safeguards and marketed a "spicy mode" to push Grok to new users, saying weak safeguards "were not a failure but a feature."5 The states also allege the companies falsely told consumers they were protected, including a "zero tolerance" policy on child sexual exploitation.67

The suits cite a Center for Countering Digital Harm study that found Grok posted about 3 million sexualized images on X in roughly eight days from late December, including about 23,000 that appeared to depict children.89 Ars Technica says the states also allege average Grok CSAM reports rose 88 percent from December 2025 to January 2026 and another 66 percent by June 2026.10 Virginia's complaint estimates about 6.4 million X accounts linked to the state and 986,624 Grok app downloads from Virginia IP addresses, and seeks $2,500 per willful violation of its Consumer Protection Act.1112

X Safety responded that sexual content involving minors is "abhorrent," blamed users who deliberately violated its terms and circumvented safeguards, and said it tightened protections promptly.1314 The states dispute this, alleging that Grok still produces such images and even advises users how to get around its moderation, and that Musk's January claim that Grok did not sexualize real people was contradicted.1516 Rayfield called the situation "an intentional business decision that they purposely profited off of," and the states seek injunctions, civil penalties and disgorgement of profits. inference1718
