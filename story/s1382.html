Amazon deploys Vulcan robotic arm

Last scan Tue Oct 20, 08:37 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 17 · 1 new article this week · 2 independent origins: ABC13, Ars Technica

Ars Technica reports that Amazon's newest robotic arm, Vulcan, is the first of its robotic arms to combine vision with force feedback sensors capable of providing a sense of touch.1 The touch capability is meant to let the robot pick and stow items within fabric-covered inventory pods.2 Dresser argued that inserting an item, creating space, or pulling something out are hard for traditional non-touch systems, such as the suction-based systems in Sparrow.34

Vulcan began operating at Amazon fulfillment centers in Spokane, Washington, and Hamburg, Germany, starting in 2025.5 It is not yet widely deployed, but Amazon's Dresser said the company expects to scale up its use in 2027.6

On worker safety, an Amazon spokesperson said its Global Recordable Incident Rate has improved 43 percent since 2019 and its Global Lost Time Incident rate has improved 70 percent, alongside its robotics growth.78 An ILR Review study published in April 2025 found a 40 percent decrease in severe injuries at Amazon's robotic warehouses compared with legacy centers, but also a 77 percent increase in less severe injuries tied to repetitive strain.910
