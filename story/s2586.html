Apollo economist says AI has not improved S&P 493 profits

Last scan Sun Nov 8, 08:53 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 30 · 2 new articles this week · single origin (Apollo)

Torsten Slok, chief economist at Apollo Global Management, says in a recent note that AI has delivered "zero financial payoff to corporate America outside of tech."12 Slok focuses on what he calls the S&P 493, which is the S&P 500 minus Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia, and Tesla.3 He finds that profit margins and revenue per worker appear to be flat, with no evidence that AI is improving productivity.4 Slok reportedly says that, with no bottom-line impact, mainstream corporate America is starting to view token spending as a cost center that weighs on margins rather than as a source of growth.5

TechCrunch reports that Slok sounded skeptical that the weak results simply reflect non-tech companies lagging behind, arguing it will be tough to turn increasingly cheap intelligence into higher revenue growth and profit margins.6 Slok argues that "in business, intelligence is mostly judgment," and that AI does not understand accountability, trust, culture, unwritten rules, ethical trade-offs or fairness.789 TechCrunch adds that recent studies have also found AI has yet to deliver significant productivity gains.10

Writing in Above the Law, lawyer Stephen Embry argues that Slok's ideas are particularly chilling for AI hype in legal, because every billable hour AI saves is an hour of lost revenue.1112 Embry says Clio and 8am reports showed billable hours going up even though AI was supposed to bring them down, and he suggests this may be explained by AI mostly cutting back-office costs.1314 Embry also contends that because law relies on judgment that AI cannot provide, the price of that judgment could go up and wholesale fee reductions may never happen.1516
