Instinct reaches $10 billion valuation

Last scan Sat Oct 10, 12:51 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 4 · 1 new article this week · single origin (The Verge)

The Verge reports the company was valued at $10 billion in late September, even after the launch of Muse, and says the quiet release drew praise from testers and a vote of confidence from investors.12 The valuation is a steep jump from its earlier round, when Instinct raised $350 million at a $2.5 billion valuation.3

Instinct remains in private beta, with no app and no monthly subscription fee for now, and access requires an invitation from a current user or approval from a waitlist.456 Shinn says Instinct is free for invite-only users and that the intent is to keep it as affordable as possible, while the reviewer expects users to pay in the future.78

Shinn, who was an early employee of Sierra, says the lack of a traditional app interface is a crucial part of what he thinks separates Instinct from the big players.910 TechCrunch says Instinct has begun giving users dedicated email addresses for their assistants and is rolling out support for placing calls, though it notes this autonomy has raised privacy and security concerns.111213 The Verge's reviewer found that Instinct handled tasks like emailing an eye doctor and starting an Ikea return well, but it missed a prerequisite class when searching for swimming lessons.1415

Analyst Avi Greengart of Techsponential says a venture-backed startup's main aim is growth, and suggests Instinct could be eyeing customer revenue or acquisition by a bigger company.1617
