Nvidia pauses server shipments to Southeast Asia

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 17:01 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 27 · 1 new article today · single origin (The National)

Nvidia has told manufacturing partners such as Dell and Super Micro to stop sending servers to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia for now, according to a Wall Street Journal report that TechCrunch relayed on November 26, 2026.12 The Journal reportedly says the halt has no specified length and is meant to give Nvidia time to check whether partners in the region comply with U.S. export rules.3 Separately, a Latent Space roundup citing The National describes a reported halt in AI chip shipments to South-East Asia, linking it to stricter U.S. enforcement aimed at keeping advanced accelerators from restricted users, especially in China.4

TechCrunch attributes the choice of countries to Chinese companies having used Nvidia chips remotely through data centers there, a loophole that it says the U.S. Commerce Department closed earlier in November, "reportedly grudgingly." inference56 According to the Journal, the Institute for Progress, a think tank, puts the share of China's AI computing power coming from such remote facilities at about 24%.7

Online commenters summarized by Latent Space claim that Blackwell and B200 server purchases are now affected, and that Southeast Asian system integrators must justify their demand before buying.8 One commenter described as working with the hardware supply chain says such justifications go to the U.S. government, prices are "multiplicatively more expensive," and lead times have gone from about a year to effectively unknown.910

Looking ahead, commenters predict that the grey market will not vanish but shift toward Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) countries or shell companies able to resell GPUs to China. inference1112 Some also worry that less access to Nvidia hardware could slow work on open models in Southeast Asia while indirectly helping well-funded U.S. labs such as OpenAI and Anthropic.13
