Jev as Judge paper published

Last scan Mon Oct 5, 14:51 UTC
2 sources · 113 points on Hacker News · tracked since Sep 29 · 1 new article today · single origin, unclear which

A paper on "Jev as a Judge" reports that TypeSafe's Jev model costs $0.044 per 1K judgments at 152ms median latency, about 277× cheaper than GPT-6.1 Per the paper, Jev stays within 3 points on RewardBench and HaluEval but trails by 14.5 points on JudgeBench.2 The paper also reports that a cascade escalating low-confidence calls to GPT-6 Astra keeps 99% of accuracy at 57% of the cost.3 Jev is described as trained with RL for Calibrated Decisions, returning typed decisions with probabilities rather than reasoning text.4

Production signals cited include Jev being the top model at 1K–10K context on OpenRouter and proving 140 Software Foundations theorems for under $1, about 130× cheaper than Astra.56 Meanwhile, TypeSafe is reportedly raising $1B+ at a $10B+ valuation, a week after a $200M round.7

A Reddit post argues Jev appears to be standard constrained-choice classification rather than a new model class, and that the right baselines are zero-shot/NLI classifiers, embedding models and rerankers.89 The post cites an external Banking77 baseline in which BGE-small plus logistic regression reportedly scored 93.3% versus Jev at 83.2%, and notes that Typesafe's own explanation only guarantees schema-valid outputs, not factual correctness.1011 Commenters were split: several likened Jev to long-standing classifiers such as spaCy and scikit-learn, while one said scaling zero-shot classifiers could still be commercially valuable.1213

A separate post presents CLM as an open-weights alternative to Jev, which it concedes is weaker on zero-shot breadth (BFCL v4 95.2% vs Jev 99.2%), while commenters dispute the "Jev competitor" framing.1415

Which rival decision models have appeared, and how does Perplexity's pplx-decider-v1-27b score against Jev?

Cloudflare released two Cloudflare-trained decision models, Clef and Clef-flash, hosted on Workers AI (Oct. 1, 2026), and says Clef is currently the leader when evaluated against the Jev Decision Index.12 Other rival models include CLM, a contrastive model that ranks candidate actions and is said to be about 9× faster than Jev, plus Fastino's GLiNER2.5-Decide and the Jev-like Tev1 0.8B.3456 Perplexity claims its pplx-decider-v1-27b averages 85.7% across 11 benchmarks, ahead of Jev.78 However, Jev's own average on those benchmarks was not given, so the size of Perplexity's lead is unknown.

Yes. One commenter raised a concern about how Clef holds up after quantization, especially below Q8.45 On tooling, llama.cpp added a /v1/systemone endpoint for local Jev-style decision-model inference, and a Kev-4B GGUF model can be launched with llama serve.
