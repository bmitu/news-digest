Astra Standard Model lattice proposal

Last scan Sun Nov 22, 14:05 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 20 · single origin (OpenAI)

Nathaniel Craig, a physicist serving as a visiting scientist at OpenAI, reports that he collaborated with GPT-6 Astra to develop a possible lattice formulation of the Standard Model; the work appears in the inaugural guest post on the OpenAI science blog.1 A second source, Latent Space, mentions the work only briefly, as a physics showcase in which Craig used GPT-6 Astra to develop the candidate definition.2

Craig says the problem is a nonperturbative definition of the full theory, one that does not rely on treating interactions as small corrections, and that he proposed a candidate in an October preprint prepared with GPT-6 Astra's help.34 Craig says the difficulty lies in the weak force, which treats left- and right-handed fermions differently, making the Standard Model chiral, and that the most natural lattice rules give an unwanted right-handed partner.56

His strategy was to represent the force fields on a succession of finer grids and compare allowed fermion states between successive grids, with the parts added up to give the phase rule on the original grid.78 Craig credits Astra with suggesting how to carry force fields onto finer grids, showing the comparisons would converge, and taking the lead in developing the detailed proof.9 He says lattice field theorists Maarten Golterman, Andreas Kronfeld, and Yigal Shamir reviewed Astra's proof and asked probing questions that helped build his confidence in the result.10

Craig frames the result cautiously: it potentially answers a question open for more than fifty years, but he calls it more a beginning than an end and says expert dialogue is needed to decide whether it has all the desired properties.1112 Craig cautions that the approach offers a theoretical solution much more than a practical one, and says he is currently giving a great deal of thought to whether the construction meets reflection positivity when the lattice spacing is finite.1314
