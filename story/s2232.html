OpenAI math and physics results

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
6 sources · 3 independent origins: OpenAI, TechCrunch, The Verge

OpenAI announced on Monday what it describes as breakthroughs in mathematics, computer science and physics.1

In mathematics, OpenAI says its models proved the local smoothing conjecture in every dimension, which it says also resolves the Kakeya and Stein restriction conjectures in all dimensions.2 Brown University mathematician Brendan Hassett told The Verge that OpenAI's math claim "looks well supported", though he had not yet checked the Lean formalization.3

In computer science, OpenAI's paper gives a deterministic subexponential-time algorithm for counting satisfying assignments of fixed-width CNF formulas, and the 3-SAT case is said to refute the Exponential Time Hypothesis.4 Ryan Williams told TechCrunch that parts of the proof are routine but two or three look "alien", and that he does not think OpenAI's algorithm is the best one possible.56 Williams also said OpenAI told him it had checked and was reasonably confident the result implies no cryptographic breaks, and Anthropic released its own refutation of the hypothesis hours after Anthropic's Levent Alpöge suggested OpenAI had built on pre-existing work. inference789

In physics, OpenAI claims a mathematically rigorous existence proof of certain interacting quantum field theories, including QCD, but The Verge notes that the work does not address the unresolved Yang-Mills "mass gap".1011

The three results were released differently: the math output went straight to GitHub, a group led by Russell Impagliazzo, Ramamohan Paturi and Ryan Williams will examine the computer science proof, and a group led by David Gross is holding back the physics artifacts. inference12131415 Several researchers criticized the choice of handpicked reviewers: physicist Andreas Karch called it "a disaster", and the Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence, which OpenAI had previously pledged to consult, had recommended against the practice a day earlier. inference161718

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman said on a podcast that "Humanity deserves fundamental knowledge" and that OpenAI should not sit on major discoveries, and he confirmed the company has more results than it has released.1920
