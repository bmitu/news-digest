Google privacy policy ad personalization

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 14:03 UTC
2 sources · 2 independent origins: Android Authority, Ars Technica

Google updated its privacy policy on October 16 so that its promise not to use Drive, Gmail or Google Photos content for personalized ads now carries an exception, which was spotted by Android Authority.12 The new line says the content is not used for personalized ads "unless you choose to make data from these services available to other Google services that support personalized ads."34 The Verge says this means people who connected Drive, Gmail or Photos to AI Mode and other Search tools through Personal Intelligence may now see ads targeted on the basis of that content.56

Ars Technica writes that the change appears to dismantle the firewall between that data and ad personalization, and that Google has a mechanism for sharing the data in Personal Intelligence, which it released in early 2026.78 Ars Technica's Ryan Whitwam argues that Google's statement essentially confirms that Workspace and Photos data may inform ads, and that Google uses prompts and pop-ups to entice users to turn on Personal Intelligence without fully explaining the consequences.910

Google says connecting apps is entirely opt-in and that ads will not be based directly on a user's Gmail inbox or Photos library.1112 The Verge adds that advertisers do not get access to Drive, Gmail and Photos information or personally identifying details, and that Google won't show ads based on sensitive categories such as race, sexual orientation, religion or health.1314

Both outlets say users can avoid this by disconnecting apps from Personal Intelligence or turning off personalized recommendations in the Search personalization settings. inference1516
