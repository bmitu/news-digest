GLM 5.3 Firefox exploits

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
3 sources · single origin (Epoch AI)

Epoch AI says its experiments found that GLM 5.3, an open-weight model released by Z.ai in August, could autonomously build a Firefox exploit using only about $1,000 of compute, by reverse-engineering vulnerabilities from Mozilla security fixes that had been published but not yet shipped to users.12 Epoch says the model built a webpage that, when visited by a Firefox user, read a file from the visitor's computer and sent it to the attacker with no further interaction, and that this worked on all six Firefox versions released between September 17 and October 26.34 Epoch reports that each version cost about $1,000 in compute to attack, though its total Firefox research spend exceeded $100,000 in API costs; GLM 5.3 was given up to 48 hours per attempt, and its fastest needed 12 hours.5678

According to Epoch, the attacks on the most recent versions chained a patch-gap vulnerability with a separate, unpublished one; Epoch shared it with Mozilla and it was patched in Firefox 159.0 on October 27.910 Epoch also says it triggered no safeguards or refusals from any provider, including Z.ai, when using GLM 5.3 in this way.11

Similar attacks on Google Chrome did not succeed despite around $100,000 in compute, but Epoch says this is not strong evidence that attacks wouldn't succeed with more advanced prompts or more capable open-weight models.121314 Epoch argues defenders should shrink the patch gap, for example by keeping fixes private until ready to deploy, shipping from beta to release faster, and avoiding patching chainable vulnerabilities together, while declining to claim anything about the overall offense-defense balance.1516

Nathan Lambert's Interconnects post cites the Epoch work as an example of why cyber risks need multiple things to align for harm, noting that a would-be hacker must plant a website looking for the exploit and that finding the exploit is only one step in a multi-stage process. inference1718 The Interconnects author argues that the evidence against open models specifically is very low, saying closed models have played a heavier part in recent hacks and that the issue is more about AI capability and diffusion generally than open versus closed.1920
