OpenAI releases batch of AI-generated math manuscripts

Last scan Sat Oct 10, 12:51 UTC
6 sources · 1345 points on Hacker News · 3 independent origins: OpenAI, TechCrunch, The Verge

OpenAI released a broad set of mathematical results produced by an internal frontier model, publishing them in a GitHub repository.123 The Verge reports the batch was 722 manuscripts covering 372 result families, and that OpenAI's model is unreleased.456 Zvi Mowshowitz says the release included 90 of the top 500 open problems in math, as per Proof of Atlas, and that three manuscripts were later withdrawn, leaving 719.78 OpenAI says the average result used the equivalent of roughly three hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking compute, and Mowshowitz says the model was asked to try about 4,000 problems.91011

Verification is uneven: OpenAI acknowledged the results are "at different stages of verification," and The Verge reports OpenAI said only 300 top-line results out of 719 manuscripts, around 42 percent, had been formalized.1213 TechCrunch reports a new paper by mathematicians at the University of Cambridge and King's College London documented at least two discrepancies between the natural-language proof and the Lean code behind the solution OpenAI has offered to a problem derived from the Navier-Stokes equations; these discrepancies don't necessarily disprove either solution.1415

OpenAI says it consulted the Institute for Advanced Study's Advisory Group on Mathematics and AI (AGMAI), but TechCrunch argues it fell short of the group's standards, and The Verge reports it did not name the model or disclose the prompts or full problem set.16171819 In a statement quoted by Mowshowitz, AGMAI said its advisory role should not be read as a judgment of the results' impact or an endorsement of OpenAI's process.20

Zvi Mowshowitz relays that Levant and others called October 6, 2026 "obviously the most significant moment in mathematical history," and Kevin A. Bryan, after going through the list, said it is "bonkers."2122 Mathematicians told The Verge it could take years to understand the release, and Bartosz Naskręcki warned of a possible "huge collapse in the academic culture."2324
