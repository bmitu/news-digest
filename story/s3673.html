AI agents find Maya site

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
2 sources · 1035 points on Hacker News · 2 independent origins: Pietro Miotti, The Verge

Google researcher Pietro Miotti claimed to have found a potentially unmapped Maya site in Mexico using a team of 48 AI agents that searched NASA lidar data.12 Miotti says INAH replied that it had checked its records and found no registered archaeological site at that location, and that it took less than 72 hours to go from his initial idea to that official reply.3456 However, the site had in fact already been documented in an academic journal more than a year earlier, and Miotti later updated his blog to say the agents had rediscovered rather than discovered it.78

Miotti notes that archaeologists are right to keep the locations of their sites private, so anyone wanting to know whether a site has already been identified has to match pictures against illustrations in published studies instead of querying a database.910 Archaeologist Sarah Parcak of the University of Alabama at Birmingham criticized the oversight on Bluesky, saying "This is why we have peer review" and that absence from a government database does not mean a site is unknown.11

Other archaeologists reportedly accused the project of spreading misinformation and taking credit from archaeologists, who have used digital tools for decades.12 Elizabeth Graham of University College London told The Verge that finding new sites is not the field's "problem" and that the bigger obstacle is funding for research.1314

Miotti still argues the outcome is encouraging because an outsider with agents landed where experts had, and he says some archaeologists have asked how the workflow could help with their own survey data.1516
