Microsoft voluntary exit offer

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
3 sources · 2 independent origins: Hunter Ash, The Verge

Microsoft is offering voluntary exit buyouts to around 60,000 employees across the US, UK, Canada, and India, about 27% of its 223,000 workers.1 The Verge says it is the first time the company has made such an offer to so many employees, greatly expanding on a retirement offer limited to around 9,000 employees earlier this year.2 The Verge also reports that Microsoft employed around 223,000 people as of June 30th, 5,000 fewer than the 228,000 in 2025, and that the offer came just months after 4,800 layoffs.34

According to The Verge, sources familiar say the terms are practically the same as regular Microsoft severance: cash based on time at the company, accelerated stock vesting, some subsidized health coverage, and several months of career services.5 The Verge expects far fewer acceptances than the earlier retirement offer, which more than 30 percent of 9,000 eligible employees accepted, and says even 5 percent would be 3,000 people.67 The Verge's writer understands eligible employees have until December to decide, and says rumors of more layoffs in November and January mean acceptances will likely inform the next round of cuts.89

The Verge ties the offer to Satya Nadella's earlier comment that people costs will go down while cost per head and GPU per researcher go up, and describes it as part of an effort to cut operating costs without reducing AI infrastructure investment.1011 Exponential View, meanwhile, notes that even vice presidents are within reach and says this is not a round of layoffs, though it may be the prelude to one.12 Exponential View argues that neither AI-driven cost pressure nor Covid-era bloat fits the facts, saying revenue per employee is up two-thirds since 2022 to $1.5 million and that the offer reflects confidence the company can run much leaner.131415 The two accounts differ in emphasis: The Verge links the offer to AI-related cost and headcount strategy, while Exponential View rejects the common AI-capex and Covid explanations in favor of a productivity bet. inference1016
