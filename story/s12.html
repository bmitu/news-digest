TypeSafe Jev decision model launch

2 sources · 155 points on Hacker News · tracked since Sep 27 · 2 independent origins: Allan Bo, TypeSafe AI

Latent Space's newsletter reports that TypeSafe is reportedly raising $1B+ at a $10B+ valuation, a week after a $200M round, and describes its Jev model as trained with reinforcement learning to return typed decisions with probabilities rather than reasoning text.12

A Jev-as-a-Judge paper reports that Jev costs $0.044 per 1K judgments at 152ms median latency, about 277× cheaper than GPT-6.3 According to the paper, Jev stays within 3 points on RewardBench and HaluEval but trails by 14.5 points on JudgeBench.4 The paper also says a cascade that escalates low-confidence calls to GPT-6 Astra keeps 99% of accuracy at 57% of the cost.5

Latent Space also notes ecosystem signals: turbopuffer's native reranking includes Jev, and Jev is the top model at 1K–10K context on OpenRouter.6

A Reddit post argues that Jev is not new technology and appears to expose standard constrained-choice classification semantics rather than a fundamentally new model class.78 That post cites an external Banking77 baseline in which BGE-small plus logistic regression reportedly scored 93.3% versus Jev's 83.2%, and notes that TypeSafe's own explanation only guarantees schema-conformant outputs, which challenges the "0% hallucination" framing.910

Commenters were split: some likened Jev to spaCy and scikit-learn classifiers, while one argued that scaling zero-shot classifiers could still be commercially valuable.111213 A separate post presents CLM as an open-weights, self-hostable Jev alternative, claiming 4×–13× lower latency in agent-style benchmarks but weaker zero-shot breadth (BFCL v4 95.2% vs Jev's 99.2%), while commenters disputed the "Jev competitor" framing.141516

How does CLM's latency, context length and coding-verifier scoring (DeepSWE, Terminal-Bench) compare with Jev's?

On context length, Jev has the advantage: CLM's calibrated context is 2K–8K, against 64K for Jev.1 On coding verifiers, CLM is reported at 81.6% on DeepSWE and 87.6% on Terminal-Bench 2.1, against roughly 71% for Jev on DeepSWE.23 CLM's edge on DeepSWE is therefore about ten points, though these are figures from the CLM post and team, not independent tests. inference4 Commenters dispute framing CLM as a Jev competitor, since Jev's core value is zero-shot broad knowledge, where CLM trails on BFCL v4 (95.2% vs 99.2%).567

How did OpenAI respond to Jev at DevDay, and how does its Decisions API differ from Jev?

At DevDay, OpenAI launched a Decisions API, which Latent Space describes as a rapid answer to the Jev model it had covered on its podcast.1 The Decisions API offers near-instant multiple-choice classification and routing on GPT-6 Luna, over text and images, and Latent Space says many read it as a Jev competitor.2 Latent Space says the API is for now a light shim over Luna, so it gets vision but lacks the calibration/RLCD that Jev has.3 Jev, by contrast, is trained with RL for calibrated decisions and returns typed decisions with probabilities rather than reasoning text.45 Latent Space also stresses that the point of Jev is System One models built for decisions, so a shim over a general model misses that point. inference67

What other cheap decision models are emerging, such as Together's tev1-4B or Fastino's GLiNER2.5-Decide, and what do they cost or how fast are they?

Together released tev1-4B, a Qwen3.5-4B classifier that cost $17 to train.1 A smaller Jev-like classifier, Tev1 0.8B, runs at about 50ms end to end locally on Ollama.23 Fastino's GLiNER2.5-Decide adds spans, relations and constraint-consistent structured decisions, and runs at 167ms on CPU and 38–47ms on GPU.45 CLM-8B, an open-weights contrastive model, is up to 9x faster than Jev at comparable zero-shot agent performance, and its weights and data have been released.678 Per-query prices for GLiNER2.5-Decide, CLM and Tev1 were not reported, and neither was a serving cost for tev1-4B.

Why does Jev's schema-conformant output not guarantee the chosen answer is factually correct, and what does that mean for the "0% hallucination" claim?

Jev returns typed decisions with probabilities rather than reasoning text, so its answer is a choice among fixed labels, and a label can be valid in form but still wrong. inference123 A Reddit post, as summarized by Latent Space, says TypeSafe's own explanation only guarantees outputs conform to the allowed schema, not that the selected valid class is factually correct.45 So the "0% hallucination" claim, as the critic reads it, describes well-formed output and not accuracy, and Jev can still pick the wrong valid answer. inference675
