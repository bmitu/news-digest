Claude discovers novel enzyme system

Last scan Mon Oct 5, 14:51 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Sep 27 · single origin (Anthropic)

Anthropic announced that earlier this year it launched a molecular biology lab in which Claude agents read and conjecture about hard biology problems while human scientists run experiments on what they report, and said the lab had made its first discovery.12 Anthropic reports that the agents identified a previously uncharacterized bacteriophage system called array-associated reverse transcriptases (ART): a reverse transcriptase gene plus an accessory gene next to a long CRISPR-like tandem repeat array.3

According to the account of the campaign, about 950 Claude agents used 210M tokens over 21 hours to collect more than 200,000 reverse transcriptases, nominate 3,500 candidate systems and prioritize 20 reports.4 Early validation found the ART array is transcribed into distinct short RNAs, but Anthropic explicitly says the system's biological function and any programmable editing utility remain unknown.5 MIT Technology Review says that what Anthropic says its system found was not a brand-new sequence; the agents instead flagged a repeating pattern surrounding a known enzyme, a particular pattern Anthropic said hadn't been catalogued before.67

The claims angered some biologists; a viral post from one, subsequently endorsed by the chair and CEO of Eli Lilly, said that finding a weird cluster of genes and repeats is often the easy part, and that the hard part, where the real discoveries come from, is figuring out what the system actually does.89 Lucas Harrington, the biologist who wrote the critical post, said AI companies should "set the bar high now" so that a real discovery of a fundamentally new biological mechanism is appreciated.1011 Mario Rodríguez Mestre, a University of Copenhagen biologist, said his team had already discovered this particular pattern, according to the New York Times; he wondered whether Anthropic's team had learned from his conversations with Claude, Anthropic denies this, and he says he is stopping all use of Claude anyway.121314

On a forum, a technical critic argued the work appears incomplete because identifying repeat arrays and showing short RNAs is a fairly standard genomics workflow, while other commenters saw the methodological result, Claude selecting a candidate and escalating it for human validation, as possible early evidence of AI as a research collaborator.1516 MIT Technology Review's newsletter writer calls whittling 200,000 candidates down to a few legitimate scientific work and notable for a general-purpose chatbot, but argues that framing the AI as making discoveries itself pushes the debate into "breakthrough or bust."1718
