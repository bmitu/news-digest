Claude discovers novel enzyme system

4 sources · 780 points on Hacker News · tracked since Sep 27 · 3 new today · single origin (Anthropic)

Anthropic announced that Claude autonomously discovered a novel enzyme system in bacteriophages, called array-associated reverse transcriptases (ART), whose layout is reminiscent of CRISPR, and the company says it does not yet know the system's function.12 Anthropic says the system has a set of characteristics found together in only a handful of other systems, all of which are programmable and cut, copy and paste DNA.3

Anthropic says roughly 950 agents spent 21 hours and 210 million tokens searching the data before one agent spotted a repeating DNA pattern next to the gene for an odd-looking reverse transcriptase.45 Anthropic also says the agents gathered over 200,000 reverse transcriptases, picked 3,500 new candidate systems and narrowed them to 20 reports.6 Anthropic says its early experiments show the ART array is expressed as distinct short RNAs, and that its involvement was limited to the initial prompt and the lab work.78

Zvi Mowshowitz judges that as biology it is not a big deal, and that a PhD student finding it would be cool but would not make headlines.9 According to a Latent Space digest, Dario Amodei called it PhD-worthy but of unclear significance, and noted that a Stanford team independently described a distinct reverse transcriptase system with a non-coding array.1011

Critics questioned the result: @suchenzang questioned the agent-hour accounting and the lack of wet-lab detail, and @iScienceLuvr said the lab work was "very limited".1213 One commenter argued the work appears incomplete because identifying repeat arrays and showing they produce short RNAs is a fairly standard genomics workflow.1415 Other commenters said the most important result may be methodological, with Claude selecting an unusual candidate and escalating it for human validation, as early evidence of AI as a research collaborator.161718
