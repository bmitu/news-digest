Kentucky religious exemption from AI

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
2 sources · 491 points on Hacker News · single origin (TechCrunch)

David Durman, a nontheistic humanist and University of Kentucky administrator, has secured a religious exemption from using AI in the workplace, according to TechCrunch.12 TechCrunch reports he is the first humanist to secure workplace accommodations allowing him to abstain from AI use, and one of the first people to publicly discuss pursuing such protections.3 TechCrunch notes Durman has not been required to use AI in his grant-support role, but some colleagues and leaders have recommended trying such tools, so he sought the exemption in case the suggestion becomes a mandate.45

Durman says he sought the exemption through the American Humanist Association (AHA) because he believes using AI runs counter to his beliefs, arguing that diminishing the active application of one's mind through AI would harm people's ability to be moral actors.67 In an AHA statement, Durman also wrote that AI interferes with people's ability to earn a living, carries environmental costs, and supplants and steals human creativity.8

University of Kentucky spokesperson Jay Blanton says the university reviews religious accommodation requests individually and determined that an accommodation could be provided while the employee continued performing the essential functions of his position.910 Blanton added that accommodations may be reviewed as technology and job responsibilities evolve to ensure they remain reasonable and workable.11

Alexandria Huiskens, an AHA staff attorney, says concern about AI and human dignity runs across several faiths and traditions, and that humanists and nontheists are also protected by workplace law.1213 Durman says he was inspired by Unitarian Universalist software engineer Erin Maus, who secured similar protections, and he hopes others with similar concerns will pursue resolutions of their own.1415
