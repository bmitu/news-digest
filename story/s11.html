Gemini 3.8 TTS release

2 sources · tracked since Sep 27 · 2 independent origins: Google, Latent Space

Google DeepMind announced two new text-to-speech models for the Gemini family, Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS and Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS, saying they turn voice generation from static presets into a dynamic creative studio.1 Google describes Flash TTS as built for deep creative direction and character design, and Flash-Lite TTS as built for high-volume, cost-efficient scale.2 Google says both models give line-by-line control of delivery, and lists long-form generation, native two-speaker scenes, and scripted vocal bursts such as <laughs> and backchanneling. It says voice remixing is coming soon.345

Google reports that Flash TTS ranked first on Hume AI’s Voice Design Benchmark with a score of 71.4 and led in accent modeling at 60.8. It says Flash TTS and Flash-Lite TTS took the #1 and #2 spots on Hume AI’s Overall Quality Index.67 Google also claims major improvements over Gemini 3.1 Flash TTS in long-form content and dual-speaker control. It says both models placed at the top in blind human preference evaluations on Voice Arena in several languages. These figures are Google's own claims, and the passages give no independent verification. inference89

Both models are rolling out starting today in the Gemini API and Google AI Studio. Flash TTS is also available to everyone in Gemini Notebook, and Flash-Lite TTS in Google Vids. Enterprise access via Gemini Enterprise is coming soon.1011 Google names Figma, HeyGen, Linguana, Wondercraft, 99.co and Ollang as partners integrating the new TTS models.12

Related questions

What safeguards does Google use to stop voice cloning from being misused?

Google says voice replication in its new Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS model requires consent verification: users must provide a verbal consent recording from the voice owner that matches the reference speaker before a voice can be created.1 Google also says voice replication works from a 30-second sample of your own voice or one you have the rights to use, and is backed by C2PA credentials alongside consent verification and SynthID.2 Google says every audio clip generated by its Gemini Audio models is watermarked with SynthID, an imperceptible watermark woven into the audio that is meant to keep AI-generated speech detectable and help prevent misinformation.3

How many voices can you choose from, and how long a voice sample do you need to clone your own voice?

Google DeepMind says its new Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS model gives access to more than 2,000 production-ready voices.1 Google also says the model can create bespoke voices from scratch using natural language prompting, and describes this as scaling up from 30 original voices to an infinite library.23 Google adds that a voice owner must also provide a verbal consent recording that matches the reference speaker before a voice can be created.4
