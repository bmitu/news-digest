Gemini 3.8 TTS release

2 sources · tracked since Sep 27 · single origin (Google)

Google DeepMind announced two new text-to-speech models for the Gemini family, Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS and Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS, saying they turn voice generation from static presets into a dynamic creative studio.1 Google says Flash TTS is built for creative direction and character design, letting users create new voices from natural language prompts, while Flash-Lite TTS is built for high-volume, cost-efficient scale such as dubbing and voice agents.23

Google lists features including voice design across more than 100 languages and dialects, a library of 2,000+ voices, and voice replication from a 30-second sample with consent verification, SynthID watermarking and C2PA credentials.456

Google claims Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS ranks #1 on Hume AI’s Voice Design Benchmark (71.4) and in accent modeling (60.8), and that Flash and Flash-Lite TTS take the #1 and #2 spots on Hume AI’s Overall Quality Index.78 Google says the models improve on Gemini 3.1 Flash TTS in long-form content and dual-speaker control, and that they placed at the top in blind human preference evaluations on Voice Arena in languages including Japanese, Hindi and Mexican Spanish.910

Both models began rolling out on the announcement day in the Gemini API and Google AI Studio, with Flash TTS also in Gemini Notebook and Flash-Lite TTS in Google Vids, and enterprise access via Gemini Enterprise coming soon.1112 Google names partners including Figma, HeyGen and Wondercraft as integrating the models; the only other coverage provided, a Latent Space newsletter, does not discuss the TTS release and covers only Gemini 3.8 Flash's text benchmarks. inference1314

How does Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS compare with Gemini 3.1 Flash TTS, and what exactly got better in long-form audio and two-speaker scenes?

Google DeepMind says Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS shows major improvements over Gemini 3.1 Flash TTS, including in long-form content and dual-speaker screenplay control.1 For long-form audio, Google says the model keeps voice quality, natural pacing and character timbre steady across hours of continuous audio with minimal speaker drift, which it pitches for podcasts and audiobooks.2 For two-speaker scenes, Google says the model can direct multi-turn conversations from a single script, keeping both voices distinctly separated with natural turn-taking.3 Google does not say how much better 3.8 is than 3.1 in these areas, and it gives no side-by-side figures for long-form or two-speaker quality.

How does Google's consent check work when someone clones a voice, and what stops people misusing it?

Google says the feature works from a 30-second audio sample of your own voice or a voice you have the rights to use, and that it is backed by consent verification, SynthID watermarking and C2PA credentials.1 On consent, Google says its voice replication system requires users to supply a verbal consent recording from the voice owner, and that recording must match the reference speaker before a voice can be created.2 Google also says every audio clip made by its Gemini Audio models carries an imperceptible SynthID watermark, which it says keeps AI-generated speech detectable and helps prevent misinformation.34 Google describes these as strict safeguards meant to protect voice talent, respect identity and keep content transparent.5

What do the vocal cues like <laughs>, <sigh> and |mhm| do, and how do they help voice agents sound natural?

Google DeepMind says Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS lets creators add scripted vocal bursts, such as <laughs>, <sigh> and <gasp>, as non-verbal cues within a script.1 Google describes cues like |mhm| and |yeah| as active-listening interjections, and lists backchanneling among the controls offered by Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS.23 Google says the non-verbal cues and active-listening interjections add realistic conversational texture, and that they are for precise comedic timing and reaction beats.456 Google positions Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS for interactive voice agents, saying it enables natural, highly expressive conversations, and says Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS is optimized for expressive voice agents with fine-grained control over tone, pacing, and expressive nuance.789 Taken together, the cues appear to make agents sound more natural by inserting the laughs, sighs and listening sounds people use in real conversation, though Google does not explicitly say so for the voice-agent use case. inference47
