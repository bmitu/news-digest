Amateur exoplanet search with AI

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 17:01 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 25 · single origin (OpenAI)

OpenAI says two amateurs, engineer Kevin Bond and Primitive founder Ethan Byrd, used its AI tools (Codex and Astra) to search public telescope data and both turned up potential exoplanets.12 The search Bond built with Codex turned up over 200 signals, ten of which came from planets already confirmed, and OpenAI says that result made him trust the program.34 Byrd, working independently of Bond, chose Astra after trying several AI models, and analyzed data from about 19 million stars, drawing on TESS and other public archives.56

One standout star, KIC 5689121 in Lyra, showed a dip every 13.8 days; if a planet causes it, it could be about 2.4 times Earth's diameter.78

Byrd sent one of his strongest candidates to researchers on NASA's TESS mission, where it is in line for further analysis, and two more candidates await submission.9

A Latent Space newsletter summarizing the announcement says a SETI astronomer independently confirmed one 13.8-day signal and that 49 TESS candidates were flagged, figures the OpenAI passage does not state. inference1011 OpenAI's passage stresses that the candidates are still unverified, noting that astronomers would need statistics, further observations and peer-reviewed publication to establish a planet.1213
