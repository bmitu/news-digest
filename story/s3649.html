Meta Muse Ember release

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
4 sources · 2 independent origins: Meta, Simon Willison

Meta Superintelligence Lab released Muse Ember on Monday, which TechCrunch describes as the lab's lightest and most cost-efficient model yet.1 Meta says Ember is designed for the repetitive work of agents, such as tool calls, coding subtasks, routing, extraction and long document parsing.23 Meta intends Ember to be paired with a larger model such as Muse Wonder, and trained it to handle text, images and audio.45

For shorter prompts, Ember costs $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens, the same as GPT-6 Luna and Claude Haiku 5.5, according to Simon Willison.6 Willison's table shows that for longer prompts (above 200k tokens) Ember's price rises to $0.30 input and $1.50 output, placing it between Luna and Haiku.78

Meta uses Artificial Analysis's composite score to argue Ember is cheaper and smarter than Haiku and Luna, and Willison says it sits on the Pareto line of Intelligence Index versus cost per task.910 Additional numbers come from Agent Arena, which measured a 1.7% gain at roughly $0.02 per task, around four times less expensive than Luna and Haiku 5.5, and from Vals, which found Ember delivers 85% of what Fable 5.2 does while costing just 0.84% as much.1112 According to Latent Space, Ember is said to match Haiku 5.5 on the Intelligence Index for roughly a third of the price, and a single user observed speeds near 400 tokens a second.131415 However, Zvi Mowshowitz is skeptical, saying Meta claims the model is good but that he does not believe them.16

Latent Space reports that the anonymous "crouton-alpha" stealth model was an early Ember checkpoint that processed over 5T tokens in testing, and that Ember launched on Google Cloud alongside Muse Wonder and Muse Spark 1.3.1718
