Alleged Chinese spy tied to Jensen Huang

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
5 sources · tracked since Oct 30 · 6 new articles this week · 2 independent origins: Politico, The New York Times

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has been caught up in an alleged Chinese espionage scandal, and Nvidia has opened an internal investigation into whether he was targeted by a Chinese intelligence operation.12 Huang acknowledged having had an affair after reports surfaced of a relationship with a Megaspeed executive; Megaspeed is a data center firm headquartered in Singapore, and the U.S. government suspects the executive of spying for China.345 According to TechCrunch, citing multiple media reports, a memo based on U.S. intelligence claimed China's Ministry of State Security recruited Alice Huang to spy on Jensen Huang, and the Financial Times reported the memo also claimed a romantic relationship.67 The Verge's podcast intro later said Huang had resigned as Nvidia CEO as the news threatened to engulf the company.8

An Nvidia spokesperson said Huang's interactions with Alice Huang were "not unusual" and that the company does not share classified information with customers.9 According to the Center for Security and Emerging Technology, Nvidia's board has stated that it sees nothing indicating trade secrets or national-security information were compromised, while China rejects the accusations.10 CSET further warns that the accusations do not establish that proprietary technology was passed on, that Huang behaved improperly with respect to national security, or that any particular change in policy is justified.11

Republican Representative Michael McCaul called for the U.S. to pause shipments of advanced AI chips to China until the incident is understood.12 Henna Virkkunen, the EU's technology chief, said Europe ought to collaborate "very closely" solely with "very trusted partners" on critical technologies, but she did not spell out whether Nvidia now seemed less trustworthy to her after the spy scandal emerged.1314 Politico reports that European leaders' quiet is also explained by a stark economic and strategic fact: the continent has no domestically built substitute for Nvidia.15
