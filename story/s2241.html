Alleged Chinese spy tied to Jensen Huang

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
4 sources · 355 points on Hacker News · single origin (The New York Times)

The New York Times reported, citing anonymous sources, that Alice Huang (also called Huang Le), a top executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, was concluded by US officials to be a Chinese intelligence asset who had developed a close relationship with Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang.1234 US agencies had reportedly surveilled her for about a year to learn about her ties to China, and the concern was that she could have been gathering information about Nvidia's plans and Jensen Huang's efforts to persuade the Trump administration to allow more advanced technology shipments to China.56

The New York Post says the report offered no evidence that Jensen Huang knew of her alleged intelligence activities, and that the Times could not determine what information she passed along or how valuable it was.78 Even so, some senior Trump administration officials now reportedly doubt that he "could have remained unaware" of her role, although it is unclear whether US intelligence has formally changed its assessment.910

Megaspeed has been under US scrutiny over suspicions that it helped Chinese firms evade export restrictions, reportedly acting as a middleman that buys Nvidia chips through Inspur's US arm and rents computing power to Chinese companies.1112 President Donald Trump was reportedly briefed on the findings about a year ago but took no action, with the Times saying he was wary of disrupting Nvidia's chip sales and complicating trade relations with China.1314

Nvidia denies wrongdoing, with a spokesperson saying Jensen Huang's interactions with Alice Huang "are not unusual or unexpected" and that regional cloud customers receive no sensitive trade secrets or national-security information.1516 Nvidia also says claims that its technology was diverted through Megaspeed were "thoroughly investigated and debunked last year."17

Chinese embassy spokesperson Liu Chang rejected the story, saying China opposes "vilifying China by peddling the so-called ‘Chinese spy’ narrative."18 Commentator Zvi Mowshowitz said the existence of such a spy is unsurprising and that he was not updating on the story, which he framed as showing Nvidia compromising the White House largely through flattery.1920
