Muse privacy and data exposure claims

Last scan Sat Oct 3, 04:20 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 1 · 1 new article on Oct 2 · single origin (TechCrunch)

Meta is disputing a claim by Inc. columnist Jason Aten that its AI agent Muse read his private messages without permission.12 Aten's report claimed Full Disk Access was off when Muse read his messages, and he said Muse told him it was syncing his "device notifications", which Aten believes means it was passing his incoming banner notifications to the AI agent.3456

Meta VP of Communications Andy Stone wrote on X that the Messages integration in the Muse app for Mac is "entirely opt-in" and that users must enable both Full Disk Access and the Messages connector for Muse to read their Messages.78 Meta Superintelligence Labs executive David Singleton told Aten on Threads that reading messages on the Mac requires "three separate steps of application-level permissions and built-in macOS system-level protections", which he said "can’t be circumvented even if the Muse application had a bug."91011 Singleton disputed Aten's explanation, saying the AI was confused and gave an incorrect account of what happened.12 According to Zvi Mowshowitz, Aten responded that Stone was flatly calling him a liar.13

TechCrunch says many people remain suspicious that Meta isn't being truthful, given the company's years of mishandling consumer data, and says that whether users can trust Muse will be a deciding factor in whether Meta wins the consumer AI market.1415

Separately, YouTuber Matt Robb said Muse mishandled a task in which he was selling things on Facebook Marketplace, leading to his address being shared, and TechCrunch's updated report says the user admitted some fault and said they allowed Muse a permission that had allowed this to happen.161718

What are the exact steps and permission levels a user has to go through to let Muse read Messages on a Mac?

Meta Superintelligence Labs executive David Singleton says letting Muse read messages on a Mac involves three separate steps of application-level permissions plus built-in macOS system-level protections.12 The first step is that the user explicitly chooses to grant Muse Full Disk Access.34 Granting Full Disk Access invokes the macOS Settings interface, where the user has to manually confirm again, and this triggers a full restart of the Muse app.567 Once Full Disk Access is on, the user can choose Muse's level of access to the Messages app: None, Read only, or Read. If Full Disk Access is off, those options are grayed out.89 Similarly, Meta VP of Communications Andy Stone wrote on X that you have to enable both Full Disk Access and the Messages connector for Muse to be able to read your Messages content.1011 However, Singleton's description does not give a clear name or order for all three steps.
