Muse privacy and data exposure claims

Last scan Mon Oct 5, 14:51 UTC
4 sources · tracked since Oct 1 · 2 independent origins: Ars Technica, TechCrunch

Ars Technica says Aten reported that Muse sent him an unsolicited notification referencing a thread between him and a co-worker in Apple Messages.1 Meta disputes the account: spokesperson Andy Stone says you have to enable both Full Disk Access and the Messages connector for Muse to read Messages content, and Meta CTO David Singleton says Muse can only read Messages content if macOS Full Disk Access is granted and the Messages connector is enabled.23 However, security expert Patrick Wardle questioned Singleton's denial, saying that with Full Disk Access any non-root file is readable.4

Separately, Apple announced in an update on Friday that it will add new limits to macOS "Full Disk Access" in response to risks posed by AI agents; Ars Technica's article is dated Oct 2, 2026.56 Apple says it wants users who genuinely wish to grant this access to do so only with "very explicit user action", and it did not say when the update will roll out.789 Ars Technica's Dan Goodin argues that Apple's statement, which names no app, seems at a minimum to contradict Singleton's denial, and that the timing makes it likely the statement refers to Muse. inference1011

Ars Technica also reports that Wardle disclosed a Muse configuration that let any app on a Mac take full control of the assistant, and that Amazon blocked Muse from its platform.1213

Why does Apple say Full Disk Access exists, and how does it get around the privacy controls users normally have?

Apple says Full Disk Access was designed so backup apps can function properly on Mac.12 Apple says some developers use it in ways that expose files, mail, messages and even browsing history without users' full knowledge and understanding, and that it can also compromise the privacy of the people users communicate with.34 Apple says AI agents will make this riskier, and it plans new controls so users who genuinely want to grant the access can do so only with "very explicit user action"; no rollout date was given.5678

How did Wardle's Muse flaw work, and how could ClickFix attacks be used to take control of the assistant?

Ars Technica reports that security expert Patrick Wardle disclosed a Muse configuration that let any app or code running on a Mac take full control of the AI assistant, 11 days before Apple's Friday, Oct 2, 2026 announcement.12 That access explicitly covered commands injected through ClickFix attacks, which Ars Technica calls increasingly effective, so an attacker who got a command running on a victim's Mac could take over Muse that way. inference34 Once in control, the attacker could reach the same resources Muse itself could.5 The technical details of the configuration flaw, and how a ClickFix attack would be carried out step by step, were not reported.
