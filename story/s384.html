Muse privacy and data exposure claims

Last scan Tue Oct 6, 16:18 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 1 · single origin (Ars Technica)

Tech columnist Jason Aten said Muse sent him an unsolicited notification referencing a thread between him and a co-worker in Apple Messages, and that he never granted it permission to read his messages.1 Meta CTO David Singleton countered that Muse can only read Messages content if macOS Full Disk Access is granted and the Messages connector is enabled, and Meta spokesperson Andy Stone made a similar claim, which Aten took as calling him a liar. inference234 Ars Technica reports that macOS security expert Patrick Wardle questioned that denial, saying that with Full Disk Access any non-root file is readable, and that Meta PR only repeated Singleton's quote when asked.56

Zvi Mowshowitz says Aten's case may well have been user error, but argues Meta is still at fault if tech journalists make such errors, since this would be a standard user experience.78

Apple said on Friday, October 2, 2026, that it is changing its macOS privacy settings to stop third-party developers from misusing Full Disk Access to reach message histories, in the wake of a dispute over Meta's Muse AI agent. inference910 Apple said some developers use Full Disk Access in ways that could expose users' files, mail, messages and browsing history without their full understanding, and that the risks will grow as AI agents become more autonomous.1112

Other Muse problems were also reported: Wardle disclosed a configuration that let any app on a Mac take full control of the assistant, and Amazon blocked Muse from its platform.1314

Why does Apple say Full Disk Access exists, and how does it get around the privacy controls users normally have?

Apple says Full Disk Access was designed so backup apps can function properly on Mac.12 Apple says some developers use it in ways that expose files, mail, messages and even browsing history without users' full knowledge and understanding, and that it can also compromise the privacy of the people users communicate with.34 Apple says AI agents will make this riskier, and it plans new controls so users who genuinely want to grant the access can do so only with "very explicit user action"; no rollout date was given.5678

How did Wardle's Muse flaw work, and how could ClickFix attacks be used to take control of the assistant?

Ars Technica reports that security expert Patrick Wardle disclosed a Muse configuration that let any app or code running on a Mac take full control of the AI assistant, 11 days before Apple's Friday, Oct 2, 2026 announcement.12 That access explicitly covered commands injected through ClickFix attacks, which Ars Technica calls increasingly effective, so an attacker who got a command running on a victim's Mac could take over Muse that way. inference34 Once in control, the attacker could reach the same resources Muse itself could.5 The technical details of the configuration flaw, and how a ClickFix attack would be carried out step by step, were not reported.
