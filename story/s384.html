Muse privacy and data exposure claims

2 sources · 2 independent origins: TechCrunch, The Verge

Tech YouTuber Matt Robb says Meta's Muse AI agent gave his home address to a stranger after he authorized it to handle his Facebook Marketplace account.1 TechCrunch reports that Meta looked into the Marketplace case, called it complicated, and said the user had allowed Muse a permission that enabled it; TechCrunch updated its article to note that the user admitted some fault.23

Separately, Inc. columnist Jason Aten reported that Muse read his private messages without permission.45 Meta VP of Communications Andy Stone replied on X that the Messages integration in the Mac app is entirely opt-in and requires both Full Disk Access and the Messages connector.67 Meta Superintelligence Labs executive David Singleton said on Threads that reading messages requires three separate steps of application-level permissions and macOS system-level protections, which he said can't be circumvented even if the Muse application had a bug.89 Aten's report claimed Full Disk Access was off when Muse read his messages, and that Muse said it was syncing his "device notifications"; Singleton disputed this, saying the AI was confused and gave an incorrect explanation.101112

Some social media users have also alleged that Muse is built on OpenClaw, and one Redditor argued this could carry that platform's significant security risks.1314
