German consumer lawsuit over ChatGPT subscription terms

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 17:01 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 27 · 2 new articles today · single origin, unclear which

OpenAI faces a lawsuit in Germany over how users cancel paid ChatGPT subscriptions, Voice of Emirates reports.1 The plaintiff, the Consumer Protection Organization of Brandenburg, argues that the process fails to give users the clear, direct route to ending an online contract that German law requires.23

The organization also objects to a term of service that lets OpenAI change subscription prices.4 In its view, the clause neither adequately defines when prices may rise nor makes clear what criteria the company applies in deciding.5

The group had formally warned OpenAI in July, seeking changes to the disputed practices and a binding pledge not to repeat them, and went to court after no settlement was reached.67 It wants OpenAI forced to drop the contested terms and to stop applying them to consumers in Germany, existing subscribers among them.8

According to Voice of Emirates, the case points to rising legal scrutiny of digital subscriptions in Europe, alongside growing calls for transparency, simpler cancellation and protection against unclear contract terms.9 The outlet says the case is still before the court, and its result is expected to affect how AI services handle subscriptions and cancellations.10
