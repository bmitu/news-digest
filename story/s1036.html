Meta commercial terms allow crawling

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 14:03 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Oct 17 · 2 new articles this week · 3 independent origins: Ars Technica, Nieman Lab, The Verge

Meta removed contested language from its commercial terms, including the word "train", after backlash that the terms could let it scrape business users' websites, including news sites, for AI training.123 The revised terms let Meta "periodically pull updates" from business users' websites to "provide, develop, improve, research, and maintain advertising and business products."4

Meta spokesperson Daniel Roberts says the consolidated crawling terms applied only to its ads and business products, that nothing changes in how Meta uses web crawlers, and that content owners can use robots.txt to signal preferences.567 Meta also says the update is not about scraping news sites and gives it no new rights that supersede those preferences; before the removal, Andy Stone and Roberts had called suggestions of a change "false".8910

Legal experts told Nieman Lab the changes do not go far enough: attorney Matt Topic argues "develop" is broad enough to include "train", and Grimmelmann says the language is "still broad enough to drive a truck through."111213 Benjamin Sobel, a University of Wisconsin law professor, told Nieman Lab that it would help if Meta added specific language agreeing to respect scraping instructions in robots.txt files, since "unless it’s a legally enforceable preference, it can still be ignored all the same."14

Publisher groups have objected: the UK News Media Association's Theo Bamber called it a "shameless land grab", the News/Media Alliance's Danielle Coffey called it "outrageous and probably illegal", and the European Publishers Council asked the European Commission to intervene.151617 Meta later delayed the terms' start from October 30 to November 20, with Roberts saying this allows more time to explain "what’s changing and what’s not."1819
