Meta commercial terms allow crawling

Last scan Tue Oct 20, 18:59 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Oct 17 · 2 new articles this week · 3 independent origins: Ars Technica, Nieman Lab, The Verge

Meta's new commercial terms, due to take effect on October 30, appeared to authorize it to crawl and scrape websites associated with businesses using its platforms, including news publishers, and to use that content to "train" its AI products, according to a Nieman Lab report.12 Ars Technica reported that the terms would not let businesses opt out and instead suggested that businesses stop using Meta services if they disagree.3 Ars Technica also said its comparison found that what Meta calls "streamlining" seems to ignore that the new terms use fresh language and grant broader provisions, noting that Meta's current commercial terms contain no mentions of "test credentials."45

Legal experts told Nieman Lab the terms could let Meta "argue its commercial terms supersede other blocking instructions," like a site's robots.txt file, and three legal experts said it is not clear whether a court would enforce the terms.678 Cornell professor James Grimmelmann said the terms clearly represent permission for Meta to train AI on any content from news publishers' sites and possibly deploy some of it in competition with them.910 Attorney Matt Topic told Nieman Lab that "this is a remarkable provision to bury in [the] terms of service, given that this would give Meta access to material that would likely cost many millions of dollars in a licensing market."11

Meta spokesperson Daniel Roberts said the company was only "consolidating" its commercial and self-serve ad terms, and that "There is no change to how Meta uses web crawlers; any suggestion to the contrary is false."121314 Roberts said the consolidated crawling terms applied only to Meta's ads and business products, that robots.txt preferences still stand, and that the updates do not give Meta new rights superseding them.1516 Meta vice president of communications Andy Stone said the update was meant to pull accurate information about products for sale on third-party websites for ad and content recommendations, not to scrape news sites.17

After backlash, Meta updated its terms again to remove language suggesting it could scrape content from sites, including news sites, to train its AI; the sentence about using any content from such domains to "train" its products no longer appears on the commercial terms page.181920
