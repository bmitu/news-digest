Meta commercial terms allow crawling

Last scan Sat Oct 17, 12:46 UTC
2 sources · 2 independent origins: Ars Technica, Nieman Lab

Meta's new commercial terms, which take effect on October 30, appear to authorize it to crawl and scrape websites associated with businesses using its platforms, including publishers, and to use the content to train its AI products, according to a Nieman Lab report.12 The terms say businesses that do not agree "may stop all access or use of our Products for business or commercial purposes," and Ars Technica reports they do not allow businesses to opt out. inference34

Meta spokesperson Daniel Roberts says the company is only "consolidating" its commercial and self-serve ad terms and that "There is no change to how Meta uses web crawlers; any suggestion to the contrary is false."567 Meta says content owners can still use robots.txt to indicate their preferences, and Andy Stone, Meta's vice president of communications, says on X the update is meant to pull accurate product information from third-party sites for ads and recommendations, not to scrape news sites.89

Legal experts told Nieman Lab the terms could let Meta "argue its commercial terms supersede other blocking instructions" like robots.txt, though three of them said it is unclear whether a court would enforce the terms.1011 Cornell professor James Grimmelmann says the terms represent permission to train AI on any content from news publishers' sites and possibly to deploy some of it in competition with the publishers.12 Attorney Matt Topic called it "a remarkable provision to bury in [the] terms of service," saying it would give Meta access to material that would likely cost many millions of dollars in a licensing market.13

Ars Technica found that Meta's current commercial terms do not mention "test credentials," which experts warned Nieman Lab could allow Meta to bypass paywalls and automate scraping, and that the term is also not mentioned in Meta's Product Catalog Terms or Ad Creative Generative AI Terms.1415 Asked to clarify whether "test credentials" were referred to in any prior policies, a Meta spokesperson pointed Ars to a Seller Agreement that requires sellers using Meta's e-commerce products to allow Meta to "use automated software and create test credentials to retrieve information from domains, webpages or other URLs you provide."1617

Nieman Lab notes that Meta recently launched a Meta One subscription requiring businesses and creators to pay to include links, which it says means Meta charges users to post links to content it may crawl for free.181920
