GPT-6.2 Astra identifies 1808 sketchbook creator

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 17:01 UTC
3 sources · 469 points on Hacker News · tracked since Nov 25 · 2 new articles today · 2 independent origins: Generative History, HistoryGPT

Mark Humphries, a history professor, says GPT-6.2 Astra needed very little prompting to work out on its own who made an unsigned 1808 sketchbook kept in the Beinecke Library at Yale.1 The task reportedly took just 22 minutes and relied on publicly available primary and secondary sources.23 The model attributed the sketchbook to the vicomte de Chabot. According to one summary of the claim, it also found a little-known microfilmed diary, in which both the handwriting and the route of travel corresponded to the drawings.45

Simon Willison's account of Humphries' write-up says Astra located a diary entry noting that Chabot visited a nearby river five days ahead of a date the sketchbook mentions for that spot, and proposed that Chabot's own travel journal could settle whether he was the author.67 Humphries says the model predicted that examining a little-known archival source would prove its hypothesis, and that it did, and he adds that the source is itself a major find of interest to Canadian and fur trade historians.89 According to Humphries, once Chabot's canoe had arrived at Fort St. Joseph on 18 June 1808, he recorded that "I took a sketch" and went on to describe precisely the view of the fort shown in the corresponding drawing.10 In his view, it is exceedingly unlikely that anyone would have named Chabot without a good deal of luck, short of devoting a life to the question, and he says several highly experienced fur trade researchers shared his surprise at the findings. inference1112

He also flags a limitation: even with a copy of the diary in hand, Astra hedged that the unsigned document might not be Chabot's and that some sketches might not be his, doubts Humphries considers highly improbable. inference1314

Humphries expects historians to face a crisis of confidence over agentic systems that can do things human historians cannot, similar to what happened in mathematics, but thinks it will be milder.1516 He argues history differs because historians constantly reinterpret the past rather than chase a single universal truth, so humanity is something of a requirement for historical writing.1718
