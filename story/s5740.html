GPT-6.2 Astra identifies 1808 sketchbook creator

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 09:22 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 25 · 2 independent origins: Generative History, HistoryGPT

Mark Humphries, a history professor, says GPT-6.2 Astra needed very little prompting to work out on its own who made an unsigned 1808 sketchbook kept in the Beinecke Library at Yale.1 The task reportedly took just 22 minutes and relied on publicly available primary and secondary sources.2 The model attributed the sketchbook to the vicomte de Chabot. According to one summary of the claim, it also found a little-known microfilmed diary, in which both the handwriting and the route of travel corresponded to the drawings.34

Simon Willison's account of Humphries' write-up says Astra located a diary entry noting that Chabot visited a nearby river five days ahead of a date the sketchbook mentions for that spot, and proposed that Chabot's own travel journal could settle whether he was the author.56 Humphries says the model predicted that examining a little-known archival source would prove its hypothesis, and that it did, and he adds that the source is itself a major find of interest to Canadian and fur trade historians.78

Humphries expects historians to face a crisis of confidence over agentic systems that can do things human historians cannot, similar to what happened in mathematics, but thinks it will be milder.910 He argues history differs because historians constantly reinterpret the past rather than chase a single universal truth, so humanity is something of a requirement for historical writing.1112
