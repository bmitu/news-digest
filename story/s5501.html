Character.AI bankruptcy privacy ombudsman

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 09:22 UTC
6 sources · tracked since Nov 22 · 1 new article today · single origin (Minnesota Attorney General)

State attorneys general say the bankruptcy court handling Character Technologies, Inc., which operates Character.AI, has directed that a Consumer Privacy Ombudsman examine any sale that would involve user accounts, conversation data, sensitive personal details or artificial intelligence assets.123 Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison announced the move on November 20, 2026, saying it came at the urging of him and a bipartisan coalition of 40 state attorneys general.456 Pennsylvania Attorney General Dave Sunday also said the order was made at the urging of him and a bipartisan coalition of states.7 Idaho Attorney General Raúl Labrador, who also announced the ombudsman appointment, said corporate bankruptcy and restructuring cannot undo the damage Character.AI did to children in Idaho.8 Pennsylvania's office stated that a month ago it joined a bipartisan group of states in submitting a filing in the bankruptcy case, which voiced significant worries over the company's handling of safety and privacy and urged that children and consumers be safeguarded if the company is sold.9 According to Pennsylvania's office, the states began investigating after a 2024 wrongful death lawsuit over a child's death by suicide, and the investigation identified potential consumer protection and privacy violations.1011

Ellison's office says the U.S. Trustee picked Lucy Thompson, an expert in data privacy, to serve as ombudsman, though Pennsylvania's office did not identify her by name. inference1213 She must submit reports to the court, ahead of any asset sale, that flag data privacy concerns and recommend safeguards.14151617

The attorneys general say the company plans to auction the collected data on December 1, 2026, and that Character.AI reports 12 million current users averaging 120 minutes per day while it keeps operating in bankruptcy.1819202122 The officials say the data includes private conversations about users' mental health, relationships and emotional struggles.23242526 Pennsylvania's office notes that the platform's chatbot characters include psychologists, therapists and doctors, personas that carry an expectation of confidentiality.27 Ellison and Sunday argue that Character.AI's voluntary restrictions on minors' access to some chatbot features are incomplete and could be circumvented or reversed by a future buyer.2829 Ellison and the coalition also say Character.AI still holds many millions of minors' sensitive chats, which are being offered to the highest bidder.3031 Sunday said that, speaking as a father, he is grateful the bankruptcy court has appointed a data privacy expert to oversee the case.3233 Sunday's office says he and other attorneys general have made clear they will fight any sale or reorganization plan that lacks strong safeguards for consumers' personal data.34 Ellison said winning the independent monitor is a good step forward, and that he and fellow attorneys general will not let up in keeping consumers' sensitive data safe, especially data on children.35
