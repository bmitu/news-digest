Apple OpenEvidence health study

Last scan Sun Nov 22, 14:05 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Nov 20 · 2 independent origins: Apple, STAT

Apple announced the Apple Health AI Usage Study, run with clinical AI platform OpenEvidence, to learn how people use conversational AI health tools and how useful the answers are.12 STAT reports the launch was announced on a Sunday on stage at the HLTH conference in Las Vegas.34 Apple's Health and Fitness vice president, Sumbul Desai, described a purpose-built research study as the wisest approach for learning how users interact with health-focused AI and judge its value.56 OpenEvidence CEO Daniel Nadler said OpenEvidence is the most trusted brand among doctors and Apple among consumers, comparing the pairing to peanut butter and jelly.78

Participants use a new iOS app to ask medical questions and rate the responses on criteria such as accuracy, detail and relevance.9 Answers are said to be grounded in clinical sources such as The New England Journal of Medicine, JAMA and Nature, and participants can optionally share HealthKit data for more personal context.1011 Apple is seeking up to 200,000 US participants aged 21 or older, and TechCrunch says the study is planned to run more than 12 months with monthly surveys.121314 The sources disagree on device requirements: The Verge says the app needs an iPhone 15 Pro and does not use Apple Intelligence, while TechCrunch says it requires an iPhone 15 or later with Apple Intelligence enabled. inference1516

Apple says the data won't be used to train AI models, and TechCrunch adds that data is anonymized before going to a secure third-party cloud and that OpenEvidence deletes queries and responses after 24 hours.171819 TechCrunch notes it is unclear whether Apple plans to bring AI answering to its Health app or Siri, though it points to large demand, citing OpenAI's figure of over 300 million weekly health queries.2021
