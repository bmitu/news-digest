Leak of PHP vulnerability details

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 14:03 UTC
4 sources · tracked since Oct 21 · 5 new articles this week · single origin (PHP Foundation)

Two remote code execution vulnerabilities in PHP, reportedly found by researchers using AI models, had their details leak before fixes were released, which caused widespread disruption. inference12 Wiz Research's timeline says it found the first vulnerability on August 19, 2026 using Wiz Atlas, Gemini 4 Argon and GPT-5.6 Sol, reported it to PHP on August 24, and that patched releases and public disclosure were planned for October 21.345

According to a PHP Foundation post quoted by Simon Willison, the two CVE IDs began circulating online around 15:00 UTC on October 20 with a wrong release time and a wrong CVSS score of 10.0 (the actual score is 9.2), though no technical details leaked.6 The PHP project then brought its pre-announcement forward to 19:30 UTC and moved the release from October 22 to October 21 at 14:00 UTC.7 Around 20:00 UTC, the security firm Calif mistakenly published a video showing its proof of concept working against a number of targets, and copies of the deleted video circulated soon after. The PHP Foundation says the video contributed to accelerated development of various PoCs.89

Latent Space cautions that claims such as about 1.5 million unpatched servers and various shutdowns are not corroborated in the supplied material, and that the cause of each outage is not established.1011 Latent Space says the supplied material does not establish precisely what leaked, when, or whether the release schedule changed. It lists successful exploitation versus scanning among the key unresolved evidence gaps.1213

Transformer says that in some sense things worked as intended, since OpenAI's Patch the Planet and Daybreak initiatives gave infrastructure providers more than two months, but it warns many sites will take weeks or months to update and some will never be patched.1415 Joshua Saxe argues that cheaper offensive capability "doesn’t immediately translate into damages", while Joshua Achiam said it "Feels like March 1, 2020 for Cyber."161718
