US DHS bans DeepSeek

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 14:03 UTC
4 sources · 580 points on Hacker News · tracked since Oct 21 · 1 new article this week · single origin (TechCrunch)

The DHS raised concerns about DeepSeek's handling of user data, warning that sensitive information from US government users could potentially be accessible to Chinese authorities under Chinese law.1 According to TechCrunch, the U.S. accused DeepSeek and five other Chinese AI companies of using large-scale model distillation to extract information from American AI systems in a September report, and OpenAI and Anthropic have separately raised concerns about DeepSeek's alleged use of model distillation.23

A law passed in December 2025 had already targeted DeepSeek's use in intelligence national security systems, and the new order extends restrictions to civilian agencies' new purchases and existing IT systems.4 TechCrunch reports the order also covers third-party products and services built using DeepSeek technology, though agencies can request exceptions.56 A summary of the order, DHS Order 2026-01, says it excludes national-security and classified systems, allows exception requests within 60 days, and requires interim monitoring and logging controls.7

China's Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said Beijing opposes "overstretching the concept of national security and politicizing technology and trade issues," and added, "We will resolutely safeguard the legitimate rights and interests of Chinese enterprises."89

Interconnects says it is unclear how the order applies to finetuned models or models trained on DeepSeek outputs, but calls the action expected and says it reinforces the need for a domestic open model ecosystem.1011 Commenters summarized by Latent Space disagree on scope: some read the order as targeting DeepSeek-operated hosting rather than open-weight models hosted elsewhere, while another raised that enforcing a ban on an open-source model and its derivatives may be difficult.1213
