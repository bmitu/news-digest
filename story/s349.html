Trump signs AI rebranding executive order

Last scan Sat Oct 3, 21:32 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Sep 30 · 2 new articles today · single origin (White House)

President Donald Trump signed an executive order officially rebranding AI as "super intelligence," according to TechCrunch's Equity podcast.12 The order came in the same week the White House got nearly every major tech CEO into one room to sign an AI safety pledge that Trump called "morally binding."345 A TechCrunch headline separately reports a surge in registrations of Slovenia's .si domain after Trump's order.6

Zvi Mowshowitz describes a separate executive order to change the name of AI, which he says Trump claims the lab leaders signed off on.7 Quoting Andrew Curran, Mowshowitz reports Trump saying the document would officially rename AI "because it's not artificial, we all agree on that."8

Investor Adam Cochran alleges the order was a criminal plot to enrich the Trump family, saying insiders profited from .si domain names before Trump's Truth Social posts.910 Mowshowitz offers counterpoints, noting that Meta already used "superintelligence" as marketing and that a months-long plan would not be Trump's style, and he presumes causation runs the other way, calling any insider trading mostly a free action.1112

How does the safety accord compare with the earlier executive order for an industry standards body that Nvidia's Jensen Huang and Mark Zuckerberg reportedly pushed Trump to drop?

According to The Information (via Ars Technica), Nvidia's Jensen Huang joined Mark Zuckerberg earlier this year in pushing Trump to pull the plug on an executive order that would have established a standards body to report AI safety and security incidents to the government; the body would have been modeled after the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority.12 The new accord is a voluntary pledge: two dozen firms committed to controls the White House recommended, including independent safety audits.3 Ars Technica reports that the accord legally requires nothing new of firms, and that Anthropic, Google and OpenAI had already committed to external audits.4 Huang and Zuckerberg, who reportedly opposed the standards-body order, both signed the accord. This suggests the industry accepted a self-policing, audit-based approach but not a dedicated incident-reporting body. inference567 Anthropic, Google and OpenAI are reportedly moving ahead with developing their own independent body, while reportedly hoping that one day the White House might get involved if enough industry consensus is reached.8 The accord's text does not say whether it requires incident reporting to the government.
