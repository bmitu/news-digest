Trump signs AI rebranding executive order

Last scan Mon Oct 5, 14:51 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Sep 30 · 1 new article today · single origin (White House)

President Donald Trump signed an executive order officially rebranding AI as "super intelligence," according to TechCrunch.12 TechCrunch's Anthony Ha said U.S. officials are now supposed to say super intelligence rather than artificial intelligence.3 Zvi Mowshowitz's post reports that Trump claims the lab leaders signed off on the renaming, and notes that Microsoft and Amazon were present and did not visibly sign, though it is unclear whether they chose not to sign or were not considered worthy of inclusion.45

The order came in the same week the White House gathered nearly every major tech CEO, including Mark Zuckerberg, Jeff Bezos, Elon Musk and Anthropic's Dario Amodei, to sign an AI safety pledge that Trump called "morally binding."67 The pledge is titled the "Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities."89 TechCrunch's Sean O'Kane called the pledge "deeply non-binding" and completely voluntary, and said it misspelled "United States."1011 Anthony Ha said the agreement seems not very meaningful, though the fact that the gesture was needed seems significant.1213

Trump later announced a Super Intelligence Force led by national intelligence director Jay Clayton, with a reported 120 days to report on AI risks and opportunities.1415 Kirsten Korosec suggested the meeting's real purpose was a rebranding of AI.16

Adam Cochran alleges the order was a plot to enrich the Trump family, claiming insiders profited from buying .si domain names before Trump's posts; Mowshowitz is skeptical and presumes causation runs the other way. inference1718

Who is on the Super Intelligence Force besides Jay Clayton, and what is it supposed to deliver in its 120 days?

Trump describes the task force's broader task as coordinating the federal government's effort to keep America leading the world in super intelligence.12 The vice chairs are FTC Chair Andrew Ferguson, Undersecretary of War for Research and Engineering Emil Michael, and Office of Personnel Management Director Scott Kupor.34 Within a reported 120 days, the task force is to produce a report on the risks and opportunities presented by AI.5 Its charter reportedly also says it will develop plans for responding to SI-enabled threats while preventing overregulation and regulatory capture.67 Whether the full membership extends beyond the chair and three vice chairs was not reported.
