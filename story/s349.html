Trump signs AI rebranding executive order

2 sources · tracked since Sep 30 · 1 new article today · single origin (The Verge)

According to The Verge, official policy websites, policy documents, and press releases will refer only to "Super Intelligence," thanks to a new executive order signed by President Donald Trump.1 The Verge reports that agencies will not have to change past regulations or documents, but that the administration is seeking to use the term as a replacement for a broader statutory definition of AI.23 Trump signed the order after a White House luncheon with tech CEOs and government officials, and afterward called it "very productive" and "extremely friendly."45

Trump said at an event announcing the launch of America.gov that "The word super is the best word of all, and it's the simplest," and argued the technology is not artificial, comparing "artificial" to "Fake news."67 Trump also said there is a belief that there should be "tremendous self-regulation," and that data centers "are going to be very popular."8910

Zvi Mowshowitz, citing Andrew Curran, reports that a separate voluntary "White House Accord on [Artificial] Intelligence" was signed, while Microsoft and Amazon were present but did not visibly sign.1112 Mowshowitz says Trump claims the lab leaders signed off on the renaming.13

Adam Cochran alleges the order was a criminal plot to enrich the Trump family, claiming insiders profited from buying .si domain names before Trump's posts.1415 Mowshowitz offers counterpoints, including that the term "superintelligence" was already being used as marketing by Meta and talked about by others, and says his strong presumption is that primary causation runs the other way, adding of any insider trading, "I don't like it, but I am not that mad about it."161718

What are the four rules in the voluntary safety accord the tech CEOs signed, and who signed it?

The accord is officially titled the Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities, and it sets out four rules for each company to ensure frontier AI models behave as intended and that problems are quickly found and fixed.12 The first rule is to implement robust internal controls to monitor the capabilities and alignment of its models.3 Speaker Johnson's summary, as relayed by Andrew Curran, also points to layers of internal and external review, but the full text of all four rules was not provided. The Verge reports it was signed by Google's Sundar Pichai, Anthropic's Dario Amodei, Meta's Mark Zuckerberg, OpenAI's Greg Brockman, xAI's Elon Musk and Nvidia's Jensen Huang.4 Microsoft and Amazon were present but did not visibly sign.5

How is the 'self-regulation' accord different from the earlier plan for a standards body modeled on FINRA that Trump was pushed to drop?

The earlier plan, which Nvidia's Jensen Huang and Meta's Mark Zuckerberg reportedly pushed Trump to drop, would have created a standards body that reports AI safety and security incidents to the government, modeled on the financial industry's FINRA.12 The new accord is instead a voluntary pledge by tech firms: they commit to controls the White House recommended, including independent safety audits, and to meeting regularly to set common safety standards and benchmarks.345 The difference is that the dropped order would have set up a body that reports incidents to the government, while the accord relies on voluntary commitments and company-led standard-setting with no legal requirement. inference67 Nothing new is legally required of firms, and when asked whether the accord was binding in any way, Trump said "I think it's morally binding, yeah."78 The accord says it "may make sense to codify" the recommendations eventually, but for now Trump stressed trusting AI firms to regulate themselves.910 Separately, Anthropic, Google and OpenAI are reportedly building their own independent body, hoping the White House might join if industry consensus emerges.11
