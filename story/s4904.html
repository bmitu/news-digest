Congress seeks Huang testimony and probes Nvidia chip sales

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 17:01 UTC
4 sources · tracked since Nov 20 · 2 independent origins: Financial Times, The Verge

Jensen Huang, Nvidia's chief executive, has been called by a congressional committee in the US House to give testimony on the extensive illegal diversion of Nvidia's artificial intelligence chips into China. Legislators say they remain worried about national security, even though Nvidia conducted its own internal probe and Huang has returned to his post.12 Sources differ on the hearing schedule: TechCrunch reported Huang would testify before Thanksgiving, The Verge said December 1, and Ars Technica said he and general counsel Tim Teter were summoned to hearings the following week. inference345

Huang acknowledged having had a relationship with Alice Huang, who used to be an executive at Megaspeed, a company that buys from Nvidia. According to reports, some American intelligence officers suspected she might be a Chinese spy. Megaspeed is also said to have come under investigation for attempts to route high-end Nvidia chips to China.67

On November 5, three Republican committee chairmen asked Huang for a closed-door briefing, and Nvidia said on November 12 that its board had accepted his resignation, with Colette Kress named interim CEO.89 Huang was later reinstated after Trump personally spoke to the board, and the board said it was confident his return poses no national security risk based on input from senior US government levels.1011

On November 12, Democrats Richard Neal and Ron Wyden launched an investigation into how the Trump administration handled Nvidia's China chip sales, and Republican Senator Tim Scott asked that Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick testify before the Senate Banking Committee.1213 Trump said the attorney general's office had investigated and exonerated Huang, but The Verge reported that the Justice Department gave no on-the-record response, and the Securities and Exchange Commission had reportedly opened an investigation into whether Nvidia misled investors.141516

Democrats say they will press on: Chuck Schumer said Democrats will use their full power to investigate wrongdoing by the Trump administration, Huang and other executives, and Hakeem Jeffries called the reinstatement "indefensible, irresponsible and incomprehensible."1718 Some Republicans also objected: Tom Cotton said the Nvidia and Megaspeed controversy is not resolved because Huang has his job back, and Don Bacon called the reinstatement a "failure of character."1920
