AI-agent hacker breaches 100+ websites

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 14:03 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 21 · 1 new article this week · single origin (Norforge)

AI security firm Norforge reports that a hacker broke into more than 100 websites using an army of AI agents that mostly operated on their own.1 Norforge does not name the targets, but Reuters reports the victims included John Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins, and that the hacker had attempted intrusions against over 1,200 sites. inference23

Norforge researchers found the attacks through exposed work on Cairn, an open-source AI platform that describes itself as a "general-purpose problem-solving engine."4 Reuters reports this hacker was the "most active" among exposed Cairn instances, targeted companies in the US, Canada, Australia and Japan, gave instructions in fewer than 100 Chinese characters, and needed about 23 hours per successful breach.567 Norforge found more than 20 exposed Cairn instances that are "likely malicious," and says it is working to get them taken down.89

Cummins spokesperson Melinda Koski said the company is investigating a potential cyber-attack on a website run by a third-party vendor.10 A John Deere spokesperson told Reuters there is no indication sensitive information was accessed, while SharkNinja said the breach hit a standalone product-support site and that it has contained the threat.1112

Cairn's creator, who goes by @le1xia0, said the platform "was never a 'hacking tool,'" arguing that "misuse is a human decision, not a feature of the engine."13 Zach Weinersmith says a world of hacker bot swarms battling defender bot swarms around the clock "doesn't seem like a *great* path forward."14
