ChatGPT Wallet

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 09:22 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 14 · single origin (The Atlantic)

OpenAI has launched ChatGPT Wallet, a feature that lets its Dots agents buy items on a user's behalf after the user reviews and approves each purchase.12 Availability is limited at first to ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the US aged 18 and older with eligible US-issued cards from American Express, Chase, Mastercard or Visa.3 TechCrunch reports the limited rollout will let OpenAI test and improve the feature before expanding it more broadly.4

OpenAI says each purchase requires a security check with biometric verification such as Face ID or a fingerprint scan.56 OpenAI says the wallet also substitutes a payment token for the real card details, so neither the agent nor the merchant sees the actual card information.78 OpenAI also says users will still earn card rewards and keep protections such as the ability to dispute transactions.9 According to Fortune, OpenAI's terms keep merchants responsible for shipping and returns and maintain the dispute rights that cards already carry. The piece argues, though, that none of this clearly determines who absorbs the loss when the problem is the agent's own judgment.1011

OpenAI expects eventually to let users delegate purchases entirely by setting limits such as spending caps, purchase categories or time periods.1213 A Fortune commentator contends that the approval safeguard in ChatGPT Wallet is temporary, and that the industry still hasn't worked out which decisions agents may make on their own, or who covers the cost when those decisions turn out badly.1415

Retailer blocking of AI agents may limit where the wallet works; The Verge and TechCrunch both point to Amazon, which has blocked agents. inference1617 The Atlantic reports that Amazon has banned AI agents, reportedly out of concern about what shopping bots could mean for its advertising business, and that Walmart announced it will let Meta's Muse agents browse for deals and make purchases on users' behalf.1819 OpenAI says websites must ensure agents are not inadvertently blocked, and it published a Commerce Partner Playbook to help retailers adapt to agentic commerce.2021

TechCrunch frames the launch as part of a push to find product-market fit for consumer AI tools, noting DoorDash and Uber have reported low agent-placed order numbers while Meta's Muse has seen AI-assisted shopping become a top activity.22 The Atlantic reports that Meta's Mark Zuckerberg said over 10 million people used Muse during its first six weeks. Ben Schwerin, Instinct's chief business officer, said users have been relying more and more on the agent to finish the buying for entire gift lists.2324
