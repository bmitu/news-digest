Texas sues OpenAI

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
3 sources · 2 independent origins: Kilgore News Herald, Texas Attorney General

Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton has sued OpenAI and its CEO Sam Altman, alleging the company made false claims about ChatGPT's safety and reliability.1 The suit was filed on a Friday in Collin County district court.2

Paxton argues that large language models can output inaccurate or harmful information without adequate safeguards, and that this risk is not disclosed to users.3 Paxton's office cites harms including mass shooters being aided, suicide encouragement, public humiliation, lost critical thinking skills and minors becoming addicted, and says they are driven by the company's quest to win the AI arms race and amass large fortunes.45 According to the lawsuit, OpenAI was valued at $17 billion in 2022 and the valuation has since risen to over $1.4 trillion, which the filing attributes to deceit and exploitation of users.67

Texas seeks to compel compliance with the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices – Consumer Protection Act and the SCOPE Act.8 Paxton's office is also requesting a wide-ranging injunction that would require OpenAI to block minors' access and obtain approval of new models from a court-appointed evaluator.9

An OpenAI spokesperson said the company has worked with experts to mitigate risks and add safeguards, and that it wants to work with Texas on AI policies that apply to the whole industry rather than one company.1011

The Texas Tribune notes that Paxton, the Republican Senate nominee, has recently sued or settled with Meta, TikTok, Snapchat and Roblox, and that Elon Musk, who has a bitter history with OpenAI and Altman, is the largest single donor to Paxton's Senate campaign. inference121314
