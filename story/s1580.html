Claude designs scorpion neurotoxin binders

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 14:03 UTC
3 sources · 117 points on Hacker News · tracked since Oct 21 · 2 new articles this week · 2 independent origins: Anthropic, Citrini Research

Anthropic says it asked an internal research model to design minibinders against scorpion neurotoxins, and that 17 of 211 lab-tested designs met its criteria for a binder.12 The work was developed with Citrini Research, and Anthropic says it is releasing all 211 designs and their data as a resource for the field.34

Anthropic says the model worked mainly autonomously over 64 hours with four follow-up prompts, generating 318,484 designs, co-folding and screening 553,052 design-toxin pairs, and selecting 180 designs for testing. Anthropic's team added 31 more.567

Each of the 17 binders bound a single toxin: seven against Cn2, four against AaH II, four against AaH I and two against LqhIII. Of the 60 single-toxin designs, 11 were binders (18%), which Anthropic says is consistent with typical protein design campaigns.89 Anthropic says its strongest binders, Cn2-B1 and Cn2-B2, bound Cn2 with affinities in the single-digit nanomolar range. It notes that three antibodies and nanobodies have reported affinities below 1 nM against Cn2 or AaH II and have protected mice.1011

Anthropic says it has not confirmed neutralization of venom and does not claim to have produced an antivenom. Latent Space's AINews summary likewise says this is not evidence of therapeutic efficacy.1213 Anthropic says two outside experts, Susana Vázquez Torres and Timothy P. Jenkins, called the work a promising first step. It adds that turning binders into safe, affordable drugs remains the challenge.1415
