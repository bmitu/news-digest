Claude designs scorpion neurotoxin binders

Last scan Wed Oct 21, 20:11 UTC
2 sources · 114 points on Hacker News · single origin (Anthropic)

Anthropic says an internal Claude research model, working with Citrini Research, designed protein minibinders against scorpion neurotoxins, and that 17 of 211 lab-tested designs met its criteria for a binder.123 Anthropic says the model worked mainly autonomously over 64 hours with four follow-up prompts, generating 318,484 designs and selecting 180 for testing, to which the team added 31 more.45 Anthropic says it is releasing all 211 designs and their data as a resource for the field.6

Each of the 17 binders attaches to a single toxin: seven against Cn2, four against AaH II, four against AaH I and two against LqhIII.7 Among the 60 single-toxin designs, 11 were binders (18%), which Anthropic says is consistent with typical protein design campaigns.8 Anthropic reports that its two strongest binders, Cn2-B1 and Cn2-B2, bound Cn2 with single-digit nanomolar affinities at both contract labs.9

The main limitation is that none of the multitoxin designs met the criteria for more than one toxin, and neutralization has not been confirmed.101112 Latent Space's newsletter, summarizing the announcement, stresses that this is not an antivenom or evidence of therapeutic efficacy, with cellular, animal and further validation still ahead.13

Anthropic says two outside experts, Susana Vázquez Torres and Timothy P. Jenkins, felt the work was a promising first step.14 Anthropic adds that turning binders into safe, affordable, widely accessible drugs remains the challenge, and that testing in mice would be needed to show protection.1516
