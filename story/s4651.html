Meta reevaluates 2030 net-zero goal

Last scan Sun Nov 22, 14:05 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Nov 20 · single origin (Meta)

Meta says in its 2026 sustainability report, released November 19, 2026, that it is re-evaluating the 2030 timeframe for its net-zero emissions goal and its near-term intensity targets.123 Meta's vice president of data centers, Rachel Peterson, and Blair Swedeen, who leads the company's global net zero and sustainability work, attributed the situation to a surge in demand for AI infrastructure that outpaced nearly all forecasts, and they said Meta's longer-term goals remain unchanged.45 According to Data Center Dynamics, Meta gave no new date and said it will share the outcome later, and the executives also pointed to lagging interconnection queues, permitting and transmission buildout.67

Meta's total emissions rose from 15.6 million metric tonnes of CO2 equivalent in 2024 to more than 21.7 million in 2025, a nearly 40 percent increase that Ars Technica says is the highest among the four major US tech giants.89 Net emissions, after renewable energy matching and carbon removal, rose 45 percent to about 11.8 million metric tons in 2025.10 Data Center Dynamics ties much of the growth to reliance on natural gas to get around grid connection delays, and notes Meta left the RE100 climate initiative in July, saying new gas power investments meant it could no longer meet the technical criteria.1112 Meta says it matched all of its electricity use, which rose 35 percent to 24.8TWh, with clean and renewable energy, having contracted more than 30GW of low-carbon power.13

On water, direct consumption fell from 825 million to 790 million gallons, but consumption in water-stressed areas rose from 198 million to 246 million gallons and water tied to purchased electricity rose to more than 26 billion gallons.141516

Other hyperscalers also reported rising emissions, with Microsoft up 25 percent, Google 18 percent and Amazon 16 percent, but Meta is the only one to announce a reevaluation of its 2030 commitment.171819 Ars Technica calls Meta's statement a significant public acknowledgment, since Amazon, Google and Microsoft have so far stuck by their goals, and argues it would be in the public interest for other companies to follow Meta in reporting indirect water use.2021
