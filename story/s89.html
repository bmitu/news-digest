Project Suncatcher TPU satellite

2 sources · tracked since Sep 27 · single origin (Google)

Google has given an update on Project Suncatcher, its initiative announced last year to put computers in space and eventually train AI systems there, according to Import AI.1 Import AI describes the launch as still being prepared, with Google and its partner Planet sending some of its chips to space on the SpaceX Transporter-18 rideshare mission.2 Latent Space's AINews reports that Google is flying four TPUs in orbit on a Planet prototype satellite aboard SpaceX Transporter-18.34

Per Import AI's account, Google found its Trillium TPUs "hold up remarkably well" and can survive a radiation total ionizing dose greater than what they would receive during a five-year space mission.5 Google is currently working on cooling in space, which Import AI expects will likely be a challenge because chips generate a lot of heat and radiating it away in a vacuum is very difficult.6

The Import AI author argues the idea makes sense given the growing scale and energy needs of AI compute, and says it seems very likely that humanity moves a very large amount of computation into orbit very quickly.78

How do orbital data centers compare with startups like Satlyt that run AI on individual satellites rather than building full space data centers?

Orbital data centers, as pursued by SpaceX, Google, Starcloud and Cowboy Space Company, involve building their own spacecraft, whereas Satlyt does not plan to build spacecraft and is instead building software to run AI models on satellites.123 Satlyt founder Rama Afullo casts the difference as platform versus product: if the orbital data center builders are the iPhone, he says, Satlyt will be an open, horizontally integrated Android that works across many companies' satellites.45 Satlyt's initial focus is operational efficiency for spacecraft, which typically rely on ground flight controllers to fix problems, and its software has already flown on two demonstration missions.67 Investor Kent Lucas of Non Sibi argues Satlyt doesn't depend on space data centers, since its growth can be driven by the number of satellites launched, and Afullo hopes to be live on 20% of satellites by the end of the decade.89 Still, the two approaches are not strictly rivals: Satlyt's software is set to launch on the same SpaceX rocket as the first prototype of Google's Project Suncatcher, which carries four TPUs. inference101112
