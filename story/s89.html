Project Suncatcher TPU satellite

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 03:07 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Sep 27 · 2 independent origins: Google, TechCrunch

Google's prototype orbital compute satellite for Project Suncatcher launched from California aboard a SpaceX rocket, which TechCrunch describes as the first time Google has sent one of its advanced chips into space.1 The satellite, built by Planet Labs, is meant to show that a Google TPU can work in space, and Latent Space reports it carries four TPUs on the SpaceX Transporter-18 mission. inference23

Once commissioned, the satellite will run its TPU in 15-minute bursts to avoid straining its power and thermal systems, and Google and Planet plan a next-year demo of two more purpose-built satellites linked by laser.45 Google's long-term vision is an orbital data center of 81 satellites flying in close formation; TechCrunch reports that the focus of this "long-term moonshot," as Google executive Travis Beals puts it, is on building for future space infrastructure and AI workloads.67

TechCrunch reports that, based on Google's analysis, a similar cost-reduction trajectory would take Starship about 1,800 launches over the next 10 years, or 180 a year, if it can fly 200 metric tons on each mission. TechCrunch calls that a big ask for a vehicle that has never flown more than five times in a year.89 The researchers stress that their analysis is not an economic feasibility study, while TechCrunch notes Elon Musk has suggested far higher Starship flight rates but adds that "Musk says a lot of things".1011

On radiation, Google says its Trillium TPUs survive a total ionizing dose greater than a five-year space mission would deliver, according to Import AI.12 TechCrunch adds that Google redid its particle-accelerator tests after realizing the chip configuration gave more shielding than it would get in orbit, which produced slightly more logic errors, though Google remains confident in inference workloads.1314 Beals says the error rate is very low for typical inference, about one in a million, but was already problematic for mega-scale training runs with thousands of chips running for months.1516

Import AI's author calls cooling a likely challenge and says it seems very likely humanity will move a large amount of computation into orbit quickly, an opinion TechCrunch's launch-rate caveats complicate. inference1718

How are Google's plans different from other space AI companies like Satlyt that are flying on the same rocket?
