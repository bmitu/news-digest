Gemini 4 Argon launch

6 sources · 1607 points on Hacker News · 2 independent origins: Artificial Analysis, Google

Google announced Gemini 4 Argon, a new frontier model it says delivers frontier performance in software engineering, enterprise knowledge work and cybersecurity defense, but it is initially available only to a set of trusted cyber defenders through its Fairwind Program.123 Google says it is engaged in the U.S. government's voluntary pre-release access process and will refine guardrails before releasing Argon to developers, enterprises and consumers, starting with paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers.456 Ars Technica notes that Google gave no specific timeline for broader availability.7

Google lists an introductory price of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with cached input discounted 95%.89 Google also raised the output limit to what it calls an industry-leading 1M tokens, up from 64K.1011 Latent Space's summary says Vals lists 262K max output, while Artificial Analysis reached 1M output tokens using Long Decode Continuation, a new API feature that pauses and resumes long responses across calls.12

Per Ars Technica, Argon scores 77.9 percent on DeepSWE v1.1, which is higher than GPT-6 Astra, Fable 5.1, and Opus 5.5, as part of the benchmarks Google presented.13 Latent Space reports Google's claim of first place on 13 of 19 published benchmarks.14 Artificial Analysis gives it a score of 53 on its Intelligence Index, against a median of 26 for similar-tier reasoning models, and says it generated 110M output tokens in testing, versus a median of 82M.1516 Latent Space's summary of the measurements says the cost advantage over Astra comes from price rather than efficiency, since Argon averages 62K output tokens per task versus Astra's 27K.17

Ars Technica says Wiz is using Argon and used it to uncover a critical vulnerability that could expose personal information in a system used at hospitals around the world; Google claims other frontier models missed this flaw, but it didn't provide any specifics.1819

Why does Google say Argon is limited to trusted cyber defenders, and what safeguards must it strengthen before a wider release?

Google says Argon was trained to be highly capable at cybersecurity defense and can autonomously find, validate and patch critical software vulnerabilities, and that trusted defenders and its own teams will get it without cyber guardrails.123 Google says safely releasing frontier capabilities at this level requires a phased approach, so Argon is rolling out first to a set of trusted cyber defenders through its Fairwind Program.456 Google says it is also taking part in the U.S. government's voluntary process for pre-release model access while it gradually expands access, and is using feedback from early testers to iterate on guardrails.789 Before a broad rollout, Google says it is strengthening critical frontier safeguards in four areas: defending against misuse, defending against prompt injection attacks, monitoring for misalignment, and hardening systems.101112 On misuse, Google says it is improving its techniques to monitor the model's internal activations to spot misuse, to prevent bad actors from using Argon for cyber or chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear attacks. On misalignment, it says it is deploying mitigations that monitor Argon's chain-of-thought and actions and stop execution when necessary.1314 Google also says it is hardening its sandboxed environments by isolating and sealing them before high-risk training or evaluations begin.15 Google did not give a firm date for wider availability, saying it will make Argon available to developers, enterprises and consumers "as soon as possible", starting with paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers.161718
