Gemini 4 Argon launch

Last scan Sat Oct 3, 04:20 UTC
7 sources · 1775 points on Hacker News · tracked since Oct 1 · 2 new articles on Oct 2 · 2 independent origins: Artificial Analysis, Google

Google announced Gemini 4 Argon, a new frontier model, which it is rolling out first to a set of trusted cyber defenders through its Fairwind Program.1 Google says Argon delivers frontier performance in software engineering, enterprise knowledge work and cybersecurity defense, and that it can autonomously find, validate and patch critical software vulnerabilities.234 Google says Wiz is using Argon and that the model uncovered a critical vulnerability exposing sensitive personal information in healthcare software used by hospitals worldwide, a severe risk previous frontier models had missed; Ars Technica notes Google didn't provide any specifics.56

Google says it is raising the output limit to an industry-leading 1M tokens, up from 64K, and Argon's introductory pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with cached input 95% off.789 Latent Space says Vals lists a 262K maximum output, while Artificial Analysis reached 1M output tokens through a new API feature, Long Decode Continuation, that pauses and resumes long responses across calls.10 A Latent Space recap also reports that Argon's lower cost per task comes from price rather than efficiency, since it averages 62K output tokens per task against Astra's 27K.11

Artificial Analysis lists Argon (High) at 53 on its Intelligence Index, against a median of 26 for similar reasoning models in its price tier.12 A Latent Space recap says a circulated Bloomberg report attributed coding weaknesses to anonymous insiders and a senior DeepMind engineer reportedly rejected it, and it treats the coding-quality dispute as unresolved.1314 Zvi Mowshowitz says it is far too early to judge Argon because Google has not released access, though he calls the initial benchmarks very promising.1516 Latent Space calls the benchmarks very respectable but notes the limited cybersecurity preview, and says the big question was when Google would catch up to peers that launched Fable and Astra class models.1718

Google says it will continue to gather feedback from early testers and iterate on guardrails before making Argon available to developers, enterprises and consumers as soon as possible, starting with paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers.192021 Google says it is engaged in the U.S. government's voluntary process for pre-release model access, and Ars Technica notes Google is not making specific timeline promises. inference222324

How does Gemini 4 Argon score on DeepSWE compared with GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5?

Google says Gemini 4 Argon scores 77.9% on the DeepSWE v1.1 software-engineering benchmark, which it calls a new state of the art.1 That is ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 at 74.2% and GPT-6 Astra at 74.1%, so Argon leads both by about 3.7 to 3.8 points. inference2 Ars Technica likewise reports that Argon's score is higher than GPT-6 Astra, Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5.3 The figures are Google's own claims, and some observers have objected to certain numbers, including DeepSWE.45

What will Argon cost once the introductory discount ends?

Latent Space's AINews reports that the standard price is $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, and that the introductory 50% discount brings that down to $2 and $10.12 Google itself only describes the $2 and $10 rates as introductory, and says cached input tokens cost 95% less than the input price.3 If the standard rate applies when the discount ends, the average cost per task would double, from $1.99 to $3.98, according to Latent Space. inference4 Latent Space's AINews also reports that no end date for the 50% introductory discount was announced.5

What has Google's own team done with Argon internally, such as saving data-center memory or migrating code to Rust?

Google says a team of Argon agents analyzed fleet-wide profiling telemetry to identify and apply memory optimizations across its data centers, freeing up over 300 TiB of memory once rolled out.1 Google estimates total memory savings could reach 500 TiB to 1 PiB.2 Google also says Argon agents are migrating C/C++ codebases to Rust, scaling from tens of thousands of lines in libraries like re2 and libgav1 to more than 800,000 lines for the Fuchsia Zircon kernel.3 Google says these rewrites are undergoing automated and manual auditing, emulation testing and review before reaching production.4 In libgav1, Google's open-source video decoder, Google says Argon agents took an existing Rust port and replaced 32K lines of SIMD code with safe Rust, producing a memory-safe decoder that runs 2.7x faster than that Rust port with identical video output.567 Latent Space notes that developer accounts of how the Gemini 4 Argon model was built and used are not independent evidence of general superiority.8

How does Google say it will stop Argon from misbehaving, and what safeguards is it adding before a wider release?

Google says it is strengthening what it calls critical frontier safeguards across four areas before rolling Argon out broadly.1 To stop misuse, including cyber and chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear attacks, Google says the model is designed to refuse harmful requests while still allowing legitimate dual-use science. It says it is also improving techniques to monitor the model's internal activations, and that internal and external red teams tested these safeguards.234 To keep Argon from misbehaving, Google says it is deploying misalignment mitigations that monitor the model's chain-of-thought and actions and stop execution when necessary. Google also says it is hardening its sandboxed environments by isolating and sealing them before high-risk training or evaluations begin.56 Access is being phased. Google says it is engaged in the U.S. government's voluntary pre-release access process and will keep gathering feedback from early testers to iterate on guardrails, with paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers first in line after the cyber testers.78910 Trusted defenders and Google's internal teams will get Argon without cyber guardrails, according to Google.11
