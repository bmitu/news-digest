UN autonomous weapons talks end without binding mandate

Last scan Sun Nov 22, 14:05 UTC
3 sources · 2 independent origins: NPR, Politico

UN talks in Geneva on regulating autonomous weapons ended on Saturday without a negotiating mandate, with countries agreeing instead to extend discussions until 2028, according to Politico.1 According to Politico, Swiss chair Julien Thöni told journalists that the 128 participating states had consented to begin drafting, across a two-year period, a global instrument covering lethal autonomous weapons systems.2 The decision as seen by Politico does not mention a legally binding instrument, and a group of intergovernmental experts is to report to a 2028 meeting of the treaty's parties.34

Before the talks, the Red Cross said binding rules should be drafted within a year, a call NPR says the UN secretary-general and Pope Leo backed; the compromise gave countries two years to propose regulations, with some rules possibly left optional.567

Politico says Russia, exploiting the consensus requirement, blocked formal negotiations and the participation of observers including the EU, so talks were held informally.8 Sources differ on who was responsible: a Brazilian diplomat said others were hiding behind Russia, two EU diplomats named the US, UK, South Korea, Japan, Turkey and Israel as also resisting, and NPR (via The Verge) named Russia, Israel and the US as the main obstructions.91011 The Brazilian diplomat said that in the final hours, supporters of regulation such as Ireland, Brazil, Belgium and Austria softened their positions for fear of leaving empty-handed.12

Civil society groups were critical: Human Rights Watch's Verity Coyle called the outcome disappointing, and Rain Liivoja of the Asia-Pacific Institute for Law and Security said it is possible, even likely, that there will be no treaty by the end of 2028.1314 Max Tegmark of the Future of Life Institute says the consensus principle produced an empty agreement and urges frustrated states to take the issue to another forum.1516
