Trump intervenes to clear Huang

Last scan Thu Nov 26, 05:11 UTC
5 sources · tracked since Nov 24 · 4 new articles today · single origin (Semafor)

Jensen Huang was reinstated as Nvidia CEO on the morning of November 23, after President Donald Trump publicly urged the board to rehire him and, according to reports, phoned the board.12 Huang had resigned after news of multiple affairs, including one with a woman tied to Chinese intelligence services.3 Trump said the government found no evidence of unlawful conduct by Huang, citing the Attorney General, and Nvidia said its board received input from senior US government levels that left it confident there was no national security risk.45

Huang disputes that Trump was the sole reason for his return, calling that claim "completely untrue" and saying the board evaluated the whole situation, though Zvi Mowshowitz dismisses this as technically-true wording.67 The market response was sharp. According to TechCrunch, the Nasdaq climbed 7%, and Semafor notes that the $1 trillion rebound on Monday made up just roughly one-third of what had been wiped out since September.89

Critics call Trump's intervention improper: TechCrunch says it is unprecedented for a sitting president to clear a CEO accused of being a national security risk and notes Huang's $25 million donation to Trump's ballroom project.1011 Senator Elizabeth Warren stated that Huang and Nvidia continue to owe Congress some answers. Chuck Schumer was reported to have said Democrats would bring all their power to bear in an investigation. Newt Gingrich, an ally of Trump, said it is "profoundly wrong" that Huang, who has ties to a Chinese Communist spy and helped China obtain advanced chips, is now being restored as CEO.121314 Semafor argues Huang is now effectively unfireable yet indebted to Trump, and TechCrunch predicts the export-control questions over chips reaching China will continue to damage Nvidia.1516
