Delaware AI company entity proposal

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
2 sources · single origin (Ars Technica)

US Rep. James Comer (R-Ky.), chairman of the House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform, told Delaware Governor Matt Meyer in a letter that a Delaware proposal to let AI agents operate companies would help the firms' human owners evade legal responsibility for reckless and harmful AI actions.1 Comer asked for documents on the proposal's development and potential AI safety risks, and suggested the probe could inform federal legislative efforts to limit state AI regulatory initiatives.23

Under a draft bill that may go to the Delaware legislature, an Artificial Intelligence Company (AIC) could have any lawful purpose except banking, would have its day-to-day affairs managed by an AI agent, and would have a single member, a person or an entity.45 The draft says the member is not liable for the AIC's acts or omissions, with exceptions for fraud, willful violations of law, or knowingly allowing business outside the AIC's stated purpose.678 The bill also proposes a regulatory sandbox lasting 30 months to test the idea.9

The framework is being developed as a public-private partnership led by Norm Ai, according to an op-ed co-written by Delaware's Patibanda-Sanchez and Norm Ai CEO John Nay.10 Patibanda-Sanchez said at a June 2026 Delaware AI Commission meeting that a new entity form would be a huge benefit to the state's revenues.1112

UC Berkeley law professor Andrew Baker told the Financial Times the proposal seems likely to be an overly generous liability shield, which seems unwarranted without some underlying policy rationale that hasn't been developed or justified well.13 Zvi Mowshowitz says he is glad the House is investigating, arguing that someone must ultimately be responsible for an AI's actions, even if that is an insurance company.14 Martin Petrin, a York University law professor, told Bloomberg that other states will likely follow with similar proposals and that a federal framework could help address safety concerns and a regulatory race to the bottom.15
