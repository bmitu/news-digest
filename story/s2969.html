Anthropic restricts Claude from cloning video games

Last scan Sat Nov 7, 03:59 UTC
5 sources · 196 points on Hacker News · 2 independent origins: Ars Technica, Tom's Hardware

Anthropic has added two sentences to Claude Code's system prompt to steer it away from recreating specific existing video games, according to Anthropic's Addy Osmani.12 Osmani said the reason is that using Claude to infringe IP is against Anthropic's usage policy, and that original games, genre games and games built from a user's own designs and assets are meant to keep working.34

A user going by "chasm" was among the first to note the change, saying a new rule seemed to be added to the Claude Code prompt on or around October 27, and XDA later independently verified, per Ars Technica, that the rule appears in version 2.1.314 of the Claude Code binary, released on October 30.567 However, The Verge noted that Osmani did not confirm the exact wording in the screenshot, though Ars Technica reports he confirmed the two-sentence addition. inference89

Ars Technica's Kyle Orland argues the rule is unlikely to slow the wave of AI-assisted game porting and copying, since copiers can switch to other frontier models or cheaper open-weight models without such restrictions.1011 Zvi Mowshowitz says a bunch of people respond as if the change is bad, when it is obviously good, while a Reddit discussion summarized by Latent Space was skeptical, with commenters saying users may route such requests to less restrictive models like DeepSeek and that the rule may overblock legitimate uses such as resurrecting or preserving classic games.1213
