Google AI publisher payment pilot and disputes

2 sources · tracked since Oct 1 · 2 new articles today · 2 independent origins: Reuters, The Information

Google's pilot program that pays publishers when their content contributes to AI answers is reportedly delivering very small payments, according to a report by The Information as relayed by Ars Technica.123 The pitch is that websites can receive payments when their content materially contributes to Gemini-powered search results such as an AI Overview.4 The Information heard from several small and mid-sized publishers that the payments equal roughly one-tenth of one percent of their advertising revenue, and several larger publishers have reportedly declined to join.56 Payouts vary widely: one early joiner is on track to earn more than $1 million per year, a more recent addition has earned $50,000 to $60,000, and various smaller sites have seen less than $1,000 over several months.789

Separately, a federal judge, US District Judge Amit Mehta, dismissed antitrust lawsuits by Chegg and Penske Media that accused Google of driving away traffic with AI search features.101112 The publishers had alleged that Google coerced them into supplying content for AI Overviews for free or risk disappearing from search results.1314 Mehta wrote that the plaintiffs had pleaded only an 'expectation' of traffic, and that 'an expectation is not an agreement.'1516 Mehta said the court is not unsympathetic to publishers' situation but that antitrust rules cannot replace a legislative decision on the economic impact of new innovation.171819

What are regulators in the UK and the European Union doing about Google's use of publisher content in AI answers?

The UK government ordered Google earlier this year to give publishers an AI opt-out that would not penalize them in organic search results.12 In the European Union, the European Commission is running an antitrust probe into whether Google fairly compensates publishers for using their content in AI answers.3 Ars Technica's reporter writes that, given Google's recent track record in the EU, it seems unlikely the EU antitrust probe will work out in Google's favor.4 The contrast with the US is notable.
