Microsoft global outage

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
5 sources · 2 independent origins: Microsoft, Politico

Microsoft suffered a major global outage that hit 365 services including Outlook, Teams and Copilot, and the company said it knew of no malicious activity behind it.12 Microsoft admitted that earlier attempted fixes were not working as expected and that many servers became unavailable because of elevated load.34 The two sources disagree on how long the outage went on: Politico puts it at almost a full day and says millions of people across 74 countries were hit, whereas the OECD's incident monitor records a duration of more than 12 hours. inference56

Effects reported included Thames Valley Police and Cumbria Police saying citizens may have issues reporting crimes, and a headline reporting that the outage gummed up markets and forced traders to use phones.78

The OECD's AI Incidents Monitor classifies the event as an AI incident because Copilot was among the affected services, while acknowledging that the company denies AI caused the failure.9 Commentator Gary Marcus says it is unknown whether the outage was a malicious cyberattack or an LLM failure, yet he still blames Silicon Valley's arrogance and Washington's ineffectiveness.1011 An Atlantic writer notes that a nationwide US ground stop coincided with the Microsoft outage but says they have no idea what caused it.1213

In Europe the outage prompted fresh calls for digital sovereignty: Mistral CEO Arthur Mensch said only 20% of European digital services run on European infrastructure, though he did not specifically address the outage.1415 French President Emmanuel Macron wrote on X that Europe's independence cannot be outsourced.16 Sebastian Toffaletti of the Digital SME Alliance called the outage a reminder of the systemic risk of concentrating digital infrastructure with a few global tech giants, and UK Liberal Democrat spokesperson Victoria Collins urged action to stop relying on a handful of companies.1718
