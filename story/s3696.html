Microsoft Lazarus backdoor

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
2 sources · single origin (Microsoft)

Microsoft researchers demonstrated a technique called Lazarus, in which editing fewer than 10 rarely used neurons in a large language model can implant a sleeper backdoor that lets attackers trigger malicious commands without retraining.12 The work was covered on 2026-11-11 by outlets including TechRadar, InfoWorld and The Next Web.345

A summary of the research indicates that a slight tweak to a model's weights might let an agent act normally during everyday testing, yet act differently when certain triggering circumstances arise.6 The OECD's monitor warns that AI agents able to access a terminal or the underlying system face serious security dangers from this method, since ordinary safety checks might fail to catch it.7 The OECD's automated assessment classes this as an AI hazard rather than an incident, noting that no real-world attacks have been reported yet but that the demonstrated capability creates a plausible risk of harm.89

Microsoft advises teams that rely on checkpoints from outside providers to lock onto specific model versions, and to confirm hashes and signatures prior to loading.10 People posting in a discussion thread wondered if separate verification of tool calls could detect a model that has been tampered with.11
