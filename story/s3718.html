South Korea AI-glasses exam cheating

Last scan Sat Nov 21, 06:54 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 14 · 7 new articles this week · single origin (The Korea Herald)

South Korea's top scorer on the national tax accountant exam, Han Won-seok, admitted to using AI to cheat, according to Fortune.1 He stated in a clip uploaded November 15 that he had used a tiny camera to capture the test items, fed the pictures into an AI program, and then looked over the responses on his phone.23 Han also said he used the same method on a separate certified public accountant exam, and he said he is cooperating with authorities.45

Fortune reported that the video had more than 1.5 million views, and, according to the Korea Herald as cited by Fortune, Han scored perfect marks in three of four subjects, including one where most candidates averaged about 30 out of 100.678

Suspicions reportedly spread to other professional exams, including appraiser and customs broker tests, and police launched a task force, according to the OECD AI Incidents Monitor.910 Separately, candidates in Korea Exchange and Korea Financial Investment Association exams were reportedly caught using AI smart glasses, leading to invalidated exams and five-year bans.1112

Law firm YK is organizing a class action against the exam authority, citing its failure to detect the cheating and the harm to other candidates.13

Platforms such as Naver and Danggeun tightened monitoring and sales controls on AI glasses and miniature cameras, and searches for AI glasses on Danggeun rose 233%.1415 Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin said the ministry would inspect every pair of glasses worn by students at the national college entrance exams, and the presidential office said it was following the case "very seriously."1617
