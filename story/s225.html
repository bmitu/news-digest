The Copilot+ PC brand is dead

2 sources · 122 points on Hacker News · tracked since Sep 27 · 1 new today · single origin (Windows Central)

Microsoft's new Surface PCs announced in September 2026 do not carry the Copilot+ PC branding, and Surface corporate VP Brett Ostrum confirmed they "are not called Copilot+ PCs" even though they meet the requirements.12 Ostrum added that Microsoft still leans into the narrative around AI on the edge and a hybrid solution.34

Ars Technica reports that Copilot+ PCs require 16GB of RAM, 256GB of storage and an NPU rated at 40 TOPS or better, and that the new Surface Pro and Surface Laptop, due October 13, have an 80 TOPS Qualcomm Hexagon NPU.56 Qualcomm's SVP of compute Kedar Kondap said Copilot+ PCs were meant to define a category of devices with a certain bar, such as a 45 TOPS NPU, and that the same experiences are now offered probably without using the terminology.789

Ars Technica says a Microsoft Copilot+ PCs landing page that was up as recently as May now redirects to a "performance PCs" page that still names Copilot+ PCs but far less prominently.10 Windows Central's author argues the brand hasn't resonated with the market and that OEMs and Microsoft are quietly pulling back, noting that more OEMs have dropped the moniker and that Nvidia hasn't gone near the Copilot+ PC brand for RTX Spark, which he suspects is a deliberate decision.111213

Ars Technica offers several possible reasons, including Recall's security concerns, the campaign possibly having served its purpose, and RTX Spark possibly taking marketing precedence, and cites IDC's Jitesh Ubrani saying general interest in AI PCs has been wavering.141516 Ars Technica also notes that Dell killed its AI PC laptop branding this year, while Satya Nadella had said in January 2025 that Copilot+ PCs were 15 percent of US premium laptop sales in the holiday season.1718
