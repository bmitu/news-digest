Securities class action against Nvidia over Huang

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
2 sources · 2 independent origins: Law360, TechCrunch

Rosen Law Firm filed a securities class action against Nvidia and CEO Jensen Huang in the Northern District of California on Monday afternoon, on behalf of retail investor Mark Kistenmacher.12 The lawsuit is brought on behalf of anyone who purchased Nvidia securities from Oct. 9, 2025, through Nov. 8, 2026; it asks for a jury trial and for damages in an amount not specified, along with interest and attorneys' fees.34 The complaint alleges Nvidia and Huang misled investors about Huang's ties to Megaspeed, where Alice Huang was considered a top executive, citing three statements it calls false or misleading because Huang was having an affair with her.56

The filing followed a board announcement that it would investigate Huang over his relationship with Alice Huang, whom U.S. intelligence believes was spying for China; Nvidia's stock fell 4.27% ($7.97 a share) on Monday.78 The stock then fell another 5.47% on Tuesday, about 9.5% over two days, and the board hired Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz for an internal investigation.910

Stanford Law professor Robert Bartlett argues the earlier company statement was "clearly a misleading statement" and that Huang's knowledge is imputed to the company, and he predicted a classic fraud-on-the-market claim.111213 However, TechCrunch cautions that neither law firm announcements nor the complaint signals a strong case, and the court will later choose the lead plaintiff, usually based on who suffered the biggest loss.1415 Ann Lipton of the University of Colorado expects shareholder suits, saying securities fraud is the easier route because it is "only about lying," while a Delaware mismanagement derivative claim is much harder.161718
