Jacob Coxon leaves Anthropic

Last scan Sat Nov 21, 06:54 UTC
3 sources · single origin, unclear which

Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon announced his resignation with a warning that his employer and OpenAI were racing toward self-improving superintelligence and gambling with people's lives.1 The Atlantic dates the departure to September, saying Coxon quit in order to argue that AI companies might kill us all.2 According to IEEE Spectrum, Anthropic's head of alignment science Evan Hubinger replied that Anthropic earnestly believes AI could kill all humans, and CEO Dario Amodei later told CNN he agreed with Coxon much more than he disagreed.34

Coxon says a slide from Anthropic's IPO roadshow was a big factor in his decision to leave; he says it shows continued superexponential algorithmic progress in pretraining compute efficiency.56

Coxon says he committed to donating all of his OpenAI equity to AI safety causes, in part to back his message that the race to superintelligence must be paused or drastically slowed.789 The Atlantic says he had earlier seemed to laugh off the idea of donating the shares in an interview, saying "I like the money, bro," and calls this naked hypocrisy before noting he has since agreed to donate.1011 Zvi Mowshowitz says it makes sense for Coxon to make the pledge because people see it that way, though he would ideally prefer Coxon keep his stock, and he argues that holding equity makes Coxon's statements admissions against interest and so more credible.1213

Coxon is due to speak at a protest outside Anthropic's San Francisco offices on the day of the IPO, which Mowshowitz reports was priced at $83 per share, about a $1.5 trillion valuation.1415
