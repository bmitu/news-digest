Cursor weighs phasing out Chinese AI models

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 17:01 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 27 · single origin (CNBC)

Cursor has informed Congress that it is weighing an end to its use of Chinese AI models over "geopolitical risk," Fortune says, citing a CNBC report.1 Cursor's Composer coding agent was built on Kimi, a model from Moonshot, one of the Chinese developers Fortune discusses. inference23 Zvi Mowshowitz interprets the geopolitical risk as the possibility of government disapproval of such Chinese models, and while he sees some chance the models themselves are compromised, he presumes that is not what worries Cursor.45

According to Fortune, increasing business adoption of Chinese open-weight models, in the U.S. and globally, has some in Washington concerned about China's sway over the technology.6 Reuters reported on Nov. 18 that the Trump administration was weighing mandatory public disclosure by American companies of their use of Chinese models, hoping to deter it without new curbs that could anger Beijing.78 In a Nov. 20 report, Representative John Moolenaar, who chairs the House Select Committee on China, cautioned about U.S. firms taking up cheap Chinese models, and wrote that federal investigators had found major Chinese developers using fraudulent accounts and proxy networks to copy American frontier models' capabilities. inference91011

Srividya Jandhyala, a management professor at ESSEC Business School, says global firms, particularly in Western markets, must weigh lower Chinese prices against worries over continued access, and may switch early to American models if they expect future policy to force their hand.121314

On the security side, Erik Cambria, an AI professor at Nanyang Technological University, argues that fears of hidden backdoors in Chinese models may be overblown, since running open weights locally without sending sensitive data outside carries quite different risks from using a remotely hosted API.1516
