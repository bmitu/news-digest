Meta launches VR Glasses

2 sources · tracked since Sep 27 · 1 new article today · 2 independent origins: Ars Technica, Meta

Meta used its annual Connect event to present Muse, its personal AI agent, as the centerpiece of a hardware-plus-agent strategy, and Mark Zuckerberg called Muse the "centerpiece" of his AI vision. inference12 Among the hardware, Meta launched new virtual reality glasses, which the Financial Times says will be available next spring for $1,300 and are five times lighter than the previous Quest 3 model.3

Latent Space's recap describes the Meta VR Glasses as Meta's first VR delivered in glasses rather than a headset, pitched as a private cinema, multi-monitor workstation and game console, with a price of $1,299 attributed to @kimmonismus.45 Meta also announced Muse Charm, a keychain-sized pendant activated by a fingerprint sensor, with a screen showing the Muse avatar and real-time voice, set for release in December.678 Other announcements included a third-generation Ray-Ban Meta with longer battery and upgraded microphones, and, per the FT, a camera-free audio-only Ray-Ban pair. inference910

Wang also teased that Meta will drop "the most capable model we have ever trained" pretty soon, but no new frontier model was released at the event.1112

The FT reports Muse has been the most downloaded app on both the Apple and Android app stores in the US since its launch two weeks ago, and that Meta's stock has bounced back since the September 8 launch, adding $300 billion to its market capitalisation.1314

The FT cautions that success depends on whether the features withstand privacy and security issues, noting Meta has already fixed a vulnerability that could let a hacker take control of another person's Muse agent, and that Amazon has blocked Meta's agents from its shopping platform.1516 Critics say Meta's camera glasses are used to record others without their knowledge, while Zuckerberg insisted Muse has "state-of-the-art privacy and security."1718

Live-blogger @kimmonismus said he was "super impressed by the VR glasses… first mover" and noted "very low latency" in demos.19

How is OpenAI responding to Muse, and what does its rival product Dots offer?

OpenAI answered Meta's Muse with its own agentic assistants called Dots, announced at its Tuesday DevDay keynote.12 OpenAI describes Dots as always-on assistants that can "do nearly anything" across connected apps in the background while learning a user's preferences over time.34 Dots run on the GPT-6 Astra model and use their own cloud computer to reach a web browser and more than 4,000 supported apps.56 Users can message a Dot in a text-message-like interface or by voice call from ChatGPT, and Dots also connect to Slack and Microsoft Teams. Texting is coming soon, and for now each user can create only one Dot.78910 TechCrunch notes that Dots present a bubbly, cartoonish persona similar to Meta's Muse agent, and that much of their functionality was already possible through Codex and similar agentic harnesses.1112

How does Meta's Muse Realtime Avatar compare with rival avatar tools from Runway and HeyGen?

Meta says its Muse Realtime Avatar came out ahead of Runway Characters and HeyGen LiveAvatar in head-to-head tests.1 In Meta's test, raters held 2–3 minute conversations with matched avatar identities on each product's own live-call experience, and they judged visual quality, audio-visual sync, character consistency and mannerisms.23 The result comes from Meta itself and should be treated with caution, as Meta reported that Muse "came out ahead on overall preference" but posted no margins or rater counts in its tweets.45 On its own specs, Muse Realtime Avatar answers in under a second, supports unbounded session length, and watermarks all output as AI without adding latency, while another recap puts its response latency target at about 870ms.678910 No comparable latency, session-length or watermarking figures were given for Runway's or HeyGen's tools, so a spec-by-spec comparison was not reported.
