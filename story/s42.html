Anthropic wet lab established

2 sources · tracked since Sep 27 · single origin (Anthropic)

Anthropic announced a new life sciences research group and molecular biology lab, saying Claude discovered a novel enzyme system with properties reminiscent of CRISPR, with only high-level direction from its scientists.12 Anthropic says it formed the research group in the spring of 2026, and that its lab, in the Bay Area, does only BSL-1 and BSL-2 work, handles no human-infecting pathogens, and has all lab work done by human scientists.34

Anthropic says that after 21 hours of searching by roughly 950 agents using 210 million tokens, one agent spotted something remarkable: a repeating pattern of DNA sequences next to the gene for an odd-looking reverse transcriptase (RT).5 Anthropic calls the system array-associated reverse transcriptases (ART), found mainly in bacteriophages, and says it has three parts: the RT, a partner gene, and a long array of evenly spaced DNA repeats.67

Anthropic says early experiments show the array is expressed as distinct short RNAs, but its function is still unknown, and the company says work to understand it is ongoing.89 Anthropic says the system shares a set of characteristics found together in only a handful of other systems, all of them programmable and able to cut, copy and paste DNA, but that much more work is needed to learn whether it can be put to similar use.1011

Feng Zhang, a CRISPR pioneer at MIT and the Broad Institute, who reviewed the pre-print, called it an exciting example of AI agents contributing to discovery and said the RNA-repeat arrays associated with reverse transcriptases merit further investigation.1213 Zvi Mowshowitz is more measured on the science, saying that as biology it is not a big deal and that a PhD student finding it would be cool but not headline-making.14 Mowshowitz says a wet lab is not obviously a bad idea if Anthropic sets it up and supervises it responsibly.15

What lab experiments did human scientists run on the ART system, and what did they show?

Human scientists ran Claude-proposed experiments that expressed the ART system in E. coli and sequenced its RNA, according to a Latent Space summary.1 Anthropic says its first experiments show the ART repeat array is expressed as a set of distinct short RNAs, which hints that something similar to CRISPR's RNA bank may be at work.2 Anthropic explicitly says the system's biological function and any programmable editing utility remain unknown, and commenters emphasize that ART is not yet demonstrated to cut/copy/paste DNA or enable gene editing.345 Critics push back on the lab work. One commenter called it "very limited", and a technical critique said that finding repeat arrays and showing they make short RNAs is a fairly standard genomics workflow.67 Anthropic says all of the lab work was done by human scientists, that further experiments are underway to determine how ART works, and that its lab does research involving only the lower biosafety levels (BSL-1 and BSL-2) and does not handle pathogens that can infect humans.8910
