Proton AI re-interviews

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
2 sources · 653 points on Hacker News · single origin (404 Media)

Proton CEO Andy Yen cancelled a series of nearly company-wide 're-interviews' about employees' AI use on Friday, according to 404 Media, which cites six current employees and audio of an all-hands meeting.12 The re-interviews had been announced on Monday in an internal email, with the implication that they may decide whether staffers have 'continued employment'.34 The cancellation followed a 404 Media investigation published Thursday, based on eight current and one former employee, Slack chats, meeting audio and an AI manifesto by CTO Bart Butler, which reported that Yen was aggressively pushing AI, in some cases despite employees' security concerns.56

In one Slack exchange from late August, Yen answered an employee's call to pentest new code before release by writing, "That’s bullshit and loser thinking. Of course it’s possible to be fast and secure."7 The investigation also reported a culture of fear in which employees feel unable to voice concerns because Yen mocks or insults them in front of others.8

At the all-hands, Yen said there was 'a communication failure on my part' and that the re-interviews had 'already served their purpose', which 404 Media explains as employees now talking and learning about AI. He added, 'I'm still going to push.'91011 Yen said a reorganization is planned for next month, "but there will be no loss of jobs from AI."12 Butler said Yen has committed to doing better on communication and that using AI 'does not mean that we’ve given up on security', and also said, "We fucked up, we know we fucked up."1314

Employee reactions were split: one said some are happy at the progress while others doubt anything will change without consequences for Yen, and another said the re-interview criteria would become part of performance reviews and called it 'damage control'.151617 A third employee said they were 'mostly happy' with how it was addressed, while 404 Media reports that users downvoted Yen's Reddit response over 1,200 times, many said they were cancelling paid plans, and some called for him to resign.181920
