Proton AI re-interviews

Last scan Sat Nov 7, 03:59 UTC
2 sources · 170 points on Hacker News · tracked since Oct 30 · 4 new articles this week · single origin (404 Media)

Proton CEO Andy Yen cancelled a planned series of nearly company-wide "re-interviews" that would have forced employees to explain how they use AI, 404 Media reports, citing six current employees and audio of an roughly two-hour all-hands meeting.12 According to 404 Media, the clear intention was that employees might be fired if Proton leadership judged they were not using AI enough.3

The cancellation came the day after 404 Media published an investigation showing Yen aggressively pushing AI across Proton, in some cases despite employees' security concerns.4 In one exchange in the security Slack channel, an employee urged Proton to take a few days to pentest new code before release, and Yen replied, per 404 Media, "That’s bullshit and loser thinking. Of course it’s possible to be fast and secure."56 Yen also reportedly wrote "stop lovingly handcrafting all our code and use AI more."7

404 Media says the cancellation likely does not address the concerns of many Proton users, who focus on the deprioritization of security; many have criticized leadership on social media and called for Yen to resign.89

Blogger Raed, a longtime paying Proton customer, writes that he is no longer sure he would recommend Proton for the first time.1011 The blogger argues that none of this is about AI, since engineers using AI to write code is the new norm and is fine as long as there are guardrails around it.1213 The blogger also claims that, when the plan was dropped after it leaked, Yen said it had already served its purpose, and that Proton as a privacy company cannot trade caution for speed.1415 The blogger dismisses employees defending the company on Reddit, who said Yen was fine with pentesting as long as it did not slow the pipeline, as reading like astroturfing and clumsy crisis management.1617
