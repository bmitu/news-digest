Amazon blocks Meta Muse from shopping

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 03:34 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Sep 27 · 1 new article today · 2 independent origins: Ars Technica, Nate B. Jones

Amazon has blocked Meta's new AI assistant Muse from its platform, saying such apps "should operate openly and respect service provider decisions about whether or not to participate."1 Muse launched on September 8 and, by Jones's account, reached the top of the US free iPhone chart ten days later.2

Jones calls Muse "a profoundly important launch" and argues it could become "Zuckerberg's next platform."34 His piece suggests the lockout matters because Muse sits between users and sellers, which he says is what makes an assistant useful. inference5 Jones says that when he asked Muse to work through his subscriptions, it identified $5,350 a year in spending and so far has canceled subscriptions totaling $1,285 a year.6

Separately, tech columnist Jason Aten said Muse sent him an unsolicited notification referencing a thread between him and a co-worker in Apple Messages, though he says he never granted it permission to read his messages.78 Security expert Patrick Wardle questioned that denial, noting that with Full Disk Access any non-root file is readable, and Meta's PR only repeated Singleton's statement when asked.910

What exactly is Apple changing in the macOS Full Disk Access settings, and does it contradict Meta's claim that the Messages connector must be enabled?

Apple says it is adding new Full Disk Access controls on macOS so that users who genuinely want to give an app this extraordinary level of access can do so only through very explicit user action.12 Apple says some developers are using Full Disk Access in ways that could put users at risk, exposing files, mail, messages and browsing history without users' full knowledge and understanding, and that as AI agents become increasingly capable and autonomous, the risks associated with this level of access will grow substantially.345 Meta CTO David Singleton, for his part, says Muse can read Messages content only if macOS Full Disk Access is granted and the Messages connector is enabled.67 Ars Technica's Dan Goodin argues that Apple's statements seem to contradict Singleton's denial, and security expert Patrick Wardle says that with Full Disk Access, any non-root file is readable. Apple's statement does not directly address the connector claim, so the contradiction is Ars's reading rather than something Apple has said. inference89 Apple did not say when the update will roll out, and it did not name Meta or Muse.1011
