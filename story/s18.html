Liquid AI DSpark speculative decoding for VLM

2 sources · tracked since Sep 27 · single origin (Liquid AI)

Liquid AI released an experimental DSpark speculative-decoding draft model for its vision-language model LFM2.5-VL-3B, which it says speeds up decoding without changing output quality.12 Liquid AI says the drafter adds about 280M parameters, or 8.9% on top of the 3B target model, and ships with day-one support for llama.cpp, MLX-VLM and SGLang.34

Liquid AI reports decode speedups of up to 3.13x on device and 2.66x on an H100, with end-to-end gains of up to 2.62x and 2.27x.5 On-device, MLX on an M5 Max decodes 2.30x to 3.13x faster depending on task, while llama.cpp on an M3 Ultra gains 1.57x to 2.14x in decoding and 1.30x to 1.77x end-to-end.67 The tests used a block size of 8 across six vision tasks following the MMSpec benchmark.89

Liquid AI acknowledges a limit: speculation speeds up only decoding, not vision encoding or prefill, so end-to-end gains can be modest when those stages dominate, which it likens to Amdahl's law.1011

The Latent Space AINews newsletter also covered the release, repeating the 3.13x, 2.14x and 2.66x decode figures.1213

How is the vision DSpark drafter built, and how does it differ from Liquid AI's text-only LFM2.5-DSpark drafters?

Liquid AI's vision DSpark drafter uses the same architecture as its text-only LFM2.5-DSpark drafters: it captures the target model's hidden states at fixed tapped layers and uses them to draft a block of candidate tokens.1 Image patches and text tokens are projected into a shared representation before those layers, so the drafter operates on hidden-state vectors of identical dimensionality regardless of input modality, and the inference algorithm is unchanged from the text models.234 Liquid AI says it trained the drafter with the DSpark recipe on a mixture of vision-language SFT data weighted toward expected workloads.5 The result is a simplified attention-only drafter with 4 layers and a block size of 9, totaling about 280M parameters, which is 8.9% on top of the 3B LFM2.5-VL-3B target.67 The text-only drafters' own sizes and layer counts were not given, so the differences are limited to the shared-representation input handling.

How do I run the DSpark drafter with llama.cpp, MLX-VLM or SGLang, and do I need special builds or pull requests?

Liquid AI says its experimental DSpark drafter for LFM2.5-VL-3B has day-one support for llama.cpp, MLX-VLM and SGLang.12 Yes, each one needs a special build. SGLang needs a build with DSpark support for LFM2 targets (PR #40651), llama.cpp needs a build from PR#29339, and MLX-VLM needs a build from PR#2280.345 For SGLang, you launch the target with the draft attached: the DSPARK speculative algorithm, the LFM2.5-VL-3B-DSpark draft path, the flashinfer attention backend, block size 9 and the radix cache disabled. You then query the OpenAI-compatible endpoint at localhost:30000/v1.67 For llama.cpp, you run llama-server with the F16 GGUF model, its multimodal projector and the DSpark drafter as the draft model. You also pass the draft-dspark spec type with a draft n-max of 8.89 For MLX-VLM, the command is mlx_vlm.server with the LFM2.5-VL-3B model and the LFM2.5-VL-3B-DSpark draft model.10 The drafter is available on Hugging Face in Safetensors and GGUF formats.11

How much slower is LFM2.5-VL-3B's decoding on an H100 without the drafter, and why does the H100 speedup range start at 20.4x in Liquid AI's post?

Liquid AI says its DSpark drafter speeds up LFM2.5-VL-3B decoding by up to 2.66x on an H100, so decoding without the drafter is at most about 2.66 times slower. inference1 However, Liquid AI's post gives the H100 decode range as "20.4x to 2.66x", which looks like a typo, since a range cannot start above its own maximum. inference2 The post does not explain the 20.4x figure, and the other reported ranges suggest the lower bound is much smaller.

How does the drafter's block size of 8 or 9 affect speed on different hardware, and which should I pick?

Liquid AI recommends a block size of 8 or 9 for its LFM2.5-VL-3B DSpark drafter, depending on the hardware.1 With block size 8, decoding ran 2.30x to 3.13x faster on an Apple M5 Max with MLX and 1.57x to 2.14x faster on an M3 Ultra with llama.cpp. On an H100, Liquid AI reports up to 2.66x faster decoding, though the same post also lists the range as '20.4x to 2.66x', which looks like a typo.2345 The drafter was trained with a block size of 9, and Liquid AI's SGLang launch example uses 9. Its llama.cpp example caps drafts at 8.678 If you want a choice, 8 is the size Liquid AI actually measured across all three setups, so it is the safer default. Try 9, the size the drafter was trained with, if you run on a GPU with SGLang and can benchmark it yourself. inference9107 Liquid AI did not say which hardware favors 8 and which favors 9.
