Anthropic usage policy update

Last scan Sat Oct 10, 12:51 UTC
3 sources · single origin (Anthropic)

Anthropic published an updated Usage Policy that, it says, takes effect on November 12, adding a ban on sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior toward its models.12 Anthropic says the abuse rule applies only in extreme cases where users repeatedly act cruelly with no discernible purpose, and not to frustration, pushback, dark creative themes, or model testing and research.345 Anthropic says Claude's ability to end such conversations remains the primary enforcement mechanism, while The Verge reports Anthropic did not comment on whether further measures such as user bans are planned. inference67

Anthropic consolidated scattered rules into a new section banning deceptive campaigns or artificial activity, saying it has seen state media outlets, government propaganda offices and commercial firms using Claude to run fake-account networks and fabricated news sites.89

Anthropic says it expanded its weapons ban to cover software and components that make weapons work, including arming drones, after seeing attempts to use its models to build weapons guidance and control software.101112 Its rewritten surveillance section bars tracking people without consent, using Claude to decide or recommend who to investigate, arrest or charge, and building surveillance tools; Anthropic says this does not change what it enforces in practice.13141516 The Verge notes that Anthropic says these rules may be modified for contracts with certain governmental customers if it judges safeguards adequate.17

For Claude connected to hardware that takes autonomous physical actions and could cause injury, Anthropic requires a qualified operator who can observe and stop the equipment, and the equipment must hold a safe state if Claude is disconnected; The Verge says it is unclear whether the operator must be human. inference1819

The Verge links the welfare-related rule to Anthropic's research into model welfare and Kyle Fish's statement that questions of consciousness and moral status are serious ones being investigated, while noting Microsoft AI has taken the opposite stance. inference2021 TechCrunch's Russell Brandom calls the abuse prohibition perhaps the most attention-getting change and notes it follows Anthropic's outreach to religious scholars, in some cases seeking to convince them that Claude could be conscious or have a soul.2223
