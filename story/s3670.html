FAA nationwide ground stop

Last scan Mon Nov 23, 13:58 UTC
7 sources · tracked since Nov 14 · 1 new article today · 3 independent origins: Derek Thompson, Semafor, TechCrunch

The FAA grounded all flights nationwide on Friday for about four hours, which disrupted thousands of flights, and the Wall Street Journal reports federal officials blamed outdated air-traffic control technology.1 Sources describe the stop as extremely rare, variously calling it the second time in 25 years or the third time this century. inference2345

According to early reports from an initial probe, a flood of data originating from an Arkansas router and a Texas radar site overwhelmed the system, and staff had to take it offline.6 Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said telecom routers had been overloaded with data, called the system incredibly old, and asked Congress for upgrade funding that he estimated at $50 billion.789 Duffy said on Saturday that the FAA and FBI did not yet have clarity on the root cause, though he later attributed the telecom issues to a contractor's wire replacement.10

White House cyber director Sean Cairncross told Semafor the outages were not a coordinated cyberattack by a malicious actor but came from updating and modernizing legacy systems, and TechCrunch reports the director of National Intelligence and President Trump also did not link them to an attack.111213 Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told CNN that Americans should be prepared for substantial investment in upgrading systems and in cybersecurity, and that any step change in technology will bring "bumps in the road."14 Senator Mark Warner argued that AI contributed indirectly, by speeding up discovery of vulnerabilities and forcing rushed patching that strains systems not built for that pace, while acknowledging no sign of a cyberattack or foreign interference.1516 Anusha Iyer of Corsha disputes the AI framing, saying a rushed mix of legacy and modern systems is the fault, not rapid AI adoption, while the FAA and its contractor said the $875 million SMART AI system was not involved.1718 Microsoft President Brad Smith, speaking on Face the Nation, warned that more outages are likely in the coming months because the world's information infrastructure is being upgraded, and said AI makes it easier to find flaws that can be used either to patch code or, by bad actors, to hack systems.1920

Jennifer Pahlka argues the FAA's repeated failures reflect outsourcing of expertise, and that more money has not made things better.212223 Techdirt criticized DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin for refusing to rule out AI and for relaying an anonymous tech figure's explanation, though the site itself guesses that layoffs of experienced staff are a more likely indirect factor.2425 The Verge reports that the FAA stop came alongside a multi-day Outlook outage and a JPMorgan Chase banking outage, and that attacks on government and commercial systems, including the USDA, JPMorgan and supermarkets, have also increased in recent months.2627
