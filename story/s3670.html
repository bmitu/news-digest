FAA nationwide ground stop

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
3 sources · single origin (The Hill)

The FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop on Friday afternoon that lasted around four hours, according to The Hill.1 Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the order followed a communication issue impacting all Air Route Traffic Control Centers, and said that "Our switches, our routers, actually got overloaded with data."23 Duffy said the airspace reopened at around 8:15 p.m.4

The Atlantic reported that more than 10,000 flights had been disrupted so far, and that CNN reported the problem may have originated with network routers at Chicago's Midway airport.56 One Atlantic writer said he had no idea what caused the ground stop, and noted that it also coincided with the Microsoft Outlook outage the day before.78 The Atlantic criticized the FAA for not explaining the cause for many hours, saying people read crises through existing worries such as AI, and that authorities should say quickly what is and is not known.910 Gary Marcus wrote that it is not known whether the cause was the malicious kind of cyberattack he warned about in January 2025 or the kind of LLM failures he warned about in May 2022.11

In September, the FAA rolled out SMART, short for Strategic Management of Airspace, Routing and Trajectories, an AI-driven system that carried an $875 million price tag for the Transportation Department.1213 Duffy asserted SMART was not involved, saying it "isn't plugged in," and its maker Air Space Intelligence said the stop was not a result of SMART or any of its products.1415

Duffy asked Congress for $50 billion to rebuild what he called an "incredibly old" air traffic control system, and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said it needs a complete overhaul.1617
