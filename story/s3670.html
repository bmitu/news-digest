FAA nationwide ground stop

Last scan Thu Nov 26, 05:11 UTC
4 sources · tracked since Nov 14 · 2 independent origins: Derek Thompson, TechCrunch

The FAA grounded all flights nationwide on Friday for about four hours, which disrupted thousands of flights, according to the Wall Street Journal as quoted by Zvi Mowshowitz.1 Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed an incredibly old air-traffic control system and telecom routers that he said were overloaded with data, and he urged Congress to fund upgrades he estimated at $50 billion.23

Sources count the stop differently: The Verge says it was only the second in 25 years, while TechCrunch and Derek Thompson call it the third of the century. inference456

Whether AI played a role is disputed: Kelly Vaughn said it was not due to AI hacking, and ASI said the stop was not a result of the SMART system.78 Trung Phan guessed there was a high probability the stop was related to an $875m AI tool called SMART that the administration had started rolling out the day before.9 Security software engineer Anusha Iyer told TechCrunch that preliminary findings point to a telecom modernization that overwhelmed legacy hardware, and she argued the cause was a rushed mix of old and new systems rather than rapid AI adoption.1011 Investor Dave DeWalt told TechCrunch he thinks the outages are probably AI-related, as AI surfaces vulnerabilities and companies rush to patch them.1213

Per TechCrunch, the director of National Intelligence said the outages don't seem related to state-sponsored terrorism, and President Trump called them "individual events."14

Jennifer Pahlka argues the FAA's repeated outages reflect outsourcing of technical expertise, and that more money has not made things better.1516 Michael Boyd, an aviation consultant whom Thompson quoted, relayed that officials traced the nationwide ground stop to a routine router upgrade at a site in Arkansas, which set off a chain reaction. He contended the whole system should be thrown out and replaced.1718

Microsoft President Brad Smith warned that more outages are likely in the coming months, saying the world's information infrastructure is being upgraded.19
