Ripple-65B Preview

Last scan Sun Nov 8, 08:53 UTC
2 sources · 339 points on Hacker News · tracked since Nov 6 · single origin (Ricursive)

Ricursive Intelligence released Ripple-65B Preview, a 65B dense model it says sets a new U.S. open-weight state of the art on agentic coding.123 Ricursive says the model was pretrained on 12 trillion tokens, supports up to 1M context, and is available on Hugging Face, Together AI, GMI Cloud, OpenRouter and Fireworks; a summary cited by Latent Space also lists an Apache 2.0 license.456 Latent Space reports that vLLM and SGLang shipped Day-0 support across several hardware platforms, and hosted access is live on Ollama Cloud, Databricks and SkyPilot.78

Ricursive reports a TerminalBench 2.1 score of 80.52, ahead of Reflection Beam 501B (80.1), Meta Muse Spark Open 816B (76.4), Thinking Machines Inkling 975B (63.8) and NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra 550B (56.4).91011 The lead over Beam is narrow, 80.5% versus 80.1%, and Latent Space notes that Beam activates 23B parameters per token.12 Ricursive says it reached this score with only 1.4M rollouts, roughly 70x fewer than Reflection Beam, the prior state of the art.13

Ricursive claims it developed and trained the model for only $32M, which it estimates is one-tenth the cost of the previous open-weight state of the art on agentic coding, "to the best of our knowledge."1415 It says the $32M is its full R&D cost, including ladder development, data mixing, evals, synthetic data generation and the final pretraining and posttraining runs.1615 The comparison rests on a footnote saying U.S. open-weight players like Reflection were reported to commit $150M per month for compute at SpaceX Colossus, and Latent Space says Prime Intellect, which ran the pretraining clusters, cites 16x pretraining compute efficiency.1718

On architecture, Ricursive chose a dense Transformer rather than a Mixture-of-Experts, with 80 layers and a d_model of 5120, and says it kept about 40% MFU using simple sharding, no custom kernels and no quantization.192021 Ricursive says its ladder experiments, starting from Olmo 3's ladder, improved code compute efficiency by up to 16x at 4B scale, while Latent Space's notes describe about a 3x gain from the new architecture and about 4x from the new dataset.2223
