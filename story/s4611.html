US interim rule on remote access to AI chips

Last scan Sat Nov 21, 06:54 UTC
9 sources · 6 independent origins: BIS, Fortune, Politico, Semafor, TechCrunch, The Wall Street Journal

The US Commerce Department issued an interim final rule on Monday, November 16, to close a "remote access" loophole that let Chinese firms use advanced US chips through data centers in third countries.12 The rule requires licenses for chips going to overseas data centers that provide "significant" computing power to companies headquartered in restricted countries such as China, and for advanced chip exports to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil.3456 The rule takes effect 90 days after Federal Register posting, exempts commercial recommender systems, and does not apply the five-country license requirement to chips destined for US-headquartered companies.789 TechCrunch reported that the regulation covers any data center or network able to provide more than 12 million in total processing performance, a threshold that works out to about the peak compute of roughly 750 Nvidia H100 or H200 chips.1011

Commerce stated that the Treasury Department and the National Security Council wrote the rule and directed its creation, which Chris McGuire described as "unprecedented."1213 It is unclear how aggressively Commerce will enforce the rule, since enforcement is up to the department that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has criticized under Howard Lutnick. inference1415

Jensen Huang stepped down as Nvidia's CEO early Thursday; Alice Huang had served as an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based IT and cloud firm that is now being investigated over the smuggling of Nvidia GPUs to China.161718 Megaspeed has denied that smuggling occurred, and US agencies have not confirmed that Alice Huang was a spy.1920

Nvidia shares fell nearly 5% on Monday as chip stocks sank after the rule was rolled out.2122 Jefferies forecast the rules could trim $44 billion to $62 billion from Nvidia's data center revenue next year, while acting CEO Colette Kress said they would not affect top-line revenue because of strong demand.2324

Singapore's digital minister Josephine Teo said her government is engaging with US counterparts on the scope of implementation and on trusted access to AI infrastructure.2526 Analysts told the South China Morning Post that the policy could "accelerate Chinese companies' shift to domestic AI chips."27
