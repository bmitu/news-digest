Anthropic gives METR employee-level access

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 14:03 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 17 · 1 new article this week · 2 independent origins: Financial Times, ZDNET

Anthropic has given external evaluators "employee-like access" to assess the risks of its models, and METR, a nonprofit, now has a team embedded in Anthropic's office with access to its hardware and internal systems, according to the Financial Times via Ars Technica.12 Anthropic has onboarded six METR employees, giving them company hardware, office space and access to internal tools "roughly on par with what our own risk assessment teams have access to."3 Anthropic is not paying METR, which does not accept donations from AI companies or their staff and is funded by individuals, philanthropic organisations and government bodies such as the UK's AI Security Institute.45

The evaluators will publish their analysis and Anthropic cannot veto publication, but it can require redaction of certain categories such as customer data, attorney-client privileged details or information about model architectures.6 Anthropic says it wants to be publicly accountable but cannot publicly share all information relevant to its safety work, arguing that sharing information publicly could benefit malicious actors or competitors and adding that its evaluators will operate similarly to bank supervision.78

Anthropic's announcement is described as the first step in implementing a proposal by Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei to embed external auditors in the leading labs, which the OpenAI and SpaceX bosses rallied behind in September, though OpenAI has yet to lay out plans to bring in evaluators.910 Anthropic says it is embedding evaluators voluntarily and believes every frontier AI company should do the same, while METR says it stands ready to work with all frontier AI developers.1112 Critics of the approach argue it could lead to conflicts of interest, pointing to issues in the accounting sector, and say greater government regulation is required.13

The announcement comes as Anthropic is under pressure to reassure investors that safety issues will not overshadow its initial public offering, expected this year at a more than $2tn valuation, and it devoted a large part of investor presentations this week to laying out its safety practices.1415
