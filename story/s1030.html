Anthropic gives METR employee-level access

Last scan Wed Oct 21, 20:11 UTC
4 sources · tracked since Oct 17 · 2 independent origins: Anthropic, Financial Times

Anthropic announced on a Thursday that it has started giving employee-like access to a team from METR, a nonprofit that evaluates frontier AI models, so that METR can independently assess its models, safety practices and risk management.12 Anthropic says it onboarded six METR employees with company hardware, office space, and access to internal tools and systems roughly on par with what its own risk assessment teams have.34 Anthropic says it is also working with SecureBio and Accenture, and is in early discussions with others including Hugging Face.5

METR will publish its analysis and Anthropic cannot veto publication, but Anthropic can require redactions in narrow, predefined categories such as customer data, attorney-client privileged information, or model architecture information.678 METR may publicly characterize the redactions and say whether it thinks they were justified, provided it does not reveal the redacted information.910 Anthropic compares the arrangement to bank supervision, where regulators place resident examiners inside the largest banks, and argues that it cannot share all safety-relevant information publicly.111213

Anthropic says it embeds evaluators voluntarily and believes every frontier AI company should do the same, while METR says it stands ready to work with all frontier AI developers.1415

The Financial Times, via Ars Technica, reports that Anthropic is not paying METR, which does not accept donations from AI companies or their staff.16 The Financial Times reports that critics have argued such arrangements could create conflicts of interest, pointing to issues in the accounting sector, and that greater government regulation is required.17 The Financial Times also says Anthropic is under pressure to reassure investors about safety ahead of an expected IPO, and that OpenAI has yet to lay out plans to bring in evaluators.1819
