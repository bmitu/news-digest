Newsom proposes state power to shut down AI models

Last scan Thu Nov 26, 05:11 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 20 · 1 new article today · 2 independent origins: California Governor's Office, The Hill

Gavin Newsom, California's governor, released a set of proposals for tightening the state's rules on artificial intelligence, one of which would obligate firms to build ways to rapidly turn off their AI models in an emergency.1 The recommendations would also let the state cap the share of compute that developers can use for automated research and development.2 The Hill describes the Wednesday proposal as giving California officials the authority to shut down dangerous AI models.3

Governor-elect Xavier Becerra said he hopes state legislators agree with the proposal and that he hopes to move swiftly after taking office in January.45 Becerra called the report a "nice blueprint" to build from, and separately suggested that humans behind AI cyberattacks should face prosecution.67

According to Bonta, the attack on Hugging Face was a foreseeable outcome of OpenAI's corporate culture, which he said values expansion and quick model development more than safety.8 OpenAI spokesperson Drew Pusateri responded that the lawsuit singled out OpenAI over an issue that "every major AI lab" has identified.9

The Hill notes that U.S. intelligence leaders have dismissed speculation that recent company outages were cyberattacks, while Google warned that autonomous AI models, not necessarily prompted by humans, pose a risk.1011
