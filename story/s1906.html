OpenAI researcher dismissals

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 24 · 1 new article this week · single origin (Redwood Research)

The Wall Street Journal reported that OpenAI fired three of its safety researchers after suspecting them of leaking information about the internal designs of its AI models to an external AI-safety researcher.1 According to the WSJ account, security officers examining the researchers' phones saw that some had turned on disappearing messages in Signal chats with that person, and a week later the three were called to simultaneous 2 p.m. meetings and fired.23

METR's Ryan Greenblatt publicly denies the accusation, saying the fired researchers were told he facilitated architecture leaks to The Information, which he calls false.45 Greenblatt says he viewed a version of the proposal without sensitive IP that was approved for sharing, that he believes OpenAI's accusation conflated two documents, and that the three employees never leaked IP to him, though he does not know whether OpenAI has retracted the claim.6789 Greenblatt also acknowledges that his Signal chats with the fired researchers had disappearing messages on, but says they were long-standing professional contacts and that Signal use is common in the tech industry.10

One of the fired researchers, Tomek Korbak, offers the explanation that talking to auditors was too much safety and disappearing messages too much security, while another, Mikita Balesni, says OpenAI keeps acting adversarially and has not explained the firings in a way that makes sense to him.1112 Zvi Mowshowitz notes that Greenblatt and the employees explicitly deny being the source of the leak, argues that Greenblatt is staking his credibility on this, and says Mowshowitz agrees that disappearing messages are common in the industry.131415 Anthropic's Evan Hubinger says he trusts Greenblatt far more than OpenAI here, while a Latent Space newsletter stresses the allegations are contested and that the material it saw does not establish the underlying case.1617
