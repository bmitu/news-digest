OpenAI Linux kernel vulnerability patching

Last scan Sun Nov 8, 08:53 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Nov 6 · 2 independent origins: OpenAI, TechCrunch

OpenAI says its security researchers built an autonomous pipeline that, in a single mid-October 2026 run with an upcoming cyber model, produced 948 working local privilege escalation (LPE) exploits against the Linux kernel, with patches developed for maintainer review.123 OpenAI says the pipeline analyzed 2,308 candidate findings, dynamically reproduced 1,724 vulnerabilities, and generated proofs of concept for 291 information leak vulnerabilities.45 OpenAI also acknowledged a validation problem: in an early run, 20 of 200 reported successful exploits had gained root through a different 0-day than the one assigned, so it added an independent check.67

OpenAI puts the run's cost at $1,213,430 in API credits, or $1,279 per LPE exploit, compared with 166 LPEs found by GPT-5.6 Sol in late July 2026 for $513,690.89 OpenAI compares the cost with historical Pwn2Own and TyphoonPWN bounties of $20,000 and $70,000, calling the exploits' hypothetical value $18.9M–$66.3M, roughly 16x to 55x the pipeline's cost.10

When the autonomous scan and manual analysis finished, OpenAI says 61 LPEs had been patched upstream, 43 had staging patches, and 299 were being reported and discussed publicly, and it says it is working with Patch the Planet partners on further reports and patches.111213 OpenAI researcher Eric Wallace said researchers typically find about 20 Linux LPEs a year, and that OpenAI is putting a significant amount of frontier compute toward pro bono open-source patching.1415

Reddit commenters were skeptical and alarmed, with one noting that 948 privilege-escalation bugs is an unusually large number and another asking whether OpenAI is actually submitting pull requests; the Reddit post itself provides no patch set, PR links, CVE list, benchmark methodology, or independent validation.1617 TechCrunch frames the effort as possibly insufficient, noting the $1.2 million Linux bug hunt was significantly less than the approximate $6 million token spend on a fluid dynamics problem, and saying more urgency may be required; it also reports that Max Justicz said OpenAI appears to have recently "nerfed" Daybreak Blue to make it less useful at finding vulnerabilities.181920
