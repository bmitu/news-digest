METR report on Anthropic AI R&D acceleration

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 09:22 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 6 · single origin (MIT Technology Review)

METR, an AI auditor, produced a report with heavy redactions suggesting that AI could be making Anthropic's AI progress go twice as fast, and Platformer characterizes its conclusion as being that the company's speed of AI research and development had approximately doubled.12 Eli Lifland sums up METR's figure as close to 2x. He says METR wouldn't be shocked by a value anywhere between 1.3x and 3x, and that it speculated roughly 80% of today's speedup stems from AI.3 Lifland says his median for a Superhuman AI Researcher moved from about 2030 to 2027, and Daniel Kokotajlo says that, if METR is right, the result implies an AI-driven acceleration 5 months ahead of the AI 2027 scenario.45

Paul Christiano says the data are consistent with AI progress having roughly doubled over the last 6-12 months, and he sees a reasonable chance of very rapid recursive-self-improvement-driven progress in that window.67 Evidence quality is a point of complaint, though: Christiano says most of what the public has is METR and Anthropic asserting their conclusions, since the full report was redacted.89

Dwarkesh Patel notes the measured doubling concerns verifiable tasks and asks whether progress on non-verifiable tasks has also doubled.1011 Zvi Mowshowitz calls it the "null METR report" and puts the probability of full AI R&D automation in 2027 under 50%.1213

In a separate METR update, models fell short on judgment-heavy tasks: none of the AI-designed evaluations was judged useful by METR employees, though newer models like Opus 5.5 reached "borderline above the bar".1415

The report landed as Anthropic went public, and critics seized on it: Platformer says the doubling would indicate Anthropic passed its own "Automated R&D" threshold, and the Future of Life Institute cited it in a statement condemning Anthropic's pursuit of recursive self-improvement. inference1617
