METR report on Anthropic AI R&D acceleration

Last scan Sat Nov 7, 03:59 UTC
4 sources · 2 independent origins: METR, Transformer

METR published a redacted report on its assessment of whether AI is accelerating AI R&D at Anthropic, based on a high-access evaluation run from August 12 to October 29, 2026.12 The headline metric is that AECI scores have grown 1.9x faster since October 2025, with a 95% interval of 1.5–2.8x.34 However, METR cautions that this figure could reflect overfitting to the benchmark or an artifact of the methodology, and that it lacks confidence AECI can reliably estimate recent acceleration.56

METR says most of its staff on the project believe Anthropic is likely improving its models' capabilities on well-specified tasks at around twice the rate of March 2024 to October 2025, though it would not be surprised by 1.3x or 3x.78 METR believes a majority of the acceleration is likely now driven by AI being used in AI R&D, but says AI cannot yet make highly significant, fully autonomous contributions to the most important parts of model training.910 Anthropic staff self-reports in Slack polls reached 12x uplift in October 2026, which METR considers only very loose evidence.1112

Anthropic redacted what METR calls a majority of the most informative evidence, citing commercial sensitivity and the risk of giving others a roadmap, so METR says the public must rely on its judgment more than ideal.1314 METR takes no position on whether the redactions were justified overall, saying it is not well positioned to weigh a company's business interest against the public interest.1516 Anthropic co-founder Sam McCandlish says METR had access to scores for unreleased models, internal projections and researcher interviews.17

Ryan Greenblatt calls the report a big update toward ASI within 1–2 years and puts the software R&D multiplier at about 3.8x.1819 A METR colleague writing on planned-obsolescence.org, speaking personally, argues Anthropic's RSP "dramatic acceleration" threshold has plausibly already been crossed.2021 Transformer reports that AI safety researchers responded to the METR report with outrage at Anthropic for its redactions and some of the most visceral panic and alarm it has seen in some time, and ex-OpenAI preparedness team member Dane Sherburn wrote that he felt sick after reading it.2223
