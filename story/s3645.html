Anthropic IPO roadshow

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
4 sources · 4 independent origins: Anthropic, Bloomberg, Simon Willison, The Wall Street Journal

Anthropic said in an updated IPO filing on Tuesday that it will offer 825 million Class A shares at an estimated $70 to $78 each, which it expects to raise roughly $57.75 billion to $64.35 billion.12 Anthropic has also agreed to a private placement in which companies such as Google, Nvidia and Microsoft will buy $26 billion worth of Class N shares, which carry no vote, priced the same as the IPO. With that sale, the company's expected net proceeds come to between $84.6 billion and $93.7 billion.34 At the top of that range Anthropic would be worth about $1.404 trillion fully diluted, up from $965 billion in May but below the $2 trillion it was reportedly aiming for earlier. inference567

Trading is expected to begin on the Nasdaq around November 19 under the ticker "ANT", per CNBC as relayed by TechCrunch, while Semafor says pricing is set for November 18. inference89 The filing puts run-rate revenue at $81 billion by the end of October, third-quarter revenue at $17 billion, and the nine-month net loss at $104 billion.101112 Simon Willison says the roadshow video, released as the roadshow began, included co-founder Tom Brown describing Anthropic training and serving Claude across four major chip families.1314

Seven co-founders, including Dario Amodei, each plan to sell nearly 3.4 million shares, worth about $250 million apiece at the $74 midpoint.1516

Semafor reports the IPO is reportedly six times oversubscribed, and says Goldman Sachs, one of the lead banks, expects Anthropic's revenue to exceed $600 billion by 2030.1718 Anthropic pressed ahead despite a tech selloff, and the Wall Street Journal reported that some institutional investors wanted a valuation below the $1.5 trillion target discussed the week before.1920 Michelle Bowman, a governor at the Federal Reserve, cautioned that stock prices could face a "large correction" should AI payoffs disappoint, pointing to $800 billion of debt tied to AI as well as circular financing arrangements.2122 Tore Opsahl of Nomura said any wobbles around the IPO might mean a selloff of the AI theme.23
