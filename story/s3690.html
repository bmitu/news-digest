Anthropic gas turbine purchase

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 09:22 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 14 · single origin (Better Data Center Project)

Anthropic is spending $3.4 billion on natural gas turbines, which TechCrunch says is a notable step for a company that has generally avoided buying physical infrastructure.1 According to a Financial Times report cited by TechCrunch, Anthropic is taking over Caterpillar generators that equipment supplier OnePWR had originally ordered, with capacity of up to 1.7 gigawatts.23 Exponential View describes it as the largest known power-equipment purchase by an AI lab and says the order was two years old.4 MIT Technology Review likewise says Anthropic has reportedly agreed to the purchase, described as the largest power generation purchase by an AI company.5

Sources differ in wording on whether the deal is paid or only agreed: Exponential View says Anthropic "paid", while MIT Technology Review says it "reportedly agreed to pay". inference67

TechCrunch reports, citing the Financial Times, that OnePWR ordered the turbines a couple of years ago but could not find financing to complete the purchase, though it says it is not clear why OnePWR was unable to secure financing.89 TechCrunch frames the purchase as looking like a last resort, since turbine backlogs stretch into the early 2030s and grid connection queues can be just as long.1011 According to TechCrunch, Anthropic probably preferred to hold onto the power-generation capacity itself instead of letting a rival grab it. The outlet adds that an analyst forecasts prices in certain markets might rise to over three times their current level before 2030 ends.1213

MIT Technology Review's writer uses the deal to illustrate that most new AI data centers elsewhere in the US bring their own gas-turbine power, an approach that she argues still affects surrounding communities and fuels local backlash. inference1415 The Better Data Center Project estimates that about 87% of Anthropic's power will come from gas and 11% from coal in 2028, based on on-site gas and the grid plants likely to serve its added load.16 According to that same group, Anthropic has not made any firm, direct pledges to buy renewable power in the U.S., whereas it has bought fossil-fuel power equipment itself, among it the purchase of generators totaling 1.7 gigawatts.1718 Joshi's climate report, cited by the Blood in the Machine newsletter, estimates that the $3.4bn purchase of 1.7 gigawatts' worth of generators by Anthropic would release roughly 6 MTCO2-e annually, comparable to putting 1.8 million cars powered by fossil fuels on the road.19 The report adds that the deal's value is over triple what xAI spent on its turbines in Memphis, which have drawn notoriety for pollution.20
