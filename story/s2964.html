Anthropic FlashProve provable inference

Last scan Sun Nov 8, 08:53 UTC
4 sources · 239 points on Hacker News · tracked since Nov 6 · single origin (Anthropic)

Anthropic released FlashProve, an open-source prototype for "provable inference" that lets a user check, via a zero-knowledge succinct argument, that a response came from a specific model without revealing the model's weights.123 TechCrunch reported that the system could be used to stop an AI company secretly swapping in a cheaper model to cut costs, or a more expensive one to inflate its performance results.4 Anthropic argues the tool could detect a compromised inference server or a more advanced model posing as a weaker authorized one, since an attacker could not produce valid proofs for the expected weights.56

Anthropic says that on Llama 4 Maverick, a 400B-parameter mixture-of-experts model in 4-bit quantization, a single H100 GPU generates proofs at around 110 tokens per second, with proofs of about 19 MB per 1,024-token request.7 Anthropic puts the cost at roughly 70-95 times the GPU time of running the model on vLLM, and describes this as much less overhead than previously expected.89 Anthropic compares this with earlier work: zkLLM proved a 13B model at about 2.5 tokens/second in 2024, and VerInf reportedly took 14.3 hours for 1,000 tokens on Llama 4 Maverick with a 93.6 GB proof.1011

The work is described as intermediate: TechCrunch reports there are no plans to deploy it at Anthropic or any other lab, and the researchers say they hope others will take it and improve it.1213 Zvi Mowshowitz's newsletter noted the release only briefly, saying Anthropic reports progress on proving that a response came from a given model, and Latent Space's roundup said Anthropic claims substantially better efficiency than prior open-source methods and listed hijack resistance and letting evaluators confirm the tested model is the one served as stated uses.141516
