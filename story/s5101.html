Dutch consumer groups sue Meta over smart glasses

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 09:22 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 20 · 1 new article today · single origin (The Hill)

Dutch consumer rights groups filed a lawsuit against Meta in October, alleging that its smart glasses violate privacy laws, and a preliminary hearing is scheduled for Nov. 26.1 Meta has said in the past that when its camera glasses are taking pictures or recording video, an indicator light flashes and users are unable to disable it.2 That same month, Hans Anders, a Dutch optical chain, halted its sales of Meta's Ray-Ban smart glasses across the Netherlands and Belgium, citing concerns about privacy.3

Separately, the Netherlands' Data Protection Authority examined six AI smart glasses models and advised consumers on Thursday not to buy the technology, citing risks to users and bystanders.4 The regulator found that some glasses could record people without their knowledge or consent, and that several models had vulnerabilities letting a nearby person connect without permission, access recordings or control the cameras.56 It also questioned the reliability of the AI advice: in a simulated medical emergency, one pair said an ambulance was on its way although none had been called.7 The Dutch regulator did not name the manufacturers of the six models, and although it can fine companies, it cannot independently impose a total ban on smart glasses.89

In Germany, the consumer protection association VZBV is reportedly suing Meta and Ray-Ban's manufacturers over glasses that can discreetly record audio and video, alleging violations of the EU's GDPR.1011
