Harvard study on data center emissions

Last scan Sun Nov 22, 14:05 UTC
4 sources · tracked since Nov 20 · 4 independent origins: Harvard, Heatmap, Nature, The Verge

A Harvard-led study in Nature Sustainability estimates that hyperscale data centers in the US emitted 25.7 million metric tons of CO2 while using about 82 terawatt-hours of electricity between May 2024 and April 2025.1 That electricity is about 1.8% of US consumption, and the emissions are comparable to Denmark's annual carbon footprint, according to the paper as reported by The Verge.23 The Harvard researchers say the emissions figure is two to three times the greenhouse gas emissions found by a previous peer-reviewed estimate from 2018.4 The authors identified 403 facilities from satellite imagery and estimated each one's power use from its electrical capacity, since operators do not disclose consumption.56

Roughly 54 percent of the electricity came from fossil fuels, which Inside Climate News breaks down as 38 percent natural gas and 15 percent coal.7 Location matters: the study found Wyoming's coal-heavy grid produced roughly seven times more carbon per kilowatt-hour than hydropower-rich Oregon, and most use was concentrated in Iowa, Ohio, Oregon and Virginia.89 Inside Climate News adds that on-site gas generation is growing, with BloombergNEF counting about 100 gigawatts planned and an Earthjustice-linked analysis putting it at 177 gigawatts.1011

Guidi argues the impacts are not inevitable, saying the same computing infrastructure can have very different consequences depending on the electricity system behind it.12 MIT's John Sterman, who was not involved in the study, says the emissions exceed those of about 113 nations and that policymakers should require operators to bring new clean energy online.1314

The researchers also released a tool, Never Just a Building, that scores proposed data center sites on environmental and public health impact, and the study has become a talking point in the Senate fight over the BAAJA permitting bill, where Senator Edward Markey and other opponents say the bill would accelerate climate pollution. inference1516
