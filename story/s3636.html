Nvidia board investigation of Huang

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
8 sources · 494 points on Hacker News · 4 independent origins: Financial Times, Nvidia, TechCrunch, The New York Times

Nvidia's board announced on Sunday that it was opening an independent investigation after reporting that CEO Jensen Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort, to be overseen by a committee of independent directors with outside counsel.123 Huang acknowledged in a message circulated internally on Sunday that he had cheated on his wife. According to reports, the relationship was with Alice Huang, an executive at Megaspeed, who American officials suspect is a Chinese spy passing information to Beijing.4567 According to the board, nothing suggests that national security or Nvidia's trade secrets were put at risk, and it still trusts Huang, who pledged to cooperate in full.8910

Nvidia's shares fell after the news: The Hill reported a Monday opening drop of 7.3 percent, and TechCrunch later reported a combined loss of about 9.5% over Monday and Tuesday.1112 The board later retained former acting US attorney Joon H. Kim to conduct the review, according to The Hill, while TechCrunch reported that it hired the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz.1314

Sources differ on how the case has been described: Nvidia says Huang continues as CEO, but Larry Kudlow called on him to step down, while Trump called him a "very honorable man." inference151617 Zvi Mowshowitz argues Huang must resign and that the board is announcing its conclusion in advance, whereas Bill Ackman urged supporting him and Shaun Maguire called him irreplaceable for America's AI leadership. inference18192021 The Chinese Embassy dismissed the allegations as "groundless smears and slanders," and no one has been charged.22

Yale's Jeffrey Sonnenfeld put the odds that Huang keeps his job at "about a 50-50," and governance professor Ann Lipton said no real precedent exists for such a case.2324 Todd Henderson said that not telling the board that you've been sleeping with a Chinese spy "seems to me to be a fireable offense," and Lipton expects shareholder suits; TechCrunch noted that a proposed class action already alleges Nvidia and Huang misled investors.2526 Semafor and others note Huang lacks dual-class stock and a clear successor, and that House Republican John Moolenaar wants him to address national security concerns in Congress.27282930
