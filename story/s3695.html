Deletion Tuning

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
2 sources · single origin (Deletion Tuning authors)

A paper called "The Surprising Effectiveness of Deleting Weights in LLM Post-Training" introduces Deletion Tuning, a method that trains a binary mask applied to the gate projection weights of transformers and sets to zero under 0.05% of the model's parameters.1 The method reportedly matches full fine-tuning across reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, supervised fine-tuning, and on-policy distillation.2 The summaries describe the claimed advantages as masks hundreds to thousands of times smaller than full fine-tuning checkpoints, and less catastrophic forgetting.3

Under reinforcement learning, accuracy on earlier tasks reportedly remains close to that of the base model, whereas full fine-tuning, LoRA and random sparse fine-tuning each drop by as many as 5.4 points.4 The authors also say, using stable rank, spectrum drift and principal-weight overlap, that Deletion Tuning's update geometry is closest to full fine-tuning among the LoRA and random sparse baselines; code and masks are on GitHub and Hugging Face.5

Some commenters offered a technical reading: the mask dampens weights that push inference down less-than-ideal routes, so that superior latent pathways the base model already contains can take over.67 A recurring view among commenters is that deletion may not create new capabilities but unmask ones learned in pretraining, which they take as a sign pretraining may be highly redundant.8910

One commenter summarized the tradeoff as making the model "dumber on everything we don't care about" to improve it on the target task, and another suggested the impact may be larger for post-training workflows than for inference-time efficiency.1112 One commenter said the approach could be "orders of magnitude more economical than LoRA fine-tuning" and might be easy to add to tools such as Heretic that already manipulate weights.1314
