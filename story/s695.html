Nolla Health announces AI acne prescription service

Last scan Sat Oct 10, 12:51 UTC
2 sources · 140 points on Hacker News · tracked since Oct 6 · 2 new articles this week · single origin (Nolla Health)

Nolla Health announced on Monday that people in Utah can use its app to scan their faces and have an AI system assess acne severity and autonomously write a prescription, as reported earlier by Bloomberg.1 Nolla Health says it is the first in the country to issue initial prescriptions rather than renewals, while other healthcare companies are also experimenting with AI-generated prescriptions in Utah, which began allowing AI to renew certain medications earlier this year.23 The service costs $4.99 a month and applies only to Utah residents aged 18 or older with mild-to-moderate acne.45

According to Bloomberg, as relayed by TechSpot, the AI generates a numerical acne severity score and combines it with the user's medical history to recommend a medication from eight approved treatments.67 Nolla Health says it will direct users to a physician if the AI cannot "confidently select a treatment."8

The pilot has gradually loosening physician oversight, with the first 100 prescriptions reviewed by doctors before being issued.910 TechSpot says the next 400 patients' prescriptions would be reviewed retrospectively by a physician weekly, followed by review of at least 10% of prescriptions monthly.1112 The sources differ in detail: The Verge says two physicians approve each of the first 100 prescriptions and that Nolla Health describes monthly sample review of at least 10% plus every escalation or side-effect case, while TechSpot describes the final phase only as a 10% monthly review. inference131412

Nolla Health says the pilot is designed to complement, not replace, physicians by handling routine, low-acuity cases so dermatologists can focus on complex, high-risk patients.1516
