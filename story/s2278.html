Google sleep continual learning framework

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
2 sources · 186 points on Hacker News · single origin (Google Research)

Google Research introduced a "sleep" framework for continual learning in LLMs, which alternates between an active (wake) phase and an offline sleep phase of memory consolidation.12 Google says it addresses the problem that deployed LLMs stay static and that continual parameter updates often cause catastrophic forgetting, while relying only on in-context learning loses knowledge once the session ends.34

During sleep, the model unlocks pre-allocated Mixture-of-Experts memory blocks and updates its weights using self-generated practice examples, with the authors stressing that the framework operates within a fixed model architecture and human-defined training rules.56 The process has two stages: memory consolidation, which distills short-term context into expanded long-term low-rank expert parameters and then resets temporary ones, and dreaming, where randomly activated experts generate synthetic samples that are filtered by gradient-based selection and refined with reinforcement learning.789

Google reports that its sleep method demonstrated near-perfect scaling up to 10M tokens on the BABILong benchmark, outperforming strong baselines like GPT-4 and Titans.10 On continual translation in Manchu and Kalamang it reportedly showed near-zero performance drop, whereas ICL, Cartridges and SFT suffered severe forgetting.11 Google also reports its sleep method achieved 48.9% on single-task and 46.2% on CPT factual knowledge evaluations, and the sleep-enhanced base model achieved 79.2%, 69.0% and 46.1% on AIME 24, AIME 25 and HMMT 25 respectively.1213

The research was conducted by Ali Behrouz, Farnoosh Hashemi, Adel Javanmard and Vahab Mirrokni, and the authors say the paradigm provides a path toward autonomous, continually learning AI systems.1415 The Latent Space AINews newsletter briefly noted the framework under continual learning, alongside a separate item saying plain RAG over raw history beats many dedicated memory systems; no independent evaluation was reported. inference1617
