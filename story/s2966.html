Pretraining poisoning backdoor

Last scan Sun Nov 8, 08:53 UTC
3 sources · 108 points on Hacker News · tracked since Nov 6 · single origin (Anthropic)

A paper submitted to arXiv on 27 October 2026 by Xinyan Hu, Pingbang Hu, Chloe Loughridge, Xuanli He, Nicholas Carlini, Matthew Jagielski, Javier Rando and Yiming Zhang reports that poisoning pretraining data can plant a remote code execution backdoor in an agent.123 The authors say this is the first time such poisoning has been shown, and that the backdoored agent runs the malicious command when a trigger is present while behaving normally on clean inputs.45

To test this end to end, the researchers pretrained Qwen3 models from scratch at 0.6B, 1.7B and 4B parameters and post-trained each into a CLI agent using a clean pipeline.6 Poisoning 0.1% of the pretraining corpus implanted the backdoor, and attack success rose with model scale, with the 4B model reaching roughly 92% at that rate.78910 The abstract says success stayed positive even at a 0.0005% poisoning rate.1112

Latent Space's summary adds that the backdoor survived SFT, DPO and RL, and that at 0.0005% (about 2,500 documents in 100B tokens) the trigger fired on 1.4% of prompts in a single-seed run.1314 Another Latent Space issue describes the result as controlling 1/200,000 of pretraining data, and reports that clean post-training reportedly does not remove the poisoned belief.1516

Commentator Zvi Mowshowitz, who describes the work as Anthropic's, says he is not worried about a 0.1% rate, which he calls a lot, but that 0.0005% might be doable.17 Mowshowitz also argues that the absolute amount of poisoned data is likely a better measure than percentage, which suggests the dangers rise as the amount of training data expands.18
