FAA SMART system

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
2 sources · single origin (FAA)

The FAA announced on November 10 that it will extend its AI-based SMART system to the airspace of all 22 centers across the country, after what it called a successful initial rollout in the National Capital Region in September.12 The Dallas Morning News reported that coverage has already grown to centers in Atlanta, Jacksonville and Miami, and that the FAA gave an example of a shorter route for a cargo flight into Dallas-Fort Worth but did not identify the flight or say how SMART helped passenger traffic.34

According to the FAA, SMART is a cloud-hosted platform that draws on weather, flight routes, traffic flow, and the number of controllers on staff to forecast how air traffic will move. It is built to recommend schedules, pinpoint the most efficient routes for flights, and work out the ideal sequence in which planes should land.56 The tool was also mentioned in a Latent Space newsletter, which said the FAA characterizes it as a planning aid that pulls weather, traffic, staffing, and flight routes together in a single view so congestion can be anticipated, and that it rolled out in September.78

The FAA says the $875 million system has helped reroute 180 flights since the September 21 Washington, DC deployment, and claims an average saving of 10 minutes and almost 80 gallons of fuel per flight.910 According to the FAA, a human team of specialists in DC validates SMART's recommendations, local controllers may accept or decline them, and the system cannot replace controllers or take over an aircraft.111213

Ed Bastian, the CEO of Delta Air Lines, and Robert Isom, the CEO of American Airlines, both voiced support in the FAA's earlier announcement.1415 The National Air Traffic Controllers Association, whose membership exceeds 15,000, told Flying magazine that it had no consultation or role in how SMART was designed, tested, or put into use, and therefore cannot comment on how it affects the workforce.16
