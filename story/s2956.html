Binance hack

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Nov 6 · 3 new articles this week · single origin (The Verge)

CBS News reported on Oct. 29 that Binance suffered a historic cyberattack in which hackers, apparently using AI, took between $6.3 billion and $6.43 billion in digital assets.1 The theft is widely cited as the largest cryptocurrency theft ever and possibly the largest single heist of any kind.2 An Inside Higher Ed article, in a list of lessons it attributes to GPT-6 Astra Light, adds that much of the Binance theft reportedly occurred within about 20 minutes.3

The attack is attributed to TraderTraitor hackers from North Korea, who may have used a number of AI agents.4 Accounts differ on how large a role AI played: The Atlantic says attackers went after Binance in part by hijacking its internal AI tools, while a speaker in The Verge says there was probably some AI component but the attack was mostly human intelligence and skilled social engineering. inference56 On the entry point, The Atlantic says the hack reportedly began with an employee clicking a malicious advertisement, whereas Inside Higher Ed says reporting points to a compromised laptop with access to transaction-approval infrastructure. inference78

The Verge's speaker says North Korea has a whole team dedicated to stealing Bitcoin and argues that companies must accept the risk of red-teaming themselves to get ahead of attackers.910 Separately, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman says he no longer believes all bugs will be patched and that defenders will need to switch to a new kind of defense shield.11
