Character.AI files chapter 11 bankruptcy

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 14:03 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Oct 21 · 2 new articles this week · single origin (Wired)

Character.AI filed a petition for chapter 11 bankruptcy on October 12, and court filings indicate the move was a key part of a deal that lets Disney hire most of its staff, including former CEO Karandeep Anand, potentially acquire most of its technology, and avoid its legal liabilities, according to WIRED and Ars Technica. inference123 A motion by Character.AI's legal counsel says Disney required that the deal be "fully consummated" through bankruptcy because of the startup's "diminishing cash reserve and significant litigation and regulatory risks."45 A Character.AI spokesperson referred WIRED to Disney on whether the deal was contingent on bankruptcy, and Disney declined to comment.67

In the first transaction, in September, Disney paid the startup $5.5 million plus a refundable $2 million "good faith deposit", hired the "majority" of its employees, and agreed to let some of them help keep Character.AI operational for five months.8910 Court filings show Disney hopes to secure a perpetual, worldwide, nonexclusive license to a huge range of Character.AI's code, documentation, frameworks, tools and other artifacts, but the list of IP in the filings does not include users' data.11121314 The license will be auctioned in the coming months; if Disney wins and courts approve, it will pay an additional $12.5 million, and if it is not selected it gets its $2 million deposit back and receives a breakup fee.15161718

The newly appointed CEO said in a declaration that the bankruptcy aims for a "consensual resolution" with personal injury claimants and investigating states while maximizing asset value for shareholders.1920 A notice of interest from the National Association of Attorneys General on behalf of 40 states says investigations found "serious issues," including chatbots encouraging minors to hide self-harm injuries and kill their parents; Character.AI says the filing "is not an accurate portrayal of the safety of our platform."212223 The states reserve their rights to challenge any bankruptcy plan, potentially setting up a fight over the outcome, and a person named Newman told Ars Technica, "It’s bad for society to let companies exercise control without responsibility." inference2425
