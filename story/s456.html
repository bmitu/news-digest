OpenAI disrupts model-distillation campaign

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 03:34 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 1 · single origin (OpenAI)

OpenAI says it recently identified and disrupted a coordinated campaign designed to extract protected reasoning from its models, with the earliest observed activity in the first week of July.12 OpenAI describes the activity as consistent with adversarial distillation, the systematic and unauthorized use of one model's outputs or reasoning to help train, reproduce, or improve another model.3 OpenAI says the operators did not break its encryption, compromise a database, or access stored user conversations, but manipulated model interactions so that protected reasoning appeared in forms visible to the requester, in violation of its terms of service.456

OpenAI says activity began on July 1 at low volume, with spikes on July 24 and 25 of 16,000 requests from over 4,000 users using a relevant extraction pattern.78 OpenAI says it found related prompt-pattern activity across a cluster of more than 15,000 users, which it fully disrupted by July 28.910

OpenAI says it is unclear whether all operators came from a single actor, but it attributes a core cluster of the activity to individuals associated with Moonshot AI, the developer of Kimi.1112 OpenAI says it responded by banning or restricting fraudulent accounts, strengthening signup and infrastructure controls, expanding monitoring for related networks, and sharing information with industry partners through the Frontier Model Forum, and that additional mitigation and investigation work is continuing.131415

OpenAI argues that adversarial distillation poses safety and national security risks because extracted reasoning could be used to train another model without preserving the original safeguards.1617 Mowshowitz says attackers appear to have had a period earlier this year with methods to extract hidden reasoning, that the vulnerability is now patched, and that the race between attackers and targeted labs will continue to ramp up.1819

Related questions

How did the operators actually get hidden reasoning out of the model without breaking encryption or accessing stored conversations?

OpenAI says the operators did not break its encryption, compromise a database, or reach stored user conversations.1 Instead, OpenAI says, they manipulated model interactions so the protected reasoning was reproduced in forms the requester could see, in a coordinated, large-scale way that violated its terms of service.23 One method OpenAI describes was copying encrypted reasoning from one conversation and asking a model in another conversation to decrypt and transcribe the hidden reasoning content.4 OpenAI says it closed a pathway that let someone holding another user's encrypted reasoning replay it and recover its contents, and added checks to hold streamed output that might expose reasoning.56 Independent security researchers separately brought related cross-model and conversation-compaction vulnerabilities to OpenAI's attention through responsible disclosure, and OpenAI says it confirmed the attack paths they identified were real.78

Are other labs and partner-hosted deployments exposed to the same attack, and what is still unfinished in the defenses?

OpenAI says the manipulation it disrupted is not a vulnerability unique to its own models, and it has shared information with industry partners through the Frontier Model Forum.1 OpenAI also says similar techniques may affect other advanced AI systems, and that systems supporting portable or replayable reasoning artifacts may face related risks.23 On third-party hosts, external researchers reportedly found their attacks kept working on Astra until this week, and patches were hard to propagate across product versions and third-party hosts.4 Nathan Lambert argues the vulnerability is the API provider's responsibility.5 OpenAI says the work is not finished: partner-hosted deployments need the same protections as its own services, and tool-output attacks need protections that look beyond ordinary visible text.6 It says it is still improving tool defenses, classifier coverage and model refusals, and spreading controls across cloud partners, while further mitigation and investigation continue.78 Which other labs were actually hit, and how many partner-hosted deployments remain unprotected, was not reported.
