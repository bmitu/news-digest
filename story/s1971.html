Kimi cyber training-data removal

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 24 · 1 new article this week · single origin (Moonshot AI)

Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro was launched with a distinctive safety step: Moonshot removed cyber-related data from mid-training and reinforcement learning, rather than relying solely on inference-time refusals.1 Posts quoting the report say an internal cyber score fell from 90.5% to 81%, a 9.5-percentage-point reduction, while coding and other capabilities continued improving.2 Moonshot's stated counterfactual was that downstream cyber capability would otherwise increase substantially.3

Despite the removal, the section heading describes the model as having the strongest public API result on cybersecurity, and Artificial Analysis reported that K3.1 Pro improved 23 index points over K3's 41%, driven especially by CyberGym rising from 58% to 95%. inference45 Artificial Analysis also observed no safety refusals on its tasks.6

Moonshot describes K3.1 Pro as a multimodal mixture-of-experts model with 2.8 trillion total parameters, 104 billion active parameters and a 1-million-token context window.7 Artificial Analysis placed K3.1 Pro fourth on its Intelligence Index with a score of 55, behind Opus 5.5 at 58 and Sonnet 5.5 and Muse Wonder Full at 56.8

Early reactions to the cyber removal called the model "nerfed" and compared its internal cyber score unfavorably with GPT-5.6 Sol, while other posts speculated that an unrestricted internal version might match stronger proprietary systems; these were commentary, not independent findings.910 One commenter questioned Moonshot's extensive use of the Claude Code harness, which is a legitimate reproducibility question rather than evidence the results are invalid.1112
