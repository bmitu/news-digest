Exponential Time Hypothesis refutation claims

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
4 sources · 3 independent origins: Anthropic, OpenAI, TechCrunch

OpenAI says its system proved that the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH), a longstanding conjecture about the computational complexity of Boolean formula satisfiability, is false, a result it says was then verified in Lean.12 OpenAI says its AI found the natural language disproof on October 16th, 2026 and formalized it in Lean the next day, and that it released the writeup and Lean proof on its public GitHub repository.34 Anthropic also posted its own refutation, which it says was found purely via Claude with no human input and dated to October 16.567

The OpenAI claim is reported as a 3-SAT algorithm running in 2^{O(n/√log n)} time, and Zvi Mowshowitz says both the OpenAI and Anthropic disproofs are at roughly exp(O(n/√log n)). inference89 Mowshowitz relays that the result shows ETH to be only "slightly" false and that it should not prompt worry that P=NP, and a commenter cited by Latent Space says the algorithm falls back to exponential time unless n is astronomically large. inference1011

Verification is to be shepherded by a group led by Russell Impagliazzo, Ramamohan Paturi and Ryan Williams, and OpenAI says it agreed to make all raw data public and to fund a conference on the implications.1213

TechCrunch reports that Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge suggested OpenAI was basing its solutions on pre-existing work, and that Anthropic released its own refutation hours later.14 Reddit commenters summarized by Latent Space were skeptical of the timing of Anthropic's claim, with one framing it as "we solved it too" after OpenAI's announcement.15

Ryan Williams told TechCrunch that parts of the proof look routine but two or three look "alien", that people should put a little more weight on P = NP's plausibility, and that he doubts OpenAI's algorithm is the best possible.161718 Williams said many papers relying on ETH will need revisiting if the claims hold up, and that OpenAI told him it was reasonably confident no cryptographic systems were broken.1920
