Canary experiment reveals AI supply chain leaks

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 09:22 UTC
2 sources · 110 points on Hacker News · tracked since Nov 20 · single origin (Dragos)

HD Moore, chief research officer at security firm Dragos, reports that he sent nearly two million prompts containing unique, trackable canary secrets through AI services and found breaches, prompt theft and widespread failures to protect customer data.12 Moore says the test ran from July through November 2026 and used URLs and canary token credentials, including AWS keys tied to empty accounts.34 Ars Technica's Dan Goodin reported the work on Nov. 20, 2026, describing how AI routers read prompts in plaintext and may log them, so data passes through many hands. inference56

Moore calls the most serious finding the compromise of three hosting providers, saying none knew of its breach until he notified them and that the earliest incident dates to mid-July.7 In the largest breach, Moore says every request sent during certain windows was stolen, and that the attacker was copying requests at the provider's front door rather than somewhere deeper in the inference stack.8910 According to Moore, the decoy keys that were taken got used to make tens of thousands of calls to AWS APIs, while in the two remaining breaches, a number of the stolen requests had been labeled Zero Data Retention.1112 He also says one adversary appears to have compromised two of the providers at the same time.1314

Moore says multiple resellers on an inference marketplace could view and keep customer prompts using ordinary logging, without exploiting a vulnerability.15 He says prompts routed through a different seller for the same model were never touched, indicating the leak followed the seller rather than the marketplace.16

Moore reports that between July and October, systems belonging to a Chinese AI lab retrieved the hidden URLs found in over half of the prompts submitted to its models, a group that covered about two-thirds of the requests flagged as no data collection or ZDR.1718 He says several U.S. hosting companies that resell the lab's models also set off that scraper, and another provider kept crawling a hidden URL over two months after it had appeared in just one inference call.1920

Moore says he is working with about a dozen providers, and that one has published a preliminary security notice while another had not publicly disclosed its incident.212223 Moore advises self-hosting if affordable, otherwise using a first-tier provider directly, avoiding default reseller pools, and seeding one's own canaries, while noting that no option guarantees no leakage. inference242526
