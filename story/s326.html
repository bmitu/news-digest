White House/Trump AI safety agreement

Last scan Mon Oct 5, 14:51 UTC
5 sources · tracked since Sep 30 · 1 new article today · single origin (CNBC)

The text of the accord, as reproduced by Zvi Mowshowitz, lists four layers of controls: internal controls to monitor model capabilities and alignment, an empowered internal team, an independent external auditor or evaluator, and an independent board committee.123 Signatories named include Sundar Pichai, Dario Amodei, Mark Zuckerberg, Elon Musk, Jensen Huang and Greg Brockman, while Ars Technica lists Sam Altman for OpenAI. inference45 Mowshowitz says Microsoft and Amazon were present and did not visibly sign, and that it is unclear why.6

Ars Technica says nothing new is legally required, and that The Information reported Anthropic, Google and OpenAI had already committed to external audits.7 Ars Technica adds that few details have been released on how audits would work or who would audit models, and that Bloomberg noted Trump seems to be politicizing the selection process.89

Reactions are skeptical in places: TechCrunch's Equity hosts were pretty skeptical the accord will amount to much, with Sean O'Kane calling it "deeply non-binding" and Kirsten Korosec suggesting the meeting's real significance may have been as "a rebranding effort around AI."1011 Mowshowitz, by contrast, calls it "nonzero Actual Progress" and a large de-risking regarding antitrust, though he says the first of the commitments are things no company would likely deny doing. inference1213 Senator Sanders posted that it was "A government of, by and for the billionaire class."14

Separately, CNBC reports the Federal Trade Commission has opened an investigation into OpenAI, Anthropic and other AI companies over potential dangers of their products, as confirmed by an agency spokesperson.15
