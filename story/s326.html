White House/Trump AI safety agreement

4 sources · tracked since Sep 30 · 2 new articles today · single origin (White House)

President Donald Trump announced a voluntary AI safety deal, the Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities (also called the White House Accord), in which tech leaders agreed to self-regulate their AI technology.12 The Verge says it was signed by Google's Sundar Pichai, Anthropic's Dario Amodei, Meta's Mark Zuckerberg, OpenAI's Greg Brockman, xAI's Elon Musk and Nvidia's Jensen Huang, while Ars Technica names OpenAI's Sam Altman as a signer instead. inference345 Zvi Mowshowitz notes that Microsoft and Amazon were present and did not visibly sign, and says it is unclear whether they chose not to sign or were not considered sufficiently worthy of inclusion.6

The accord sets out four layers of controls for each company: internal controls monitoring model capabilities and alignment, an internal team to ensure the controls, monitoring and detection operate as intended and issues are remediated, an independent external auditor or evaluator, and an independent board committee to oversee reports and remediation.78910 The companies also agreed to meet regularly to set standards and best practices, and the text says it "may make sense to codify" the steps into laws or regulations over time.111213 Nothing new is legally required, and Ars Technica, citing The Information, reports that Anthropic, Google and OpenAI had already committed to external audits.14

Trump called the deal "morally binding" and said "It’s almost like a constitution, in a way," even though it carries no legal weight, according to Bloomberg as relayed by Ars Technica.1516 Critics are skeptical: The Verge calls it "little more than a pinky promise" with no penalties, and Bernie Sanders posted that it showed "A government of, by and for the billionaire class."1718 Zvi Mowshowitz is more positive, calling the agreement "nonzero Actual Progress rather than a step backwards" and saying it is "still a large de-risking" regarding antitrust, though he notes it is not a formal waiver.192021

Ars Technica says very few details have been released about how the new safety audits would work, that it is unclear who Trump is tapping to audit frontier models, and that Bloomberg noted Trump seems to be politicizing the selection process.22 The announcement came amid AI security incidents, including OpenAI's Hugging Face and Australian government hacks, and Delaware and California are investigating possible governance failures at OpenAI. inference2324
