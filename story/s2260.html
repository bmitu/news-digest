Texas data center permit pause

Last scan Sat Nov 7, 03:59 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Oct 30 · 3 new articles this week · 2 independent origins: 404 Media, SemiAnalysis

On September 21, Texas Governor Greg Abbott directed a key state regulator to halt data center permits, a move MIT Technology Review says came as bipartisan distaste for the facilities grew.12 However, 404 Media disputes that the pause was real, saying Texas regulators kept approving projects and only shut the state's permitting web portal after 404 Media reported on it.3 SemiAnalysis, as summarized by Latent Space, discusses the Texas TCEQ permit freeze and the 30.3 GW of planned 2028 capacity that may be at risk amid a cross-party backlash.4

In the midterms Abbott lost his bid for a fourth term to Democrat Gina Hinojosa, who made data centers a central campaign issue, and Democrat James Talarico won the US Senate seat over Ken Paxton.567 404 Media reports Abbott lost by more than 700,000 votes, and MIT Technology Review adds that Democrats will take control of the Texas House for the first time in 24 years.89

According to 404 Media, data center proposals were on the ballot in more than 40 races across 13 states, and voters rejected them in 38 of them so far.10 Michael Whitesides of Local Progress says the midterms sent a clear message that people do not want data centers in their backyards.11

Hinojosa supported data center tax breaks in 2023 but began pushing for a full moratorium during her campaign, which MIT Technology Review says would be a problem for Big Tech given large projects already underway in Texas such as the 1,820-megawatt Stargate Frontier and the 6,400-megawatt Nexus Hubbard.121314 MIT Technology Review says the Texas picture is mixed: Dallas County went blue, but Ellis County, which leads in permitting requests, went for Abbott and Paxton, and west Texas counties with projects also went red.1516 MIT Technology Review cautions that following through may be complicated, since local officials may resist sweeping state restrictions and construction unions that backed some Democrats could push back on a moratorium.1718
