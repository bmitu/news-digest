Texas data center permit pause

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
2 sources · 2 independent origins: SemiAnalysis, The Verge

Texas Gov. Greg Abbott has announced statewide moratoriums on permits for data center projects pending audits of their electricity and water use, after directing agencies in June to require data center operators to cover the costs of new infrastructure built to serve them.12 The pause is limited in scope: projects that already have permits are still being built, and the audits cover only projects that connect to the power grid, even though Texas has more data centers building their own on-site power supply than any other state.345 SemiAnalysis, as relayed by Latent Space, says six campuses totaling 8.7GW need air permits during the freeze, and its base case is a slip of about one quarter.6

An October investigation by Public Citizen and FracTracker reportedly found public records indicating that state agencies are still processing data center permits, but Abbott's office denies that any approvals have been issued since he directed the pause.7 Separately, the NAACP sent Abbott's office a letter criticizing the moratoriums for not going far enough, and coalition member Bledsoe says the pause looks like "an attempt to run out the clock before the election."89

The political backdrop is public opposition: an August poll found 57 percent of Texans oppose the construction of data centers, including 43 percent of Republicans, 75 percent of Democrats and 54 percent of independents.1011 Jess Heck of the industry's Data Center Coalition says its members share the same goals as Abbott and the legislature, while The Verge notes Abbott has reportedly taken millions in campaign contributions from data center interests.1213
