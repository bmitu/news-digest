Shared robots.txt AI opt-out signal

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
2 sources · 2 independent origins: OpenAI, Search Engine Roundtable

OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and Anthropic say they will adopt a new standard that lets publishers opt out of AI scraping, as The Verge reports.1 The shared "policy token" signal would live in a site's robots.txt file and lets publishers apply their AI training preferences across multiple AI companies instead of setting them individually.23 The Verge reports that the signal is proposed by Kevin Kelley, a technical lead for standards at Anthropic, as part of the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF).4 The four companies say they will restrict their AI crawlers from a site if they encounter the shared token.5

Timing differs: Anthropic has already adopted the signal, Google and Microsoft plan to support it next year, and OpenAI says it aims to implement it in the coming months.67 OpenAI describes the effort as voluntary and says it draws on the IETF's AI Preferences working group.89 OpenAI also says the signal will not replace its existing robots.txt crawler controls, which will continue to be respected.1011 OpenAI says it believes the signal is a practical step toward an open web and encourages other AI providers to honor it.1213

Microsoft says it will offer granular controls to opt out of AI training or AI grounding, and that sites will still appear in Bing search results regardless of preferences.1415 Meta communications head Andy Stone signaled support, calling a common signal "an important step", but the company did not say whether it will honor the setting.1617
