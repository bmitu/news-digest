AMD acquires World Labs for $8.2B

Last scan Tue Oct 6, 16:18 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Sep 29 · 1 new article today · single origin (DeepLearning.AI)

AMD and World Labs announced that AMD will acquire the world-models AI company in a deal valued at $8.2 billion, pending regulatory approval and expected to close by the end of the year.12 A separate newsletter item described the purchase only as a reported deal, saying AMD "reportedly bought World Labs for $8.2B".3

Fei-Fei Li founded World Labs in 2024 with Justin Johnson, Christoph Lassner and Ben Mildenhall, with $230 million in funding, some of which came from AMD.4 Under World Labs' announcement, Li will become executive vice president and chief scientist at AMD, while Johnson and Mildenhall will continue leading the World Labs team inside AMD.56

AMD's announcement says World Labs' expertise will give it deeper insight into how workloads are evolving and help shape its future technology roadmaps.7

Ars Technica's Samuel Axon says AMD has made its own models, such as the open source Micro-World, but has not produced a stack that competes with Nvidia's Cosmos, and that Nvidia has a strong lead in robotics training data.89 Axon argues the deal came sooner than expected for such a young company but should not be a surprise, since the firms had partnered before and AMD needs such expertise to compete with Nvidia.1011

What is Marble, World Labs' first public tool, and what can people make with it?

World Labs describes Marble as creating 3D worlds from text, images, video or coarse 3D layouts, which users can edit, expand and combine, and then export as Gaussian splats, meshes or videos.12 Ars Technica says Marble generates small 3D spaces using Gaussian splats, which can be exported as 3D assets for film production, game development and other uses.3 In a demo earlier this year with AMD CEO Lisa Su, Fei-Fei Li showed Marble creating a 3D scene from a few images, CNBC reported.4

How does AMD's lack of a Nvidia Cosmos-style world-model stack compare with Nvidia's lead in robot training data?

What is Atlas, and how does it predict new camera views from 2D images better than specialized models?

Li says that, like an LLM predicting the next token from text, Atlas was trained from scratch to predict the next view from an input of 2D images. Li says it outperforms state-of-the-art results, including those of specialized models.12 Li attributes this to combining generative models with multiview geometry. She says this has essentially solved sparse reconstruction, a long-standing computer vision problem.3 The claim comes from World Labs' own post, and no benchmark figures or named competing models were given to back up the comparison with specialized models.
