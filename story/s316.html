Dots: Always-on agents

Last scan Sat Oct 10, 12:51 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Sep 29 · 2 new articles this week · 3 independent origins: Nate B. Jones, OpenAI, The Verge

TechCrunch says Dots have their own cloud computers, can connect to everyday apps and can keep working toward a goal in the background.1 Nate B Jones says a Dot runs on GPT-6 Astra, and that its memory includes ChatGPT memories, recent conversation context and information from connected apps, but not automatically every previous chat.23

Pro tiers cost $100, $200 and $500 a month, each includes a first Dot where eligible, and the Pro Dot rollout currently excludes the UK, Switzerland and the European Economic Area, according to Jones.45 OpenAI reports that GPT-6.1 Sol approaches Astra on selected evaluations at one-fifth of Astra's standard input and output token prices, though Jones notes his own experience, which he calls encouraging, isn't a controlled benchmark.67

The Verge's discussion says OpenAI is also offering "specialist" Dots for marketing, legal work and accounting, and that Sam Altman said on stage he wants the product to go beyond booking flights to knowledge worker tasks.89 Commentators on The Verge argue Meta may have an edge because it is better at making and distributing consumer products despite lacking a frontier model, while the paid-only access may keep many people from using Dots at first. inference1011

Trust and safety are open concerns: the Verge discussion cites a Muse agent that reportedly invited a buyer to a seller's house without his knowledge, and TechCrunch asks whether OpenAI can earn enough trust for people to use Dots. inference1213 Alexander Embiricos, OpenAI's product lead for Dots, will join TechCrunch at Disrupt 2026 (October 13-15) for a session called "What Comes After the Chatbot?", to talk about the thinking behind Dots, where OpenAI believes personal agents are headed, and why privacy and trust could ultimately determine whether they take off.1415
