Dots: Always-on agents

5 sources · 684 points on Hacker News · tracked since Sep 29 · 11 new today · 2 independent origins: OpenAI, TechCrunch

At its DevDay keynote on Tuesday, OpenAI announced Dots, which it describes as "remarkably capable, always-on agents built to handle everything" and which are powered by GPT-6 Astra.12 Dots are rolling out to Pro and Business Premium users in eligible markets, and Enterprise, Edu and Healthcare users can try a beta if their workspace admin enables it.34 The first Dot is included in the plan at no extra cost, and OpenAI says conversations with it don't count toward usage limits, but Codex or ChatGPT Work tasks it starts do.567

OpenAI says Dots start with built-in rules for when to act independently, that users can set Custom Rules to allow, require approval for, or block actions, and that Dots can still make mistakes, so consequential work should be reviewed.8910 OpenAI also previewed enterprise "specialist dots" with their own identity and credentials, and says it is working with Microsoft to integrate them with Agent 365 security controls.111213

The Verge framed Dots as OpenAI's response to Meta's Muse, and separately noted that concerns are growing over Muse's safety. TechCrunch noted that much of the functionality was already possible through Codex and similar tools, but that Dots bundle it with a focus on independent action.141516

Casey Newton reportedly spent a few hours with his Dot, which he said saved him a couple of hours of work on day one, making it the most impressive agent he has tried.17 OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar told CNBC that OpenAI's vision is to bring Dots to its whole consumer base.18

How does Dots' safety approach work in practice, such as reviewing actions automatically, protecting saved passwords and pausing the Dot if something looks wrong?

OpenAI says Dots ship with built-in rules for when to act on their own and when to ask for approval, and users can add Custom Rules that allow specific actions, require approval or block them.12 For automatic review, OpenAI says Dots use auto-review to check actions that could affect accounts or share information against the user's instructions, Custom Rules and safety requirements, which decides what proceeds, what needs approval and what the user must do.345 Certain sensitive tasks, such as changing a password, always stay with the user.67 On saved passwords, OpenAI says Dots can use them to sign into supported websites without exposing them to the model, and each Dot runs on its own cloud computer, separate from the user's machine unless the user connects it.8910 OpenAI says built-in security safeguards defend against malicious instructions and monitor for harmful behavior, and if its monitoring system detects a safety concern it can pause or stop the Dot's work.11 OpenAI also cautions that Dots can still make mistakes, so users should always review consequential work.12

What real-world tasks have early testers given their Dots, and what did those Dots actually do?

OpenAI says that inside OpenAI, a bug appears in Slack and dots immediately start investigating, and a new design arrives and dots turn it into a working app.12 OpenAI also says one early tester outside the company had a Dot notice he had forgotten to invoice a publication; it prepared the invoice and sent it after his approval.3 Latent Space reports that early testers report proactive behavior, e.g. negotiating with customer service to cut ~$500/yr in charges.4

What will Dots be able to do later, like adding more Dots or scaling how much work each one handles, and will that cost extra?

For now, users can create only one Dot, though OpenAI plans to let people deploy multiple agents at once.1 OpenAI says that in the future users will be able to add more Dots and scale each Dot's output, either by making it faster or by raising the total amount of work it can take on per month.23 The first Dot comes at no extra cost with Pro or Business Premium plans, and those plans include an allowance for deeper work, with extended limits for the first month after launch.45 Pricing for the additional Dots or the higher speed and workload options was not reported, though the wording about scaling by speed or monthly work suggests these could be paid upgrades. inference64

Can a Dot use my own laptop, and what happens to my computer and its files if I connect it?

Yes, OpenAI says you can give your Dot permission to connect to and use your laptop, so it can work directly alongside you.1 According to OpenAI, your computer and its contents stay separate from the Dot's cloud computer unless you choose to connect it.2 OpenAI also says a Dot's background "proactive research" uses read-only tools that can't send messages, change app content, or control your browser or computer.3 OpenAI adds that its monitoring can pause or stop a Dot's work if it detects a safety concern, and it warns that Dots can still make mistakes.45 However, the reports do not say exactly what files or settings on a connected laptop a Dot can read or change.
