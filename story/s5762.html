NYT warns photographers about Photoshop AI

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 09:22 UTC
2 sources · 454 points on Hacker News · tracked since Nov 25 · single origin (The Verge)

The New York Times has asked its freelance photographers not to use the newest version of Adobe Photoshop, saying the 2027 update's features use AI in ways that may not be apparent to users.1 In an email seen by The Verge, Meaghan Looram and Josh Haner, two of the Times' photography leaders, wrote that using these features can result in metadata identifying photographs as AI-generated.2 The email requires all contributing photographers to avoid Photoshop 2027 (release 28.0.0 and later updates) when working on assignment for the Times, and Times spokesperson Graham James confirmed the requirement to The Verge.34

Among the new AI capabilities in the November release are an "AI Assisted Editor" view, an assistant that gives editing guidance one step at a time, image edits driven by text prompts, additional AI upscaling choices, and a tool that automatically reworks an image's composition to suit different canvas dimensions.56 The Verge reports that it is not always clear which Adobe tools are AI-powered, and even older options such as automated selection tools may now count as part of the AI suite.7 It remains unclear which specific Photoshop features the Times means, and Adobe had not immediately responded to The Verge's request for more information.89

Adobe has been adding support for C2PA metadata to its apps since August, a feature that records whether an image was produced by AI or altered using AI tools; the Times says the limit it has set is intended to prevent such labels from showing up unintentionally on its photographs.1011 The Verge argues that such metadata is a blunt description of post-production work and, for a newsroom that keeps photo editing to a minimum, risks costing readers' trust.1213
