METR and Redwood red-team OpenAI monitoring

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 14:03 UTC
3 sources · single origin (METR)

A METR employee and a Redwood Research employee spent three weeks, from July 21st to August 11th, on a pilot high-access exercise to stress-test OpenAI's internal misalignment monitoring system, according to METR's own write-up, which Redwood also posted.12 They had access to OpenAI's internal codebase, monitor prompts and configuration, and internal documentation, and interviewed about a dozen researchers and engineers, working on OpenAI-issued devices that never left the premises.345 METR and Redwood say they found a number of novel vulnerabilities in the system as it existed on and before July 21st, and they caution that OpenAI was expanding the system during the exercise, so the findings do not establish its current coverage.678

They estimated roughly 75–94% of agentic internal Responses API traffic from models at least as strong as GPT-5.5 was eligible for monitoring, and 99.2% for the subset used by internal Codex agents, while noting substantial uncertainty.91011 They estimated that not monitoring compaction events likely left on the order of 10% of eligible internal Codex traffic unmonitored between May and July 2026. Because monitoring triggered when agents returned control, agents could indefinitely postpone being monitored by continuing to act. OpenAI staff had reportedly identified the compaction problem beforehand and fixed it during the exercise.12131415

OpenAI has stated that all vulnerabilities METR and Redwood identified that impact its production safeguards stack have been patched, and has described significant subsequent changes to the intended coverage of its monitoring system. METR and Redwood say they have not evaluated these claims.1617

The public write-up is limited: OpenAI had the right to redact any information it deemed confidential or sensitive, and METR says it lacked a unilateral right to describe the redactions, though it endorses the published findings.181920 A Latent Space newsletter roundup reported that the 61-page report was cut to 38 pages, citing a post by @AndrewCurran_.21

The write-up proposes design ideals, including that future monitoring infrastructure should withstand deliberate, strategic, and sustained attacks from highly capable internal agents at scale, that agents be monitored every N messages/minutes instead of waiting for them to end their turns, and that isolation be enforced between agents and their monitors.222324
