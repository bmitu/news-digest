Anthropic Cyber Mission and OSS Scanner

Last scan Sat Oct 10, 12:51 UTC
3 sources · single origin (Anthropic)

Anthropic announced the Anthropic Cyber Mission, a long-term effort to support defenders with tools, research and resources, and launched OSS Scanner, which gives open-source projects regular security scans from its strongest models for free.12 The Cyber Mission starts with two areas, critical infrastructure and open-source software, and Anthropic also introduced a Critical Infrastructure Defense Program that brings frontier models, on-site engineers and threat research to defenders of operational technology.345 Anthropic's founding partners for the Critical Infrastructure Defense Program are Accenture, Booz Allen, CrowdStrike, Deloitte, Dragos, Hitachi, Insane Cyber, Nozomi Networks, Palo Alto Networks, PwC and Rockwell Automation.6

OSS Scanner is opt-in, and Anthropic says each report includes a proof of concept, an explanation and a suggested fix where one is available.78 The reports are model-generated and sent without human review, which Anthropic says means maintainers get them faster but that some will contain inaccuracies, such as a wrong severity rating.910 Anthropic says it expects a true-positive rate above 90% and will work to improve it over time.11 Anthropic says the service is meant for projects with the capacity to keep up with findings, and it will keep sharing human-verified disclosures for the others.12

The Verge's Stevie Bonifield notes that the lack of human review could mean projects hear about problems sooner, but also that reports may be incorrect or invalid.10 The Verge also says OSS Scanner is far from the first AI bug-hunting helper, and that some open-source projects are struggling to keep up with the onslaught of AI-generated bug reports, including Linus Torvalds and Google.1314

Anthropic forecasts that AI will favor defense in two years, but says that may not hold in the near term because verifying and fixing vulnerabilities is slow and still depends on people.151617
