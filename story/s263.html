Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 launch

3 sources · tracked since Sep 29 · 1 new article today · 2 independent origins: Anthropic, Simon Willison

Anthropic released Claude Sonnet 5.5, the second model in the Claude 5.5 family, one week after Opus 5.5 and the day before OpenAI DevDay, according to Latent Space's AINews roundup.1 Anthropic called it "a clear upgrade over Sonnet 5" and claims it runs more than 30% faster and costs up to 30% less for most work.2 Anthropic's Mike Krieger (@mikeyk) said Haiku 5.5 will "round out the family in the coming weeks," and Simon Willison says Anthropic's announcement reiterates that Haiku 5.5 will be available "in the coming weeks"; no Haiku launch is described in these passages. inference34

Simon Willison says Sonnet 5.5 is priced the same as Sonnet 5 but appears to beat it on every benchmark.5 Zvi Mowshowitz lists base pricing at $2/$10, half of Opus, and says the model is advertised as using 30% fewer tokens on average.6 Latent Space reports that early independent evals place it at or near Opus 5.5 on several leaderboards, and Willison says it appears almost as good as Opus 5.5 on some coding tasks.78

Willison reports that, as with Opus 5.5, the "max" thinking effort run on his pelican test thought for 128,000 tokens at a cost of $1.28 and failed to produce an SVG.9

Willison calls the most interesting point that Sonnet 5.5 now powers claude.ai's free tier, which he says gives Anthropic a much more capable free offering than ChatGPT's Luna 5.6.1011 Mowshowitz is less enthusiastic about switching: he says there is only a narrow window where the discount is worthwhile and he would mostly stick with Opus 5.5.12

Why is Anthropic extending preserved thinking so that reasoning traces stay inside the organization that generated them?

Anthropic extended "preserved thinking" to counter distillation carried out by switching accounts, according to the @ClaudeDevs account.12 Under the change, reasoning traces stay in the organization that generated them, and if a session moves to another account, Claude rereads it and regenerates the thinking instead of reusing the original traces.3 The reasoning behind the move is therefore anti-distillation: keeping the original traces from travelling between accounts makes them harder to collect and use for training other models, though the reports do not spell out that mechanism. inference24
