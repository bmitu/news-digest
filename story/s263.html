Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 launch

Last scan Sat Oct 3, 14:34 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Sep 29 · 1 new article on Oct 3 · 3 independent origins: Anthropic, Latent Space, Simon Willison

Anthropic released Claude Sonnet 5.5, the second model in the Claude 5.5 family, one week after Opus 5.5 and the day before OpenAI DevDay, according to Latent Space's AINews roundup.1 Anthropic says Sonnet 5.5 is a clear upgrade over Sonnet 5 that runs more than 30% faster and costs up to 30% less for most work.23 Anthropic's @mikeyk said Haiku 5.5 will "round out the family in the coming weeks", and Simon Willison notes the announcement repeats that Haiku 5.5 will arrive "in the coming weeks".45

Simon Willison says Sonnet 5.5 is priced the same as Sonnet 5 but appears to beat it on every benchmark, while Zvi Mowshowitz lists base pricing of $2/$10, half of Opus.67 In an independent leaderboard summary, Sonnet 5.5 [Max] debuted at #3 and ranked #1 in the Chat category, at $2.74 per task versus $1.58 for #2 Opus 5.5, which keeps it off the Pareto frontier.8 On WebDev, Sonnet sits 2 points behind GPT-6 Astra [Max] at 80% lower cost.9

Evaluators differ on its value against Opus 5.5: Willison says it appears almost as good as Opus 5.5 on some coding tasks, while Mowshowitz presumes the discount is worthwhile only in a narrow window and would mostly stick with Opus 5.5. inference1011 Willison also reports that the "max" thinking effort pelican test ran for 128,000 tokens at a cost of $1.28 and failed to produce an SVG, the same bug he saw with Opus 5.5.12

Willison calls the most interesting aspect that Sonnet 5.5 is now the model used for the free tier on claude.ai, and says that, because ChatGPT's free tier uses Luna 5.6, Anthropic currently has a much more capable free offering.131415

Why did Anthropic extend 'preserved thinking', and how does it work when a session moves to another account?

Anthropic's @ClaudeDevs account says it extended "preserved thinking" to counter distillation carried out by switching accounts.1 Under the change, reasoning traces stay in the organization that generated them.2 If a session moves to another account, Claude rereads it and regenerates thinking.3
