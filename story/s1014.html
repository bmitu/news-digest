Anthropic builds Scion source control

Last scan Mon Oct 19, 22:55 UTC
5 sources · 1574 points on Hacker News · tracked since Oct 17 · 2 independent origins: Anthropic, Rain (sunshowers)

Anthropic published a post saying it built Scion, an in-house version control system, and fully migrated its monorepo from git in five months.12 Anthropic says a team of seven people built both Scion and its code review tool Antique, plus about half a dozen related systems.3

Simon Willison quotes Anthropic's stated scale figures: almost 100 GB of main-branch history, a compressed working copy over 25 GB, and on the order of 25 million code checkouts per day for CI (before developer environments, agent sandboxes and research jobs), which fetched naively would be over 600 PB of git traffic per day.45

Anthropic says Scion is not a suitable general replacement for git and is hard to offer to other organizations, so it plans to share its thinking so that others can apply parts of it to git.67 Anthropic says engineers often had to tell Claude "no" and that adversarial review could be myopic and lead to over-engineering.8910

Sunshowers argues that Anthropic crediting Sapling only for "hard-won lessons", and not mentioning Mononoke at all, borders on plagiarism. He says the level of imitation goes well beyond similar insights, and that he does not object to the copying itself.1112 In a follow-up, the same author says he privately received confirmation that the copying was intentional and that personnel overlapped, calls the omission dishonest, and says the problem is attribution rather than copying.1314

The author notes that Anthropic's Nick G. has responded on Hacker News and that sufficient attribution has been added to Anthropic's blog post, and says he stands by his posts as of the time he wrote them.15 Willison's update says Anthropic credits Google's Piper and Meta's Sapling as prior art, and that the "derived data" concept appears heavily inspired by Mononoke.16

Zvi Mowshowitz reacts with "holy shit" to the rise in code volume while acknowledging that lines of code is not a robust measure, and he quotes Anthropic's claim that an early internal Claude Mythos Preview snapshot rewrote the git-to-Scion ingestion pipeline to use 20x less memory and run 4x faster.1718 Latent Space calls the report concrete evidence of agent-driven pressure on developer infrastructure but cautions that merge volume alone does not measure delivered value.19
