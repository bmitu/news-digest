Apple macOS Full Disk Access restrictions

Last scan Mon Oct 5, 14:51 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Oct 3 · 3 independent origins: Apple, Ars Technica, TechCrunch

Apple said it will add new controls around macOS "Full Disk Access" so that users who genuinely wish to grant an app that level of access can do so only with "very explicit user action," in response to risks posed by AI agents.123 Apple said some developers use Full Disk Access in ways that could expose files, mail, messages and browsing history without users' full knowledge, and that the risks will grow substantially as AI agents become more capable and autonomous.456 The Verge reports that Apple did not say when the update will roll out, and that Apple did not immediately respond to The Verge's request for comment; TechCrunch reports Apple did not respond to its inquiry.78

Meta disputes this: spokesperson Andy Stone and CTO David Singleton say Muse can read Messages only if both macOS Full Disk Access and the Muse Messages connector are enabled.910 Security expert Patrick Wardle questioned that denial, saying that with Full Disk Access any non-root file is readable.11 Ars Technica notes that Apple did not name Meta or Muse, says the timing makes it likely the statement referred to the Muse incident, and says that at a minimum Apple's statements seem to contradict Singleton's denial.1213

TechCrunch says the report raised questions about trust in desktop-based AI, and adds that a Wired report cited a flaw in ChatGPT's Mac app that could have allowed hackers to access sensitive data.1415 Ars Technica's Dan Goodin writes that the events suggest Muse may not be worthy of the extraordinary access it must have to work as billed by Meta, and that people who use the assistant should configure permissions carefully, though he notes that precaution only goes so far.1617
