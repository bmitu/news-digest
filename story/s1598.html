Attorneys general in Character.AI bankruptcy

Last scan Sun Nov 22, 14:05 UTC
5 sources · tracked since Oct 21 · 2 new articles today · single origin (Virginia Attorney General)

A bankruptcy court overseeing Character Technologies, Inc., the company behind Character.AI, has ordered a Consumer Privacy Ombudsman appointed to review any sale of users' private data, at the urging of a bipartisan coalition of state attorneys general.12 The United States Trustee selected data privacy expert Lucy Thompson, who will file reports identifying privacy issues and proposing protections before any assets change hands.345

The company plans to auction its assets, including user accounts and sensitive chat data, on December 1, 2026, to the highest bidder.67 Character.AI remains operating during bankruptcy, with 12 million users averaging 120 minutes of daily use, and the data includes private chats about mental health, relationships and emotional struggles.8910

State investigations began after a 2024 wrongful death lawsuit alleging a chatbot fostered an exploitative relationship with a child and contributed to his suicide.111213 Character.AI has on its own initiative limited what minors can do with certain chatbot features, but Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison and his coalition contend that those steps are optional, only partial, and a later owner of the company could undo them.14

Ellison and the coalition say they will object to any sale or bankruptcy plan lacking strong protections for personal information.15 Pennsylvania Attorney General Dave Sunday said the states filed a notice in the bankruptcy last month setting out privacy and safety protections for any sale, and the attorneys general of California, New York and the District of Columbia joined that filing. inference1617 Ellison described naming the independent monitor as "a good step forward." He added that he and the other attorneys general will keep pressing to protect consumers' private personal information, with particular attention to the children's data Character.AI gathered.1819 Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield said children's words to chatbots "were never meant to be sold," and Alaska's Acting Attorney General Cori Mills said a company's responsibility to protect information does not disappear in bankruptcy.2021 Sunday, who spoke as a parent, welcomed the court's decision to appoint a privacy specialist to supervise the case, adding that kids who use the chatbots shouldn't see their personal data auctioned off to whoever pays most or put to other improper uses.2223
