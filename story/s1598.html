Attorneys general in Character.AI bankruptcy

Last scan Wed Oct 21, 20:11 UTC
7 sources · 2 independent origins: Michigan Advance, Virginia Attorney General

Massachusetts Attorney General Andrea Joy Campbell and a coalition of 39 other state attorneys general filed a notice in the bankruptcy proceedings of Character Technologies Inc., according to an Oct. 14 press release from Campbell's office.12 The filing seeks to guarantee consumer protections, particularly for children, and to ensure sensitive user data, including conversations about mental health and relationships, stays protected if the business is sold or transferred.34

The coalition is asking the court to ensure users can access and delete their information before any sale and to limit how a future buyer can use private conversations, according to North Carolina Attorney General Jeff Jackson's account.5 Michigan Advance reports that Character.AI announced its bankruptcy filing on Monday and said it would sell $20 million in assets to Disney.6 The attorneys general say any purchaser should be prepared to use age verification beyond birthdates, remove characters posing as licensed professionals such as therapists or lawyers, and implement a privacy program.7

The states began investigating after a 2024 wrongful death lawsuit alleging the chatbots fostered an exploitative relationship with a child and contributed to his death by suicide; Michigan Advance says that case was settled in January.89 The states say Character.AI's recent voluntary restrictions on minors' access are not compulsory, are incomplete, and could be circumvented or reversed by a future purchaser.10 The coalition says it will stay engaged in the proceedings and has reserved the right to challenge any proposed sale or plan that fails to adequately safeguard consumers.1112

Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones said, "Bankruptcy cannot be an escape hatch from responsibility," while Michigan Attorney General Dana Nessel said voluntary safety measures are not enough if a business's next owner can reverse them.1314 Jackson said the case could set an important precedent for how personal data is handled when an AI company goes bankrupt.15
