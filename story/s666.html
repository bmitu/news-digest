Reflection Beam model launch

Last scan Tue Oct 6, 16:18 UTC
2 sources · single origin (Reflection AI)

Reflection AI unveiled Beam, its first frontier open-weight model, in a blog post on Monday, claiming it matches leading Chinese open models on advanced reasoning benchmarks at far lower compute cost.123 Reflection says it will release Beam's weights and full technical details this month, and Latent Space reports the weights are due under Apache 2.0.45

Beam is a text-only mixture-of-experts model with 501 billion total and 23 billion active parameters, pretrained on 23.8 trillion tokens, with a 1 million token context window.678

Reflection says Beam scores on par with Z.ai's GLM-5.2 on advanced reasoning benchmarks and outperforms today's leading Western open models while using "3-4x less inference compute"; GLM-5.2 has roughly 744 billion total parameters with 40 billion active.91011 A summary of Reflection's claims cited by Latent Space gives a score of 80.9 on SWE-bench Verified.12 Reflection's own benchmarks show Beam outscoring Thinking Machines Lab's Inkling on four coding tests, though Inkling is multimodal and Beam is text-only.13 TechCrunch notes the performance claims have not been independently verified.14

Latent Space says Reflection compares itself to Inkling, Nemotron and GLM 5.2, but that newer models such as GLM 5.3, Kimi K3, Qwen 3.8 Max and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash are generally ahead.15 Latent Space argues that because Beam was trained from scratch in the US, part of the market has been waiting for such options, and that Reflection has announced its arrival as a functional neolab.1617

TechCrunch reports Reflection is aiming Beam at enterprises and sovereign nations with "AI factories", and has raised roughly $4.7 billion per PitchBook.1819

How much compute did Reflection use to train Beam, and how long did pretraining and reinforcement learning take?

A summary of Reflection's claims, cited by Latent Space, says Beam took four weeks each of pretraining and reinforcement learning on about 10,500 Nvidia GB300 chips.12 Pretraining covered 23.8 trillion tokens.34 Team posts also describe a stable RL run on 10K GB300s with more than 100M rollouts across about 1M tasks.56 A total compute figure, such as FLOPs or GPU-hours, was not reported, and the timings come from a summary of Reflection's claims, not from the company's own technical report, which is still to come.

What is an "AI factory," and which customers has Reflection already started testing it with?

An "AI factory," per TechCrunch's description of Reflection's pitch, is a product that would let institutions build their own customized, local AI system by training Reflection's AI models on their own proprietary data.1 Reflection is aiming Beam and future models at enterprises and sovereign nations with this offering.2 Axios reported that hedge funds and trading firms are among those eager to build such systems.3 According to TechCrunch, Reflection has already begun testing the concept of a sovereign AI factory partnership with Shinsegae Group in South Korea.4

What did Artificial Analysis, which has early access to Beam, say about how efficient it is?

Artificial Analysis, which has early access to Reflection's Beam, expects the model to be among the most token-efficient open models for its level of intelligence.1 This is an expectation from early access, not a published measurement, and Reflection's own claim of 3-4x less inference compute than GLM 5.2 has not been independently verified. inference23
