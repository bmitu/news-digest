OpenAI perplexity-based AI development limits

Last scan Mon Nov 23, 13:58 UTC
2 sources · single origin (OpenAI)

OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki published a blog post arguing that perplexity, a measure of how well a model predicts the next token, should be established as a metric for comparing model developers and informing shared safety standards.12 He proposes common, perplexity-based limits that developers would commit not to cross until their ability to understand and monitor AI behavior reaches a concrete milestone, such as a successor to chain-of-thought monitors.34

TechCrunch places the post in a wider debate over slowing AI, noting that Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published a plan to "pace the frontier" that called for limits on the rate of unchecked AI progress, and that setting a speed limit requires agreeing on how to measure speed.56

Pachocki reports that OpenAI's log-perplexity was about 0.99 for GPT-3.5 and about 0.91 for GPT-4, and that losses below 0.8 have coincided with transformative capabilities in cybersecurity and mathematics.78910 He says a model at log-perplexity of about 0.76 began producing groundbreaking mathematics results, and he expects further transformative capabilities in the roughly 0.68-0.7 window, though he does not know what they will be.1112

Pachocki argues that benchmarks of downstream capabilities become outdated quickly, whereas progress on pretraining is likely to stay informative.131415 He acknowledges limits of perplexity as a shared metric: labs use different datasets and data mixtures, perplexity does not directly match a task people care about, chain-of-thought reasoning does not lower it despite boosting capabilities, and its floor is unknown.16171819

Pachocki says publishing OpenAI's internal figures gives competitors a clearer view of where it stands, but that this concern is outweighed by the urgency of shared safety measures, and TechCrunch reports Sam Altman called the post "the most important thing OpenAI has published in a while."2021
