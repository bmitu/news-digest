OpenAI perplexity-based AI development limits

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 17:01 UTC
7 sources · tracked since Nov 23 · 2 independent origins: METR, OpenAI

OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki has proposed industry limits on AI development, tied to perplexity and other measures, that developers would pledge not to pass until they reach a set milestone in understanding and monitoring AI, such as a better successor to today's chain-of-thought monitors.1234 Perplexity gauges how well a model guesses the next token, with lower scores better, and Pachocki argues it could be a common yardstick for general intelligence, while tests of downstream abilities may quickly become dated. inference56789

He reports log-perplexity of about 0.99 for GPT-3.5 and 0.91 for GPT-4, and says that as the figure dropped under 0.8, transformative abilities appeared in areas including mathematics and cybersecurity.1011121314 Pachocki expects further breakthroughs of this kind, whose nature he says he does not know, at roughly 0.68–0.7, and suggests a flood of basic advances in pretraining is conceivable as early as 2027.15161718 METR, analyzing OpenAI's data, found the loss falling about seven times faster since early 2026 than in 2022–2025, but says it has no consensus on how much this should shift capability forecasts, noting that the data could overstate progress, for instance through overfitting.19202122

Pachocki himself concedes drawbacks to the metric: comparing labs is difficult, it does not directly match tasks people care about, it misses advances outside pretraining, and nobody knows how low it can go. inference232425 Critics cited by Latent Space also argue that the narrow code dataset may reflect shifts in data mix, that shared limits could favor OpenAI over Anthropic because their tokenizers differ, and that perplexity may be the wrong gauge since post-training likely uses most computation. inference262728 Responding to questions about the measurement, OpenAI staff said the test set stays unchanged over time and leaves out code written by models.29

Among reactions, Microsoft's Mustafa Suleyman endorsed Pachocki's effort, and Microsoft AI reported that its MAI-2 Base model scored 0.819 on OpenAI's evaluation set. inference3031 OpenAI CEO Sam Altman described Pachocki's post as "the most important thing OpenAI has published in a while," while Latent Space argues that at least a couple more leading labs, especially Anthropic, must join before it can gain traction as a genuine self-regulatory cap. inference323334
