Gemini 4 Argon cyber vulnerability discoveries

Last scan Mon Oct 5, 14:51 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Oct 1 · single origin (Google)

Google announced Gemini 4 Argon, a new frontier model, saying it is rolling out first to a set of trusted cyber defenders through its Fairwind Program.12 Google says Argon can autonomously find, validate and patch critical software vulnerabilities, and that it will release the model without cyber guardrails to trusted defenders and its own internal teams.345

Google says Wiz, using Argon through its Scan for Good initiative, uncovered a critical vulnerability exposing sensitive personal information in healthcare software used by hospitals worldwide, a risk Google says previous frontier models had missed.67 On CWE-bench v1, which evaluates vulnerability remediation, Google says Argon ties for first place with a top score of 68%.89 Google also says Argon shows big leaps in vulnerability discovery over 3.8 Flash Cyber, finding exposures across codebases in 20 programming languages on its internal benchmark and outperforming that model on Wiz's internal black-box penetration testing benchmark.101112

Ars Technica's Ryan Whitwam notes that Google claims other frontier models missed the Wiz flaw but did not provide specifics.13

Google describes the release as phased, saying it is engaged in the U.S. government's voluntary pre-release access process and will gather feedback from early testers before wider availability.1415 Whitwam reports that Google gave no specific timeline for general availability, which would start with paid API users and Google AI Ultra subscribers after the cybersecurity tests.1617

How much will Gemini 4 Argon cost to use through the API, and does that price last?

Google has announced API pricing for Gemini 4 Argon: for a limited time, rates of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with cached input tokens discounted 95 percent.12 The price does not appear to be permanent: Google calls it an introductory price, and Ars Technica describes the rates as available for a limited time.23 It was not reported how long the introductory pricing will last or what the price will be afterward.

What has Google already done with Argon inside the company, such as saving memory or moving code to Rust?

Google also says its engineers have been using Argon for daily tasks, from everyday debugging to large-scale codebase migrations and algorithm designs, and that Argon is helping its quantum computing researchers optimize the spacetime resources of subroutines, in one example beating the published baseline by 40% in minutes.123 Google says Argon agents analyzed fleet-wide profiling telemetry and autonomously applied memory optimizations across its data centers, freeing over 300 TiB of memory once rolled out, with an estimated 500 TiB to 1 PiB in total savings.45 Google also says Argon agents are migrating C/C++ codebases to Rust, scaling from tens of thousands of lines in core libraries like re2 and libgav1 up to more than 800,000 lines for the Fuchsia Zircon kernel.67 For libgav1, Google's open source video decoding software, Google says Argon agents took an existing Rust port and replaced 32K lines of SIMD code with safe Rust, producing a memory-safe video decoder that runs 2.7x faster than the Rust port, with identical video output.8910 Google says these large-scale rewrites are undergoing rigorous automated and manual auditing, emulation testing, and review before rolling out to production.1112
