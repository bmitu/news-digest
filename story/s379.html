Gemini 4 Argon cyber vulnerability discoveries

3 sources · single origin (Google)

Google announced its new frontier model, Gemini 4 Argon, on September 30, 2026, but is initially rolling it out only to a set of trusted cyber defenders through its Fairwind Program. inference12 Google says Argon can autonomously find, validate, and patch critical software vulnerabilities, and that it will release the model without cyber guardrails to trusted defenders and its own internal teams.34

Google says Wiz, using Argon through its Scan for Good initiative, found a critical vulnerability exposing sensitive personal information in healthcare software used by hospitals worldwide, a risk Google says previous frontier models had missed.5 Ars Technica notes that Google did not provide specifics on the claim that other frontier models missed the flaw.6 On CWE-bench v1, which evaluates remediation of security vulnerabilities, Google says Argon ties for first place with a top score of 68%.7

Google says it is strengthening safeguards before broad release, including monitoring the model's chain-of-thought and actions to stop execution when necessary, and it is engaged in the U.S. government's voluntary pre-release access process.8910 Ars Technica reports that Google gave no specific timeline for wider availability, saying general availability will start with paid API users and Google AI Ultra subscribers after cybersecurity tests.1112

Beyond cyber, Google claims a state-of-the-art 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1, which Ars Technica says is higher than GPT-6 Astra, Fable 5.1, and Opus 5.5.1314 The Verge noted that Google shared a chart showing Gemini 4 beating OpenAI and Anthropic models on many benchmarks, and that the launch came a day after OpenAI's DevDay.1516

How much will Gemini 4 Argon cost to use through the API, and does the cheaper launch rate expire?

Google says Gemini 4 Argon will launch at an introductory API price of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with cached input tokens priced at 95% off the input price.12 Ars Technica reports that these rates apply only "for a limited time."3 According to Latent Space, the standard price is $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, and the introductory rate is a 50% discount from that.4 According to Latent Space, no end date for the introductory discount has been announced.5 The model is still limited to trusted cyber defenders, so most developers can't use it at these prices yet. inference67

What has Google's own staff used Argon for, such as saving memory or migrating code to Rust?

Google says its engineers have been using Argon for daily tasks, from everyday debugging to large-scale codebase migrations and algorithm design.1 On memory, Google says a team of Argon agents analyzed fleet-wide profiling telemetry and applied memory optimizations across its data centers, freeing over 300 TiB once rolled out, with estimated total savings of 500 TiB to 1 PiB.2 Google says Argon agents are migrating C/C++ codebases to Rust across the company, scaling from tens of thousands of lines in core libraries like re2 and libgav1 up to more than 800,000 lines for the Fuchsia Zircon kernel.3 Google adds that these large rewrites are undergoing rigorous automated and manual auditing, emulation testing and review before they reach production.4 Google also says internal agent loops built on Argon helped complete the CK conjecture.5

How can Argon produce 1 million output tokens when Vals lists a 262K maximum?

Google says Gemini 4 Argon has an industry-leading 1M-token output limit, up from 64K, while Vals lists a 262K maximum output.123 Latent Space calls Long Decode Continuation experimental and presents it as an industry first that raises output up to 1M tokens.4 So the 1M figure appears to come from stitching output across multiple calls, not from a single response exceeding Vals' 262K cap, though the reports do not say this outright. inference52
