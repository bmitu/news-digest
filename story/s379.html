Gemini 4 Argon cyber vulnerability discoveries

Last scan Sat Oct 3, 21:32 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Oct 1 · 1 new article today · single origin (Google)

Google announced Gemini 4 Argon, a new frontier model, saying it is rolling out first to a set of trusted cyber defenders through its Fairwind Program.12 Google says Argon can autonomously find, validate and patch critical software vulnerabilities, and that it will release the model without cyber guardrails to trusted defenders and its own internal teams.345

Google says Wiz, using Argon through its Scan for Good initiative, uncovered a critical vulnerability exposing sensitive personal information in healthcare software used by hospitals worldwide, a risk Google says previous frontier models had missed.67 On CWE-bench v1, which evaluates vulnerability remediation, Google says Argon ties for first place with a top score of 68%.89 Google also says Argon shows big leaps in vulnerability discovery over 3.8 Flash Cyber, finding exposures across codebases in 20 programming languages on its internal benchmark and outperforming that model on Wiz's internal black-box penetration testing benchmark.101112

Ars Technica's Ryan Whitwam notes that Google claims other frontier models missed the Wiz flaw but did not provide specifics.13

Google describes the release as phased, saying it is engaged in the U.S. government's voluntary pre-release access process and will gather feedback from early testers before wider availability.1415 Whitwam reports that Google gave no specific timeline for general availability, which would start with paid API users and Google AI Ultra subscribers after the cybersecurity tests.1617

The model is still in limited testing, according to Ars Technica.12 Google says Gemini 4 Argon will cost $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens through the API for a limited time.3 Latent Space's roundup says those figures are a 50% introductory discount off standard pricing of $4 and $20 per million input and output tokens, with no end date announced.4 Cached input tokens are discounted 95 percent.56

How is Google stopping Argon from misusing its cyber abilities or going out of bounds, given it ships without cyber guardrails to defenders?

The no-guardrails version is limited to trusted defenders and Google's internal teams. Argon is rolling out only to a small group through the Fairwind Program, and Google says it will keep iterating on guardrails before broader release. inference1234 Google says it is deploying misalignment mitigations that monitor Argon's chain-of-thought and actions and stop execution when necessary, in order to prevent Argon from stepping out of bounds.56 Google says it used a similar monitoring system during training runs, sending alerts to a dedicated incident response team. It says it took care not to feed the findings back into training, so Argon's reasoning would not be shaped to evade the monitoring.78 Google also says internal and external red teams tested these safeguards with manual and automated attacks. It also says it is hardening its sandboxed environments by isolating and sealing them before high-risk training or evaluations begin.91011 Google did not say whether the chain-of-thought monitoring applies specifically to the guardrail-free defender version.

What has Google's own engineering teams done with Argon so far, such as memory savings and code migrations?

Google says a team of Argon agents analyzed fleet-wide profiling telemetry and applied memory optimizations across its data centers, freeing up over 300 TiB of memory once rolled out.1 Google estimates total savings from this work at 500 TiB to 1 PiB.2 Argon agents are also migrating C/C++ codebases to Rust across Google, from tens of thousands of lines in libraries such as re2 and libgav1 up to more than 800,000 lines in the Fuchsia Zircon kernel.3 In libgav1, Google's open source video decoder, Google says Argon agents replaced 32K lines of SIMD code in an existing Rust port, producing a memory-safe decoder that runs 2.7x faster with identical video output.45 Google also says Argon is helping its quantum computing researchers optimize the spacetime resources of subroutines, and that in one example it beat the published baseline by 40% in a matter of minutes.67 Google says these rewrites are still being audited and tested before they go to production.8

How does Argon's 77.9% DeepSWE score compare with GPT-6 Astra, Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5?

Google says Gemini 4 Argon's 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1 sets a new state of the art on the benchmark, which measures real-world long-horizon software engineering.12 Ars Technica reports that the score is higher than those of GPT-6 Astra, Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5.34 Latent Space's roundup of Google's claimed results puts Opus 5.5 at 74.2% and Astra at 74.1%, so Argon leads them by about 3.7 and 3.8 points. inference5 Fable 5.1's DeepSWE score was not reported.
