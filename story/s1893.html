Black October CVE surge

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Oct 24 · 1 new article this week · single origin, unclear which

Interconnects says the term "Black October" was coined last week after an onslaught of critical vulnerability disclosures, including PHP2Shell and GitLab, which it describes as the moment the cyber risks of AI models began to materialize.1 A Reddit thread summarized by Latent Space describes X/Twitter posts alleging AI agent swarms and a rapid cluster of severe CVEs and incidents across web infrastructure, hosting providers, AI tooling, TLS/PKI and consumer platforms, with purported incidents including PHP, NGINX, GitLab, LiteLLM, HTTP/3, Tor relays and a WordPress supply-chain compromise.23 Latent Space's summary says several of the claims in the Reddit selftext are framed sensationally and would need independent CVE or advisory validation before being treated as a coordinated internet-wide failure.4

One viral X post claimed that 3% of all recorded critical CVEs were found in the last month, with an embedded CVE Tracker chart showing a sharp rise near 2026.56 A commenter noted that CVEs have only been consistently scored as critical since around 1999-2000, so the baseline is roughly 26 years, and that a 3-month span is already about 1% of the scoring era, making 3% in a month notable but dependent on how the baseline is defined.78 Commenters were skeptical of the scale and wording, asking whether October is meaningfully worse than baseline levels of breaches, ransomware and CVE disclosures, and one argued any exceptional-crisis claim needs comparison against historical baselines such as total and exploited-in-the-wild CVEs.910

Some commenters raised a concern about "AI-speed vulnerability discovery and human-speed remediation," while others were optimistic that AI-assisted discovery and fixing may expose long-existing security debt faster than attackers can exploit it.1112

Interconnects' author says the best guess is that behind-the-scenes work has accelerated and more people and resources are being brought in to patch the internet, citing a Wall Street Journal report that cyber first responders are strained.1314 The author says the chaos has so far been confined to those running large-scale digital products, and that it is a fine line between tech companies needing to work harder and public infrastructure actually going down.1516 The author argues the events are on an exponential trend that predates ChatGPT, citing cybersecurity commentators such as Joshua Saxe and Chris Hughes, and says there is very low evidence that open models are to blame, with closed models playing a heavier part in recent hacks.1718

The Cognitive Revolution podcast also covered "Black October security" as a chapter in an episode alongside discussions of recursive self-improvement and the GPU credit crunch.19
