METR publishes updated time horizon report

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 09:22 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 20 · 2 independent origins: METR, MIT Technology Review

METR, an AI evaluation nonprofit, published an updated version of its time horizon report, which MIT Technology Review says finds some AI capabilities still improving exponentially but models weaker on judgment-heavy tasks than on coding.1 The update adds tasks that humans are estimated to need far longer to finish than those in the original suite, as well as more open-ended ones.2 The original plot showed the length of tasks frontier models could complete doubling every four months.3

A Latent Space summary of the update, citing scaling01, reports a doubling time of 3.6 months, with GPT-6 Astra at 40.3 hours and Opus 5.5 at 39.0 hours, and a 95% confidence interval spanning roughly 11.9 hours to 110 days.4 Latent Space adds that the time spans models can handle are far greater for tasks whose results are simple to check than for those that are difficult to check.5

On the new judgment-laden tasks, models fell short: none of the AI-designed evaluations was good enough for METR staff to consider useful for their research, and models also trailed humans at scoring philosophical arguments.67 There are signs of improvement, though: newer models such as Opus 5.5 tend to reach "borderline above the bar" on evaluation design, whereas a year ago every model scored "clearly below the bar."89 The report also has methodological limits: completion times for the new tasks are mostly estimates because paying people for very long or expert-only tasks is prohibitively expensive, and one person rated most of the philosophical arguments.1011

METR staff member Matt Putz says he does not expect trends for hard-to-verify tasks to become as clean and robust as for easy-to-specify ones.12 Sayash Kapoor welcomes the effort to broaden the range of evaluations. In his view, automating AI R&D will run into a major obstacle in the form of research taste. Other people argue that models trained on tasks that are simple to check will in time be able to handle open-ended ones too.1314 Najoung Kim says she is not sure yet how to think about how the observed improvement would extrapolate.15
