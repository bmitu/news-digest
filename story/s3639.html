Huang to testify before Congress

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
3 sources · 2 independent origins: BBC, NBC News

TechCrunch, drawing on reports from Bloomberg and NBC News, says Brian Mast, who chairs the House Foreign Affairs Committee, announced Thursday that Jensen Huang, formerly the chief executive of Nvidia, has consented to appear and give testimony on Capitol Hill.1 The agreement came after senior lawmakers threatened to subpoena him, and Huang is expected to appear before Thanksgiving.23 The testimony will reportedly be behind closed doors, though Mast said he expects it to be recorded on video and transcribed.4

Representatives Mast, John Moolenaar and Rick Crawford had written to Huang on November 4 asking him to appear before their committees about his relationship with Alice Huang, a Megaspeed executive and suspected Chinese spy.56 Moolenaar said Huang had until the end of the day Wednesday to respond or would likely be subpoenaed, and Mast said his committee was preparing a subpoena.78

Huang resigned as Nvidia's president, CEO and a director after reports of affairs with Alice Huang and with Nvidia executive Kimberly Powell, and Colette Kress is interim CEO.91011 Beijing rejects the spying allegations as a smear, while Nvidia's board has said it had no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised.1213

Mast said he and Huang have been at odds over export controls and that Huang has "very serious questions to answer," while saying he does not want to see Nvidia hurt.1415 Moolenaar said the goal is to get Huang in as soon as possible to understand the facts, and Mast said the resignation "does change the dynamic" of the testimony.1617
