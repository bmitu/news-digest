OpenAI and NATO cyber partnership

Last scan Sat Nov 21, 06:54 UTC
2 sources · single origin (OpenAI)

OpenAI says it works with NATO's Communications and Information Agency (NCIA) to give NATO's core institutions access to advanced cybersecurity models to help protect Allied networks.1 OpenAI states that, in the course of this effort, NATO has processed as many as 300 billion raw input and output tokens in a single month.2 Dr Dylan Browne, NCIA General Manager, calls AI-powered cyber defence models a "generational leap" and says that as adoption scales NATO will learn more about token consumption and costs, which will help it evaluate competing solutions and make procurement decisions.34 Commentator Zvi Mowshowitz notes the announcement as a cybersecurity partnership with NATO and says it revealed that NATO was already using tokens equal to 8 Claude Ultra subscriptions.5

OpenAI further claims it backed Crossed Swords 2026, an exercise run by the NATO Cooperative Cyber Defence Centre of Excellence. Participants there created, deployed and assessed AI agents for cyber operations with OpenAI Daybreak, and the exercise stressed keeping humans in charge of oversight.678 Sasha Baker, OpenAI's Head of National Security Policy, says Daybreak puts more capable tools in defenders' hands to find and fix vulnerabilities before attackers can exploit them.9

OpenAI says it has also given cyber-model access to defenders including the EU's ENISA and national cyber agencies in France, Germany and Poland, and that Ukraine's Ministry of Digital Transformation is using Daybreak to strengthen civilian infrastructure defence.1011 OpenAI reports that ENISA used its models to identify four vulnerabilities with fixes published, and that a joint ENISA and CERT-EU report found 95% of 88 potential issues assessed by maintainers across tested models were genuine.1213
