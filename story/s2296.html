Character.ai bankruptcy

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
2 sources · single origin, unclear which

Character.ai filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in Delaware and says it will keep operating while a court-supervised process sells the business, with a close expected in late November or early December 2026.1 TechCrunch reports the company cited legal, regulatory, and operational headwinds, filing just five years after launching, after families sued alleging the chatbots contributed to teen suicides and self-harm, and after Disney sent a cease-and-desist letter over unauthorized use of its characters.23 The company barred under-18 users from open-ended chats late last year and agreed, along with Google, to settle several of the suits in early 2026, but TechCrunch says that was not enough.4

Character.ai was founded in 2021 by Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas, raised $150 million at a $1 billion valuation in 2023, and Google reportedly paid $2.7 billion in 2024 to license its technology and bring its founders back.567 Pennsylvania claims the company had more than 20 million monthly active users, and the Kentucky complaint counts more than 2.1 million U.S. accounts with a declared age under 18 out of 21.6 million as of March 2025.89

Disney is the opening (stalking horse) bidder, offering $20 million for a license to some of its intellectual property, which Techdirt describes as non-exclusive and perpetual.1011 According to a notice filed by forty U.S. state attorneys general, the Disney license excludes the app, the user data and the chat content, and the company's lawyer told the court that no buyer has yet made an offer for the user data or the chatbot business.1213

The attorneys general appeared before Judge Brendan Shannon on October 14 to ask that any buyer protect the chat histories, and they reserved the right to seek a consumer privacy ombudsman, as in the 23andMe case.1415 New York Attorney General Letitia James said the company had collected "some of the most private thoughts children have ever shared."16 Techdirt argues that the privacy policy allows chat transcripts, voice recordings and images to be disclosed in a sale without requiring a buyer to follow the original terms, and urges bankruptcy courts to appoint a privacy ombudsman before any bid for the chat logs is considered.1718
