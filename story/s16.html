OpenAI said earlier this month that its own team's agents had cracked a million-dollar problem in mathematics, according to MIT Technology Review's newsletter.1

Latent Space host Swyx describes the effort as semi-public in cost, at 10,000 agents running for 88 hours. The episode's summary line adds 130B output tokens and a roughly $40M-equivalent cost.23

MIT Technology Review says the piece did not argue that OpenAI's solution was wrong, only that the result may not be the one mathematicians care most about.45 A mathematician has also accused the models of possibly using some of his work without credit. How does the roughly $40M cost of OpenAI's math effort compare with what a human-led approach would need, and could expert input have made it cheaper?

Alex Zhang says OpenAI's swarm approach reached a point where you can throw $40 million at a problem and solve an unsolved one.1 Zhang argues expert input could have made it cheaper: someone who knows the problem might uncover something that would erase a trillion-token spend.2 He adds that compute can't always be thrown at problems and that efficiency still matters, though he says the trends are not clear.34 Zhang's general point about experts applies to the math effort only by inference, since he does not quantify the saving for that specific problem. inference51 A human-led approach's cost compared with the $40M was not reported.
