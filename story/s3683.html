Fractile Series C

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
2 sources · single origin (Fractile)

British AI chip startup Fractile has raised a $720 million Series C round, with Britain's Sovereign AI Fund among the backers.12 The round was led by Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed Venture Partners, and Redpoint, with Dragoneer, DST Global, BlackRock, T. Rowe Price and CapitalG joining as new investors alongside returning backers Accel, Conviction and Felicis, according to Data Center Dynamics.34 The UK government's Sovereign AI fund and the British Business Bank also took part, but the size of their investments was not disclosed.5

The British government put the valuation at "over £5 billion", and TechCrunch says that is quite possibly $7 billion, a figure it attributes to the Financial Times and a Reuters source.6 Data Center Dynamics, by contrast, states flatly that the round values the company at $7 billion.7

TechCrunch says Fractile pursues a full-stack approach, including a machine learning compiler, and that its hardware now centers on DRAM stacked on each chip, which it hopes will bypass the usual trade-off between memory and capacity.89 Data Center Dynamics instead describes the chips as using in-memory compute, which Fractile founder Walter Goodwin said in 2024 aims to cut power consumption and improve performance.1011

In a LinkedIn post, Goodwin said there is still a lot of work ahead, that Fractile has already extracted 20x higher speeds from DRAM, and that the funding will let it ship its first chips and systems next year.1213 TechCrunch places Fractile alongside Axelera, Etched and OLIX as one of the top-funded startups in the race for purpose-built AI chips.14
