Protein watermarking research (SynthID Bio)

Last scan Sun Oct 4, 17:18 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Oct 1 · 2 independent origins: Ars Technica, Google DeepMind

Google DeepMind has introduced SynthID Bio, a proof-of-concept system that embeds an imperceptible watermark in AI-generated proteins while, it says, preserving biological function in laboratory testing.12 Ars Technica reports that DeepMind published the research paper on a Wednesday, and Demis Hassabis said it was published in Nature with open-sourced tools. inference34 In wet-lab tests on three targets (VEGF-A, the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein RBD and PD-L1), DeepMind says its watermarked binders matched unwatermarked versions in hit rate, binding affinity and natural sequence diversity.56

According to Ars Technica, the sequence method works by having a keyed SynthID variant suggest amino acids during ProteinMPNN design, with ProteinMPNN rejecting suggestions that would not give a functional protein.78 Detection is statistical, requiring a scan of the whole sequence with the key, so Ars Technica notes the cutoff strongly affects false positives and false negatives.910 For protein folding, DeepMind says it fine-tuned part of AlphaFold 3's diffusion network and that this preserves prediction accuracy while offering near-perfect detectability.11

The main intended use is biosecurity: DeepMind says novel AI designs can bypass traditional DNA synthesis screening, and Ars Technica says synthesizers given keys from trusted organizations could focus scrutiny on designs that lack a trusted source. inference1213 DeepMind itself says that making the watermark more robust against deliberate tampering remains a key challenge, and that no single biosecurity intervention is a silver bullet.141516

Sarah Carter, a biosecurity policy expert who reviewed the work, called it an important piece of the puzzle for tracking provenance, and James Diggans of Twist Bioscience called watermarking a promising addition to the biosecurity toolbox.171819

Is Google planning to watermark whole genomes, and has it tested that on anything alive yet?

With the Hie lab at Stanford and Arc Institute, Google says it built SynthID Bio into the genomic model Evo 2 to watermark the genome of an Evo 2-designed bacteriophage.12 Google says early lab testing in bacterial cultures confirmed that the watermarked bacteriophages, which are viruses that infect bacteria, are functional.34 The published protein work is separate: Google's wet-lab tests on three target proteins found watermarked binder designs matched unwatermarked ones in hit rate, binding affinity and sequence diversity.56 Google has not yet published the details and says a technical manuscript will follow. It also says it believes the work could help address some biosecurity risks of genome design.7

What could go wrong with the keys, short proteins or padding, and which design tools can't use the watermark yet?

The team behind Google's protein watermark says the whole system is only as secure as the system used to distribute and maintain the watermarking keys.1 Very short proteins may carry too few watermark amino acids to be identified.2 Padding is another weak spot: fusing the AI-designed protein with something lacking the watermark, such as a natural fluorescent protein, might dilute it.3 Not every design tool can use the watermark yet. Many AI protein design packages don't rely on ProteinMPNN, and only some use a similar one-amino-acid-at-a-time approach that should work with SynthID Bio. Until Google handles other forms of integration, not everyone will be able to watermark their designs.456 Detection is statistical, so where the cutoff is set makes a big difference to false positives and false negatives. Google DeepMind also lists making the watermark more robust against deliberate tampering as a key remaining challenge.789 The reporting does not name specific design tools that are still unsupported.

How does the watermarked AlphaFold 3 hold up against noise or small changes to the predicted 3D coordinates?

Google DeepMind says the watermark in its SynthID Bio version of AlphaFold 3 holds up against digital noise and minor changes to the predicted 3D coordinates.1 DeepMind says the watermark is built into the model's weights, so the predicted coordinates carry a detectable signature regardless of who runs the model.234 However, DeepMind lists making the watermark more robust against deliberate tampering as a key challenge going forward.5 The reported results come from DeepMind's own announcement, and no numbers were reported for how much noise or coordinate change the watermark can withstand.

Why was it unclear that SynthID could work on proteins at all, when it works on text and images?

SynthID normally works by biasing the choices an AI makes so the signal spreads through text or images, which is why proteins, with little room and strict function, were an open question. inference12 Proteins are small compared with images: images often contain millions of pixels, while a 500-amino-acid protein is fairly large, which leaves a lot less raw material to hide any signal in.3 That left two possible failures: the watermark might be too faint to detect in a typical protein, or stuffing in enough signal to detect might leave the protein inactive.45
