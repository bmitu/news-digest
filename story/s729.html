Common Sense Media rates ChatGPT for Teens

Last scan Sat Oct 10, 12:51 UTC
2 sources · 2 independent origins: Common Sense Media, OpenAI

Common Sense Media, a nonprofit that reviews media and tech for families, rated OpenAI's ChatGPT for Teens an "unacceptable risk," saying its teen protections "fall short of what the company promised."123 Its assessment found that ChatGPT "doesn't send alerts to parents when it should," doesn't offer "the right help in crisis situations," and "still does kids' homework."4 Tom Siegel, executive director of Common Sense Media's Youth AI Safety Institute, said a teen could spend an hour talking about self-harm without a parent getting an alert, and that ChatGPT should be for adults only until OpenAI fixes this and proves it with independent testing.5

The report said ChatGPT for Teens' engagement cues were "pervasive even in crisis situations," and that its responses to young users in crisis earned a failing score on three of the five severe harms the group treats as Red Lines.67 The researchers said some protections, such as refusing sexual roleplay, worked, but others failed or even got worse with the launch of ChatGPT for Teens.8 Per TechCrunch's account of the report, the model pointed teens to a trusted adult in 94% of crisis prompts involving risk from another person, but rarely did so when the risk was the teen's relationship with ChatGPT itself.910 Across nearly 2,000 prompts, testers reportedly encountered only two break reminders, both in conversations lasting about 90 minutes.11

OpenAI disputed the assessment, with spokesperson Eric Porterfield saying the testing did not accurately reflect how the safeguards work in practice and that the bulk of testing "may have begun and concluded before activation of parental controls was complete."121314 Siegel replied that the group stands by its methodology, having confirmed with OpenAI that the Teen mode features were fully launched before testing.15 Siegel said OpenAI disclosed after the tests that its systems can take several hours to activate on newly linked accounts, but that some test accounts were linked for significantly longer and still provided no notifications.16

TechCrunch noted that OpenAI did not explain how its methodological concerns affect the report's findings on engagement cues and relational behavior.17 Separately, OpenAI released its own data saying teens spend under 15 minutes a day on ChatGPT on average and fewer than 2% spend more than three consecutive hours.1819
