United Airlines bans AI agents

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
4 sources · 677 points on Hacker News · 3 independent origins: CBS News, The Wall Street Journal, United Airlines

United Airlines announced a ban on AI agents booking flights for travelers, pointing to security concerns and the chance that such bookings might break federal regulations.12 The airline says it is restricting access both directly on its digital platforms and indirectly through travel agencies and aggregators, and is updating its booking, ticketing and distribution policies.345

United warns that sharing account credentials with AI assistants can expose sensitive information and lead to unintended purchases, account changes, or use of MileagePlus miles.67 It also says AI agents could skirt federal requirements, such as that a person attest at check-in that their bags hold no banned substances; Zvi Mowshowitz calls one such explanation silly.8910

According to The Verge, citing the Wall Street Journal, United has not yet seen many people try to use AI agents to book flights, and agents can still show users United flights but cannot complete the purchase.1112 United CEO Scott Kirby reportedly said, "To be honest, I don’t want to create another middleman," and the airline suggests it could reconsider as agents advance and protections are established.1314

Aviation analyst Henry Harteveldt of Atmosphere Research Group says the policy suggests United wants to keep control of booking and avoid the cost of a surge of AI bot flight searches.1516 Harteveldt said United fares should remain bookable through aggregators such as Kayak, Hopper and Google Flights; CBS News separately notes that Southwest has taken a different approach by making its flight inventory viewable to ChatGPT users.1718

Coverage frames the move as part of a wider trend, with eBay and several major banks also reported to have restricted AI agents, and Bank of America urging customers not to share passwords with AI assistants.1920
