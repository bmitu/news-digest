FCC approves SpaceX orbital data center

Last scan Thu Nov 26, 05:11 UTC
2 sources · single origin (FCC)

On Wednesday the FCC said it had given SpaceX permission to launch as many as one million satellites carrying AI chips, as part of a project the company calls "Starmind."1 According to The Verge, the agency has never before approved an entire constellation of AI satellites, though firms like Google and Blue Origin are likewise working on data centers in orbit.23 The approval requires SpaceX to launch at least one satellite by 2033 and reach the 1 million mark by 2040, and The Hill describes it as a 15-year permit.45 However, the FCC deferred SpaceX's request to send satellites above 600km to graveyard orbits instead of letting them burn up, saying the company had not given sufficient information about its disposal plans.67

SpaceX presents Starmind as a means of harvesting sunlight in orbit to run AI systems on the ground; lasers tied into Starlink would carry the data down, and the chips would come from a Terafab plant that is still planned.8910

FCC Chair Brendan Carr said that by moving compute beyond Earth, SpaceX and its competitors are "meeting the moment," calling it American ingenuity that can reinforce economic leadership.1112 Elon Musk replied to Carr's post that it was much appreciated and would be a great asset to America and the world.13 NASA administrator Jared Isaacman voiced support for the concept in August, saying it would have immense benefits.14

The Hill notes the push comes as data centers have lost favor among Americans, with many cities voting against projects or permits, and that SpaceX did not immediately respond to its request for comment.1516
