Meta announces Muse Charm keychain

2 sources · tracked since Sep 27 · 1 new today · 2 independent origins: Ars Technica, Meta

Meta announced Muse Charm, a keychain-sized device for talking to its Muse personal AI agent, at its Connect event, and says it will ship in December.12 The Financial Times, in the Ars Technica piece, describes the Charm as a pendant-like device activated by a fingerprint sensor, with a screen showing the Muse avatar and real-time voice interaction.34 Mark Zuckerberg said the Charm would be "by far the fastest way to talk to your Muse and to show what's going on around you" for people not wearing glasses.5

The Charm was one part of a wider Connect lineup that included Muse voice and real-time video, a Muse Realtime Avatar, Muse on Meta glasses, and new VR glasses. inference678 Latent Space's recap says Meta teased, but did not release, a new frontier model, quoting Alexandr Wang saying "pretty soon we are dropping the most capable model we have ever trained."9

According to the Financial Times, Muse has been the most downloaded app on both the Apple and Android app stores in the US since its launch two weeks earlier, and Meta's stock has added $300 billion in market capitalisation since the September 8 launch.1011 Zuckerberg said Muse would "make users money," so Meta would offer a free tier and expects to profit over time by taking a small fee from transactions.121314

The Financial Times says the strategy's success will depend on whether the features can withstand privacy and security issues, noting that Meta has already fixed a vulnerability that could let a hacker take control of another person's Muse agent and that Amazon has blocked Meta's agents from its shopping platform.151617 Zuckerberg insisted Muse has "state-of-the-art privacy and security," while critics say Meta's camera glasses are used to record others without their knowledge. inference1819

Which retailers and apps can Muse connect to, and how many integrations does it have?

Muse's connector platform has more than 1,500 applications, including Lovable and ElevenLabs.1 On the retail and commerce side, the named integrations are Walmart, Best Buy, Gap, Sephora and Instacart, along with others.2 Productivity integrations include Box, GitHub, Granola and Notion, and partner graphics were also shown for Spotify and an apparent Temu integration.34 Meta's small-business version of Muse adds integrations including Shopify, Dropbox and Slack, plus Asana, Box, Canva, Figma, Granola, HighLevel, Intuit QuickBooks, Klaviyo, Lovable, Notion, Stripe and Zoom. It can also link to Instagram professional account analytics, Facebook pages and Meta ad accounts.567 Amazon has blocked Meta's agents from accessing its shopping platform, according to the Financial Times, as part of efforts to prevent third-party bots from using its systems.89

How does Meta's new Muse Realtime Avatar compare with rival avatar products like Runway Characters and HeyGen LiveAvatar?

Meta says its Muse Realtime Avatar beat both Runway Characters and HeyGen LiveAvatar in head-to-head tests.1 The test was run by Meta itself. Raters held 2–3 minute conversations using each product's own live-call experience, with matched avatar identities, and judged visual quality, audio-visual sync, character consistency and mannerisms.23 The result is thin: Meta reports Muse "came out ahead on overall preference" but posted no margins or rater counts, and the source advises treating the evaluation with caution.45 On specs, Muse's avatar answers in under a second, supports unbounded session length and watermarks all output as AI, and another report puts its target latency at about 870ms.678 No comparable latency, session-length or watermarking figures for Runway Characters or HeyGen LiveAvatar were reported, so a spec-by-spec comparison isn't possible.

Why is Meta pushing Muse onto glasses, a keychain and other hardware instead of leaving it as a phone app?

Zuckerberg calls Muse the "centerpiece" of his AI vision, and he has bet that smart glasses and other portable devices will become one of the main ways consumers interact with AI.12 One commentator, @kimmonismus, summarized Meta's thesis as "personal Superintelligence coming soon," which means people need hardware to interact with it, so Meta is going all-in on AI glasses.3 For the Muse Charm, Zuckerberg pitched speed and convenience: he says that for people not wearing glasses it will be "by far the fastest way to talk to your Muse and to show what’s going on around you."4 Glasses also let users speak to Muse by name, since Muse is coming to all Meta glasses with a wake word.5 Meta's own reasons beyond these were not spelled out, but the timing follows Muse's app success, which lifted the stock and comes as Meta plans to eventually take a small fee from transactions. inference67
