Eastern District of Kentucky denies dismissal in AI facial recognition arrest suit

Last scan Thu Nov 26, 05:11 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 14 · single origin, unclear which

A federal judge in the Eastern District of Kentucky, S. Chad Meredith, denied a Florence police detective's motion to dismiss a lawsuit over an arrest that allegedly relied solely on AI facial recognition.12 Techdirt says the court held that the Fourth Amendment does not let a detective delegate the probable cause assessment to AI, and that no reasonable detective could conclude otherwise.345

Alain Osorio, who lived in Nebraska, was arrested and extradited to Kentucky after Detective Ryan Kilmer ran a screenshot from bank CCTV footage through the police department's facial recognition program, according to Techdirt.678 He was released on bond the day after his January 24, 2025 arrest and indicted in March 2025, and the charges were later dismissed after he showed he was working in Nebraska when the check was passed.91011 Osorio sued the detective for false arrest and imprisonment, malicious prosecution, and assault and battery, and the detective moved to dismiss, arguing among other things that qualified immunity shielded him.1213

According to Above the Law's account, Meredith found that, at this stage, the allegations that the detective delegated probable cause entirely to AI without independent judgment and made misstatements or omissions in his affidavit were enough to deny dismissal.1415 The court reportedly noted that Osorio's booking photo looks nothing like the suspect in the footage, and that Kilmer's affidavit claimed he used "investigative tools" though he used only one, contrary to Florence police policy that facial recognition alone does not establish probable cause.161718

Techdirt says the detective isn't shielded by qualified immunity, which means the ruling is sure to be appealed, and it describes a settlement as the alternative to making this an appellate case.192021 Commentators praised the ruling: Above the Law's author called it the right, studied way for courts to treat AI and contrasted it with a Texas judge's reaction to lawyers' AI use, while Techdirt's author said it could have implications for predictive policing and AI-written police reports. inference222324
