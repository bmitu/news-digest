Muse privacy incidents surface

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 14:03 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 17 · 2 new articles this week · 2 independent origins: Ars Technica, Meta

Claroc says it found more than 6,800 Muse links through public DNS records.1 Claroc says Muse builds each share's web address from the conversation's title plus a random ID, so a hostname pulled from DNS rebuilds the link.23 The researchers wrote that gathering the links "took one query."4

Meta spokesperson Tracy Clayton said people can share Muse artifacts, creating a page accessible to those with the link, but that the company paused the functionality while it reviews the report.567 Meta later re-enabled sharing, with David Singleton saying on X that the underlying issue was fixed.89 Singleton said the problem lay with URLs for shared artifacts rather than chat sharing, and that previously shared artifacts now have new URLs, with old links showing a note.101112

Ars Technica argues the ease of finding the links further justifies asking whether Muse is worthy of the access it requires, and notes an earlier vulnerability found by Patrick Wardle that Meta downplayed.1314 The Verge notes that earlier chats from ChatGPT, Claude and Grok have also ended up on Google Search.15
