DeepSeek V4.1 Flash release and evaluations

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
3 sources · 4 independent origins: Artificial Analysis, DeepSeek, Fangcun Leap, vLLM

DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash 1017 model has been released and documented on DeepSeek's API docs, and it drew independent evaluations and wide commentary. inference12 Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index v5 puts DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (max) and GLM-5.3 (max) at 32 among released-weights models, 26 points behind the leader and below Claude Haiku 5.5 (max) at 36.34 A separate post said Artificial Analysis shows the model at about 28%, a figure scaling01 gave as a correction to an earlier identification.5 Other reported results include 77% on DeepSWE v1.1 and 46% on FrontierMath Tier 4, tied with Haiku 5.5.6

Fangcun AI, which describes itself as China's first lab dedicated to independent third-party frontier AI safety evaluation, published a 34-evaluation report on the model and, per Zvi Mowshowitz's summary, found a clear improvement over previous DeepSeek models.789 A separate independent assessment reported that the model classified 32 of 69 real user conversations as evaluations, with a 46.4% false-positive rate on EvalDetectBench, and its authors argue public benchmark results alone may not predict deployment behavior.1011

Reddit commenters questioned the Artificial Analysis v5 update, arguing that benchmark definitions tend to shift when open-weight models approach closed-frontier performance, though the thread offered no concrete evidence.1213 Some users also disputed Artificial Analysis's cost-per-task estimates, claiming DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and GPT Sol deliver similar practical output while Sol costs at least five times as much, not the roughly 2x the chart implies.14

The model has also been adopted for downstream uses: vLLM reported $38.96 revenue per GPU-hour serving it at DeepSeek off-peak prices, and a particle-1 model is built on it.1516 Security concerns surfaced as well: Abliteration offers a version with guardrails removed, and Andrew Curran says many recent lone-actor incidents used ARTEX with DeepSeek 4.1 Flash as the primary model, including South Korean bank attacks documented by CrowdStrike.1718
