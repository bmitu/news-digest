2026 midterms and data center ballot measures

Last scan Thu Nov 26, 05:11 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Nov 6 · single origin (MultiState)

Semafor reports that after the 2026 midterms, more than 40 ballot measures seeking limits on data center growth had passed across the country.12 Fortune's op-ed counts 41 data-center ballot measures, of which 40 went against the industry.3

Semafor says new governors from Texas to Ohio have pledged to stop data center construction.4 Texas voters elected Democrat Gina Hinojosa governor, ending a 36-year drought for her party in that office. She has called for pausing any new data center construction until the legislature puts a regulatory system in place.56 Under her proposals, the industry would also have to hire union workers, safeguard water resources, keep other customers from facing sudden jumps in their bills, and lose its tax breaks.7

According to Timothy B. Lee, Democrats took control of the House and the Senate, and they are expected to make regulating AI one of their top priorities when the new session begins in January.89 A Semafor commentator, however, doubts Democrats will manage to pass significant AI regulation in the new Congress, while state governors and lawmakers are ready to act.1011

Alphabet's Ruth Porat acknowledged that AI companies have come up short in convincing voters the data center spending is worth it, though she argued most Americans are excited by the job opportunities.1213 Semafor's commentator says many in Silicon Valley treat the anxiety as a marketing, education or location problem rather than a reason to stop.14

Interpretations of the vote differ: Irene Liu of the Future of Life Institute podcast says voters channeled broader anxiety about AI, jobs and safety into data center measures because that is something they can control at the ballot box.1516 A Fortune op-ed author goes further, arguing the vote shows Americans want a pause on AI development itself, a view that is the author's opinion and not the publication's.1718
