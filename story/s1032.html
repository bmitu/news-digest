Humanity's Sixth Sense benchmark

Last scan Mon Oct 19, 23:45 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 17 · single origin (Elorian and Scale AI)

Elorian and Scale AI have built and released Humanity's Sixth Sense (HSS), a benchmark that they say measures how a model interprets and reasons through information a visual scene implies but does not explicitly show.1 HSS contains 522 questions covering 288 images and 234 short video clips totalling 17.6 hours, with example tasks such as asking whether a bookcase can fit more books.23

Humans score an aggregate pass@1 of 93.1, while the best AI system in Import AI's account, GPT 6 Astra, scores 53.6. inference45 Import AI reports that Chinese open weight models did poorly, with GLM-5.3-flash at 26.8 and Kimi K3 at 25.5.6 The benchmark's authors say the two most common mistakes were missing a detail that decides the answer (21% of failures) and misidentifying an object, person, or role (20%).7

Latent Space's roundup differs on the top spot: it says Scale's benchmark puts Argon first at 55.4%, ahead of Astra at 53.6%.8

Import AI's author argues that the fact LLMs like GPT and Claude do as well as they do on HSS is something of a counter to the claim that true visual reasoning will rely on developing spatially aware 2D and 3D world models.910
