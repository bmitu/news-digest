SpireClimb benchmark

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 14:03 UTC
2 sources · single origin (Epoch AI)

Epoch AI says its new SpireClimb benchmark, which tests AI models on the deck-building game Slay the Spire, shows that in six months the best models went from struggling to beat the game to winning over 90% of runs at base difficulty.12 Epoch says SpireClimb requires computer interactions, so a model that does well may be better positioned to take on computer-based work.3

At the original game's hardest difficulty, Epoch reports that GPT-6.1 Sol wins 20% of runs and Astra wins 17%, while the other models tested win almost none.4 Epoch estimates top human players win about 85% of runs at the original's hardest difficulty and about 95% in the sequel.5

To check whether prior knowledge of the nearly decade-old game inflated scores, researchers also tested Slay the Spire 2, where Astra wins 35% of runs at the hardest difficulty, though Epoch notes that setting is easier.6 Epoch concludes that, because models know less about the newer game and the top models still did reasonably well, their scores on the original did not come solely from prior knowledge.7

Commenting on the results, Zvi Mowshowitz says the win rate is higher than he expected to see so soon and that Sol and Astra are doing much better than the average player, even confined to those who unlocked max difficulty and choose to play it.8 Thomas Friedel asked whether OpenAI had an RL environment to train Astra specifically for Slay the Spire, and Benjamin Ou, who says he created the benchmark, answered that this is probably the case.910 Ou says the labs like the game so all signs point to RL, but adds that there is also some generalization to other strategy card games; Mowshowitz says the working theory is a mix of both.111213
