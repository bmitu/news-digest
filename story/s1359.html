Disney named lead bidder for Character.AI assets

Last scan Wed Oct 21, 20:11 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Oct 17 · 1 new article this week · single origin (Wired)

Disney has been named the lead ("stalking horse") bidder for Character.AI assets after the chatbot startup filed for chapter 11 bankruptcy, so another company could still come forward with a higher offer. inference12 Through the bankruptcy, court filings show Disney hopes to secure a perpetual, worldwide, nonexclusive license to a wide range of Character.AI's code, documentation, frameworks and tools, including its trust and safety moderation stack, training infrastructure and age-prediction technology, according to Ars Technica.3

In a first transaction in September, Disney paid the startup $5.5 million plus a refundable $2 million "good faith deposit", and it would pay an additional $12.5 million at closing if it is chosen as winning bidder and courts approve.45 If Disney is not selected, it will be refunded the $2 million deposit and receive a breakup fee.6

A legal filing spotted by Bloomberg says Disney will make employment offers to at least 60 Character.AI employees, the majority of the workforce, at an estimated $60 million in annual compensation, and TechCrunch reports the "vast majority" of those offered jobs accepted.78 Former Character.AI CEO Karandeep Anand has become Disney's first CTO.910 To preserve a competitive process, Disney agreed to let the winning bidder interview and potentially make offers to up to 80 percent of certain newly hired employees, though Ars Technica says experts worry that moving staff between companies within months is unrealistic.1112

The states that filed a notice of interest have reserved their rights to challenge any bankruptcy plan, potentially setting up a fight over the outcome; Disney declined to comment.1314
