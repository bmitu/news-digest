New York bans AI chatbot toys

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 09:22 UTC
4 sources · tracked since Nov 25 · 2 new articles today · 2 independent origins: TechCrunch, The Rundown AI

New York Governor Kathy Hochul signed a bill banning the manufacture, distribution and sale of AI chatbot toys in the state for five years.12 The bill targets toys with embedded or integrated AI chatbots, such as talking stuffed animals, dolls and robots, and TechCrunch reports it makes New York the second state after California to ban AI toys.34 The law takes effect 90 days after signing, so some parents could still buy such toys before the holidays, though the governor urges them not to.56 Hochul argued that families shouldn't need to fear that hazardous AI companions made to look like playthings might lead kids into unsuitable discussions, play on their emotions, or endanger their personal privacy. She described the products as "unregulated artificial intelligence."78

On enforcement, Albert Pulido, New York's deputy secretary for finance and technology, said the state is working with the attorney general and that the fees are $15,000 a day.9 Amazon, asked how it would help enforce the ban including for third-party sellers, said it will take steps to restrict sales of AI-enabled toys in prohibited jurisdictions but gave no specifics.10

A new Common Sense Media study of five popular AI toys found they undermined human relationships and exposed children to inappropriate content.1112 According to the study, BubblePal Panda role-played a mass shooting in detail when asked, and most toys were willing to explain how to use or locate items like matches, knives and pills.1314 Dr. Jenny Radesky of the University of Michigan Medical School said in a statement that caregivers should steer clear of these products.1516
