GTA VI launches

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 17:01 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 25 · 2 independent origins: Fortune, TechCrunch

Rockstar Games' Grand Theft Auto VI came out last week, according to TechCrunch, which reports that it took in $3.79 billion on its first day and could be the largest entertainment event to date.12 TechCrunch also says the title, which arrived after a 13-year wait and two delays, debuted with Metacritic's best rating ever for a game. inference345 When setting TechCrunch's release-day figure of $3.79 billion beside earlier expectations, note that brokerage Oppenheimer's projection, cited via Reuters, was lower and covered a longer span: roughly $1.85 billion gross over three days. inference67

As for cost, Take-Two Interactive, Rockstar's parent company, has not disclosed what the game cost, Fortune reports, though estimates place the development budget between $1 billion and $1.5 billion. inference89 Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick said in February that generative AI played no part in Rockstar's work on the game, whose worlds he described as "handcrafted," and TechCrunch reports that about 5,000 people spent more than a decade on it. inference101112

Ben Feder, a former Take-Two chief who is now managing partner of Tirta Ventures, a firm investing in AI-native game projects, believes GTA is likely the final game of its sort to be built this way, although he expects the franchise itself to endure and probably grow stronger. inference131415 Feder also argues that AI could let creators who would otherwise make films produce games and that $2 billion budgets will become harder to justify, while expecting only a small share of a flood of new titles to break through. inference161718

Views in the tech world diverge: Zelnick, speaking around the launch of Google's Project Genie earlier this year, called the idea of push-button hit games "laughable," whereas Elon Musk wrote that AI will work out which game a person would like best, and TechCrunch says Musk even suggested AI could make a game like GTA VI. inference192021

TechCrunch argues that the game's appeal owes much to the inefficiency of its creation, which left developers room to experiment and rework ideas, in contrast with the AI industry's focus on efficiency during the same week Anthropic went public. inference222324
