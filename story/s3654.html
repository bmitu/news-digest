AlphaGenome 2

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
3 sources · 2 independent origins: Google DeepMind, The Verge

Google DeepMind has unveiled AlphaGenome 2, the follow-up to its earlier AlphaGenome model; the system forecasts the way DNA sequences, and changes to them, affect particular types of human cells.12 DeepMind describes AlphaGenome 2 as a conditional model, meaning it forecasts what DNA does within a single condition at once, where a condition can be a tissue, a cell type, or a cellular state.3

According to DeepMind, the model learned from over 100 million single-cell profiles spanning 68 tissues, and it detects close to double the number of variants that alter expression compared with the prior best model.456 A Latent Space roundup cites numbers from the TenK10K dataset: the new model pinpoints 30% of causal variants against 16% for the original, and captures 43% of directional gene effects against 23%.7

The Verge reports that AlphaGenome 2 could make predictions about new types of cells and biological states without being retrained, and DeepMind says it can predict unmeasured properties to help researchers prioritize experiments.8910

Žiga Avsec, DeepMind's genomics lead, told The Verge that specificity matters for drug development because drugs target specific proteins and specific cell types, and that the model's more detailed predictions are "more expensive" to produce than its predecessor's.1112 That cost would have made it impractical for the genome-wide Atlas DeepMind released in September, according to The Verge's account of Avsec's comments.13

AlphaGenome 2 is available for non-commercial research through the AlphaGenome API, with a limited private preview for commercial research, and DeepMind says AlphaGenome 2 Skills will soon come to Google Antigravity.1415 A partner quoted in DeepMind's post, apparently from a platform called Mystra, called it "an impressive advance in the field"; the Verge piece carries no outside criticism. inference16
