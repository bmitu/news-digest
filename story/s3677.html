IGF jury against generative AI

Last scan Sat Nov 14, 15:53 UTC
2 sources · single origin (IGF jurors)

A group of Independent Games Festival (IGF) jurors published a signed statement saying they would not vote for any game that uses generative AI in any capacity, and Ars Technica reports 19 of the 50-plus jurors signed it.12 The jurors cited a significant increase in submitted games made with generative AI tools, listed ecological, economic, psychological and labor harms, and urged the IGF to adopt a strong anti-AI policy for future years.34 The jurors say the statement does not speak for the IGF as an organization or for all jury members, and some reportedly worried about signing without professional blowback.5

Big Walk, from Untitled Goose Game developer House House, was involved in the debate, with House House saying LLMs were used sparingly near the end of development to help work through difficult technical challenges and bugs.67 House House estimates that well under 0.1% of the game's code involved this usage, which it says came after a Unity engineer told it a fix for a shadow bug wasn't possible, and the chatbot's suggestion was modified extensively into about 10 lines of final code.8910 House House said it disclosed the AI use in its IGF submission but not on Steam, because Valve's platform does not request disclosure of code or chatbot usage; it also called the lack of an AI policy a failure and apologized that its usage may not meet critics' standards.111213

Ars Technica reports that Big Walk's Steam page received over 400 negative reviews, though Steam's anti-review-bomb tools appear to be limiting the effect on its overall Very Positive rating.14 Sam Barlow, a signing juror, said Big Walk was not the impetus for the discussion but became a tricky edge case, and that the statement was not a call to boycott a game or hurt a team economically.1516

Views differ on the matter. Liz Ryerson, who judges for the IGF, posted online that House House's use of AI wasn't something she personally considered grounds for disqualification. Brendan Keogh, meanwhile, argued in his own post that taking a hardline stance against all generative AI ends up doing more harm than good at this point.171819 Others disagree: AI researcher Mike Cook wrote that House House's actions are part of the same problem, and Hannah Nicklin called zero tolerance a viable political position.2021
