Wave of tech outages blamed on patching

Last scan Sat Nov 28, 09:22 UTC
2 sources · tracked since Nov 20 · single origin (CNBC)

The Trump administration says a string of recent US tech outages stemmed from organizations rushing to patch and upgrade systems, not from cyberattacks, and several experts say AI-driven discovery of vulnerabilities is behind the rush. inference12 The Hill reports that over about six days the outages hit banks, Microsoft, Apple and government agencies, including an hours-long nationwide FAA ground stop and a Monday outage of the NSA's classified network.34 A White House official said its assessment found the outages resulted from upgrades, patching and hardware being applied, with "no indication" of a cyber attack.5

The Hill reports that cybersecurity experts found no evidence of coordinated malicious activity, and Randy Rose of the Center for Internet Security described a compounding effect of AI-assisted vulnerability discovery and simultaneous modernization.67 Nick Carroll of Nightwing said the timing involved "unfortunate coincidences" and that the window for testing emergency patches is starting to dry up, while Josh Bressers of VulnCheck wrote that fixing vulnerabilities is still hard and a completely different story from finding them.8910

However, the explanation is not uniform: the FAA cited a router in Arkansas needing an upgrade and a Texas radar facility that caused a data flood, and The Atlantic says the ground stop does not appear to have been caused by AI-related patching. inference1112 Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told a congressional committee the timing "can raise questions" but that the outages were coincidental and tied to maintenance, and The Atlantic notes he blamed patching but not AI.1314 The Atlantic says President Donald Trump told the public to "Relax", and says that companies have been rapidly patching newly discovered flaws with the help of AI rather than simply replacing old equipment.1516

Semafor reports that White House National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross is out and being replaced by Kirsten Davies, with sources disputing whether he was fired or resigned.1718

Semafor also reports that Senators Maria Cantwell and Mike Rounds wrote to Clayton urging a national effort on AI-enabled cyber threats, which sources see as a sign of bipartisan legislative interest in January.1920
