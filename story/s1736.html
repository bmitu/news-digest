Distributed GPT-Rosalind agents analyze mouse brain CRISPR atlas

Last scan Sat Oct 24, 14:03 UTC
3 sources · tracked since Oct 21 · 3 new articles this week · single origin (OpenAI)

OpenAI, PerturbAI and Scripps Research say they used thousands of AI agents powered by GPT-Rosalind to analyze existing experimental data from millions of mouse brain cells in 32 minutes.12 The research team estimated that comparable human analysis, done one perturbation at a time, would take months to a year.3 OpenAI says that at the largest workload distributed agents cited more than 700 references against 11 for a single agent, and that blinded human experts preferred distributed-agent reports 75% of the time.45

The agents reportedly found that Tsc2 disruption altered dozens of neuronal-communication genes in the cerebellum while Tsc1 disruption produced little change, a difference OpenAI says may be relevant to human disease.67 However, Latent Space's newsletter summary cautions that these are hypotheses for experimental follow-up, not demonstrated treatments or validated drug targets.8

Sarah Teichmann of the University of Cambridge says AI could help more researchers explore cell atlases, and Tomasz Nowakowski of UCSF says the possible link between neuronal function and cholesterol metabolism shows how combining scattered clues can offer a new way to think about disease biology.910
