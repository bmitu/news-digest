OpenAI and Anthropic price cuts

Last scan Sat Oct 31, 06:24 UTC
2 sources · 443 points on Hacker News · single origin (Max Woolf)

Max Woolf's minimaxir.com post says OpenAI and Anthropic cut LLM prices by up to 80% within a few months, starting with OpenAI's July 30, 2026 announcement that GPT-5.6 Luna was cut by 80% to $0.20 / $1.20 / $0.02.12 The post says OpenAI announced GPT-6 Sol at $2 / $10 / $0.20, half the input and output price of the temporarily discounted GPT-5.6 Sol, and simultaneously released GPT-6 Luna at $0.10 / $0.50 / $0.01.34 Anthropic announced Claude Opus 5.5 that day at $4 / $20 / $0.20, which the post says is a 40-60% total cost reduction versus Opus 5, with Anthropic stating that the model requires less compute to serve and that its pricing reflects that.56

The post says GPT-6 Sol underperformed, so a week later OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Sol and halved the cache price to $2 / $10 / $0.10, making the model family cumulatively 75% cheaper.78 The author says the cuts also apply to subscription harnesses such as Codex and Claude Code and that the models do not appear to have cut corners on output quality.910

Max Woolf suspects the July GPT-5.6 Luna cut was meant to compete with smaller open-weight models such as DeepSeek V4 Flash, whose low cache-read proportion (2%, versus 5%-10% for OpenAI and Anthropic models) makes its effective cost lower than advertised.1112 He notes that Moonshot AI's Kimi K3.1 Pro scores 55 against Opus 5.5's 58 at half the input/output price, and that DeepSeek Flash V4.1 1017 is now hosted by 29 providers on OpenRouter versus 13 for the earlier model.1314

Woolf rejects the theory that prices are artificially low and will rise, arguing OpenAI and Anthropic are not a duopoly able to set prices, though he suspects subscription value may be cut before base model prices if capital runs short.1516
