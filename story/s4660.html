Chinese labs distilling US models

Last scan Sat Nov 21, 06:54 UTC
2 sources · single origin (OpenAI)

The OECD AI Incidents Monitor, summarizing press coverage dated mid-November 2026 including a Reuters exclusive, says Chinese researchers and companies are increasingly using US model outputs to train domestic systems, a practice it says Beijing encourages to offset US chip export restrictions.12 The monitor's AI-generated assessment classifies this as an AI hazard rather than an incident, since no direct harm has yet occurred, though it flags intellectual property and national security concerns.3

OpenAI published a post on combating "adversarial distillation," describing how it detects and disrupts unauthorized use of its models' outputs to train other models that lack comparable safeguards.45

On defenses, OpenAI reports that in an adaptive black-box evaluation the average attack success rate for extracting protected reasoning on GPT-6.2 Astra with updated safeguards was below 1% per attack type, substantially lower than on GPT-6 Astra with its original safeguards.67 OpenAI says its API now ties protected reasoning to the conversation history that produced it, dropping later reasoning if an earlier message is rewritten, and blocks older model families from reading it.8910

OpenAI argues that export controls and distillation defenses work together to help maintain a U.S. lead in frontier AI.11
